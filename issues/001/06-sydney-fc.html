There is no gentle way into this season. For the first time ever, the A-League Men campaign opens with a Sydney Derby: Sydney FC against Western Sydney Wanderers, under the lights at Allianz Stadium on Friday night. It will be the 45th meeting between the clubs, and if you've never been to one, here's a warning: it is loud. Brilliantly, gloriously, ear-ringingly loud.

And this one comes with an extra twist. The man in the Wanderers dugout, Ufuk Talay, was Sydney FC's coach until March, when he was replaced by Patrick Kisnorbo. Talay has since crossed the city to the enemy. You could not script it better if you tried.

Word Watch

Derbynoun
A match between two teams from the same city or area. It's pronounced "dar-bee" in England, "der-bee" in Australia, and either way it means trouble for the losers.
Cacophonynoun
A loud, jumbled mixture of sounds, like 40,000 people singing different songs at once.
Partisanadjective
Strongly supporting one side. A partisan crowd cheers its own team and boos the other.

Last seasonSo close

Sydney FC's 2025–26 season ended in heartbreak. After finishing fifth in the regular season, they went on a terrific run through the finals all the way to the Grand Final, only to lose 1–0 to Auckland FC in New Zealand, with Cam Howieson scoring the only goal. Auckland became the first New Zealand club ever to win the A-League Men championship. The Sky Blues will be desperate to go one better.

Matchday guideHow to do derby night properly

Watching on TV is fine. Being there is completely different. You hear the cacophony before you even see the stadium, and you feel the anticipation building as the teams come out. Here's how to make the most of it.

Getting there

Allianz Stadium is at Moore Park, right next to the Sydney Cricket Ground. The easiest way to arrive is the light rail (the L2 and L3 lines) to the Moore Park stop, which is only a short walk away. On big nights the roads around the stadium get very busy, so leave the car at home if you can. Kick-off is 8pm, but aim to arrive an hour early: watching the warm-up and the crowd filling up is half the fun.

Your derby-night checklist

  • Ticket (on your phone, charged)
  • Something sky blue: scarf, jersey or face paint
  • A warm layer, as October nights get chilly
  • Opal card or contactless card for the light rail
  • A refillable water bottle (check the stadium rules)
  • Your loudest voice

Watch like a pundit

Next time you're at a live game, try this. For five minutes, ignore the ball completely and watch just one player, say Sydney's captain. Where does he stand when Sydney have the ball? Where does he go when they lose it? Who is he pointing at and shouting to? You'll be amazed how much of football happens away from the ball, and television never shows you.
