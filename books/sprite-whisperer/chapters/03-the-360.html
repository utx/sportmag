Chapter 3
Slide. Spin. Smash.

Okay. This is it. This is the move. If you watched the video, you heard me say it about a hundred times: "Slide, 360, pickaxe!" Every time I found a pumpkin, I did exactly the same thing, and every time, a Sprite came flying out. I was honestly losing my mind. "There is no way this is working every time."

Here it is, one step at a time, with the actual screenshots.

The 360 Pickaxe, step by step

Step 1: Pickaxe out, and slide in

Pumpkin spotted, dead ahead. Pickaxe out, sprint, then crouch to slide the last few metres.

Switch to your pickaxe the moment you spot the pumpkin, so your hands are free. Sprint towards it, then slide in for the last stretch. It's faster than running, and it looks cool. Looking cool is important.

Step 2: The 360

Mid-spin. Swing your camera all the way round, a full circle, right next to the pumpkin.

As you reach the pumpkin, whip your camera all the way round in one full circle: a 360. (It's called that because a full turn is 360 degrees.) I'm picky about this part. One time I said, "I didn't come from the right angle," and went round to try it from another side. Another time: "That wasn't a good 360." Precision, chat.

Step 3: Smash!

Facing the pumpkin, SMASH prompt showing. Hit it.

Finish the spin facing the pumpkin and hit the Smash button. Hold it until the pumpkin bursts.

Step 4: Grab your Sprite

There it is, floating in the air, waiting for you. Grab it, read the card, and celebrate appropriately.

Real talk: does the 360 actually work?

Okay, chat. Time for me to be honest with you, because a real Sprite Whisperer respects the truth.

Remember what we learned in Chapter 2? Every Pumpkin Chest drops a Sprite. Every single one. So when I said "It works every time!", I was right, but not because of the spin. It worked every time because pumpkins always give you a Sprite, spin or no spin.

The Sprite you get is chosen at random by the game when the pumpkin opens. There's no evidence that spinning round first changes which Sprite you get, and Epic has never said it does. So the 360 is really a ritual: something I do the same way every time because it feels lucky. Some people might even call it a superstition.

And you know what? That's totally fine. Lots of top athletes have rituals too: bouncing the ball the same number of times before a serve, or putting their boots on in the same order before every match. A ritual can help you feel calm, focused and confident. It just doesn't control the dice.

The brain trick that fooled me

Why did the 360 feel so powerful? Because of something scientists call confirmation bias. When the trick seemed to work, I yelled about it ("Three in a row!"). When it didn't, I explained it away ("That wasn't a good 360"). My brain remembered the hits and quietly forgot the misses.

Everybody's brain does this. The trick is to notice it.

Be a scientist: test it yourself

Here's how to find out for real. It's called a fair test. Next time you play, keep a tally on paper:

  • Smash some pumpkins with the 360 and some without. Try to do about the same number of each.
  • Write down what you get every time, not just the exciting ones. That's how you beat confirmation bias.
  • Don't decide after three pumpkins. Three is a tiny sample. Twenty is better. Fifty is better still.
  • Then compare. If the 360 really worked, the top row would have a lot more Trick or Treat Sprites than the bottom row.

So, is it the 360 or is it coincidence? My honest guess: the pumpkins are doing the work, and the probability of a Trick or Treat Sprite is the same whether you spin or not. But I'm still doing the 360. Every time. Because it's fun, and because, chat… what if?
