Chapter 6
Pick your fights

Here's a strange thing about Fortnite: the player with the most eliminations often doesn't win. That's because every fight is a risk. Even if you win, you lose health, shield and ammo, and you make a lot of noise. Do that too many times and someone eventually catches you at the wrong moment.

The players who win the most aren't the ones who fight the most. They're the ones who choose their fights well. They fight when the odds are on their side, and they walk away (or sneak away) when they're not.

The third party

Here's one of the most important ideas in Fortnite. When two players (or teams) fight, the loud noise spreads across the map. A third player hears it, sneaks over, waits for the fight to finish, then attacks the winner, who is hurt, low on ammo, and probably healing. This is called third-partying, and it happens in almost every match.

That gives you two lessons, one for each side:

  • Be the third party. When you hear a big fight, you don't have to rush in. Be an opportunist: get closer quietly, wait until one side wins, then strike while they're weak. It needs patience, but it's how lots of matches are won.
  • Don't get third-partied. After you win a fight, assume someone heard it. Don't stand in the open celebrating or looting. Move to cover, heal up, then loot carefully, glancing around between each pickup.

Win the fights you take

  • Take the high ground. Being above your opponent means you can see more of them than they can of you, and it's harder for them to reach you.
  • Use cover. Fight from behind something: a wall, a rock, a tree, a car. Peek out, shoot, and duck back. Never stand in an open field trading shots.
  • Peek from the right. Your camera sits over your character's right shoulder. So when you peek out from the right-hand side of cover, you can see round the corner while most of your body stays hidden. Peeking from the left exposes much more of you.
  • Push when they're cracked. The moment you break their shield (white damage numbers, Chapter 1), they'll want to heal. That's the time to rush in.
  • Swap, don't stop. Fire your shotgun, then swap straight to your SMG or rifle to keep the damage coming while the shotgun gets ready again.

The art of the getaway

Sometimes a fight goes wrong. You're losing, you're low on health, and there are more of them than you thought. Knowing when to retreat isn't cowardly. It's smart. A player who escapes with 20 health can heal and win the match. A player who stays and fights to the end goes back to the lobby.

Four ways to get away

Common mistake: chasing the elimination

You knock someone's shield off, they run, and you chase them across the map, through a field, round a hill... straight into their teammates, or a different team who were watching the whole time. If someone escapes, it's often best to let them go. Your job is to win the match, not every single fight.

Try this drill: the three-second pause

For one session, every time you see an enemy who hasn't seen you, count to three in your head before shooting. Use those three seconds to check: Am I healed? Have I got the right weapon out? Is there a better spot? You'll be surprised how often the answer changes what you do.
