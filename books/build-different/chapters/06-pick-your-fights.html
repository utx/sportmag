Chapter 6
Pick your fights

Here's a curious fact about Fortnite: the player with the most eliminations frequently doesn't win. That's because every fight carries a risk. Even when you win, you lose health, shield and ammunition, and you make an enormous amount of noise. Repeat that too often and, sooner or later, somebody catches you at exactly the wrong moment.

The players who win most often aren't the ones who fight most often. They're the ones who choose their fights wisely, engaging when the odds favour them and walking (or sneaking) away when they don't.

Should I take this fight?

Before you start shooting, run through a few quick questions. It sounds slow, but with practice the whole check takes about a second.

You won't always be able to tick every box. Sometimes you have to fight, because someone is chasing you or standing between you and the safe zone. But the more boxes you can tick before the first shot, the more fights you'll win. Good players take calculated risks, not wild ones.

The third party

This is one of the most important ideas in the whole game. When two players (or teams) fight, the noise carries across the map. A third player hears it, creeps closer, waits for the fight to finish, then attacks the winner, who is injured, short of ammunition and probably in the middle of healing. This is called third-partying, and it happens in almost every match.

That gives you two lessons, one for each side of the situation:

  • Be the third party. When you hear a big fight, there's no need to rush in. Be an opportunist instead: approach quietly, wait until one side wins, then strike while they're weakened. It demands patience, but it's how countless matches are won.
  • Don't get third-partied. After winning a fight, assume someone heard it. Don't stand in the open celebrating or looting. Move to cover, heal, and only then loot carefully, glancing around between each pickup.

Win the fights you take

  • Take the high ground. Being above your opponent means you can see more of them than they can see of you, and it's harder for them to reach you.
  • Use cover. Fight from behind something solid, such as a wall, a rock, a tree or a car. Peek out, shoot and duck back again. Never stand in an open field trading shots.
  • Peek from the right. Your camera sits over your character's right shoulder, so when you peek from the right-hand side of cover, you can see round the corner while most of your body stays hidden. Peeking from the left exposes far more of you.
  • Push when they're cracked. The moment you break their shield (white damage numbers, Chapter 1), they'll want to heal, which makes it the perfect moment to rush in.
  • Swap, don't stop. Fire your shotgun, then switch immediately to your SMG or rifle to keep the damage flowing while the shotgun prepares its next shot.

The art of the getaway

Sometimes a fight goes badly: you're losing, your health is low, and there are more opponents than you expected. Knowing when to retreat isn't cowardly; it's intelligent. A player who escapes with 20 health can heal and still win the match, whereas a player who stubbornly fights to the end goes straight back to the lobby.

Four ways to get away

Common mistake: chasing the elimination

You knock someone's shield off, they flee, and you chase them across the map, through a field and round a hill... directly into their teammates, or into a different team who had been watching the whole time. When someone escapes, it's usually best to let them go. Your goal is to win the match, not every individual fight, and giving up a tempting advantage is sometimes the smartest decision available.

Try this drill: the three-second pause

For one session, whenever you spot an enemy who hasn't seen you, count to three in your head before shooting. Use those three seconds to check: Am I healed? Do I have the right weapon out? Is there a better position nearby? You'll be surprised how often the answers change what you decide to do.
