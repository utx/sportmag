Chapter 4
Ears wide open

Have you ever been eliminated by someone you never even saw? It feels deeply unfair. Usually, though, the player who caught you didn't possess superpowers. They simply knew where you were before you knew where they were, and the main way they found out was by listening.

Fortnite is a remarkably noisy game, and almost everything you do produces a sound: running, opening chests, smashing walls, healing and, of course, shooting. Good players treat those sounds as clues, and this chapter will turn you into a detective who can interpret them.

Headphones are a superpower

If possible, play with headphones. TV speakers blend all the sounds together, making it hard to tell whether footsteps are to your left or right, in front of you or behind. Headphones separate them clearly. Make sure the game volume is loud enough to hear footsteps, and turn the music down if it's drowning everything else out. (Look after your ears, too: if you can't hear someone speaking to you while you're wearing them, they're too loud.)

If you can't use headphones, or you're deaf or hard of hearing, switch on Visualize Sound Effects (Chapter 2), which shows you where sounds are coming from as icons on your screen. Plenty of players use both together.

Two things stand out. First, crouch-walking is almost silent, because crouching makes your footsteps much quieter, which is ideal for creeping up on someone or slipping away unnoticed. Second, almost everything useful is loud. Whenever you open a chest, smash a wall, build or shoot, you're announcing your position to everyone nearby. You can't avoid that completely, but you can choose when to make noise.

Listen like a detective

Whenever you hear something, ask yourself three questions:

  1. Which direction? Left, right, ahead or behind? Above or below, on the stairs or the floor beneath you?
  2. How close? Loud and clear means close; faint and muffled means further away, or behind walls.
  3. What are they doing? This is the clever part, because it allows you to deduce what you should do next.

Eyes open, too

Sound tells you about things close by, and your eyes handle everything else. Good players develop a few looking habits until they become completely automatic:

  • Look around frequently. Every few seconds, swing your view round to check behind you, especially while looting. It takes half a second and prevents a great many nasty surprises.
  • Check the minimap. It shows the storm, your teammates' positions and any markers, so glance at it whenever things are quiet.
  • Watch the player count. The number of players remaining tells you how crowded the Island still is. If lots are left and the circle is already small, expect fights everywhere.
  • Climb high to see far. From a hill, a rooftop or a tall build, you can spot players long before they spot you.
  • Notice movement. Human eyes are superb at detecting motion, so stand still for a second and scan the horizon: a player sprinting across a field will leap out at you.

Be quiet yourself

Now reverse the situation. If other players are listening for you, make yourself difficult to hear. Being stealthy wins fights before they even begin.

  • Crouch-walk when you suspect enemies are close, especially inside buildings.
  • Don't sprint into a fight. Sprinting is noisy, and you can't shoot while doing it.
  • Don't harvest or open chests immediately after hearing someone nearby. Deal with them first.
  • Heal before you push, not halfway through, because healing sounds reveal your position and leave you defenceless.

Pro move: predict where they'll be

The very best players don't merely react to sounds; they anticipate. If you hear footsteps on the stairs below, you know which doorway they'll emerge from, so you aim at it before they appear. If the storm is closing in from the north, you can predict which way other players will be running. It's like knowing where the ball will land before it has even been kicked. That level of awareness is what people mean by "game sense".

Try this drill: eyes closed

During a quiet moment in a match (or on a Creative island with a friend), close your eyes for five seconds and simply listen. Point to where you think each sound is coming from, then open your eyes and check. It sounds odd, but it trains your ears to judge direction, and you'll be astonished by how much more you start to hear.

Common mistake: looting with blinkers on

Opening chests and sorting your inventory feels urgent, so it's easy to stare at your loot and forget about the world around you. That's precisely when players get caught. Make it a habit: open a chest, take what you need, then look around before moving on to the next one.
