Chapter 4
Ears wide open

Have you ever been eliminated by someone you never even saw? It feels unfair. But usually, the player who got you didn't have superpowers. They just knew where you were before you knew where they were. And the main way they knew was by listening.

Fortnite is a noisy game, and almost everything you do makes a sound: running, opening chests, smashing walls, healing, shooting. Good players treat those sounds like clues. This chapter turns you into a detective.

Headphones are a superpower

If you can, play with headphones. TV speakers mash all the sounds together, and you can't easily tell whether footsteps are to your left or right, in front or behind. With headphones, you can. Make sure the game volume is loud enough to hear footsteps, and turn the music down if it's drowning things out. (And look after your ears: if you can't hear someone talking to you while you're wearing them, they're too loud.)

If you can't use headphones, or you're deaf or hard of hearing, switch on Visualize Sound Effects (Chapter 2). It shows you where sounds are coming from as icons on your screen. Plenty of players use both.

Two things jump out. First, crouch-walking is almost silent. Crouching makes your footsteps much quieter, which is perfect for sneaking up on someone, or sneaking away. Second, almost everything useful is loud. Every time you open a chest, smash a wall, build or shoot, you're telling everyone nearby where you are. You can't avoid that completely, but you can choose when to make noise.

Listen like a detective

When you hear something, ask three questions:

  1. Which direction? Left, right, in front, behind? Above or below (on the stairs, or the floor beneath you)?
  2. How close? Loud and clear means close. Faint and muffled means further away or behind walls.
  3. What are they doing? This is the clever bit, and it lets you deduce what to do next.

Eyes open, too

Sound tells you about things close by. Your eyes do the rest. Good players have a few looking habits that become automatic:

  • Look around, often. Every few seconds, spin your view round to check behind you, especially while looting. It takes half a second and saves you from a lot of surprises.
  • Check the minimap. It shows the storm, your teammates' positions and markers. Glance at it every time things are quiet.
  • Watch the player count. The number of players left tells you how busy the Island still is. Lots left and the circle is small? Expect fights everywhere.
  • Get high to see far. From a hill, a rooftop or a tall build, you can spot players long before they spot you.
  • Notice movement. Your eyes are brilliant at spotting things that move. Stand still for a second and scan the horizon: a player running across a field jumps out.

Be quiet yourself

Now flip it round. If other players are listening for you, make yourself hard to hear. Being stealthy wins fights before they start.

  • Crouch-walk when you think enemies are close, especially inside buildings.
  • Don't sprint into a fight. Sprinting is noisy, and you can't shoot while sprinting.
  • Don't harvest or open chests when you've just heard someone nearby. Deal with them first.
  • Heal before you push, not halfway through (healing sounds give you away and make you a sitting duck).

Pro move: think where they'll be

The very best players don't just react to sounds. They anticipate. If you hear footsteps on the stairs below, you know which doorway they'll come out of, so you aim at it before they appear. If the storm is closing from the north, you can guess which way other players will be running. It's like knowing where the ball is going to land before it's kicked.

Try this drill: eyes closed

In a quiet moment in a match (or in a Creative island with a friend), close your eyes for five seconds and just listen. Point to where you think each sound is coming from, then open your eyes and check. It sounds strange, but it trains your ears to pick out direction, and you'll be amazed how much you start to hear.

Common mistake: looting with blinkers on

Opening chests and sorting your inventory feels urgent, so it's easy to stare at your loot and forget the world. That's exactly when people get caught. Make it a habit: open a chest, grab what you need, then look around before the next one.
