Chapter 3
The first sixty seconds

The Battle Bus doors open. A hundred players hold their breath. In the next sixty seconds, some of them will land in exactly the right place, grab a weapon and a shield, and be ready for anything. Others will land a second too late, find nothing but a pickaxe, and be back in the lobby before they've finished their chips.

The good news: the first minute is one of the easiest parts of Fortnite to get better at, because it's the same every match. Do it well a few times and it becomes a habit.

Step 1: choose before you jump

Good players decide where they're landing before the bus doors open. As soon as you're on the bus, open your map, look at the line the bus will fly along, and pick your spot. There are two big questions:

Hot or quiet? A hot drop is a popular, contested place where lots of players land. You'll get into a fight straight away, which is great practice for fighting, but you'll also get eliminated early quite often. A quiet drop is somewhere hardly anyone goes. You'll have time to loot in peace, but you'll get less fighting practice and the storm might chase you.

Close to the bus line, or far away? Places right under the bus's path are quick to reach, so they're often busy. Places far from the path take longer to reach, so they're usually quieter.

Which should you choose?

It depends on what you want to get better at. Want to win more matches? Land somewhere quiet, loot fully and play smart. Want to get better at fighting? Land hot, and accept that you'll lose a lot of early fights while you learn. Many good players mix it up: hot drops in casual matches to practise fighting, and quieter drops when they're trying to win. Our Tier Pressure book ranks this season's landing spots.

Step 2: the dive

Once you jump, you're skydiving. Skydiving is faster than gliding, so the longer you can stay in a skydive, the faster you'll reach the ground. Your glider opens automatically when you get close to the ground, so you don't need to worry about crashing.

So the trick is:

  • For a spot near the bus line: wait until the bus is close, then jump and point straight down. You'll drop like a stone and land before players who opened their gliders early.
  • For a spot far away: jump earlier and skydive at a shallower angle (looking ahead rather than straight down) to cover distance, then glide the last part.
  • Look around as you fall. Count the other gliders near you. If five people are heading for the same building, you might want to swerve to the next one.

Step 3: land on something useful

In a hot drop, whoever lands next to a weapon first usually wins the first fight. So don't just land "in the town". Aim your trajectory at something specific:

  • A chest. You can often hear and see chests from the air: they glow and make a twinkly humming sound. Landing on a roof right above one is a classic move.
  • Floor loot. Weapons lying on the ground glow too.
  • A spot with a backup plan. If somebody beats you to the first chest, where will you go next? Pick a building with two or three chests close together.

Notice what's not on that list: being picky. In the first scramble, any weapon is better than no weapon. A common grey pistol beats a pickaxe every time. You can upgrade later.

Common mistake: the pickaxe chase

You land, and an enemy lands right next to you. Neither of you has a gun. So you chase each other round a house swinging pickaxes, until a third player turns up with a shotgun and eliminates you both. If you don't have a weapon, don't chase: find one. The first player to pick up a gun almost always wins.

Step 4: loot fast, not forever

Once you have a weapon and some shields, the danger isn't other players. It's spending too long looting. Players who open every single box in a town often get caught by the storm, or by a team that's already finished looting and is looking for a fight. A good rule: once you have a shotgun or SMG, a longer-range weapon, and some shields, you're ready. Everything else is a bonus.

In Build mode, there's one extra job: materials (often called mats). Before the first storm closes in, try to have at least a couple of hundred wood from trees, pallets and furniture. Harvesting with your pickaxe is noisy, so don't do it when you think enemies are close.

Pro move: the landing plan

Pick one landing spot and land there for ten matches in a row. You'll learn where every chest is, which roof to aim for, and where enemies usually come from. After ten matches, your first minute will be fast and smooth, almost like instinct. Pros do exactly this: they have favourite spots that they know like their own bedroom.

Try this drill: the stopwatch landing

Ask someone to time you (or count in your head) from the moment you jump out of the bus to the moment your feet touch the ground. Try to beat your time over five matches by jumping later and diving straighter. Then try timing how long it takes to have a weapon and a shield. Under 30 seconds is excellent.
