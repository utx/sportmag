Chapter 7
Brick by brick

Building is the thing that makes Fortnite different from every other battle royale game. In a split second, you can put a wall between you and a bullet, make a staircase to the top of a hill, or trap an opponent in a box. It looks impossibly fast when pros do it. But every pro started exactly where you are: placing one wall, very slowly, and getting shot while they did it.

This chapter is for Build matches. If you only play Zero Build, skip ahead to Chapter 8 (but you might enjoy this anyway).

The four pieces

Every structure in Fortnite, from a tiny hiding box to a sky-high tower, is made from just four pieces. Each one takes up one square of an invisible grid that covers the whole Island. (Each square is about five metres wide, a bit bigger than a car parking space.)

When you're about to build, a see-through outline shows where the piece will go. Blue means it can go there. Red means it can't. Builds must connect to the ground or to another build that does: knock out the bottom of a tower and the whole thing collapses.

Three materials

Every piece costs 10 materials (or "mats"), and you can carry up to 500 of each kind. Editing a piece is free. You collect materials by hitting things with your pickaxe, or by picking them up from chests and from eliminated players.

Wood builds fastest, so it's the best choice when you need a wall right now. But it's the weakest, and it can catch fire. Metal is the most durable, but takes longest to reach full strength, so it's best for something you'll stay behind for a while, like a base in the final circle. Stone sits in the middle. Most players build with wood in fights, simply because it's quickest and the easiest to find.

Don't run out!

Five hundred wood sounds like a lot, but it's only 50 pieces, and a big fight can use 200 or more. Keep an eye on your materials, and top up whenever it's safe. Running out of mats in a build fight is like running out of ammo in a gunfight.

Move 1: the panic wall

This is the first move every builder learns, and the one that saves you most often. Someone starts shooting at you. Before you even turn round to look, put a wall between you and them. Then add a ramp behind it for extra cover. It takes under a second, and it buys you time to think: shoot back, heal, or escape.

The trick is to make it automatic. You hear a shot hit you, you press the wall button. No thinking. That takes practice (see the drill at the end).

Move 2: box up

A box (sometimes called a 1x1) is four walls around you, with a floor or cone on top. It's your safe room. Inside it, you can heal, reload, and catch your breath. Pros do it so fast it looks like a magic trick.

Move 3: the ramp rush

To charge at someone who's above you, or to cross open ground safely, you build a ramp and run up it, putting a wall in front of each ramp as you go. The wall blocks bullets coming at you, and the ramp keeps you climbing. Ramp, wall, ramp, wall: it's a sequence, like a dance step. Many players also put a floor under each ramp, so that if someone shoots out the bottom, the whole thing doesn't collapse.

Move 4: taking height

In build fights, the player who is higher up usually has the advantage: they can see you and shoot down at you, while you can only see their builds. Good builders are always looking for ways to get above their opponent. The fastest way is a move called 90s (nineties), where you build a wall and a ramp, turn 90 degrees, jump onto the ramp and repeat, climbing straight up in a tight spiral. It's tricky, and it takes plenty of practice. Learn the box and the ramp rush first. Then, when they feel easy, look up a 90s tutorial and practise it in Creative.

Editing: doors, windows and surprises

Any piece you've built can be edited. Walls and floors have a 3 by 3 grid of squares. Select some squares, confirm, and those squares disappear, leaving a door, a window or a gap. Edits are free, and quick edits win fights: you make a hole, shoot through it, and close it again before your opponent can react.

Switch on Confirm Edit on Release (Chapter 2), so that your edit happens the instant you let go. And remember that only you and your teammates can edit your builds. An enemy can't open your door: they have to shoot through it.

Simple Build and Simple Edit

On a controller, Fortnite has easier versions of building and editing. With Simple Build (switched on by default since Chapter 7 began), you press one button and the game builds a piece depending on where you're looking: straight ahead makes a wall, down makes a floor, up makes a cone, and up at an angle makes a ramp. Simple Edit lets you choose a ready-made edit with one button. Both are brilliant for learning. Many players switch them off later, because the normal controls give you more exact control. Try both and see what you prefer.

Common mistake: building too much

Some new builders build a giant tower every time they hear a shot. It takes ages, uses all their materials, and makes so much noise that every player nearby comes to see what's going on. Build with a purpose: a wall for cover, a box to heal, a ramp to get height. Then stop.

Try this drill: the ten-minute build warm-up

Find a quiet Creative island with plenty of space (or a free-build practice island). Then, every day for a week:

  1. Panic walls: spin round and place a wall, 20 times. Then wall and ramp, 20 times.
  2. Boxes: box up 10 times, as fast as you can, without missing a wall.
  3. Ramp rush: ramp, wall, ramp, wall, ten pieces high. Do it 5 times.
  4. Edits: build a wall, edit a window, reset it, edit a door, reset it. Ten of each.

Go slowly at first. Speed comes later, once the moves are smooth and fluent. Slow and correct beats fast and messy.
