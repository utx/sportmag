Chapter 9
Storm chasers

You can out-aim everyone, out-build everyone and out-think everyone, and still lose to the one enemy that never misses: the storm. It closes in on every match, squeezing a hundred players into a smaller and smaller space until only one is left standing.

Moving around the map to stay ahead of the storm is called rotating. Good rotation is one of the biggest differences between players who reach the final circles and players who don't. And, unlike aiming, you can get much better at it just by understanding how the storm works.

How the storm works

The storm moves in steps called phases. In each phase, a white circle appears on your map, showing where the next safe zone (the "eye") will be. You get a countdown to reach it. Then the storm wall moves in until it matches the circle. Then there's another wait, a new, smaller circle, and the whole thing happens again, until the storm closes completely.

Your minimap helps: when you're outside the next circle, a white line shows the shortest way into it. If you're caught inside the storm itself, a blue line shows the quickest way out.

The storm gets nastier

Being in the storm hurts, and it hurts more as the match goes on. Remember from Chapter 1: storm damage skips your shield and goes straight to your health.

Look at that jump. Early on, the storm takes just 1 health per second, so you can survive in it for well over a minute (and even longer if you heal). But in the final phases it takes 10 per second: a player on full health has just ten seconds before they're knocked out. (These numbers are from the Fortnite Wiki's table for standard Battle Royale in Chapter 7. Epic tweaks them now and then, but the pattern of "gentle early, deadly late" never changes.)

There's more. If you spend too long in the storm over a whole match, you get storm sickness, and the storm hurts you three times as much. So even early on, the storm isn't somewhere to hang around.

Storm Surge: why hiding forever doesn't work

Some players try to win by hiding in a bush all match and never fighting. Fortnite has a rule to stop that, called Storm Surge. If there are still lots of players left when a storm phase ends, the players (or teams) who have done the least damage to other players get zapped: 25 damage every 5 seconds, even inside the safe zone. A message under your minimap shows whether you're safe from it. The lesson: hiding is fine for a while, but you'll need to join in at some point.

The golden rule: rotate early

The storm is inevitable: you can't stop it, so plan for it. Most players who get caught by the storm made the same mistake: they left too late. They were finishing a fight, or looting one more house, or just didn't look at the timer. Then they had to sprint across open ground, with the storm behind them and other players watching.

Players who rotate early get there first. They choose a good spot (a building, a hill, a rock) and settle in. Then they are the ones watching the late players run across the open.

Edge or middle?

When you reach the new safe zone, where should you go? There are two main strategies, and both work:

Many good players use the edge in the early and middle phases, because it's easy to defend and they can catch late players coming in from the storm. Then, in the final phases, they move towards the middle if they can, so they're never stuck on the wrong side of a small circle. Whichever you choose, the most important thing is to choose on purpose.

Planning your route

As soon as a new circle appears, open your map and ask:

  1. How far is it? If it's a long way, leave now. If you're already inside, relax and loot (but keep an eye on the timer).
  2. What's in the way? Rivers, open fields and valleys are dangerous. Hills, towns and forests give you cover.
  3. Where will everyone else go? Other players will be heading the same way. Busy towns on the edge of the circle are often full of fights.
  4. Can I get a ride? A car or boat makes long rotations easy, though vehicles are noisy and easy to spot.
  5. Where will I end up? Pick a destination with cover: a building, a hill or a big rock, ideally on high ground.

Common mistake: one more chest

The storm timer says 20 seconds, and there's a chest glowing in the next room. You grab it. Then another one. Then you realise the storm is on top of you and the safe zone is miles away. Every player has done this. Decide on a "leave by" time when the circle appears, and when it arrives, go, even if there's loot left.

Pro move: use the storm as a shield

Players arriving late have to come from the storm side. If you're on the edge of the zone, you know exactly which direction they'll come from, and they'll be in a hurry, out in the open, often hurt. Position yourself where you can see the storm wall, and you can catch them as they arrive.

Try this drill: storm narrator

For one match, every time a new circle appears, say your plan out loud: "Circle's to the north-east, about a minute away. I'll go along the river through the trees and take the hill." Saying it forces you to make a real plan, instead of just running towards the white line.
