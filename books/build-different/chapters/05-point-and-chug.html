Chapter 5
Point and chug

Aiming is the skill everybody wants to be good at. And it's true: if you can't hit your shots, it's hard to win fights. But here's something surprising. Great aim isn't mostly about fast reactions. It's mostly about where your crosshair already is before the enemy appears, and about when you pull the trigger. Both of those can be learned.

Rule 1: crosshair at head height

Where do you point your crosshair while you're walking around? Lots of players look at the floor in front of them, or at the sky. Then, when an enemy appears, they have to move their aim a long way, and they miss.

Good players keep their crosshair at head height, pointed at the places enemies are most likely to appear: doorways, corners, the tops of ramps, the edges of rocks. When someone steps out, the crosshair is already almost on them. It's called crosshair placement, and it's the biggest aiming upgrade you can make.

Try this drill: the doorway game

Next time you walk through a building, practise moving your crosshair to every doorway, window and corner before you can see round it, always at head height. Do it for a whole match, even when there's nobody there. After a few days, you'll notice yourself doing it without thinking.

Rule 2: stand still to shoot straight

In Fortnite, your bullets don't always go exactly where your crosshair points. They wander a little, and the amount they wander (the spread) depends on what you're doing. Many players call this "bloom", because the crosshair blooms (grows bigger) as you shoot.

So, especially at medium and long range: stop, crouch if you can, then shoot. Running around while spraying feels exciting, but most of those bullets miss.

Rule 3: the first shot is the best shot

Many weapons, especially rifles and pistols, are perfectly accurate on the first shot, as long as you're standing still or crouching. If you hold the trigger down, the spread grows and grows. If you pause for a moment between shots, it resets.

That's why good players tap-fire or fire in short bursts at long range: tap, pause, tap, pause. It feels slower, but far more of your bullets land. Up close, with an SMG or automatic shotgun, holding the trigger is fine, because the target is too close to miss by much.

Different weapons, different aim

  • Shotguns are for close range. Aim at the upper body or head, because the pellets spread out and a headshot does extra damage. Take your time with the first shot: with a slow shotgun like a Pump, a miss gives your opponent time to hit back.
  • SMGs and pistols are for spraying at close to medium range. The skill here is to track your target: keep your crosshair moving smoothly with them as they run and jump.
  • Assault rifles are for medium range. Aim down sights, stand still or crouch, and fire in short bursts.
  • Long-range weapons (sniper rifles and the like, when they're in the game) need you to lead your target: aim slightly ahead of a moving player, because the bullet takes time to arrive.

Aim assist (controller players)

If you play on a controller, Fortnite gives you a little help called aim assist. When you aim down sights near an enemy, your crosshair slows down and gently follows them. It doesn't do the work for you, but it helps. To get the most from it, aim down sights when the enemy is close to your crosshair, then make small adjustments.

Reload at the right time

Running out of ammo in the middle of a fight is one of the most common ways to lose. Good habits:

  • Reload after every fight, before you do anything else, even if your magazine is only a little bit empty.
  • Reload behind cover, never in the open.
  • Switch weapons instead of reloading when you're mid-fight. Swapping to another loaded gun is usually faster than reloading. (In Chapter 7, Fortnite started saving your reload progress if you switch away, so you won't lose it.)
  • Listen for their reload. When an enemy stops shooting, they may be reloading. That's your moment.

Common mistake: panic spraying

An enemy appears, your heart jumps, and you hold down the trigger while jumping and running in circles. It almost never works. The players who win surprise fights are the ones with composure: they take a quick breath, put their crosshair on the target, and fire with purpose. Calm hands are precise hands.

Pro move: aim training islands

Fortnite's Creative mode has lots of islands built by players for practising aim, with targets that pop up, move and strafe. Spending just five or ten minutes in one before you play warms up your hands, the same way footballers warm up before a match. Chapter 12 has more on how to practise, and how to choose islands safely.
