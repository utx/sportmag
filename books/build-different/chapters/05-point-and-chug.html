Chapter 5
Point and chug

Aiming is the skill everybody wants to master, and it's true that if you can't hit your shots, winning fights is difficult. But here's something that surprises most players: great aim depends far less on lightning-fast reactions than on where your crosshair already is before the enemy appears, and on when you choose to pull the trigger. Both of those can be learned.

Rule 1: crosshair at head height

Where does your crosshair point while you're walking around? Many players stare at the floor in front of them, or up at the sky. Then, when an enemy appears, they have to drag their aim a long way, and they miss.

Good players keep their crosshair at head height, pointed at the places where enemies are most likely to appear: doorways, corners, the tops of ramps and the edges of rocks. When someone steps into view, the crosshair is already almost on them. This habit is called crosshair placement, and it's the single biggest aiming upgrade you can make.

Try this drill: the doorway game

Next time you walk through a building, practise moving your crosshair to every doorway, window and corner before you can see round it, always at head height. Keep it up for an entire match, even when nobody is there. After a few days, you'll notice yourself doing it without thinking.

Rule 2: stand still to shoot straight

In Fortnite, your bullets don't always travel exactly where your crosshair points. They wander slightly, and the amount they wander (the spread) depends on what you're doing at the time. Many players call this "bloom", because the crosshair blooms, or expands, as you fire.

The lesson, especially at medium and long range, is simple: stop, crouch if you can, then shoot. Running around while spraying bullets feels exciting, but the majority of those bullets miss.

Rule 3: the first shot is the best shot

Many weapons, particularly rifles and pistols, are perfectly accurate on the first shot, provided you're standing still or crouching. If you hold the trigger down, the spread keeps growing; if you pause briefly between shots, it resets.

That's why skilled players tap-fire or shoot in short bursts at long range: tap, pause, tap, pause. It feels slower, yet far more of your bullets actually land. Up close, with an SMG or automatic shotgun, holding the trigger is fine, because the target is too near for the spread to matter much.

Different weapons, different aim

  • Shotguns are designed for close range. Aim at the upper body or head, because the pellets scatter and a headshot deals extra damage. Take your time with the first shot: with a slow shotgun such as a Pump, a miss gives your opponent time to strike back.
  • SMGs and pistols are for spraying at close to medium range. The essential skill here is to track your target, keeping your crosshair moving smoothly with them as they run and jump.
  • Assault rifles suit medium range. Aim down sights, stand still or crouch, and fire in short bursts.
  • Long-range weapons (sniper rifles and similar, when they're in the game) require you to lead your target, aiming slightly ahead of a moving player because the bullet takes time to arrive.

Aim assist (controller players)

If you play on a controller, Fortnite provides some help called aim assist. When you aim down sights near an enemy, your crosshair slows down and gently follows them. It won't do the work for you, but it certainly helps. To make the most of it, aim down sights when the enemy is already close to your crosshair, then make small adjustments.

Reload at the right time

Running out of ammunition mid-fight is one of the most common reasons for losing. These habits prevent it:

  • Reload after every fight, before you do anything else, even if your magazine is only slightly empty.
  • Reload behind cover, never out in the open.
  • Switch weapons instead of reloading during a fight, since swapping to another loaded gun is usually quicker. (Since Chapter 7 began, Fortnite saves your reload progress when you switch away, so you won't lose it.)
  • Listen for their reload. When an enemy suddenly stops shooting, they may be reloading, and that's your opportunity.

Common mistake: panic spraying

An enemy appears, your heart leaps, and you hold down the trigger while jumping and running in circles. It almost never works. The players who win surprise encounters are the ones with composure: they take a quick breath, place their crosshair on the target and fire with purpose. Calm hands are precise hands.

Pro move: aim training islands

Fortnite's Creative mode contains many player-built islands designed for aim practice, with targets that pop up, move and dodge from side to side. Spending just five or ten minutes on one before you play warms up your hands, in the same way footballers warm up before kick-off.
