Chapter 10
Squad goals

Playing Duos, Trios or Squads is different from playing Solos. You're not just trying to survive. You're part of a team, and a good team is far stronger than its players added together. Four average players who talk to each other, stick together and help each other will beat four brilliant players who all run off on their own.

Callouts: say it short, say it clear

A callout is a quick message telling your team something important. In a fight, there's no time for long speeches, so the best callouts are concise. A good callout answers three questions: who, where and what.

The "where" is the tricky part. "Over there!" doesn't help anyone, because your teammates can't see where you're looking. That's what the compass at the top of your screen is for. It shows numbers all the way round, from 0 (north) to 360. If you say "240", every teammate can turn to face 240 and look exactly where you're looking. Add a landmark ("on the hill", "in the blue house", "behind the big rock"), and they'll find the enemy in a second.

Markers: callouts without talking

Not everyone plays with voice chat, and that's fine. Fortnite's marker (sometimes called a ping) lets you point things out without saying a word. Press your Mark button while looking at something, and your teammates see a marker on their screens and minimaps. Mark an enemy, and it shows up as a red danger marker (especially if you switch on Mark Danger When Targeting, from Chapter 2). Mark an item, and your teammates can see what it is. Mark a spot on the map, and everyone knows where you want to go. Markers are quick, clear, and work in any language.

Stick together

The most common way squads lose is by splitting up. One player wanders off to loot a distant house, gets caught alone, and the rest of the team has to cross the map to help (usually too late). Good squads move together and coordinate:

  • Land together, or at least close enough to help each other.
  • Stay within shouting distance, about one building apart, not one town apart.
  • Fight together. If one player starts a fight, the others should join in straight away. Two against one wins almost every time.
  • Share. If you've got spare shields, a better weapon or extra materials, drop them for a teammate who needs them. A team where everyone has some healing is far stronger than one where one player has everything.

Some revive and reboot tips:

  • Make it safe first. Reviving takes about 10 seconds, and you can't shoot while you do it. If the enemy who knocked your teammate is still nearby, deal with them first, or (in Build mode) put walls up around you both. A revive in the open often ends with two knocked players instead of one.
  • Knocked? Crawl to cover. If you're the one who's down, crawl towards your teammates or behind something solid. Knocked players can crawl faster for a bit (it uses stamina) and roll to dodge shots. Don't crawl out into the open.
  • Reboot Vans are loud. A van charging up makes a big noise and shows a beacon above it. Everyone nearby will know what you're doing. Check the area first.
  • Missed the card? If you couldn't grab a teammate's Reboot Card in time, you can sometimes buy it back at a Reboot Van with Gold Bars (in-game money you collect during a match, not real money).
  • Share your stuff. A rebooted teammate arrives with almost nothing. Drop them shields and a weapon before the next fight.

Be the teammate everyone wants

The best teammates aren't always the best players. They're the ones who make everyone else better. They communicate, they share, and they encourage. Nobody plays well when someone is shouting at them.

  • Praise good plays. "Nice shot!" costs nothing and makes your team play better.
  • Don't blame. If a teammate makes a mistake, they already know. Saying "no worries, next one" keeps the team together.
  • Own your mistakes. "My fault, I pushed too early" is the sign of a great teammate.
  • Listen to the plan. If your squad decides to rotate, go with them, even if you'd rather loot one more house.

Playing safely with others

Only use voice chat with people you know in real life, like friends from school or family. Fortnite has parental controls that let a grown-up choose who can talk to you and send you messages, so ask them to help you set it up. If anyone online is unkind, asks personal questions, or asks you to share your account or password, don't reply: tell a grown-up, and use the in-game report and block options.

Try this drill: the silent squad

Play one match with your squad where nobody is allowed to talk: only markers. Then play the next one using only proper callouts (who, where, what). You'll quickly see which information really matters, and your callouts will get shorter and sharper.
