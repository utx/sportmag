Chapter 10
Squad goals

Playing Duos, Trios or Squads is fundamentally different from playing Solos. You're no longer simply trying to survive; you're part of a team, and a well-organised team is far stronger than its individual players added together. Four ordinary players who talk to one another, stay together and support one another will regularly defeat four brilliant players who all wander off on their own.

Callouts: say it short, say it clear

A callout is a quick message that tells your team something important. In the middle of a fight there's no time for speeches, so the most effective callouts are concise. A good callout answers three questions: who, where and what.

The "where" is the tricky part. Shouting "Over there!" helps nobody, because your teammates can't see which way you're facing. That's exactly what the compass at the top of your screen is for: it displays numbers all the way round, from 0 (north) to 360. If you say "240", every teammate can turn to face 240 and look precisely where you're looking. Add a landmark ("on the hill", "in the blue house", "behind the big rock") and they'll locate the enemy within a second.

Markers: callouts without talking

Not everyone plays with voice chat, and that's perfectly fine. Fortnite's marker (sometimes called a ping) lets you point things out without saying a word. Press your Mark button while looking at something, and a marker appears on your teammates' screens and minimaps. Mark an enemy, and it shows up as a red danger marker (especially if you've switched on Mark Danger When Targeting, from Chapter 2). Mark an item, and your teammates can see what it is. Mark a spot on the map, and everyone knows where you want to go. Markers are fast, unambiguous and work in any language.

Stick together

The most common way for squads to lose is by splitting up. One player wanders off to loot a distant house, gets caught alone, and the rest of the team has to cross the map to help, usually arriving too late. Successful squads move as a unit and coordinate their actions:

  • Land together, or at least close enough to support each other.
  • Stay within shouting distance: about one building apart, not one town apart.
  • Fight together. If one player starts a fight, the others should join in immediately, because two against one wins almost every time.
  • Share. If you have spare shields, a better weapon or extra materials, drop them for a teammate who needs them. A team in which everyone carries some healing is far stronger than one in which a single player hoards everything.

Some revive and reboot advice:

  • Make it safe first. Reviving takes about 10 seconds, and you can't shoot while you're doing it. If the enemy who knocked your teammate is still nearby, deal with them first, or (in Build mode) put walls up around you both. A revive attempted in the open frequently ends with two knocked players instead of one.
  • Knocked? Crawl to cover. If you're the one who's down, crawl towards your teammates or behind something solid. Knocked players can crawl faster for a short time (it uses stamina) and roll to dodge shots, but never crawl out into the open.
  • Reboot Vans are loud. A van charging up makes a lot of noise and displays a beacon above it, so everyone nearby will know exactly what you're doing. Check the area first.
  • Missed the card? If you couldn't collect a teammate's Reboot Card in time, you can sometimes buy it back at a Reboot Van with Gold Bars (in-game money you collect during a match, not real money).
  • Share your gear. A rebooted teammate arrives with almost nothing, so drop them shields and a weapon before the next fight.

Be the teammate everyone wants

The best teammates aren't always the most skilful players; they're the ones who make everyone around them better. They communicate clearly, share generously and encourage others, because nobody plays well while being shouted at.

  • Praise good plays. "Nice shot!" costs nothing and lifts the whole team.
  • Don't blame. If a teammate makes a mistake, they already know about it, and saying "no worries, next one" keeps the team united.
  • Own your mistakes. "My fault, I pushed too early" is the mark of a great teammate.
  • Follow the plan. If your squad decides to rotate, go with them, even if you'd rather loot one more house.

Playing safely with others

Only use voice chat with people you know in real life, such as friends from school or family members. Fortnite has parental controls that let a grown-up choose who can talk to you and send you messages, so ask them to help you set it up. If anyone online is unkind, asks personal questions, or asks you to share your account or password, don't reply: tell a grown-up, and use the in-game report and block options.

Try this drill: the silent squad

Play one match with your squad in which nobody is allowed to speak, using markers only. Then play the next using only proper callouts (who, where, what). You'll quickly discover which information genuinely matters, and your callouts will become shorter and sharper.
