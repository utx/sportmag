Chapter 2
Settings sorted

This is the least exciting chapter in the book, and possibly the most useful. Fortnite has hundreds of settings, hidden away in the menu. Most players never touch them. But a handful of switches can make you hear more, see more and fight better, straight away, without any practice at all.

You'll find everything below in the Settings menu, which you open from the lobby or during a match. Settings are grouped into tabs, such as Game, Video, Audio and Controller. Fortnite tweaks its menus from time to time, so if something isn't exactly where we say, look around the nearby tabs.

Word Watch

Sensitivitynoun
How strongly something reacts. In games, it's how far your view turns when you move your mouse or thumbstick.
Consistentadjective
Staying the same each time. A consistent player plays well again and again, not just once.
Indicatornoun
A sign, light or symbol that shows you something, like the arrow that shows which way a car is turning.
Customiseverb
To change something so that it suits you better.

Before you change anything

If you share the game with family members, ask before changing settings: they might like theirs the way they are. And if a setting makes things feel worse, you can always change it back. Nothing in this chapter is permanent.

The number one setting: see the sounds

If you only change one thing, make it this one. In the Audio settings, switch on Visualize Sound Effects. A ring appears around your crosshair, and whenever there's an important sound nearby (footsteps, gunshots, chests, vehicles, gliders), a little icon appears on the ring, pointing towards where the sound came from.

It was designed to help players who are deaf or hard of hearing, and it's brilliant for them. But it helps everybody: even with great headphones, it's easy to miss a sound, or to mix up whether footsteps are in front of you or behind. Lots of top players keep it switched on all the time. There's more about using sound in Chapter 4.

Sensitivity: slow is often faster

Sensitivity decides how far your view turns when you move your mouse or thumbstick. High sensitivity means a tiny movement spins you round. Low sensitivity means you need a bigger movement to turn the same amount.

Lots of new players think higher is better, because it feels fast. But very high sensitivity makes it hard to aim precisely: your crosshair keeps flying past the target. Most good players use something in the middle, and many use lower settings than you'd expect.

The most important rule isn't the number. It's to stop changing it. Your hands learn exactly how far to move to turn a certain amount, and every time you change your sensitivity, they have to learn it all over again. Pick something comfortable, play with it for at least a couple of weeks, and only change it if there's a clear problem (like always overshooting, or not being able to turn round fast enough). That's how you become consistent.

There are also separate sensitivities for aiming down sights, for build mode and for edit mode. Leave those alone at first. Once you're comfortable, you can customise them one at a time.

Controller or keyboard?

You can be great at Fortnite with either. Professional players use both. Controller players get a little help from aim assist, which gently slows and pulls your crosshair onto targets. Keyboard and mouse players can usually turn and aim more precisely, and have more buttons for building. Use whichever you have and enjoy. The skills in this book work for both.

If you play with a controller, look at the controller presets: Old School, Quick Builder, Combat Pro and Builder Pro. Most experienced builders choose Builder Pro, because each building piece has its own button. If you're new, Combat Pro or the default layout is fine. Choose one and, just like sensitivity, stick with it.

Pro move: frames, not fancy graphics

On a computer, the Video settings let you choose between beautiful graphics and a smoother game. Many competitive players pick the smoother option (on PC, there's a Performance rendering mode), because more frames per second makes aiming feel quicker and more responsive. It makes the Island look a little plainer, but it can help on older computers. Ask a grown-up before changing these.

Common mistake: copying a pro's settings

It's tempting to look up a famous player's exact settings and copy them. But they chose those numbers for their hands, their screen and their years of practice. They're a good starting point at most. The best settings are the ones that feel right to you.

Try this drill: settings check-up

Spend ten minutes in the settings menu before your next session. Switch on Visualize Sound Effects and Auto Sort Consumables. Then play three matches and write down one thing that felt better and one thing that felt worse. Keep the good, undo the bad.
