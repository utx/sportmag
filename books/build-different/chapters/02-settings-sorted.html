Chapter 2
Settings sorted

This is probably the least thrilling chapter in the book, and quite possibly the most useful. Fortnite has hundreds of settings tucked away in its menus, and most players never touch them. Yet a handful of switches can help you hear more, see more and fight better immediately, without a single minute of practice.

Everything below lives in the Settings menu, which you can open from the lobby or during a match. Settings are grouped into tabs such as Game, Video, Audio and Controller. Fortnite rearranges its menus from time to time, so if something isn't exactly where we describe, explore the neighbouring tabs.

Word Watch

Sensitivitynoun
How strongly something responds. In games, it's how far your view turns when you move your mouse or thumbstick.
Consistentadjective
Staying reliably the same each time. A consistent player performs well again and again, not just occasionally.
Accessibilitynoun
How easy something is for everyone to use, including people with disabilities.
Customiseverb
To adjust something so that it suits your own needs or preferences.

Before you change anything

If you share the game with family members, ask before changing settings, because they might prefer theirs the way they are. And if a setting makes things feel worse, you can always switch it back: nothing in this chapter is permanent.

The number one setting: see the sounds

If you change only one thing, make it this. In the Audio settings, switch on Visualize Sound Effects. A ring appears around your crosshair, and whenever there's an important sound nearby (footsteps, gunshots, chests, vehicles or gliders), a small icon appears on the ring, pointing in the direction the sound came from.

It's an accessibility feature, designed to help players who are deaf or hard of hearing, and it's brilliant for them. But it benefits everybody, because even with excellent headphones it's easy to miss a sound, or to confuse footsteps in front of you with footsteps behind. Plenty of top players leave it switched on permanently. Chapter 4 explains much more about using sound.

Sensitivity: slow is often faster

Sensitivity determines how far your view turns when you move your mouse or thumbstick. With high sensitivity, a tiny movement spins you round; with low sensitivity, you need a larger movement to turn the same distance.

Many new players assume that higher must be better, because it feels fast. In reality, very high sensitivity makes precise aiming extremely difficult, since your crosshair keeps flying past the target. Most good players use something in the middle, and many use lower settings than you'd expect.

The most important rule, though, isn't about the number. It's to stop changing it. Your hands gradually learn exactly how far to move to turn a certain amount, and every time you alter your sensitivity, they have to relearn it from scratch. Choose something comfortable, use it for at least a couple of weeks, and only adjust it if there's an obvious problem, such as constantly overshooting targets or being unable to turn round quickly enough. That's how you become consistent.

There are also separate sensitivities for aiming down sights, for build mode and for edit mode. Leave those alone at first; once you're comfortable, you can customise them one at a time.

Controller or keyboard?

You can be excellent at Fortnite with either, and professional players use both. Controller players receive a little help from aim assist, which gently slows your crosshair and draws it towards targets. Keyboard and mouse players can usually turn and aim more precisely, and have more buttons available for building. Use whichever you have and enjoy: the skills in this book apply to both.

If you play with a controller, look at the controller presets: Old School, Quick Builder, Combat Pro and Builder Pro. Most experienced builders prefer Builder Pro, because each building piece has its own dedicated button. If you're new, Combat Pro or the default layout is perfectly fine. Choose one and, just as with sensitivity, stick with it.

Pro move: frames, not fancy graphics

On a computer, the Video settings let you trade beautiful graphics for a smoother game. Many competitive players choose smoothness (on PC, there's a Performance rendering mode), because more frames per second make aiming feel quicker and more responsive. The Island looks slightly plainer, but older computers in particular can benefit. Ask a grown-up before changing these.

Common mistake: copying a pro's settings

It's tempting to look up a famous player's exact settings and copy them. But they chose those numbers to suit their own hands, their own screen and years of practice, so at best they're a starting point. The best settings are the ones that feel right to you.

Try this drill: settings check-up

Spend ten minutes in the settings menu before your next session. Switch on Visualize Sound Effects and Auto Sort Consumables, then play three matches and write down one thing that felt better and one that felt worse. Keep the improvements, and undo the rest.
