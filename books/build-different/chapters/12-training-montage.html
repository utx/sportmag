Chapter 12
Training montage

In every sports film, there's a training montage: the music kicks in, the hero runs up some steps, lifts some weights, falls over, gets up, and suddenly they're brilliant. Real life takes a bit longer than three minutes. But the idea is right: players get better by practising, not just by playing.

What's the difference? Playing is doing matches and hoping you improve. Practising is choosing one skill, working on it on purpose, and checking whether it's getting better. Ten minutes of real practice can teach you more than an hour of random matches.

One skill at a time

The biggest secret of deliberate practice is to focus on one thing. Not "get better at Fortnite" (too big), but "box up faster" or "keep my crosshair at head height" or "rotate before the timer hits 30 seconds". Small, clear goals are easier to practise and easier to measure.

Here's an example week. It's only a suggestion: swap things in and out to suit what you need to work on, and fit it around homework, sport and everything else.

A practice week (an example)
MONAim: 10 minutes on an aim-training island, then play.
TUEBuild or move: 10 minutes of drills from Chapter 7 or 8.
WEDDay off. Rest is part of training. Go outside!
THUReplay: watch one of your matches back. Find one mistake.
FRIFun day: play anything you like with friends. No pressure.
WEEKENDGame plan: pick one skill and focus on it in real matches.

Notice the day off and the fun day. Players who practise every day for hours tend to get tired, frustrated and bored, and stop improving. A good routine is short, regular and leaves time for everything else in life.

Warm up first

Footballers don't walk straight off the bus and take a penalty. They warm up. You can too. Five to ten minutes before your first match is enough:

  1. Aim: a few minutes of shooting at targets.
  2. Build or move: a few boxes, ramps and edits (Build), or a short parkour run (Zero Build).
  3. Then play. Your hands will be ready from the very first fight.

Creative islands: your training ground

Fortnite's Creative mode has thousands of islands made by players. Lots of them are built for practice: aim trainers with moving targets, edit courses where you race through walls, build-practice islands with unlimited materials, and one-against-one arenas for practising fights with a friend. You can find them by searching in the Discover menu for things like "aim trainer", "edit course" or "build practice".

Choosing islands safely

Creative islands are made by other players, not by Epic, and they aren't all suitable for younger players. Every island shows an age rating on its page, so check it before you play, and ask a grown-up if you're unsure. Some island makers also ask you to follow them, join their social media, or buy things: you don't have to do any of that to practise. Stick to islands that do what they say on the tin.

Watch yourself back: replays

Here's something the best players in the world all do: they watch their own matches back. On PC, PlayStation and Xbox, Fortnite can record your matches. Switch on Record Replays in the settings (Chapter 2), then find your matches in the Replays section of the Career tab in the lobby. (Replays aren't available on Nintendo Switch or mobile at the moment.)

In a replay, you can fly the camera anywhere, slow things down, and even watch from your opponent's point of view. It's like having a superpower: you can see exactly where the enemy was, what they did, and what you could have done differently.

Don't try to fix everything at once. Pick one thing from each replay to work on. That's how small improvements add up to big progress. And it's fine if replays feel a bit embarrassing at first: everybody's do. That's the point.

How Ranked works

Once you're comfortable, you might want to try Ranked. It's just like normal Battle Royale, but you're matched with players of a similar level, and you earn (or lose) progress based on how well you do. It's a great way to measure how much you're improving.

Things worth knowing about Ranked:

  • Placement and eliminations both count. The higher you finish, and the more eliminations you (or your team) get, the more progress you earn.
  • Late eliminations are worth more than early ones, and eliminating higher-ranked players is worth more too. So landing hot and getting knocked out early isn't a good way to climb.
  • Teams climb together. Everyone on a team gains or loses the same amount, and the team plays at the level of its highest-ranked player.
  • Build and Zero Build have separate ranks, so you can be Gold in one and Silver in the other.
  • Ranks reset each Ranked season, and a fresh start is decided by your recent results.
  • Some fun extras are switched off. This season, for example, Overrides and Loot Hacks don't appear in Ranked, and Sprites lose their special powers there, to keep things fair.

Should you play Ranked?

If you enjoy a challenge and like seeing your progress, yes! But remember the rank is just a measure, not a judgement of who you are. Plenty of brilliant players never bother with it. If Ranked starts making you stressed or grumpy, take a break from it (and read the next chapter).
