Chapter 8
Zero Build hero

In Zero Build, nobody can put up a wall to save themselves, and that changes everything. The Island itself becomes your shield, so every tree, rock, car, hill and house is a potential hiding place, and the way you move between them becomes your most powerful weapon.

Zero Build players are sometimes told that it's the "easy" mode. Don't believe it. The finest Zero Build players move like parkour athletes, read the map like a chessboard, and aim as accurately as anybody. Here's how to join them.

Your movement toolkit

Over the years, Fortnite has added a whole range of movement techniques, and in Zero Build you need every one of them. These are available to every player, with no special items required:

A few extra details make these moves considerably more useful:

  • Sprinting uses stamina. When your stamina runs out, you slow down until it refills, so don't waste it running round in circles. Save it for crossing open ground or escaping danger.
  • Sliding is faster downhill and slower uphill, and you can shoot while you slide. Sliding into a fight down a slope lets you arrive quickly while being difficult to hit, and if you slide into an opponent, you'll even kick them backwards.
  • Mantling cancels fall damage. If you're falling from a height and grab a ledge on the way down, you're safe.
  • You can perform up to three wall kicks in a row, each slightly weaker than the last. Chaining kicks between two walls that are close together is a sneaky way to reach a roof.
  • Ledge jumps: jump right at the edge of a drop while moving quickly, and you'll leap further and higher than with an ordinary jump.
  • Roll landings restore some of your stamina, so a big drop followed by a roll keeps you sprinting.

Combine them and you become remarkably agile: slide down a hill, hurdle a fence, wall kick onto a roof, and you've crossed an entire fight zone in a few seconds.

Cover is everything

The golden rule of Zero Build is straightforward: never stand where you can be shot from many directions at once. Before you move anywhere, identify where your next piece of cover is, then travel from cover to cover, like stepping stones across a river.

When you reach each piece of cover, pause for a moment to look and listen (Chapter 4), then choose the next one. It feels slower than sprinting straight to your destination, but you'll arrive with your health intact. Players who sprint across open fields are exposed the entire way, which makes them the easiest targets in the game.

Use the land

Success in Zero Build depends heavily on the terrain:

  • Hills are free high ground. In Build mode you can create height, but in Zero Build you have to find it. Hilltops, cliffs and tall buildings are prime real estate, particularly later in the match.
  • Valleys and rivers are traps. Anyone above you can see you clearly, so cross them quickly or go round.
  • Buildings are fortresses. They offer thick walls, doors that creak when enemies open them, and windows to shoot from. In the late game, getting inside a good building can win you the match.
  • Behind the hill, not on top. On a hill, stay just behind the crest and peek over to shoot, because standing right on top displays your whole body to everyone.

Mobility items are your ramps

In Build mode, you escape by building; in Zero Build, you escape with mobility items, such as launch pads, grenades that fling you into the air, gliders, vehicles and anything else that lets you move fast. Each season brings different ones (this season, the Overdrive Grenade and the Crash Pad Jr. are favourites). Whatever they happen to be called, try to carry one, because a mobility item can rescue you from a losing fight, carry you away from the storm, or lift you onto the high ground in the final circle.

Zero Build bonus: Overshield

As Chapter 1 explained, Zero Build usually gives you up to 50 extra Overshield, which refills automatically if you avoid damage for a few seconds. In other words, breaking line of sight is a form of healing: take some damage, duck behind a rock, wait a few seconds, and some of your protection returns for free.

Pro move: slide-jump

Start a slide down a slope, then jump at the end of it. You'll carry your momentum into the jump and travel much further than with a normal jump. Chain slide, jump, slide, jump down a long hill, and you'll cover ground very quickly indeed.

Common mistake: peeking the same spot twice

You peek over a rock, shoot, and duck down. Then you peek from exactly the same place again, and your opponent, who has been aiming at that spot all along, hits you instantly. After each peek, reposition: pop up from the other side of the rock, or slide to a different piece of cover entirely.

Try this drill: the parkour run

Choose a route through a town on the Island (or a Creative parkour island) and try to cross it using as many different moves as possible: slide, hurdle, mantle, wall kick and roll landing. Then attempt it without stopping once. You'll discover which walls you can kick off, which roofs you can mantle onto, and how far a slide-jump carries you, all of which you'll use in real fights.
