Chapter 14
Myth or fact?

Fortnite players say a lot of things in the lobby, at school and in video comments. Some of them are true. Some are half true. And some are complete rubbish that everyone repeats anyway. Time to be sceptical and check the evidence.

"Skins make you a better player"

Verdict: MYTH. Outfits, back blings, pickaxes and emotes are cosmetic: they change how you look, not how you play. Every player has exactly the same health, speed and weapons, whatever they're wearing. A player in the default outfit can beat anyone. (Some people even think default skins are a disguise for brilliant players. That's a myth too: it just means they're wearing the default.) So if anyone tells you to buy something to get better, they're wrong.

"Crouching makes your shots more accurate"

Verdict: FACT. Crouching and standing still are the most accurate ways to shoot, and jumping is the least accurate (Chapter 5). Crouch-walking is also much quieter. The catch: you move more slowly when you're crouched, so it's not for running across open fields.

"Higher sensitivity is always better"

Verdict: MYTH. High sensitivity lets you turn fast, but makes precise aiming much harder. Most good players use medium or even low settings. What matters most is choosing one and sticking with it (Chapter 2).

"You have to build to be good"

Verdict: MYTH. Zero Build is a full part of Fortnite, with its own Ranked ladder and its own expert players. It needs different skills (movement, positioning, using the land), not fewer. Building is a great skill to learn, but it's not the only way to be good.

"Your shield protects you from the storm"

Verdict: MYTH. This catches out loads of players. Storm damage, and fall damage, skip your shield completely and go straight to your health (Chapter 1). You can survive longer in the storm by healing your health, but a full shield bar won't help at all.

"The player with the most eliminations wins"

Verdict: MYTH. A Victory Royale goes to the last player (or team) standing, not the one with the most eliminations. Plenty of matches are won by players who chose their fights carefully and only fought when they had to (Chapter 6). In Ranked, eliminations do help you climb, but placement matters a lot too.

"You can win by hiding all match"

Verdict: MYTH (mostly). Hiding works for a while, and good positioning is a real skill. But Fortnite's Storm Surge rule punishes players who haven't done enough damage while lots of players are still left (Chapter 9), and eventually the circle gets so small that you have to fight. You can't hide from the last opponent.

"You can't shoot while sprinting"

Verdict: FACT. When you sprint, your character puts their weapon away to run faster. So sprinting into a fight means you can't shoot back until you stop. Slide instead (Chapter 8): you keep your speed and can still shoot.

"Landing hot makes you better faster"

Verdict: HALF TRUE. Landing at busy spots does give you lots of fighting practice in a short time. But you'll also lose many matches in the first minute, and you won't practise rotating, endgames or anything else. A mix is best: some hot drops for fighting practice, some quieter ones to practise the rest of the game.

"You need expensive gear to be good"

Verdict: MOSTLY MYTH. A smoother screen, a comfortable controller or good headphones can help a little, especially at the very top level. But the skills in this book (listening, positioning, rotating, aiming, keeping calm) matter far more than anything you can buy. Plenty of excellent players started on old consoles and hand-me-down headphones.

Be a myth-buster

Next time someone tells you a Fortnite "fact", ask: "How do you know?" Is there evidence, like a patch note, a test, or a reliable website? Or is it just something people say? Testing a misconception in a Creative island with a friend is a fun way to find out for yourself. That habit of checking works for everything you read online, not just Fortnite.
