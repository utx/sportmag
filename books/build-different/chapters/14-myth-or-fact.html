Chapter 14
Myth or fact?

Fortnite players claim all sorts of things in the lobby, at school and in video comments. Some of those claims are true, some are half true, and some are complete nonsense that everyone repeats regardless. It's time to be sceptical and examine the evidence.

"Skins make you a better player"

Verdict: MYTH. Outfits, back blings, pickaxes and emotes are purely cosmetic: they change how you look, not how you play. Every player has exactly the same health, speed and weapons, whatever they're wearing, so a player in the default outfit can beat anyone. (Some people even believe default skins are a disguise worn by secretly brilliant players. That's a myth too: it simply means they're wearing the default.) If anyone tells you to buy something in order to improve, they're wrong.

"Crouching makes your shots more accurate"

Verdict: FACT. Crouching and standing still are the most accurate ways to shoot, while jumping is the least accurate (Chapter 5). Crouch-walking is also much quieter. The drawback is that you move more slowly while crouched, so it's no good for crossing open fields.

"Higher sensitivity is always better"

Verdict: MYTH. High sensitivity lets you turn quickly, but it makes precise aiming considerably harder. Most good players use medium or even low settings, and what matters most is choosing one and sticking with it (Chapter 2).

"You have to build to be good"

Verdict: MYTH. Zero Build is a complete part of Fortnite, with its own Ranked ladder and its own expert players. It demands different skills (movement, positioning and using the land), not fewer. Building is a valuable skill to learn, but it isn't the only route to being good.

"Your shield protects you from the storm"

Verdict: MYTH. This catches out an enormous number of players. Storm damage and fall damage bypass your shield entirely and go straight to your health (Chapter 1). You can survive longer in the storm by healing your health, but a full shield bar won't help in the slightest.

"The player with the most eliminations wins"

Verdict: MYTH. A Victory Royale goes to the last player (or team) standing, not to whoever collected the most eliminations. Plenty of matches are won by players who chose their fights carefully and only fought when they had to (Chapter 6). In Ranked, eliminations do help you climb, but placement matters a great deal too.

"You can win by hiding all match"

Verdict: MYTH (mostly). Hiding works for a while, and good positioning is a genuine skill. However, Fortnite's Storm Surge rule punishes players who haven't dealt enough damage while many opponents are still alive (Chapter 9), and eventually the circle becomes so small that you have to fight. Nobody can hide from the final opponent.

"You can't shoot while sprinting"

Verdict: FACT. When you sprint, your character puts their weapon away in order to run faster, so sprinting into a fight means you can't shoot back until you stop. Slide instead (Chapter 8), because sliding preserves your speed and still lets you shoot.

"Landing hot makes you better faster"

Verdict: HALF TRUE. Landing at busy spots certainly gives you plenty of fighting practice in a short time. But you'll also lose many matches in the first minute, which means you won't practise rotating, endgames or anything else. A balance is best: some hot drops for fighting practice, and some quieter ones to rehearse the rest of the game.

"You need expensive gear to be good"

Verdict: MOSTLY MYTH. A smoother screen, a comfortable controller or good headphones can help slightly, particularly at the very highest level. But the skills in this book (listening, positioning, rotating, aiming and staying calm) matter far more than anything you can buy, and plenty of excellent players started out on old consoles with hand-me-down headphones.

Be a myth-buster

Next time someone tells you a Fortnite "fact", ask: "How do you know?" Is there evidence, such as a patch note, a test or a reliable website? Or is it simply something people say? Testing a misconception on a Creative island with a friend is an entertaining way to find out for yourself, and that habit of checking applies to everything you read online, not just Fortnite.
