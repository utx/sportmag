About this book

Big Brain Chug is an unofficial fan book made by The Chug Jug, a fan magazine about Fortnite. It isn't made, approved or endorsed by Epic Games. It's free, and it will never ask you to spend money. If you ever want to buy anything in Fortnite, ask a grown-up first.

Portions of the materials used are trademarks and/or copyrighted works of Epic Games, Inc. All rights reserved by Epic. This material is not official and is not endorsed by Epic.

Pac-Man, Tetris and Sonic the Hedgehog belong to other companies (Bandai Namco, The Tetris Company and SEGA). They're mentioned in this book only because areas of the Island were inspired by them this season. We haven't shown any of their pictures.

Version

The Fortnite parts of this book describe Chapter 7, Season 4: Override, with the Fortnitemares update (version 42.30) on the Island, as it was on 12 October 2026. The Honey Sprite was due in the next update, on 15 October 2026; what we say about it comes from Epic's early details and may change.

Where the facts came from

We checked every history, science and maths fact against at least one reliable source, and where sources disagreed, we've said so in the text.

Falling and parachutes: NASA's Apollo Lunar Surface Journal (the Apollo 15 transcript, for David Scott's exact words); NASA Science (the hammer and feather drop); History.co.uk and the Mental Floss and Engines of Our Ingenuity histories of the parachute; Galileo's Two New Sciences (1638), as described by historians of science.

Circles and pi: PBS NOVA, "Approximating Pi"; ProofWiki, "Archimedes' Limits to Value of Pi"; NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, "How Many Decimals of Pi Do We Really Need?"

Percentages and fractions: Smith's Dictionary of Greek and Roman Antiquities (the centesima rerum venalium); the British Museum (the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus).

Tetris and shapes: Wikipedia's articles on Tetris and Alexey Pajitnov, cross-checked with other histories of the game.

Speed and sound: the US Air Force (Edwards Air Force Base) on Chuck Yeager's flight; Astronomy magazine, "Oct. 14, 1947: Flying faster than sound"; World Athletics (Usain Bolt's 100 m world record, still standing in September 2026); histories of Concorde.

Algorithms: Wikipedia, "Ghosts (Pac-Man)"; Game Informer's 2020 interview with Toru Iwatani, and the Pac-Man Wiki's collection of his interviews; histories of al-Khwarizmi and the House of Wisdom; Wikipedia, "Note G" (Ada Lovelace); National Geographic Education, "World's First Computer Bug".

Building: Australian Geographic, "On this day: Sydney Harbour Bridge opens"; the National Museum of Australia (the bridge, and Francis de Groot); the University of Melbourne's Encyclopedia of Australian Science and Innovation; Structurae; histories of the Great Pyramid and the Pantheon.

Maps: WorldAtlas and Mental Floss on the Mercator projection; the State Library of New South Wales, "Eight days in Kamay"; the National Museum of Australia, "Kamay Botany Bay".

Codes: Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Caesar cipher"; Guinness World Records and the Smithsonian's Mall History project (Morse's first telegraph message); the IEEE Milestone "First Breaking of Enigma Code by the Team of Polish Cipher Bureau, 1932–1939"; the Crypto Museum (the Bombe).

Bees and bats: Thomas Hales, "The Honeycomb Conjecture" (1999); the Nobel Prize press release, 1973; Brito and others, "Brood comb construction by the stingless bees Tetragonula hockingsi and Tetragonula carbonaria" (Swarm Intelligence, 2012) and Live Science; Gerald Wilkinson, "Reciprocal food sharing in the vampire bat" (Nature, 1984); the National Honey Board's "The Honey Files".

Tulip mania: Smithsonian Magazine, "There Never Was a Real Tulip Fever" (on Anne Goldgar's research); the John Innes Centre, "Why do tulips break?"

Myths: Today I Found Out and iFixit (blowing into cartridges); Scientific American and Sky at Night magazine (the Great Wall); How It Works and Practical Fishkeeping (goldfish memory); History.com (jack-o'-lanterns).

Data: NRICH (University of Cambridge), "Florence Nightingale: the compassionate mathematician"; histories of John Snow and the Broad Street pump; FNCS Global Championship 2026 results as reported in The Chug Jug, issue 1.

Fortnite details: Epic Games' update notes and in-game news, fortnite-api.com, the Fortnite Wiki, and our own research for Tier Pressure.
