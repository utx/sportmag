Here's every answer in the book, with the thinking that gets you there. If your answer was different, don't just fix it: find the exact step where your thinking went a different way. That's the step your brain will remember next time.

Some puzzles (especially the Spot the Flaw ones) have more than one good answer. If you found a different flaw and you can explain it clearly, give yourself the point.

Before You Drop

0.1 28 is perfect. How: the factors of 28 (except 28) are 1, 2, 4, 7 and 14. Check each one divides exactly: 28 ÷ 7 = 4, 28 ÷ 14 = 2. Add them: 1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14 = 28.

0.2 12 is abundant. How: its factors (except 12) are 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6. They add up to 16, which is more than 12.

0.3 The third perfect number is 496. How: 16 × 31 = 16 × 30 + 16 = 480 + 16 = 496. Its factors (except itself) are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 31, 62, 124 and 248. Add the first five: 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 = 31. Then 31 + 31 = 62, 62 + 62 = 124, 124 + 124 = 248, and 248 + 248 = 496. Perfect!

Spot the Flaw: Two things sharing one quality doesn't make them the same thing. A cat has four legs and a table has four legs, but a cat is not a table. Also, "perfect" means two different things here: a maths rule about factors, and Geno's opinion of himself.

1 · Bus Stop Science

1.1 9 steps in the 5th second, and 36 steps in total after 6 seconds. How: the odd numbers go 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11. The totals are the square numbers: after 6 seconds, 6 × 6 = 36 (or 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 36).

1.2 125 metres. How: after 5 seconds the total is 5 × 5 = 25 "steps", and each step is 5 metres, so 25 × 5 = 125 m. (Check the pattern: 5, 20, 45, 80, 125.)

1.3 312 years, then 174 years. How: 1797 − 1485 = 312, and 1971 − 1797 = 174.

1.4 Fishstick, Peely, Jonesy, Brite Bomber. Fishstick first, Brite Bomber last. How: Peely and Jonesy are a pair, Peely then Jonesy. Brite Bomber is somewhere after Jonesy, and Fishstick is somewhere before Peely. With only four places, the only order that works is Fishstick, Peely, Jonesy, Brite Bomber.

1.5 10 seconds, and 19 steps in the last second. How: 100 = 10 × 10, so it's the 10th square number. The steps in each second go up by the odd numbers, and the 10th odd number is 19 (it's always double the number of seconds, minus 1: 2 × 10 − 1 = 19). Check: 81 + 19 = 100.

Spot the Flaw: Mistake 1: weight doesn't make you fall faster. In Fortnite everyone falls the same, and in real life it's air resistance, not weight, that makes the difference. Mistake 2: even if he did land first, that doesn't mean he'd get the best loot. Where you land matters more than when.

2 · Storm Maths

2.1 Radius 5 m; about 31.4 m around (about 31 m). How: the radius is half the diameter. Circumference is about 3.14 × diameter = 3.14 × 10 = 31.4.

2.2 ¼, then 1⁄16 of the original. How: halving the radius leaves a quarter of the area. Halving it again leaves a quarter of a quarter: ¼ × ¼ = 1⁄16.

2.3 9 times smaller. How: the radius is multiplied by itself in the area rule. 300 × 300 = 90,000 and 100 × 100 = 10,000, and 90,000 ÷ 10,000 = 9. Or: one-third as wide means 1⁄3 × 1⁄3 = 1⁄9 of the area.

2.4 22⁄7, so Pi Approximation Day is 22 July (22/7). How: 3 whole ones are 21⁄7, plus 1⁄7 makes 22⁄7. And 22 ÷ 7 = 3.142857…, very close to pi.

2.5 Route B is about 57 metres further. How: the whole way around is 3.14 × 100 = 314 m, so half of it is 157 m. Straight across is 100 m. 157 − 100 = 57 m. Cutting across the middle is always shorter.

Spot the Flaw: Half as wide means a quarter of the space, not half, because the area depends on the radius times itself. Three-quarters of the room is gone.

3 · Shield Sums

3.1 70 shield = 70% = 7⁄10. How: 100 − 30 = 70. 70 out of 100 is 70%, and 70⁄100 simplifies to 7⁄10 by dividing top and bottom by 10.

3.2 Plan A ends with 100 shield; Plan B ends with only 50. Plan A is better. How: Plan A: 0 + 25 + 25 = 50, then the big potion adds 50 to make 100. Plan B: the big potion takes you to 50, and then the small potions don't work, because they only go up to 50. Order matters!

3.3 3 bandages, taking 10.5 seconds. How: 30 → 45 → 60 → 75, and then they stop working. 3 × 3.5 = 10.5 seconds.

3.4 9 pieces, healing 90. How: 1⁄5 of 15 is 3, so 3⁄5 is 3 × 3 = 9. Then 9 × 10 = 90.

3.5 (a) 6 body shots (b) 4 headshots (c) 5 shots. How: (a) 5 body shots do 5 × 36 = 180, not enough; 6 do 216. (b) 3 headshots do 162, not enough; 4 do 216. (c) Two headshots do 108, leaving 92. Two body shots do 72, not enough; three do 108. So 2 + 3 = 5 shots.

3.6 2⁄3 is bigger, by about 7 points (exactly 62⁄3). How: 60% of 100 is 60. 2⁄3 of 100 is 662⁄3. 662⁄3 − 60 = 62⁄3.

3.7 (a) ¾ = ½ + ¼ (b) 5⁄6 = ½ + 1⁄3 (c) 7⁄8 = ½ + ¼ + 1⁄8. How: start with the biggest unit fraction that fits, then see what's left. For 5⁄6: ½ is 3⁄6, which leaves 2⁄6 = 1⁄3.

Spot the Flaw: Geno's shield is really 75. Down 50% from 100 is 50. Up 50% of 50 is only 25 more, making 75. The two percentages are of different amounts, so they don't cancel out.

4 · Block Party

4.1 120 squares; and from 50 squares, 12 tetrominoes with 2 left over. How: 30 × 4 = 120. 12 × 4 = 48, and 50 − 48 = 2.

4.2 C. How: turn the L a quarter turn clockwise. The tall bar lies down along the top, and the foot that pointed right at the bottom now points down at the left. That's C. The others only match if you flip the shape over.

4.3 I = 10, T = 10, O = 8. The O has the smallest perimeter (all the others have 10). How: count the outside edges of each square that don't touch another square. In the O, each square hides two of its edges against neighbours, so the shape is more "squashed together".

4.4 B, and it clears 3 lines. How: the gap is one square wide in the bottom two rows, then two squares wide in the third row, opening to the left. Only the L-shaped piece B fits exactly, and then all three bottom rows are full.

4.5 I, O and T. Each one has a line of symmetry, so its mirror image is the same shape. S, Z, J and L all have mirror twins.

4.6 (a) 36°, because 3 × 108 = 324 and 360 − 324 = 36. (b) Four would need 432°, more than a full turn, so they'd overlap. (c) 2 × 135 = 270, leaving a 90° gap, which is exactly a square's corner.

Spot the Flaw: The first half is right: every block covers 4 squares. The second half is wrong: the O has a perimeter of 8, but all the others have 10. Same area doesn't mean same perimeter.

5 · Gotta Go Fast

5.1 12 Rings. How: you need 100 − 40 = 60 health, and 60 ÷ 5 = 12.

5.2 About 3 km, then about 1.5 km: the storm is coming closer. How: sound travels about 1 km every 3 seconds, so divide the seconds by 3. 9 ÷ 3 = 3; 4.5 ÷ 3 = 1.5.

5.3 90 km/h. How: 3 km in 2 minutes is 1.5 km each minute. 1.5 × 60 = 90 km in an hour.

5.4 (a) A bit more than 10 metres per second (about 10.4). (b) About 33 times faster. How: 100 m in 10 seconds would be exactly 10 m per second, but Bolt took less than 10 seconds, so he went a bit faster than that. 343 ÷ 10.4 is about 33. (Using 10, you'd get about 34: a fine estimate.)

5.5 About Mach 2. How: double 1,060 is 2,120, which is very close to 2,180. So Concorde was flying at about twice the speed of sound up there (just over Mach 2).

5.6 9:00 in the morning in New York. How: 10:30 + 3½ hours = 2:00 pm London time. New York is 5 hours behind, so 2:00 pm − 5 hours = 9:00 am. The strange part: the clocks say you landed an hour and a half before you took off!

Spot the Flaw: Geno has assumed that a cheetah can keep running at top speed for 9 hours. In fact, a cheetah can only sprint flat-out for a short burst, a few hundred metres, before it has to stop and rest. Always check your assumptions, not just your sums.

6 · Chomp Logic

6.1 D3, facing east. How: F F takes it from A1 to A3. R turns it to face east. F F F moves it to B3, C3, D3.

6.2 It ends on E6, facing north. It goes right past the Chug Jug on E5! Delete the last F to fix it. How: A1 → A3, turn right, → E3, turn left, then three F's: E4, E5, E6. One step too many.

6.3 A square, 2 squares on each side. It finishes back on A1, facing north, after moving 8 squares. How: path A1, A2, A3, B3, C3, C2, C1, B1, A1. Four lots of 2 forward steps = 8.

6.4 One answer: F F F R F F L F R F F. The smallest number of F commands is 8. How: E5 is 4 squares across and 4 squares up from A1, and each F moves just one square, so you need at least 4 + 4 = 8. The chest on C4 is on the way, so 8 is possible. Check the path: A2, A3, A4, B4, C4 (chest), C5, D5, E5. Other routes with 8 F's are just as good.

6.5 (a) F2 (b) C5 (c) B6. How: count four squares in the direction the player is facing.

6.6 Clyde north-west, Blinky north-east, Inky south-east, Pinky south-west. How: Blinky and Clyde share the two north corners. Clyde isn't in the east, so Clyde is north-west and Blinky is north-east. Inky is directly south of Blinky: south-east. Pinky gets the last corner.

Spot the Flaw: Clyde wasn't scared. He was following his rule: chase when far away, go to his corner when close. Geno invented a reason (fear) without any evidence. Clyde would have done exactly the same thing to anyone.

7 · Builder's Brain

7.1 (a) 60 materials (b) 3 boxes, with 20 wood left. How: 4 walls + 1 floor + 1 roof = 6 pieces, and 6 × 10 = 60. 3 × 60 = 180, and 200 − 180 = 20.

7.2 One piece, going diagonally from one corner to the opposite corner. It turns the rectangle into two triangles.

7.3 5 triangles: 4 small ones (including the upside-down one in the middle) and 1 big one.

7.4 13 triangles. How: 6 small ones pointing up, 3 small ones pointing down, 3 medium ones (made of 4 small ones each), and 1 big one: 6 + 3 + 3 + 1 = 13.

7.5 19 months; its 100th birthday is in 2032. How: August 1930 to August 1931 is 12 months. August 1931 to March 1932 is 7 more. 12 + 7 = 19. And 1932 + 100 = 2032.

7.6 (a) 30 (b) 55 (c) square numbers. How: 1 + 4 + 9 + 16 = 30. Add a 5 × 5 layer: 30 + 25 = 55.

Spot the Flaw: Geno judged by appearance: the towers look strongest, so he decided they must be doing the work. But looking important isn't evidence. The real work is done by the steel arch, which rests on giant steel pins at its base.

8 · Map Masters

8.1 (a) Chopped Shop (b) Shaken Sanctuary (c) E2 (d) Lifty Lodge (C1), Nightmare Neighborhood (C3) and Green Hill Zone (C4).

8.2 (a) Reality's Reign (b) Chopped Shop (c) south (d) west.

8.3 X is north, Y is south-west, Z is south-east.

8.4 South-east, and you'd need to turn 225° clockwise. How: north → east → south, then 45° anticlockwise is south-east. From south-east, clockwise to north: 45° to south, 90° more to west, 90° more to north. 45 + 90 + 90 = 225.

8.5 (a) 14 km (b) 4.5 cm. How: 7 × 2 = 14. And 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5.

8.6 (a) 975 (b) 1,304. How: 0.00658 × 0 = 0, so the answer is just 975. Then 658 × 50,000 = 32,900,000, and ÷ 100,000 = 329. 975 + 329 = 1,304.

Spot the Flaw: Geno forgot that flat maps of a round world have to stretch something. Mercator's map stretches places near the poles, like Greenland. And "experts made it" isn't enough: the map was made for sailing, not for comparing sizes. Always ask what something was designed to do.

9 · Vault Codes

9.1 CHUG JUG. How: go back three letters: F → C, K → H, X → U, J → G.

9.2 OODPD. The double letter LL turns into a double letter OO, and both A's turn into D's. Patterns survive the cipher, and code-breakers hunt for exactly these clues (double letters, repeated letters, and the most common letters, like E).

9.3 The shift is 5, and the message is VAULT OPEN. How: if AFZQY is VAULT, then V became A. Counting forward from V: W, X, Y, Z, A is 5 steps. Shift each letter of TUJS back 5: T → O, U → P, J → E, S → N.

9.4 (a) LLAMA (b) STORM.

9.5 528. How: "4 1 0: nothing is right" rules out 4, 1 and 0. From "8 4 5", two digits are right but 4 isn't, so the code contains 8 and 5. 8 isn't first and 5 isn't last. From "2 8 1", two are right but 1 isn't, so the code contains 2 and 8. 2 isn't first and 8 isn't in the middle. So the digits are 2, 5 and 8. The 8 isn't first or in the middle, so it's last. The 2 isn't first, so it's in the middle. That leaves 5 first: 528. Check "1 2 3": the 2 is right and in the right place. ✓

9.6 (a) 1,000 codes (000 to 999) (b) 2,000 seconds = 33 minutes 20 seconds (c) 10,000 codes. How: each dial multiplies the possibilities by 10: 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000. 2,000 ÷ 60 = 33 remainder 20. A four-dial lock would take 20,000 seconds, more than 5½ hours. Every extra character makes a password much harder to guess.

Spot the Flaw: Mistake 1: GENO123 is one of the first things anyone would guess. Putting "123" on the end of your name doesn't make it strong. Mistake 2: he told someone else. Never share your password with anyone, not even a friend.

10 · Sprite Science

10.1 12 bees for 1 teaspoon; 60 bees for 5. How: twelve twelfths make a whole. 5 × 12 = 60.

10.2 (a) 0.28 units less (b) about 7%. How: 4.00 − 3.72 = 0.28. As a fraction of 4, that's 0.28 ÷ 4 = 0.07, which is 7%.

10.3 (a) Fly towards the sun. (b) Fly directly away from the sun. (c) Fly at 90° to the left of the sun.

10.4 (a) 1⁄3 (b) ½ (c) 1⁄5. How: (a) 2 Vampires out of 6 Sprites = 2⁄6 = 1⁄3. (b) 3 of the 6 aren't Spooky Dash = 3⁄6 = ½. (c) First grab: 3⁄6 chance of a Spooky Dash. Then there are 5 left, and 2 are Spooky Dash: 2⁄5. Multiply: 3⁄6 × 2⁄5 = 6⁄30 = 1⁄5.

10.5 A. How: the newest Sprite must be Movement or Healing. All Healing Sprites are purple, and it isn't purple, so it can't be Healing. It must be Movement. (C and D might be true, but the facts don't tell us, so they're not certain. B is definitely false.)

Spot the Flaw: The first statement, "everything with wings can fly", is false: penguins, ostriches and emus all have wings and can't fly. An argument can follow a perfect pattern and still be wrong if it starts from a false fact. (And a cape isn't wings.)

11 · Shop Smarts

11.1 (a) 1,200 more (b) 2½ battle passes. How: 2,000 − 800 = 1,200. 800 + 800 = 1,600, and half of another 800 is 400, making 2,000.

11.2 Two 4-packs, for $4.00 (you get 8 lollies). It's cheaper than one 10-pack ($4.50), even though the 10-pack is better value per lolly. Best value isn't the same as cheapest for what you need.

11.3 900 V-Bucks, saving 300. How: 25% is a quarter. A quarter of 1,200 is 300. 1,200 − 300 = 900.

11.4 Both cost $40. The deals are exactly the same! How: Shop A: 5 × $10 = $50, and 20% of $50 is $10, so you pay $40. Shop B: you pay for 4 toys (4 × $10 = $40) and get the 5th free.

11.5 1 = Hurry up. 2 = Nearly gone. 3 = The fake bargain. 4 = Everyone's doing it. 5 = Big promises. Line 5 is the most obviously untrue: nothing you can buy could make you "never lose again", and "the best bundle ever made" is an opinion dressed up as a fact.

11.6 (a) 7 weeks (b) $3 left over (c) 8 weeks. How: after 6 weeks you have $30 (not enough); after 7 you have $35. $35 − $32 = $3. If you spend $1 a week, you save $4 a week, and 8 × $4 = $32 exactly.

Spot the Flaw: Geno fell for "Everyone's doing it" (lots of people wearing something doesn't make it the best) and "Hurry up!" (the countdown). And "never, ever again" probably isn't true: the Item Shop rotates, and most items come back eventually.

12 · Mythbusters Medkit

12.1 B is the fairest test. A only tests one game each, which could easily be luck. C changes two things at once (the Sprite AND the mode), so you can't tell which one made the difference. D collects opinions, not evidence.

12.2 Fishstick is wrong. Jonesy: 3⁄10 = 30%. Peely: 6⁄20 = 3⁄10 = 30%. Exactly the same! Fishstick only won twice as many games in Peely because they played twice as many games in it.

12.3 B. If putting the cartridge back in without blowing works just as well, then the blowing wasn't what fixed it. The others don't test the claim at all.

12.4 Possible hidden causes: (a) bigger towns have more buildings (so more fires) and more fire engines; also, fire engines are sent because of fires, not the other way round. (b) Age: older kids have bigger feet AND can read harder books. (c) Rain: rainy days make people buy umbrellas AND make footpaths slippery.

12.5 (a) Myth (b) Fact (c) Myth (d) Myth.

12.6 Not fair. Good reasons include: 6 wins against 5 is a tiny difference that could easily be luck; 20 players is a small test; we don't know if the two groups were equally good at the game; and one small experiment can't "prove" anything. The headline exaggerates.

Spot the Flaw: Two games is far too few to tell anything. There's no comparison with games played in other outfits. He might have won anyway (coincidence). And "100%" sounds impressive but means just two games. A fair test would need lots of games, with and without the outfit.

13 · Numbers Don't Lie

13.1 (a) 100 points (525 − 425) (b) 4 points (525 − 521).

13.2 Mean eliminations: 37. Mean points: 487.4. How: 39 + 45 + 42 + 28 + 31 = 185, and 185 ÷ 5 = 37. 525 + 523 + 521 + 443 + 425 = 2,437, and 2,437 ÷ 5 = 487.4.

13.3 In order: 28, 31, 39, 42, 45. The median is 39 and the range is 17 (45 − 28).

13.4 (a) 5 and 3 (b) about 1.7 times taller (5 ÷ 3) (c) No! 525 is only 2 more than 523, much less than 1% bigger.

13.5 (a) 6⁄20 = 3⁄10 = 30% (b) 12⁄20 = 60% (c) The storm is the easiest to prevent: start moving as soon as the next circle appears. (Falls are only 10%, and fights are the hardest to avoid.)

13.6 Team B wins, 21 to 15. How: Team A: 10 + 3 = 13 for the win, then 0 + 1 and 0 + 1, making 15. Team B: 5 + 2 = 7 each game, and 3 × 7 = 21. Doing well every time beat one big win, just like at the FNCS.

Spot the Flaw: Geno's axis starts at 101, so his bar is 3 units tall (104 − 101) and yours is 1 unit tall (102 − 101). That's why it looks three times taller. The real difference is just 2 points, about 2%. Hardly "destroyed".

14 · The Final Circle

Circle 1 36. They're the square numbers, the same totals Galileo found for falling balls (1, 4, 9, 16…).

Circle 2 1⁄64. How: each halving leaves a quarter of the area: ¼ × ¼ × ¼ = 1⁄64.

Circle 3 VICTORY ROYALE.

Circle 4 1 = B, 2 = A, 3 = D. Sentence C is about feathers in general, which doesn't fit anywhere. Look for linking words: "After all" explains why people believed something, "Instead" shows what Galileo did instead of timing falls directly, and "Still" sets up the Moon experiment.

Circle 5 Peely: Lifty Lodge, Dumpster Dive. Jonesy: Freaky Fields, Spooky Dash. Fishstick: Heatwave Harbor, Vampire. Brite Bomber: Stone Sanctum, Honey. How: Peely is at Lifty Lodge (clue 1). Jonesy can't be at Stone Sanctum or Heatwave Harbor (clue 3), so he's at Freaky Fields. Fishstick and Brite Bomber share Stone Sanctum and Heatwave Harbor. The Heatwave Harbor player found Vampire (clue 2), but Brite Bomber found Honey (clue 4), so Brite Bomber is at Stone Sanctum and Fishstick is at Heatwave Harbor with Vampire. Spooky Dash and Dumpster Dive are left for Jonesy and Peely. Jonesy is at Freaky Fields, which didn't get Dumpster Dive (clue 5), so Jonesy has Spooky Dash and Peely has Dumpster Dive.

Circle 6 C, the cross. When you fold the straight line of six, the fifth and sixth squares land on top of the first two. The 2 by 3 rectangle can't fold up without squares overlapping and one side left open. And with five in a row, the fifth square wraps round on top of the first.

Circle 7 About 2 km, then 4 km: it's moving away.

Circle 8 Pack B, by 4 cents per lolly. How: A: $1.20 ÷ 3 = 40c each. B: $1.80 ÷ 5 = 36c each.

Circle 9 B. Everyone who reached the final circle had a full shield, and Raven didn't, so Raven can't have reached it. We don't know how Raven was knocked out (A), whether everyone with a full shield got there (C), or whether Raven had any shield at all (D).

Circle 10 (a) 120° (b) 270° (c) 150 seconds, so you can run 375 metres. How: 20 minutes is 1⁄3 of an hour, and 1⁄3 of 360 is 120. 45 minutes is ¾ of an hour, and ¾ of 360 is 270. 2 minutes 30 seconds is 60 + 60 + 30 = 150 seconds. 150 ÷ 10 = 15 lots of 25 metres, and 15 × 25 = 375.
