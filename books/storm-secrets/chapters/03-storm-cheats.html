Chapter 3
The storm’s sneaky trick

The storm is coming. A new circle appears on your map, and you have to get inside it before the purple wall closes in. Stay outside and the storm chips away at your health until you're back in the lobby.

You've done this a thousand times. But have you ever noticed that the end of a match gets crowded way faster than it should? You're not imagining it. The storm has a sneaky trick. Once you know it, you'll never get caught rotating late again.

Storm survival, the pro way

  • Move the moment the circle appears. Don't finish looting that last house. The champions in chapter 14 won by getting to the next circle first and letting everyone else run into them.
  • Cut straight across. Running around the edge of the storm takes far longer than going straight through the middle.
  • Travel along cover. Trees, rocks and buildings, not open fields.
  • Grab a Storm Scout. This Sprite gives you a burst of speed after the storm hurts you. Level it all the way up and it shows you where future circles will be.

The sneaky trick

Here's the secret. When the next circle is half as wide as the last one, you don't lose half your space. You lose three-quarters of it. Look at these two circles drawn on squared paper:

Half as wide, a quarter of the space
Each little square is 1 unit by 1 unit

The big circle covers about 50 squares. The small one is exactly half as wide, but it covers only about 12 and a half squares: a quarter of the space. That's because the space inside a circle depends on its width multiplied by itself. Half the width, times half the width, is a quarter. So every time the circle shrinks, the lobby gets squashed much harder than it looks.

True story: the man who trapped a circle

Wrap a string around any circle (a coin, a plate, a Ferris wheel) and it's always a bit more than three times as long as the circle is wide. About 3.14 times, in fact. That number is called pi, and its digits go on forever: 3.14159265… About 2,250 years ago, a Greek genius called Archimedes pinned it down by drawing shapes with 96 sides around a circle, doing every sum by hand. Today computers have worked out trillions of digits, but NASA says about 15 are plenty for flying spacecraft around the solar system.

IRL Challenge: find pi in your kitchen. Wrap a piece of string around a round tin or cup, mark where it meets, and measure it. Then measure straight across the top. Divide the first number by the second (a grown-up's calculator is fine). How close to 3.14 did you get?

Quest 3.1

Imagine a safe zone that shrinks to half as wide. What fraction of the space is left? Then it shrinks to half as wide again. What fraction of the original space is left now?

Turn the page for the answer.

Answer: Quest 3.1

A quarter, then a sixteenth.

How to crack it: each time the circle gets half as wide, the space shrinks to a quarter (half times half). After two shrinks, it's a quarter of a quarter. A quarter of ¼ is 1⁄16. So after just two shrinks, fifteen-sixteenths of the safe space has gone!
