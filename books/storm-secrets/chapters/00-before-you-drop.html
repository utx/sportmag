Geno has locked the vault

Bad news, Island. Geno has done it again.

You know the Cursed Cartridge, the one he jammed into the Override console to kick off Fortnitemares? He's pulled it out, stuffed it inside a secret vault, and locked the door with a 13-letter code. As long as it's in there, nobody can stop his plans.

But Geno has a problem: he's terrible at remembering passwords. So he scribbled each letter of the code on a scrap of paper and hid one scrap in every chapter of this book. He thinks nobody will ever find them all.

He has clearly never met you.

The small print on the story

The vault story is ours, made up just for this book. Everything else (the secrets, the true stories, the records, the history) is real, and we've checked it.

How to crack the vault

Every chapter has a handful of quests, and one very special one: the Vault Quest. Its answer is always a number from 1 to 26. Turn the number into a letter using the Code Key (1 is A, 2 is B, and so on), then write the letter in the matching box of your Vault Code Card. Chapter 1's Vault Quest gives you box 1, chapter 2 gives you box 2, all the way to box 13.

Copy the card onto a piece of paper now, so you can fill it in as you go. When all 13 boxes are full, head to The Final Circle and type the code into the vault.

Geno's motto is "Perfection, embodied." He's sure he's never wrong about anything.

He is wrong about lots of things.

In every chapter, Geno drops a Worst Take: an argument that sounds convincing, maybe even clever, but has a big hole in it. Your job is to roast it: find the exact step where his thinking falls apart. (Once you can do this, you'll start spotting bad takes everywhere. Especially in comment sections.)

Warm-up: Is Geno perfect?

Here's something Geno doesn't know. More than 2,000 years before he came along, people already had a special meaning for the word "perfect". A perfect number is a number whose factors (the numbers that divide into it exactly, not counting the number itself) add up to the number itself.

The smallest one is 6. Its factors are 1, 2 and 3, because 6 ÷ 1, 6 ÷ 2 and 6 ÷ 3 all work with nothing left over. And 1 + 2 + 3 = 6. Perfect! The Greek thinker Euclid wrote about perfect numbers around 2,300 years ago, and in all the time since, people have found only around fifty of them. Geno is not one of them.

Is 12 a perfect number? Add up its factors (not counting 12) and find out. If the total is too big, mathematicians call the number abundant. If it's too small, they call it deficient. Which is 12?

Quest 0.3 · Epic · 30 XP

The third perfect number is between 400 and 500, and it's 16 × 31. Work out what it is, then prove it's perfect. (Hint: start with 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and keep doubling. Then try 31, 62…)
