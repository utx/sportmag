Chapter 13
Lucky or not?

Lots of players have a "lucky" outfit. You wore it, you got a Victory Royale, so it must be lucky, right? Some players have a lucky landing spot, a lucky emote they always do on the bus, even a lucky pair of socks.

But is any of it real? Here's how to find out, starting with the most famous gaming myth of all.

The cartridge myth

The whole Fortnitemares event started when Geno jammed a Cursed Cartridge into his machine. Before games were downloaded, they came on plastic cartridges that you pushed into the console. And if a cartridge didn't work, almost every kid in the world knew the fix: pull it out, blow into it, and push it back in.

Did it work? Repair experts now say: probably not. Your breath is damp, and over time moisture can make the metal contacts go grotty. The most likely reason it seemed to work is that pulling the cartridge out and pushing it back in properly fixed the connection. The blowing just happened at the same time. A whole generation was fooled by a coincidence!

How to test your lucky skin

Scientists have a trick for finding out whether something really works. It's called a fair test, and you can use it on anything:

1. Ask a clear question"Does the Spooky Dash Sprite help me win more fights?"
2. Change only one thingWith the Sprite, or without it. Nothing else.
3. Keep everything else the sameSame mode, same squad, same time of day.
4. Test it lots of timesOne game proves nothing. Twenty is better. A hundred is better still.
5. CompareCount the results with AND without. That second group is called the control.
6. Be honestBelieve the results, even if they're not what you hoped.

Real or fake?

Goldfish forget everything after three seconds

Fake! Scientists have trained goldfish to push levers for food and to respond to sounds, and they remember what they've learned for months.

Lightning never strikes the same place twice

Fake! The Empire State Building in New York gets hit by lightning about 20 times a year.

The first jack-o'-lanterns weren't pumpkins

Real! Fortnitemares has a Snack-O'-Lantern, but the first jack-o'-lanterns were carved out of turnips in Ireland and Scotland, with a glowing coal inside. When people moved to North America, they discovered pumpkins were much bigger and easier to carve.

Quest 13.1

Fishstick wore the Jonesy outfit for 10 games and won 3. Then Fishstick wore the Peely outfit for 20 games and won 6. Fishstick says: "Peely's my lucky outfit. I won twice as many games in it!" Is Fishstick right?

Turn the page for the answer.

Answer: Quest 13.1

No! Fishstick did exactly as well in both.

How to crack it: in Jonesy, Fishstick won 3 out of 10 games. In Peely, 6 out of 20. And 6 out of 20 is the same as 3 out of 10 (just double both numbers). That's 3 wins in every 10 games, both times. Fishstick only won twice as many games in Peely because they played twice as many games in it.

Geno's Worst Take

"I wore my Vertex outfit twice this week, and I won both times. Two out of two! That's 100%! Vertex makes you win. It's science."

Find at least two problems with Geno's "science".

Turn the page for the roast.

The roast

Two games is far too few to prove anything. There's nothing to compare with, like games in other outfits. He might have won anyway (coincidence!), maybe because of the lobby or the loot. And "100%" sounds impressive, but it's just two games. A sceptical player would want lots of games, with and without Vertex. Nice try, Geno.
