Chapter 12
Don’t get played

Every day at the same time, the Item Shop changes. New outfits, new emotes, countdown timers, big "NEW!" labels. It's fun to look at. It's also designed to make you want things, right now, before they're gone.

So here's how to be the smartest shopper in the lobby. (And the most important rule of all: always ask a grown-up before spending a single V-Buck.)

The best-value thing in Fortnite

It's the battle pass. This season's is called Override, and it costs 800 V-Bucks (or comes free with Fortnite Crew). You play, you earn XP, and you unlock outfits as you go, including Geno himself, the brawler Mali, and Wrixel from Build-A-Ville. Compare that with a single Legendary outfit in the Item Shop, which can cost 2,000 V-Bucks on its own. There's a bonus this season, too: gift the pass to a friend, and you both get a special Peril Peak style for Mali.

The five tricks adverts use

Adverts use the same handful of tricks again and again: on TV, on websites, in game shops, even on the cereal box. Once you know them, you'll spot them everywhere.

TrickWhat it sounds likeWhat it's doing
Hurry up!"Only 3 hours left!" "Last chance!"Making you decide quickly, before you have time to think.
Nearly gone"Rare!" "Limited edition!"Using scarcity to make something feel more precious.
Everyone's doing it"Everyone's got one!"Making you worry about being left out. (This is called the bandwagon trick.)
Big promises"The best ever!" "You'll never lose again!"Exaggerating, or dressing up an opinion as a fact.
The fake bargain"Was 2,000, NOW 1,500!"Showing a big "before" price so the real price feels small. Was it ever really 2,000?

Real or fake? Free V-Bucks

Fake. Every single time. Websites, videos and messages promising "free V-Bucks" are scams. They want your password, or they want you to download something nasty. The only real ways to get V-Bucks are buying them in the game, using a gift card from a proper shop, or earning them in the battle pass.

Quest 12.1

A shop sells Chug Jug lollies (sadly, these are made up) in three packs: 4 for $2.00, 10 for $4.50 and 25 for $12.50. You need at least 6 lollies for a party. What's the cheapest way to get them?

Turn the page for the answer.

Answer: Quest 12.1

Buy two 4-packs, for $4.00. You get 8 lollies.

How to crack it: one 4-pack isn't enough. One 10-pack costs $4.50. Two 4-packs cost $2.00 + $2.00 = $4.00, which is cheaper, and 8 lollies is plenty. Here's the twist: per lolly, the 10-pack is the best deal (45 cents each, against 50 cents in the other packs). But "best deal per lolly" isn't the same as "cheapest way to get what you need"!

Vault Quest 12

You get $5 pocket money a week, and you're saving up for a $70 skateboard. You don't spend any of it. How many weeks will it take?

Turn the page for the answer and your vault letter.

Answer: Vault Quest 12

14 weeks.

How to crack it: $70 ÷ $5 = 14. (Check: 10 weeks is $50, and 4 more weeks is $20. $50 + $20 = $70.)

Vault letter: N (the 14th letter of the alphabet). Write it in box 12.

The roast

Geno fell for "Everyone's doing it": lots of people wearing something doesn't make it the best. He also fell for "Hurry up!": the countdown made him panic. And "never, ever see it again" probably isn't even true. The Item Shop rotates, and most items come back eventually. Sleep on it, Geno.
