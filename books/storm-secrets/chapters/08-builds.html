Builds that never break

In Build mode, you can throw up a wall in half a second. You get four pieces, walls, floors, ramps and roofs (the pointy roof is called a cone), and every piece costs 10 materials. Wood goes up fastest but ends up weakest. Metal takes ages but ends up toughest. Brick sits in the middle.

Playing Zero Build instead? You can't build, so you need gadgets: the Port-A-Bunker pops up an instant shelter, and the Shield Bubble Jr. throws a protective dome over you.

The classic box

The secret of the triangle

Real engineers don't get to build in half a second, but they know a secret that makes things super strong: triangles. Make a square out of four sticks with loose joints at the corners and push it: it squashes flat into a diamond. Make a triangle and push it: nothing happens. A triangle can't change shape unless one of its sides changes length. That makes it rigid.

Once you know the trick, you'll see triangles everywhere: in roofs, cranes, electricity towers, bike frames and big bridges.

IRL Challenge: Make a square and a triangle from paper strips or straws, joined at the corners with split pins or pipe-cleaners. Push them. Then add one diagonal strip across the square and push again.

True story: Sydney's Coathanger

The Sydney Harbour Bridge is one giant steel arch, packed with triangles. Workers built the two halves of the arch out from each shore at the same time, with nothing holding them up in the middle except huge steel cables anchored in tunnels behind each end. On 19 August 1930, the two halves finally met over the water.

The bridge opened on 19 March 1932, and something amazing happened. Just as the Premier was about to cut the ribbon, a man in uniform rode forward on a horse and slashed it with his sword! His name was Francis de Groot. He was arrested and fined £5, the ribbon was tied back together, and the Premier cut it again.

Answer: Quest 8.1

13 triangles.

How to crack it: count them by size so you don't miss any. Smallest triangles pointing up: 6. Smallest pointing down: 3. Medium triangles (each made of 4 small ones): 3. The big one: 1. That's 6 + 3 + 3 + 1 = 13. (In figure A: 3 small up, 1 small down, 1 big = 5.)

Geno's Worst Take

"The Harbour Bridge has four gigantic stone towers at its ends. They're the biggest, strongest-looking things there. So obviously they're what holds the whole bridge up."

Here's a surprise: engineers say the bridge would stand up fine without those towers. So what's wrong with how Geno decided?

Turn the page for the roast.

The roast

Geno judged by looks. The towers (called pylons) look the strongest, so he decided they must be doing the work, without any evidence. In fact they're mostly there to make the bridge look grand. The real work is done by the steel arch, which rests on giant steel pins at its base. Looking important isn't the same as being important. (Geno should know.)
