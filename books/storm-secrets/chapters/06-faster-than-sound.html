Sound travels at about 1,235 km/h, more than ten times faster than a car on the motorway. When something goes even faster than that, it makes a sonic boom: a bang like thunder, as it smashes through its own sound waves.

True story: the broom that broke the sound barrier

On 14 October 1947, a test pilot called Chuck Yeager climbed into a bright orange rocket plane called the Bell X-1 to try to become the first person to fly faster than sound. There was a problem. Two nights earlier, he'd fallen off a horse and cracked two ribs, and he was in so much pain he couldn't pull the cockpit door shut. So his friend, the engineer Jack Ridley, sawed off a piece of a broom handle for him to use as a lever. Yeager shut the door, fired the rockets, and went supersonic. People on the ground heard the boom. Fortnite's Witch Broom is cool, but it has never broken the sound barrier.

The thunder trick

Light is much faster than sound: about 300,000 kilometres every second. That's why you see lightning before you hear the thunder, even though they happen at the same moment. And you can use that to work out how far away a storm is:

The thunder trick
Count the seconds from flash to bang, then divide by 3
IRL Challenge: Next time there's a thunderstorm, count the seconds between the flash and the bang, then divide by 3. That's roughly how many kilometres away it is.

Quest 6.1

You see a flash of lightning and count 9 seconds before the thunder. About how far away is the storm? A minute later you see another flash, and this time you count only 4½ seconds. Is the storm coming closer or going away?

Turn the page for the answer.

Answer: Quest 6.1

About 3 km away at first, then about 1.5 km. It's coming closer!

How to crack it: sound travels about 1 km every 3 seconds, so divide the seconds by 3. 9 ÷ 3 = 3 km. Then 4½ ÷ 3 = 1½ km. The gap got shorter, so the storm is heading your way. Time to go inside.

Geno's Worst Take

"A cheetah can run at about 100 km/h. It's about 900 km by road from Sydney to Melbourne. So a cheetah could get from Sydney to Melbourne in 9 hours. Faster than a car!"

Geno's sum is right: 900 ÷ 100 = 9. So what's wrong?

Turn the page for the roast.

The roast

Geno has assumed a cheetah can keep running at top speed for 9 hours. It can't. A cheetah can only sprint flat out for a short burst, a few hundred metres, before it has to stop and rest. Right sum, wrong idea. Always check what someone has assumed, not just their maths.
