The Big Club Guide
19 clubs, ranked

Forty-five clubs ran Mixed JDL teams in 2026. This chapter is about nineteen of them: big clubs and small ones, old clubs and brand-new ones, from the inner city to the far south coast. Some you'll train against in pre-season. Some you'll play on a Saturday morning. Every one of them is a place where young players are learning the game properly.

How we ranked them

Here's the tricky part. In the JDL, match results aren't published, so we can't rank clubs by a ladder. Instead, our ranking is our opinion, based on four things we think matter most to a new player:

  1. Pathway: where can you go next at the club? We looked at the leagues its youth and senior teams played in during 2026.
  2. Program: Football NSW's official rating badges for the club's JDL programs.
  3. People and style: how the club coaches, the way it wants its teams to play, and the players who have come through it.
  4. Proof: things the club has achieved, like cups and titles.

The official badges are worth explaining. Football NSW rates every JDL program on its plans, its coaching, its coaches' qualifications and how many players and coaches stay. The Mixed JDL badges below are the ones Football NSW published for the 2026 season (from its 2025 assessment). Where a club also runs a Girls JDL program, we've included that badge too, including the newest ratings, published in October 2026. Badges tell you a lot about how well organised a program is, but they're not the whole story, and some clubs on this list were too new to have one.

Remember

Being lower on this list doesn't make a club a bad place to play. A brand-new JDL club has no badge yet, and a "Development Committed" club is one Football NSW is actively helping to grow. Every JDL club has qualified coaches and the same rules. The best club for you is usually the one in your zone where you feel at home.

Number 1: Sydney University SFC

  • Zone: East. Based at the University of Sydney, Camperdown.
  • Founded: 1946.
  • JDL technical directors: Jean Narde (Mixed) and David Cameron (Girls).
  • Badges: Mixed JDL Bronze (2026). Girls JDL Silver (2026), and Silver again in the October 2026 ratings.

Sydney Uni SFC is the football club of the University of Sydney, part of Sydney Uni Sport and Fitness. In 2025 its JDL teams played home games at Gunyama Park in Zetland, on a full-size synthetic pitch. We've ranked it number one for two reasons: how it develops players, and the way it wants them to play.

How it develops players

Lots of clubs say they're about development. Sydney Uni has written down exactly what that means. Its "Training DNA" is a set of targets that every coach must try to hit in every coach-led session, "to ensure the enjoyment of all players and give us the best chance to develop our players":

Sydney Uni's Training DNA
Five targets for every coach-led session
Enjoyment
All sessionDesigned so every player is interested and challenged.
Opposed
70% of the sessionPractise against real opponents: 4 v 2, 5 v 3 and so on.
Purpose
All sessionKeep-ball games always have a direction and a way to win.
Match-like
70% of the sessionGoals, end zones, gates or target players: it looks like a game.
Both sides
All sessionAttackers and defenders get a target, so everyone learns.

Think about what that means for you. Instead of standing in a queue waiting to dribble round a cone, most of your training is against opponents, towards a goal, with a decision to make every few seconds. That's exactly how the JDL measures players: involvement, technical progress, mental engagement and enjoyment. It's also how the four core skills stick, because you learn a first touch with a defender coming at you, not in an empty space. The club describes the aim of its JDL program as giving players "the technical 'tools' required to play the game at the highest possible level", with "an immediate skills transfer into positioning and small-sided games", across a Foundation Phase and a Development Phase.

The way it plays

Sydney Uni's training rules point to a clear style: possession with a purpose. Keep the ball, but always to go somewhere: forward, towards a goal, as a team. That fits the JDL perfectly. The retreat line is there so teams can build from the back with passing, and a club that trains its players to keep the ball under pressure, in every session, gets the most out of it.

There's some Brazilian flair in the mix, too. The club says its Mixed JDL technical director, Jean Narde, came through the famous São Paulo youth academy into its first team, played in Europe, and played alongside Brazilian legends Kaká, Ronaldinho and Ronaldo, before working in junior academies in São Paulo. At Sydney Uni, the club says, he's "dedicated to developing confident and skilled individuals". Confident, skilful players who want the ball: that's the style.

Support around the player

Being part of a university sports club brings some serious back-up. Sydney Uni lists performance support for its players and coaches: medical care, nutrition advice, specialist goalkeeper coaching, a sports psychologist, strength and conditioning, and data and video analysis. And the results speak for themselves: the club says thirteen Matildas and one Socceroo have played for it, including several you'll meet in Chapter 11.

To be fair, its Mixed JDL program was rated Bronze for 2026, and Football NSW hadn't yet published the newest Mixed ratings when this book was finished. But for the quality of its coaching and the way it wants young players to play, Sydney Uni is our number one. The club's own advice for JDL players is worth remembering: "Not all players are going to play football at the highest level, but if you have a dream to do so this is the place and time to start."

Number 2: Eastern Suburbs FA

  • Zone: East. The football association for Sydney's Eastern Suburbs.
  • Badges: Mixed JDL Silver (2026). Girls JDL Silver (2026), and Silver again in the October 2026 ratings.

Eastern Suburbs FA (ESFA) is different from most clubs on this list: it's an association, the organisation that runs community football for lots of local clubs in Sydney's east. Its JDL teams pick the best young players from that whole area. ESFA has held its JDL trials on the synthetic pitch at Heffron Park.

Why it's number two: ESFA was one of only four clubs in the whole state rated Silver for both its Mixed and Girls programs in 2026 (South Coast Flame was another). ESFA has described its program as the Eastern Suburbs pathway aligned with the Sydney FC Academy, with coaches who hold advanced coaching licences. Its program is built on the four core skills, and in its own words, "emphasis is placed on development rather than winning matches". ESFA has also run the White Knights program at Heffron Park, which gives footballers with disability a place to play: a sign of a football community that's about everyone.

Number 3: Sydney Olympic FC

Belmore Sports Ground, home of Sydney Olympic. Photo: Jason L, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

  • Zone: East. Home ground: Belmore Sports Ground.
  • Founded: 1957, as Pan-Hellenic. Colours: blue and white.
  • Teams in 2026: NPL Men's NSW, NPL Women's NSW, Boys Youth League One and Girls Youth League One.
  • Badges: Mixed JDL Bronze (2026). Girls JDL Bronze (2026), rated Silver in the October 2026 ratings (with its 2027 entry still to be confirmed).

Sydney Olympic was started by Greek-Australian migrants in 1957 and grew into one of the most famous clubs in the country. It was a founding member of the old National Soccer League in 1977 and won the national championship twice, in 1990 and 2002. In 2025 it was one of the founding clubs of the Australian Championship, a new national competition.

Why it's number three: no club on our list has a bigger pathway. In 2026 Olympic had teams in the top league at every level: NPL Men's, NPL Women's, and Youth League One for both boys and girls. A JDL player here can see exactly where the road goes.

4. South Coast Flame FC

  • Zone: South (the Illawarra). Founded: 2017.
  • Badges: Mixed JDL Silver. Girls JDL Silver (2026), then Gold in October 2026.

Football South Coast started the Flame in 2017 to give the Illawarra's huge number of junior players a pathway close to home. It's already one of the best-run programs in NSW: in Football NSW's October 2026 ratings, Flame's Girls JDL was the only Girls JDL program in the state rated Gold. Its senior women play in League One.

5. Blacktown Spartans FC

  • Zone: West. Founded: 2002.
  • Badges: Mixed JDL Bronze. Girls JDL Bronze (2026), then Silver in October 2026.

The Spartans were formed in 2002 with one goal: to field elite youth teams in Western Sydney. In 2026 their men won the League One premiership, and their girls won the girls' title at the April 2026 Junior Development Cup. With men's and women's teams in League One and youth teams in Youth League Two, the Spartans have a pathway that's on the rise.

6. Bonnyrigg White Eagles FC

  • Zone: South. Founded: 1968, as Avala. Colours: red shirts, blue shorts, white socks.
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Bronze.

Started by Serbian-Australian migrants, Bonnyrigg was crowned NSW champions in 2013 and 2015, back when it played in the state's top league. In April 2026, its Under 10s won the Mixed U10 title at the Junior Development Cup, the only real trophy a JDL U10 team can win. Its technical director, Enzo Palumbo, explained the club's approach: "Our focus has been on creating an environment that prioritises long-term growth over short-term results."

7. Inter Lions FC

  • Zone: East (Concord). Founded: 1983, as Majors Bay. Colours: blue and black stripes.
  • Badges: Mixed JDL new in 2026. Girls JDL Silver, in both 2026 and October 2026.

Inter Lions borrowed its colours from the Italian giant Inter Milan. Its men's and women's teams both play in League One, and its Girls JDL program has been rated Silver twice in a row. Its Mixed JDL program only joined in 2026, so it's one to watch.

8. Gladesville Ryde Magic

  • Zone: North. Founded: 1977, as Gladesville United. Colours: blue and white.
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Bronze.

Founded by Greek-Australians, Magic plays at Christie Park, which the club calls "Fortress Magic". Its crest includes the Gladesville Bridge. It's the only North zone club on our list, with men in League Two and boys in Youth League Two.

9. Nepean FC

  • Zone: West (Penrith and St Marys). Founded: 2011.
  • Badges: Mixed JDL Development Committed. Girls JDL Development Committed (2026), then Silver in October 2026.

Nepean was created by the local football association so players from the Penrith area had a pathway without leaving home. Fun fact: in 2012 Nepean hosted the very first match ever played by the Western Sydney Wanderers. (The Wanderers won 5–0.) Its girls' program made the biggest jump on our list in the October 2026 ratings, from Development Committed to Silver.

10. Mounties Wanderers FC

  • Zone: South (Mount Pritchard). Founded: 1978.
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Development Committed.

Started by Maltese-Australians as Mount Pritchard Soccer Club, Mounties still has a Maltese cross on its badge. In 2026 its men finished top of League Two, earning promotion to League One.

11. Hawkesbury City FC

  • Zone: West (the Hawkesbury, north-west of Sydney).
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Bronze.

Hawkesbury City gives players from the Hawkesbury region a JDL team close to home. Its men play in League Two, where they finished second in 2018, and its boys in Youth League Three.

12. Southern Phoenix FC

  • Zone: Regional (South Nowra, on the NSW South Coast).
  • Badges: Mixed JDL Bronze. Girls JDL Bronze, in both 2026 and October 2026.

Southern Phoenix is one of the newest clubs on this list. It was formed by joining together two clubs, Southern Branch Football (boys) and South East Phoenix FC (girls), and 2026 was its first full year. It calls itself the official representative club for southern NSW, so players from a huge area get a JDL team without travelling to Sydney.

13. Dulwich Hill FC

  • Zone: East. Home ground: Arlington Oval. Colours: blue and yellow.
  • Badge: new to the Mixed JDL in 2026.

Dulwich Hill was founded by migrants from the Portuguese island of Madeira, and its badge is based on Madeira's flag. Its men play in League One, and games against Fraser Park are known as the Portuguese derby.

14. Canterbury Bankstown FC

  • Zone: East. Formed: 1886. Nickname: the Berries.
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Development Committed.

One of the oldest football clubs in Australia. Canterbury won the NSW championship in 1958 and 1960, and one of its most famous players was Johnny Warren, "Captain Socceroo", who joined the club at 15. You'll meet him in Chapter 11. Its men play in League One.

15. Rydalmere Lions FC

  • Zone: West (Rydalmere, in the City of Parramatta). Founded: 1979. Colours: red.
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Development Committed.

The club began in 1979 as St Joseph's Zgharta Soccer Club, named after a town in Lebanon, and joined with Fairfield City Lions in 2014. Its men play in League One.

16. Camden Tigers FC

  • Zone: South (Camden). Colours: orange and black.
  • Badges: new to both JDLs in 2026. Girls JDL rated Development Committed in October 2026.

The Tigers joined the JDL in 2026. With women in League One, men in League Two, and boys' and girls' teams in Youth League Two, there's already a full pathway to grow into.

17. Fraser Park FC

  • Zone: East (Marrickville). Founded: 1961. Colours: red shirts, green shorts.
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Development Committed.

Fraser Park was started by Portuguese migrants, and its home kit matches Portugal's. It plays at Fraser Park in Marrickville and has been in junior development since the SAP days.

18. Granville Rage

  • Zone: West. Founded: 2000. Home ground: Garside Park, Granville.
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Development Committed.

The Rage started out in Prairiewood and moved to Granville, where it plays at Garside Park. Its men play in League Two and its boys in Youth League Three.

19. Macarthur FA

  • Zone: South (Campbelltown, Camden and Wollondilly).
  • Badge: Mixed JDL Development Committed.

Like ESFA, Macarthur FA is an association, which runs football for 24 member clubs across Campbelltown, Camden and Wollondilly. Its JDL program gives the whole Macarthur region's best young players a team to aim for.
