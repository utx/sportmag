Ball Mastery
Technical skills

The ball is your best friend and your trickiest opponent. It rolls where it wants, bounces at funny angles, and never does what you expect, until you've touched it so many thousands of times that it starts to feel like part of your foot. That feeling has a name: mastery. It's the reason the JDL exists.

Remember the four core skills from Chapter 1? Here they are properly, with what to think about and a drill for each that you can do at home with a ball, a wall and a few cones (or shoes, or drink bottles).

1. 1v1: beating a player, and stopping one

Football is full of little one-on-one battles. In attack, a 1v1 is a chance to get past a defender and create something. In defence, it's your job to stop the attacker. The JDL loves 1v1s, because the more you try them at nine and ten, the braver and better you'll be at fifteen.

Attacking: run at the defender with the ball close. Get them to commit, to lean one way, then go the other way with a quick change of direction and a burst of speed. One good fake and a fast exit beats five fancy stepovers.

Defending: get close quickly, then slow down as you arrive so you don't fly past. Stand side-on, knees bent, on your toes, and show the attacker the way you want them to go, usually towards the touchline. Be patient. Wait for a heavy touch, then pounce.

2. First touch

Your first touch decides your second one. A great first touch moves the ball out of your feet and into space, ready for your next pass, dribble or shot. A bad one leaves the ball stuck under you, or bouncing away to an opponent.

  • Get in line with the ball early, and get your body open, so you can see more of the pitch.
  • Cushion it: relax your foot as the ball arrives, like catching an egg.
  • Touch it somewhere useful: away from the defender, towards where you want to go next.
  • Practise with both feet. A player who can only control with one foot is only half as hard to stop.

3. Running with the ball

Running with the ball is different from dribbling past a player. It's what you do when there's space in front of you: push the ball a little ahead with the laces or the outside of your foot, sprint onto it, and push again. The more space, the bigger the push. Get your head up between touches so you can see what's coming. When a defender gets close, bring the ball back under control with smaller touches.

4. Passing and striking

Passing is football's language. The inside of the foot gives you accuracy for short passes: ankle locked, toe up, standing foot pointing at your target, follow through. For longer passes and shots, strike through the middle of the ball with your laces, toe pointing down, and land on your kicking foot. Most goals in junior football come from shots that are low and on target, not the hardest ones.

The 100 touches

Professional players often warm up with simple "ball mastery" moves: lots of quick little touches without moving very far. You can do these in a backyard, a garage or a hallway (ask first). Try to do all five, every day, and time yourself once a week.

Try this: the juggling ladder

Juggling (keeping the ball up with your feet, thighs and head) isn't a skill you'll use much in a game, but it's brilliant for your touch. Start by letting it bounce between each touch. Then try two touches without a bounce. Keep a note of your record and try to beat it every week.

Coach's corner

Spend extra time on your weaker foot. It feels clumsy at first. That clumsy feeling is your brain learning. Amy Sayer, one of the stars in Chapter 11, is known for being able to score with either foot.
