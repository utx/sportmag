Fitness

Football at your age isn't about who can lift the heaviest weight or run the longest. It's about being quick, nimble and balanced, and having enough energy to stay switched on until the final whistle. You'll build most of that just by training three times a week and playing every Saturday. This chapter is about the rest.

The ABCs

Coaches sometimes talk about the "ABCs" of movement: agility, balance and coordination. Agility is changing direction fast. Balance is staying on your feet when someone bumps you, or when you're standing on one leg to strike the ball. Coordination is getting different parts of your body to work together, like your eyes, your feet and your arms when you jump to head a ball.

At nine and ten, your body is brilliant at learning these, and not only through football. Football NSW's own guidance says playing other sports is good for talented young players. Swimming, basketball, tennis, gymnastics, dancing and chasing your friends around the park all build an athletic body that football can use.

Every day, get moving

Australia's official guidelines for children aged 5 to 12 say you should do at least one hour of moderate to vigorous activity every day, the kind that makes you breathe harder. On at least three days a week, some of it should be vigorous, where you're puffing too much to chat. JDL training covers some days; bike rides, swimming, scooting and backyard games cover the rest.

Sleep: the secret weapon

Here's something most kids don't believe: sleep makes you a better footballer. While you sleep, your body repairs your muscles and grows, and your brain files away all the skills you practised that day. The guidelines say children your age need 9 to 11 hours of sleep a night. If training finishes at 7 pm and school starts at 8.30 am, do the maths with a grown-up and work out your bedtime. Screens off well before sleep helps a lot.

Warm up properly

A good warm-up isn't boring. It's how you avoid injuries. FIFA, world football's governing body, created a warm-up program for children called FIFA 11+ Kids, with running, balance, jumping and falling exercises. When scientists studied teams that used it, they found that players got about half as many injuries as teams who warmed up the usual way. If your club uses it, do every exercise properly. If you get to training early, start moving: some easy jogging, skipping, side-steps and a few touches of the ball.

Listen to your body

  • Pain is a message. If something hurts, tell your coach or a parent. Playing through an injury usually makes it worse.
  • Head knocks are serious. If you bump your head and feel dizzy, sick or strange, stop and tell an adult. Remember the JDL rule: "If in doubt, sit it out."
  • Growing hurts sometimes. Kids your age grow fast, and sore knees or heels can come with it. A grown-up can get it checked.
  • Rest days count. Your body gets stronger after exercise, while it rests. That's why the season ends with a break.

Try this: the stork challenge

Stand on one leg, arms out, for 30 seconds. Easy? Close your eyes. Still easy? Do it while passing a ball against a wall with your other foot. Do both legs. Balance training like this helps your striking and your tackling.
