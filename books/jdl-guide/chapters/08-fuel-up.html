Fuel Up
Healthy eating

A car won't go far on an empty tank, and neither will you. Food is fuel: it gives you the energy to sprint in the last five minutes, the concentration to make good decisions, and the building blocks to grow and recover. Young athletes don't need special diets, powders or fancy drinks. They need plenty of normal, healthy food, eaten at the right times, and lots of water.

When to eat on game day

Timing matters. NSW Health's advice for junior sport is to have a meal 2 to 4 hours before you play, and if you're hungry closer to the game, a small snack 1 to 2 hours before. Eating a big meal right before kick-off leaves your tummy busy digesting when your legs need the energy. Good pre-game meals are things like breakfast cereal with milk and fruit, or baked beans on toast.

Water wins

Water is the best drink for junior footballers. Bring a full bottle to every training and game, and sip regularly. You don't need sports drinks: NSW Health says they're not the best choice for junior sport, and they're full of sugar. Energy drinks are a definite no for kids. On hot days, drink more.

And the half-time oranges? Lovely, but not essential. Advice used by the ACT Government says a half-time snack isn't needed for games shorter than 75 minutes, which a JDL game definitely is. Water at half-time is the important part.

The most important rule

Eat enough. Growing footballers who train three times a week need plenty of food. Healthy eating isn't about eating less, or about how your body looks. It's about having the energy to play the game you love. Sometimes foods, like cake at a birthday party or a treat after a big win, are part of life too. If you ever have questions about food, talk to a parent, your doctor or a dietitian, not the internet.

Coach's corner

Pack your water bottle the night before, with your boots and shin guards. Forgetting it is the easiest way to run out of energy.
