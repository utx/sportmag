Your Season
The calendar

A JDL season is long. Football NSW recommends that clubs run a program of at least 36 weeks, so you'll be part of your squad for most of the year. That's a good thing: skills grow slowly, like a tree, and you need a lot of weeks to see the difference. Here's what a year looks like, using Football NSW's dates for the 2026 season.

Trials

Every JDL club holds trials, usually in spring for the following year. Football NSW sets the earliest date clubs are allowed to start: for the 2026 season it was 12 October 2025. For the 2027 season, several clubs announced trials from early September 2026. Clubs are not allowed to charge you a fee to trial.

Trials usually mix skill stations with small-sided games. Coaches watch your touch, decisions, effort and attitude, including how you react when something goes wrong, not just who scores most. If a trial doesn't go your way, you're in good company: plenty of professionals missed out on teams when they were young.

The training phase

Once squads are picked, the training phase begins, around October or November, with a break over Christmas. This is where a lot of the real learning happens, and your club can arrange friendly matches against other clubs.

JDL clubs train three times a week. That's plenty. Football NSW's guidance says that most players in a good JDL program don't need extra football-specific training on top, and that playing other sports and "informal" football, kicking a ball around with family and friends at home, is a great idea. Clubs aren't allowed to charge players for extra training, or make extra sessions compulsory.

The game phase

In 2026, JDL games ran from the weekend of 7 and 8 March to the weekend of 29 and 30 August. You'll play one game a week and keep training three times a week. Each club has two teams in every Mixed age group, and they usually play one after the other, so you'll often see the rest of your club's U10 squad on game day.

Your club will tell you where and when each game is. Football NSW publishes the full fixture list on its competitions website, usually in early December. Scores aren't published, of course.

The Junior Development Cup

The one time results really are counted is the Junior Development Cup, or JD Cup, a two-day tournament at Valentine Sports Park in Glenwood, Football NSW's home ground. The April 2026 Cup was for Mixed Under 10s and Girls Under 11s, held on 16 and 17 April during the Easter school holidays. Sixteen teams entered each age group. They were split into two groups of eight, played at least six games each, and the top four from each group went through to quarter-finals, semi-finals and a final. A second Cup, in July, was for Mixed Under 12s and Girls Under 13s.

Valentine Sports Park in Glenwood, Football NSW's home and the venue for the JD Cup. Photo: Crico, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

In the April 2026 Cup, Bonnyrigg White Eagles won the Mixed Under 10 title and Blacktown Spartans won the girls' title. There was also a Skill School on day one, where one player from each team showed off their technique. Even at the Cup, Football NSW spoke to coaches and families before kick-off about fun, learning and long-term development. Paul Jackson, who runs Blacktown Spartans' girls program, said what stood out was "the spirit in which the games were played, with all teams focused on development rather than winning."

Football NSW also runs a JDL Futsal Cup. Futsal is five-a-side football on a hard court with a smaller, heavier ball, and it's brilliant for quick feet and quick thinking.

What's included

This part is mostly for grown-ups. Football NSW caps what a club can charge for a JDL season: in 2026 the most was $1,995, including registration, insurance and your kit (playing and training kit, a tracksuit, a polo shirt and a kit bag). Clubs can charge less, but not more. Optional extras, like a tour with overnight stays, can cost more, but can't affect selection.

Your coaches will be qualified, too. In 2026, every JDL head coach needed at least a Football Australia C-Diploma, and each club's Head of JDL needed a B-Diploma.

When the season ends

When the last game is played, everything stops: Football NSW says all JDL training and matches must finish on the league's final day, so players get a proper break. Rest, play other sports, and kick a ball at the park just for fun. You'll come back hungrier.

Coach's corner

Take a photo of your juggling record, or film yourself doing your favourite skill, on the first day of pre-season. Do it again on the last day of the season. You'll be amazed.
