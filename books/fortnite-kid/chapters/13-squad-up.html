The first thing Billy heard when he landed back in the lobby was Kai's mum.

"...on the kitchen floor, Kai? At ten to seven? With the iPad and the console and the oven timer?"

"Mum, I can explain."

"I'd love to hear it."

Billy stood very still on the stage in the purple void, with Pip on his shoulders, and listened, through Kai's headset, to the sound of his best friend's life falling apart. There was a long, horrible pause. He could picture it perfectly: Kai's mum in her dressing gown, with her arms folded, and her glasses pushed up into her hair, and the expression she wore when somebody had tracked mud across her clean floor.

"Billy's in trouble," said Kai.

"Billy is going to be in a great deal of trouble when I ring his mother."

"No, Mum, I mean... he needs help. In the game. It's really, really important. I can't explain it, you wouldn't believe me, I don't even believe me. But it's one more game. Just one. And then I'll come off and I'll do anything. Anything you want."

Another pause. Billy held his breath.

"One game," said Kai's mum. "And then you are unstacking the dishwasher every day until Christmas."

"Deal."

"And you're telling me what this is all about."

"Deal," said Kai, slightly less confidently.

"And you're putting that oven timer back where you found it."

"Deal."

Footsteps faded away. A door closed. And then there was a very long breath out on the line, the sound of someone who has just been let off the hook and doesn't quite believe it yet.

"Until Christmas," said Kai faintly. "Billy, it's October."

"I'll help," said Billy. "I'll come round and do half."

"You'd better." A crackle. "Right. Where's Mira?"

* * *

Her platform was already drifting in out of the dark. It came closer and closer, and this time it didn't stop a metre away. It kept coming, slowly, until its edge bumped gently against the edge of Billy's, the way a boat bumps against a jetty.

Mira was standing in the middle of it, looking up. Hanging in the air in front of her face, glowing white, was a panel.

FRIEND REQUEST
KAI_THE_DESTROYER wants to be friends.
ACCEPT · DECLINE

"KAI_THE_DESTROYER," Mira read aloud, in a voice so flat it could have ironed a shirt.

"I was eight," said Kai. "Everyone makes mistakes when they're eight."

"Billy's is BillyBlastz2016."

"And that's worse."

Mira looked across at Billy. And for the first time since he'd met her, Billy saw her hesitate in a way that had nothing to do with shotguns. She looked exactly like someone standing at the top of the high diving board, looking down. He understood. Accepting meant trusting. It meant believing that a boy in Sydney and his friend with the oven timer could do something that she hadn't managed to do alone in nine hard, lonely matches.

"Together," said Billy.

Mira reached out and pressed ACCEPT.

There was a chime, bright and silvery, like somebody tapping a wine glass with a spoon. And with a gentle, grinding clunk, the two platforms locked together, edge to edge, and became one. The fog swirled. The lanterns brightened. The pumpkins shuffled up to make room. And then, with a pop, a third figure appeared on the stage between them.

It was a banana.

A tall, bright-yellow, slightly bendy banana, with stubby legs and a big, cheerful, goofy face. It wobbled on the spot. It took two jerky steps forwards, then three steps backwards, then turned all the way around and walked into a pumpkin.

"Sorry," said Kai. "Sorry. Getting used to the controls. It's weird when you can see the real you on the stage next to me." The banana turned round. Its big eyes blinked at Billy. "Okay, that's really weird. Billy, you're standing next to me in pyjamas."

Billy had been holding things together pretty well, all things considered. He had fallen into a television. He had been chased, shot, sniped, stormed, eliminated and unravelled. He had not cried once. But seeing his best friend's banana standing next to him on the stage, looking at him with its silly, kind, goofy face, made something go tight and hot behind his eyes, and he had to look away for a moment, out into the purple dark.

"Hi, Kai," he said, when he could.

"Hi, Billy," said the banana. And then, to Mira: "Hi. I'm Kai. I'm the banana."

"I gathered," said Mira. But Billy could hear she was smiling.

* * *

Above them, in the sky, the floating menu shifted. The word SOLO, which Billy hadn't even noticed hanging there, dissolved like a sugar cube, and a new word assembled itself in its place, letter by glowing letter.

SQUADS · ZERO BUILD

"Okay," said Kai, and his voice changed. It went the way it did when he was explaining patch notes: fast, and clear, and certain. "Strategy. We've got one game, and it's ten to seven, and your mum gets home at quarter past. So no messing around. Here's what I'm thinking."

He laid it out, and Billy and Mira listened.

They would drop at Reality's Reign. It was central, so they'd never be far from the final circle. There were good chests. And there was an Override Console right next door.

"If it switches on, we go for it," said Kai. "And if one of the choices is Squad Extra Life, we take it."

"Squad Extra Life," said Mira slowly. "Everyone in your squad comes back once."

"Right. A spare life each. You get eliminated, you come back through a rift, with your loot. But here's the catch: the second we pick it, all three of us get marked on everybody's map as Hackers. For the rest of the game. Every single squad will know exactly where we are."

"So they'll all come for us," said Billy.

"Every one of them." The banana nodded solemnly. "It's a gamble. But I think we take it. I think an extra life is worth more than hiding. And I think..." He hesitated. "I think if the game wants us to win together, then it probably wants us to have to fight for it together. Does that make sense?"

"Yes," said Mira.

"Yes," said Billy.

They checked their gear. Billy equipped Pip, who settled onto his shoulders with a firm, satisfied wriggle. Mira equipped Honey, who buzzed around her antlers in a delighted little halo before settling between them like a bird in a nest. Billy checked his Loot Hack: still the Hunting Rifle. Mira had picked the Gatekeeper Shotgun. Kai, who didn't need any of this, did the default dance on the spot to show he was ready, which made Mira snort.

Then, all three at once, they turned and looked up at the vast floating word PLAY.

"For Rainbow's End," said Mira.

"For scrambled eggs," said Billy.

"For the dishwasher," said Kai heavily, "which I will now be unstacking every day until the end of time."

Three yellow words lit up over three heads. READY. READY. READY.

The clock in the corner of Billy's eye said 6.54.
