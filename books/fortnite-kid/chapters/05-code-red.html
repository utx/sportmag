The trees turned red.

Not autumn-red, not the soft orange-gold of Golden Grove far away to the east. These were a deep, glossy, alarming crimson, the colour of a fire engine, the colour of a warning, and they grew in fat round clumps on twisted black trunks, so that walking beneath them was like walking through a forest of giant toffee apples. The grass underfoot was a purplish pink. The sky above was going violet. And through gaps in the canopy, very close now, Billy could see the towers of Reality's Reign.

Geno's fortress. It looked like somebody had taken the inside of a gaming computer and made it the size of a city. Towers shaped like graphics cards stood in rows, with glowing slots down their sides. Giant fans turned in the walls, slow and heavy. Thick cables and cooling pipes, lit up in neon pink and blue, snaked between the buildings like rivers of light. And beyond it all, at the very heart of the Island, rose the black bulk of Geno's Machine, with its storm boiling slowly up into the sky.

Billy didn't like looking at the storm. Every time he did, he felt as if it were looking back.

"Billy," said Kai, "it's 5.21. The circle's about to start closing. You need a Cheat Code. Then you're Level 3. Then you just need a Crown Win and... whatever the last thing is."

"Tigether," said Billy.

"It's not tigether. Nothing's called tigether." Kai sounded tired and cross and frightened all at the same time, which was a combination Billy recognised, because he was feeling it too. "Look, Cheat Codes are always stuck on stuff. Machines, walls, trucks. They glow purple. Keep your eyes open."

"Pip!" said Pip, and pointed.

* * *

It was on the side of a digger.

A big orange digger, with a long arm and a toothy bucket, parked at a crazy angle in a clearing among the red trees, as if the driver had simply walked off halfway through a job and never come back. On the side of its cab, glowing like a neon sign outside a fish-and-chip shop, was a panel of purple light. Across the panel ran a line of golden arrows.

Billy approached it the way you might approach a strange dog. Up close, the panel hummed. The arrows pulsed one after another, left to right, left to right, like the lights on a runway.

"Right, left, right, up, right," Kai read out. "That's the code. You do it in order. Get it wrong and you get nothing, and they don't come back. So concentrate."

"How do I even... press them?"

Billy reached out and touched the panel. A prompt floated up: INPUT CODE. Beneath his hand, the purple light rippled like the surface of a pond, and a little pad appeared, four arrows in a cross, the same as on a controller. He touched the right arrow. It lit up gold, and the first arrow on the panel lit up gold, too, with a pleasing bleep.

"Easy," said Billy. "This is easy."

He touched the left arrow. Bleep.

And then something went past his head with a sound like a sheet being ripped in half.

It hit the digger a hand's width from his ear. Sparks flew. There was a hollow CLANG, like a bin lid hit with a cricket bat, and a fist-sized dent appeared in the metal of the cab, and a moment later, rolling across the clearing from somewhere far away, came the long, flat crack of a rifle.

Billy hit the ground. He hit it so fast and so hard that Pip flew off his shoulders and landed upside down in the grass, legs waving.

"Sniper!" Kai yelled. "Billy, that's a sniper! But there aren't any snipers! There are no sniper rifles in the loot pool! Unless..." His voice changed. "Unless it's a Hunting Rifle. They only came in on Thursday. You can only get one if you've hacked it into your loot in the lobby. Whoever that is, they knew exactly what they wanted before they jumped off the bus."

"Where are they?"

"Don't know! Somewhere north! Up high!"

Billy raised his head, very carefully, a centimetre at a time. North of the clearing, beyond the red trees, was a ridge, and on the ridge was a rocky outcrop, and on the outcrop, just for a second, something glinted. A small, cold, silver gleam, like sunlight bouncing off a watch face.

A scope.

And behind the scope, against the purple sky, rising from a dark head like the branches of a winter tree: antlers.

"It's him," breathed Billy. "The one from the temple. The one with the red eyes."

"Who?"

"I don't know. He was watching me. When I landed. He was on the tower. He's been following me."

Another shot. This one hit the ground a metre in front of Billy's nose and threw a spray of pink dirt in his face. Billy spat. Pip, who had righted itself, scurried back up his arm and clung to his hood, squeaking furiously at the ridge.

"You can't stay there," Kai said. "He's got the high ground. He's got all the time in the world. The digger's between you and him, sort of, but if you get up to put the code in, he can see your head."

"So what do I do?"

"You put the code in really, really fast," said Kai, "and you don't miss."

* * *

Billy counted to three. He had always been told that counting to three helped. It didn't, particularly, but it gave his legs time to decide to stand up.

On three, he rose, pressed his back against the cab of the digger, and slapped his hand on the panel.

The arrows were still there, still pulsing. Two of them lit gold. Three left to go.

Right. Bleep.

Up...

Another shot. The bullet hit the edge of the cab right beside Billy's fingers, and the panel flickered, and the arrows wobbled and blurred in front of his eyes, and for one horrible moment he could not remember which way was up. His finger hung in the air over the pad. His brain had gone completely white, like a TV screen with no signal.

"Up!" screamed Kai. "Up, then right!"

Up. Bleep. Right. Bleep.

The whole panel blazed gold. The digger shuddered. There was a fanfare (an actual fanfare, as if a tiny brass band were hiding in the engine) and the panel burst outwards in a shower of golden squares, and out of the squares, spinning like a coin, came a Sprite.

It was small and round and solid gold, with a little lightning-bolt antenna on its head, and it glittered so brightly Billy had to squint. The card said: GOLD STORM SCOUT SPRITE. Golden numbers fizzed up into the air above it: +1,000 XP.

Billy grabbed it, and it went into his pocket with a satisfied clink, like a coin into a piggy bank.

And then his own card flashed in front of him.

TRICK OR TREAT BILLY SPRITE · LEVEL UP! · Lvl 3

MASTERY: W_N TOGETHER

"Together," said Kai, wonderingly. "Win together. It says win together."

"Together with who?" said Billy. "I'm playing solos!"

Before Kai could answer, the whole world gave a low, deep shudder, like a giant clearing its throat.

The light changed. At the very edge of the sky, far off past the red trees, a wall of shimmering purple had appeared. It stretched up and up, higher than any building, higher than the clouds, curving away on both sides as far as Billy could see. It flickered with sparks of lightning. And very slowly, very steadily, it had begun to move.

A message slid across the top of Billy's vision:

STORM SHRINKING · 1:29

And at the same moment, from somewhere among the graphics-card towers of Reality's Reign, there came a sound Billy had heard a thousand times through his speakers: a rising electronic chime, and a voice like a robot announcing a train, and a column of pink light shooting straight up into the sky.

OVERRIDE CONSOLE ONLINE.

On the ridge, the antlered figure lowered his rifle. He turned his head towards the pink light.

Then he started to run.
