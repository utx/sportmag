By 7.08, there were two squads left.

The final circle sat right at the foot of Geno's Machine, in a crater of cracked concrete and tangled cables, with the black towers of the Machine rearing up on one side like the walls of a castle. Above it, filling half the sky, the strange storm turned. It was so close now that Billy could see the inside of it: layer after layer of purple cloud, spinning slowly, and deep in the middle a darkness that was not quite cloud and not quite sky. Lightning flickered through it constantly, without thunder, silent and white.

And all around the crater, closing in from every side, came the ordinary storm, the purple wall, crackling and relentless, squeezing what was left of the match into a space not much bigger than a school playground.

Billy, Mira and Kai were crouched behind the wreck of an enormous fallen cable, as thick as a tree trunk, at the north edge of the crater. On the far side, behind a heap of shattered concrete, was the other squad.

There were four of them. They all wore the same outfit: sleek black suits with silver stripes and mirrored silver visors that hid their faces completely. They moved together like a single animal, with a kind of quiet, frightening coordination, and they hadn't said a word or made a single mistake all game. They had been hunting the Hackers since the moment the red skulls appeared, and they had finally caught up.

"They're sweats," said Kai. "Proper sweats. That's a tournament squad, I reckon. They've probably been practising together for months."

"So have we," said Mira.

Kai's banana turned to look at her.

"Well," she said. "For about forty minutes. But it's been a very intense forty minutes."

* * *

The silver squad came over the concrete heap all at once, four of them, spreading out, firing as they came.

It was the loudest, fastest, most terrifying minute of Billy's life.

Bullets chewed chunks out of the fallen cable. Sparks fountained. A rocket went off somewhere to the left with a roar like a train in a tunnel, and the shockwave knocked Billy flat on his back. Kai was shouting. Mira was shouting. Honey's bees were buzzing in golden clouds. And above it all, the storm over the Machine was groaning louder and louder, as though the fight were feeding it.

Kai went first. The banana popped up to fire, and two of the silver players hit him at the same time, and he went straight from standing to gone, in a swirl of blue squares, without even being knocked.

"Kai!"

"Extra life!" Kai's voice crackled. "I'm coming back! Look up!"

Billy looked up. High above the crater, a glowing rift had torn open in the sky, a jagged, shimmering crack of light, and out of it dropped a small yellow figure, falling, with its default glider opening. Kai. Back from the dead, just as the Override had promised.

He landed right in the middle of the silver squad.

For about three seconds, it was the bravest and stupidest and most magnificent thing Billy had ever seen. The banana came down like a meteor, spraying with the Bonerattler, and the silver players scattered in surprise, and one of them went down to his knees, knocked, glowing red. Then the other three turned on Kai together, and Kai's banana dissolved again, for good this time.

"I'm out," said Kai, panting, from his kitchen in Sydney. "I'm out, I'm out. But I got one. Go. Go. It's still a squad win if you win. It counts for all of us."

Three silver players. Billy and Mira.

And then Billy felt something hit him in the back.

* * *

He never saw who did it. The fourth silver player, maybe, the knocked one, revived. All he knew was the crash of his shield going, and the drain of his health, and then that feeling again: the loose thread, the pulling, the unravelling. He was coming apart. His hands were becoming purple squares, his arms, his hoodie.

Not again, he thought. Please, not now.

Then the squares turned gold.

It was the most beautiful thing Billy had ever felt. The unravelling stopped, and reversed, like a film running backwards. The golden squares spun around him in a whirlwind and he was lifted up, up, up, high above the crater, into the cold bright air, and the sky split open in a jagged rift of light and spat him out again, falling, with his umbrella blooming above him and the whole final circle laid out below him like a map.

Squad Extra Life. His spare.

He could see everything from up there. He could see Mira, crouched behind the fallen cable, alone now, her shield gone, facing three. He could see the silver squad moving in on her: one from the left, one from the right, and one, the leader, the one with the rocket launcher, climbing onto the concrete heap to get the high ground.

And he could see the storm over Geno's Machine.

From up here, he could see right into it. Into the darkness in the middle of the whirlpool. And in that darkness, just for a moment, he saw a face. A smooth, pale mask, with two narrow eyes that glowed like distant stars, looking down at the crater. Watching. Waiting.

Geno.

The face turned, very slowly, and looked straight up at Billy.

Billy looked back. He was frightened. Of course he was. But he was also, he realised, angry: really, properly, deeply angry, the kind of angry that makes you brave. This was the thing that had pulled him out of his bedroom. This was the thing that had kept Mira lonely and afraid for four hours. This was the thing that wanted every player on the Island to believe, for ever, that there was only one way to win.

"Not today," Billy said out loud.

Then he closed his umbrella, and dived.

* * *

He landed on top of the concrete heap, right behind the silver squad leader, just as the leader raised his rocket launcher at Mira.

"Mira!" Billy yelled. "Bees!"

Mira didn't hesitate for a fraction of a second. She stood up from behind the fallen cable and fired the Gatekeeper at the two silver players on the ground, BOOM, BOOM, BOOM, and wherever her shots hit, a golden cloud of bees exploded upwards and went for the silver squad in a furious, glittering, humming storm of their own. The two silver players on the ground staggered back, swatting at the air, blinded.

The leader swung round towards Billy.

Billy jumped. He jumped again. "PIP!" screamed Pip, at the top of its tiny voice, and Billy dashed, straight at the leader, straight through the rocket as it left the launcher (he felt it pass through him, hot and roaring, like a hair-dryer in the face), and came out of the dash so close to the silver visor that he could see himself in it: a boy in a school hoodie, with a little jack-o'-lantern on his shoulders, and a shotgun.

He fired.

The leader broke apart in blue.

Below him, through the cloud of bees, Billy saw Mira's shotgun flash once, twice, and two more clouds of blue squares spin up into the air.

And then there was a silence so total that Billy could hear the storm crackling, and Pip panting, and his own heart.

One silver player left. The one who had been knocked by Kai, and revived. He stood alone in the middle of the crater, with the bees still buzzing round his visor, looking from Billy to Mira and back again.

Billy looked at Mira. Mira looked at Billy.

Then they both raised their weapons, together.
