They jumped together.

Three of them, out of the back of the Battle Bus, one after another: a boy in socks, a girl with antlers, and a banana. They fell side by side through the bright morning air with their arms tucked in, three dark specks against the clouds, and Billy, glancing across, saw Mira grin for the first time. A real, wide, wild grin, wind-whipped, the grin of someone who has forgotten for a moment to be frightened.

"Yeeeeeeaaah!" yelled Kai, on the comms, as his banana did a slow, wobbly somersault in mid-air.

Their gliders bloomed open, one, two, three. Billy's Override 'Brella, rippling purple and pink. Mira's, the same. Kai's, a plain old default glider, because Kai, as he pointed out loudly, had not had the advantage of being trapped inside the game all morning to practise.

Below them, Reality's Reign rose up to meet them: the graphics-card towers, the slowly turning fans, the glowing cooling pipes. And beyond it, closer than it had ever been, Geno's Machine, and the tall storm turning above it.

Billy looked at that storm, and felt it look back.

It was bigger. He was sure of it. Bigger than it had been in the first match, bigger even than in the patch-note pictures, a vast slow whirlpool of purple cloud spinning upside down above the Machine, crackling with lightning. Every few seconds it gave a groan so deep it rattled his teeth. And underneath the groan, faint as a whisper in another room, he could hear the voices again.

He told himself it was the wind. He didn't believe it.

* * *

They landed on a rooftop and went through Reality's Reign like a hurricane through a washing line.

It was completely different with three. Billy had spent the whole morning alone, listening to every footstep, flinching at every shadow. Now there was always someone at his back. Mira ran ahead, fast and fearless, her shotgun up, calling out what she saw: "Chest, left. Player on the far tower. Ammo here, Billy, catch." Kai brought up the rear, his banana jogging slightly sideways, reading the map, watching the storm timer, telling them where to go. And Billy, in the middle, opened every chest and checked every corner, and found that for the first time all morning, he wasn't scared.

He found his Hunting Rifle in a chest at the top of a tower. Mira found the Gatekeeper, a heavy, fat-barrelled shotgun with spikes along its side, and gave it a little affectionate pat. Kai found a fishing rod, and a Bonerattler, and an enormous number of bandages, and handed the bandages round like sandwiches at a picnic.

Then, at 7.01 by the clock in Billy's eye, with the first storm circle just beginning to close, the sky over Reality's Reign lit up pink.

OVERRIDE CONSOLE ONLINE.

"That's us," said Kai. "Go, go, go!"

* * *

The NITE Units were waiting, the same three as before, or three exactly like them. Tall and grey and bucket-headed, with their single red eyes sliding slowly from side to side.

But this time, Billy wasn't on his own.

Mira went left. Kai went right, his banana bobbing behind a pipe and popping up again with the Bonerattler rattling like a cutlery drawer falling downstairs. The NITE Units' red eyes swung after them, confused, and their arms began to whine as they charged. And while they were looking left and right, Billy went straight up the middle, jumped, jumped again, and dashed through the nearest robot with that strange buzzing tickle, and slapped both his hands on the front of the console.

CHOOSE ONE

Three cards slid onto the screen. One was BIG LOOT. One offered a magical sword for everybody in the squad. And one, glowing brighter than the others, in a deep rich gold, said:

SQUAD EXTRA LIFE
Your squad respawns once when eliminated.
Your squad will be marked as HACKERS.

Billy's hand hovered.

"Do it," said Mira, from behind the pipe, between shotgun blasts.

"Do it," said Kai.

Billy did it.

The console blazed gold. A fanfare blared, but this one was strange, a little bit wrong, as if the brass band had been told to play something exciting and something sinister at the same time. Over the towers of Reality's Reign, a new ring of letters appeared in the sky, not white this time but a fierce, flickering, neon red:

HACKERS DETECTED

And all over the Island, on every map in every player's corner, three new markers appeared. Three red skulls. All in the same place. All flashing.

"Well," said Kai. "Here they come."

* * *

They came from everywhere.

Billy had thought he knew what being hunted felt like. He had been hunted by Nightantler all through the first match. But that had been one player, patient and clever and alone. This was different. This was like kicking a wasps' nest. Squads came out of the forest to the north. Squads came over the hills from the east. A squad came screaming across the circuit-board ground of Reality's Reign in a car, with four players hanging out of the windows, all shooting at once.

"Car!" yelled Kai. "Car, car, car!"

Mira stepped out from behind a graphics-card tower, raised the Gatekeeper, and fired. The car's windscreen became a spiderweb. The car swerved, clipped a cooling pipe, and spun into a giant fan, which caught it and threw it, slowly, majestically, end over end, into the air. Four players tumbled out of it like socks out of a washing machine.

Mira fired again as they hit the ground, and from the spots where her shots struck, clouds of bright golden specks rose up. Bees. A swarm of furious, glittering, cartoon bees, which went straight for the four players and chased them in circles around the tower, buzzing like a hundred tiny chainsaws. Honey, between Mira's antlers, hummed with enormous satisfaction.

Billy was laughing so hard he could barely hold his rifle.

Then Kai went down.

It happened very quickly. A player in a black hood came out of nowhere, from behind a fan, and opened up with an SMG, and Kai's banana staggered and fell. But he didn't vanish. In squads, you didn't vanish straight away. He dropped onto his knees instead, glowing red, crawling slowly and pathetically across the ground. KNOCKED, said the message over his head.

"Billy!" yelled Kai. "Revive! Revive!"

Billy dashed. He came out of the dash right beside the hooded player and hit him with the Sweeper twice, boom, boom, and the hooded player went down in a crumple of red. Then Billy dropped to his knees beside Kai's crawling banana, put both hands on its yellow back, and held on.

A ring of light appeared around them, filling up slowly. Billy had revived teammates a thousand times through a screen. He had never felt it. It felt like holding a warm mug on a cold morning. Like passing something precious from his hands to Kai's.

The ring filled. The banana stood up, wobbling.

"Thanks," said Kai breathlessly.

"Any time," said Billy.

Above them, the storm over Geno's Machine groaned, deeper than ever. Billy looked up.

The circle was closing. And it was closing right underneath it.
