The alarm went off at 4.52 a.m., and Billy was already awake.

He had been awake since 4.31, actually, lying perfectly still under his doona and staring at the glow-in-the-dark stars on his ceiling, vibrating with a kind of anticipation so fierce it felt like he'd swallowed a beehive. He silenced the alarm before its second beep. The house had rules about noise before six, and the loudest of those rules was asleep in the spare room, snoring like a leaf blower with a head cold.

Nan was staying for the weekend. Mum was on night shift at the hospital and wouldn't be home until quarter past seven. Which meant that between now and then, for two glorious, unsupervised hours, the inner west of Sydney belonged to Billy and to Billy alone.

Well. To Billy, and to the jet that chose that exact moment to thunder over the roof on its way down to the airport, rattling the window in its frame. You stopped noticing the planes when you lived under the flight path. Mostly.

He slid out of bed, padded across the carpet in his socks, and eased his bedroom door shut with the meticulous care of a bomb-disposal expert. Then he dropped into the beanbag, picked up his controller, and pressed the button.

The screen bloomed into life. Purple sky. Orange pumpkins. A fog that crept along the bottom of the lobby like something alive.

Fortnitemares.

Billy grinned in the dark.

* * *

Here is what you need to understand about Billy: he was ten years old, he was in Year 5, and he was, by his own careful calculation, the third-best Fortnite player in his whole school. (The best was a Year 6 called Dimitri who'd once got a Victory Royale with nothing but a pickaxe and a fishing rod. The second-best was Billy's best mate, Kai, which Billy accepted, mostly, on the days when it was true.)

And here is what you need to understand about that particular Sunday, the eighteenth of October: it was Power Hour.

Thursday's update had dropped right on time, version 42.40, and Kai, who read patch notes the way other people read comics, had messaged Billy every single detail before the servers were even back up. A new Sprite called Honey that sent angry bees after anyone you shot. Hunting Rifles you could hack into your matches. The Witch Broom vaulted ("RIP broom," Kai had written, with seven crying faces). A mysterious giant pirate chest that had appeared on the beach east of Sunken Shores, which nobody could open and everybody had a theory about.

But the bit that had made Billy put his toast down mid-bite was the bit at the bottom: Power Hours: Trick or Treat Sprites. For one hour, on one day, the spooky Halloween versions of the Sprites would turn up more often than ever. In America the Power Hour started at two in the afternoon on Saturday. In Sydney, because the world was round and annoying, that meant five o'clock on Sunday morning.

Billy had nine Trick or Treat Sprites. There were twenty-four. He had a checklist stuck to the inside of his wardrobe door, and every empty box on it was a small, personal insult.

He wanted the Spooky Dash most of all. Everyone did. It was a little candy-orange jack-o'-lantern with glowing purple eyes and a grin like a slice of melon, and when you equipped it you could jump, then jump again in mid-air, and dash, straight through things, like a ghost in a hurry. It was the most wanted Sprite on the whole Island. Billy had never even seen one in real life. Well, in game life.

His headset buzzed. A message slid in from the corner of the screen.

KAI_THE_DESTROYER: u awake

Billy pulled the headset on and joined the party. There was a crackle, a yawn so enormous it sounded like a cave, and then Kai's voice, thick with sleep.

"Did you sleep?"

"Nope," said Billy happily. "You?"

"Mum confiscated my alarm clock. I had to use the oven timer. I've been lying on the kitchen floor since half past four so I could stop it before it beeped." Kai lowered his voice to a conspiratorial whisper. "If anyone asks, I was getting a glass of water. For an hour."

Billy snorted, then clamped a hand over his mouth. Down the hall, Nan's snoring hiccupped, paused, and resumed.

"Two minutes," Kai said. "I just have to wait for my update to finish. Ninety-eight per cent. Ninety-eight. Still ninety-eight. Come on." A pause. "Why's it always stuck on ninety-eight? It's like it does it on purpose."

"I'll queue solos while I wait," said Billy. "Just one."

"Don't you dare find a Spooky Dash without me."

"I'll find two. I'll give you one."

"You won't."

"I definitely won't," Billy agreed, and selected Solo, Zero Build, because his building looked like a deckchair that had fallen down the stairs, and everyone, including him, knew it.

* * *

Outside, the storm that had grumbled over Sydney half the night was still going. Rain tapped on the window. Far away, out over the harbour maybe, thunder rolled like furniture being moved in the sky.

Billy didn't notice. He was in the Sprite Garden, checking his collection one last time. There they were, his nine Trick or Treat Sprites, sitting on their little glowing pedestals. Vampire, all teeth. Birthday, wearing a slice of cake like a hat. Jonesy, in sunglasses, being cool about everything.

And then there was one he had never seen before.

It was right at the end of the row. Its pedestal flickered. The picture on the card kept glitching, breaking into coloured squares and pulling itself back together, like a reflection in a puddle when someone throws a stone in it. The name underneath said:

TRICK OR TREAT ??? SPRITE

Billy frowned and leaned closer. He'd never seen a glitch like it.

"Kai," he whispered. "Are there any secret Sprites in the update?"

"There's the Quack one, but you have to do all the Mastery stuff to get it. Why?"

Billy opened his mouth to answer.

And the lightning hit.

It wasn't close, not really. It must have hit something down the road, a power pole, a transformer, the big fig tree in the park. But the light came through the curtains white and total, and a split second later came the crack, so loud and so near that Billy felt it in his teeth. The lamp went out. The fan on the console whined. The TV went black.

Then it came back on.

Purple.

Not lobby purple. Not Fortnitemares purple. This was a deep, impossible, electric purple, the colour of a bruise lit from inside, and it was pouring out of the screen like water out of a burst pipe. It spilled across the carpet. It climbed the walls. It turned Billy's hands violet on the controller.

Letters scrolled across the middle of the screen, one at a time, as if someone were typing them very slowly with one finger.

CURSED CARTRIDGE DETECTED.

PLAYER OVERRIDE: ACCEPTED.

"Billy?" said Kai in his ear. "Billy, your screen just went weird on mine. You've got like a purple... thing. Around your name. Billy?"

Billy tried to answer. He couldn't. The purple light had reached his socks, and it was warm, warm and fizzing, like stepping into a bath full of lemonade. He tried to pull his feet up onto the beanbag.

His feet were not there.

They were not gone, exactly. They were becoming something else. They were becoming small, bright, perfect squares of colour, and the squares were floating upwards and drifting gently towards the screen, the way dust drifts in a sunbeam.

There was an ominous sound, a deep electronic hum, rising and rising, like the noise a spaceship makes in a film right before it jumps to light speed.

"KAI," Billy managed.

"What? What's happening? Billy, your character's just standing on the... hang on. Hang on, that's not your skin. That's... Billy, that looks like you."

The last thing Billy saw in his bedroom was the controller, dropping out of hands that were no longer there, and bouncing, once, twice, on the beanbag.

The last thing he heard was Nan, still snoring, peacefully, down the hall.

Then the purple swallowed everything, and Billy fell into the screen.
