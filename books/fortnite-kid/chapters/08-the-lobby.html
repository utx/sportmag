Billy was standing on a stage.

It was a round platform, like the top of a giant drum, and it was floating in the middle of nothing. Below it, above it and all around it stretched a vast purple emptiness, deep and soft and endless, like the sky at dusk with all the stars taken out. Fog curled around the edges of the platform and spilled off into the void like a waterfall of dry ice. Pumpkins were piled up at the back of the stage, grinning. Purple lanterns hung in the air on no strings at all. Somewhere, very faintly, spooky organ music was playing on a loop.

And above him, floating in the sky in letters as tall as the Harbour Bridge, were the words of the menu.

PLAY · OVERRIDE · SHOP · LOCKER · PASSES · QUESTS · COMPETE · CAREER

"Oh," said Billy. "Oh, that's so weird."

It was the lobby. The actual lobby. The place where your character stood around waiting while you sorted out your loadout and argued with your friends about where to land. Billy had spent hours of his life looking at this exact scene on a screen. He had never once wondered what it would be like to stand in it. It was surreal. It was like walking into your own bedroom and finding it had been built in the middle of the ocean.

"Pip?" said Pip, uncertainly, from his shoulder.

"Yeah," said Billy. "Me too, mate."

"Billy!" Kai's voice crackled in his ear, louder and clearer than it had been in the match. "Billy, you're in the lobby! I can see you! You're just standing there in your pyjamas looking confused. It's the best thing I've ever seen."

"What time is it?"

A pause. "Five fifty-one."

Billy did the maths. Mum home at quarter past seven. One hour and twenty-four minutes. One match had taken nearly forty minutes, from the bus to the banner.

"Two more matches," he said. "Maybe three, if they're quick."

"So we make them count," said Kai. "Okay. Look up. Find the Sprites tab. Under Override."

* * *

The Sprite Garden was a real garden.

Billy walked through a door that appeared in the fog at the side of the stage (it said VISIT THE SPRITE GARDEN above it, in curly purple writing) and came out into a little round walled garden, with soft blue grass and glowing paths, and a fountain in the middle that bubbled with something that looked like liquid starlight. All around the walls stood rows of short stone pedestals, and on each pedestal, sitting, standing, snoozing or bouncing, was a Sprite.

His Sprites. Every Sprite he had ever extracted. Jonesy, in his sunglasses, lounging on his pedestal with his arms folded. Birthday, nibbling at its own cake. The Vampire, which saw Billy coming and clacked its jaws at him in a way that suggested it had not forgiven him for the crate. And a new one, the golden Storm Scout, which glittered so brightly the whole wall seemed to glow.

On the very last pedestal, the one that had flickered and glitched on his screen that morning, there was no Sprite at all. There was just a card, standing on its edge, with a tiny picture of Billy on it. Under the picture it now said, very clearly:

TRICK OR TREAT BILLY SPRITE · Lvl 4
MASTERY: WIN TOGETHER
If eliminated: lose one level.

Billy read the last line three times.

"Lose one level," Kai repeated. "Okay. Okay, that's not so bad. That's not, like... that's not the worst thing it could've said."

"It could be bad," said Billy. "If I lose a level every time I get eliminated, I could go backwards forever." But in fact his knees had gone weak with relief. All through the first match, without ever saying it out loud, he had been imagining something much, much worse.

* * *

Kai talked fast after that. He had a plan, and when Kai had a plan he was like a small, determined general.

First: Pip. Pip was saved now, extracted forever, and Pip could be equipped, which meant Pip could come with him into the next match. Billy pressed EQUIP on Pip's pedestal, and Pip, who was already sitting on his shoulders, gave him a look as if to say obviously.

Second: the Portable Extractor. You could buy one with Sprite Dust, the shimmery stuff you earned by playing, and it would let you extract a Sprite anywhere, any time, in five seconds, no crate required. The catch was that you could only buy one a day. Billy bought it. It appeared in his hand, a heavy little device the size of a torch, with a glowing blue window in its side, and he slipped it into his hoodie pocket.

Third: the Loot Hack.

"Hunting Rifle," said Kai firmly. "If antler boy has one, you want one. Then it's fair."

So Billy spent his Sprite Dust, and a little hologram of a long, wooden-stocked rifle spun above his palm, and then winked out. Somewhere in the next match, in one of his chests and no one else's, it would be waiting for him.

And it was then, while he was standing at the edge of the stage looking at the place where the rifle had been, that he noticed the other lobby.

It was a long way off at first. Just a dot of light, out in the purple darkness. But it was drifting closer, the way a boat drifts on a slow current, and as it came closer, Billy saw that it was another platform, exactly like his: the same round drum-top stage, the same fog, the same pumpkins and lanterns. Only this one was emptier and darker, and its lanterns were burning low.

Sitting on the edge of it, with its legs dangling over the void, was Nightantler.

The two platforms drifted until they were maybe twenty metres apart, and then they stopped, hanging in the nothing, turning very slowly, like two leaves on a pond. Nightantler didn't look up. He was hunched over, elbows on knees, staring down into the dark. His great black antlers drooped. In the lobby light, he didn't look so tall.

Billy's heart was thudding.

"Hey!" he shouted across the gap.

Nightantler's head jerked up. The red eyes found him.

"You're stuck too," called Billy. "Aren't you? I saw your squares. When I... when you got eliminated. They were purple. Everyone else's are blue."

For a long time, Nightantler said nothing at all. Then a voice came back across the gap. It wasn't the deep growl Billy had imagined. It was a girl's voice, about his age, maybe a bit older, with an accent that made Billy think of the New Zealand cousins at his friend Jacob's birthday party.

"You cost me a level."

Billy opened his mouth. Shut it again.

"I was Level 4," said the girl. "I'd been working since five o'clock this morning. That's three o'clock for you, if you're an Aussie, and you sound like one. Nine matches. Nine. And I was one Crown Win away. One. I was going to win that match, and then I was going to find out what the last word meant, and then I was going to go home." Her voice wobbled, very slightly, and then hardened, like water turning to ice. "And then you dashed through a truck."

"I didn't know," said Billy. "I didn't know you were... I thought you were just a sweaty."

"I am a sweaty." She stood up. In the lobby, standing on her own stage, she was still tall and dark and antlered and frightening. But Billy could hear now how tired she was. "You read your card, right? Win. That's all the game cares about. One player wins. Everyone else goes home."

"It says win together," said Billy.

"Together with who? It's solos." She laughed, but it wasn't a happy laugh. "There's no together in solos. There's one winner and ninety-nine losers. That's the whole game. That's the only game there is."

Billy didn't know what to say to that.

"So here's how it's going to go," said Nightantler. "I'm going to ready up. And you're going to be in my match, because the game seems to like putting us together. And I'm going to win it. And if you get in my way again, Aussie..." She raised one hand and pointed it across the void at him, finger and thumb, like a gun. "I'm not losing another level for you. Understand?"

Above her head, a word appeared in bright yellow letters: READY.

Her platform began to drift away.

"Wait!" shouted Billy. "What's your name? Your real name?"

But the platform was already shrinking into the dark, and the girl had turned her back, and the only answer was the spooky organ music, still playing on its loop, and the fog spilling quietly off the edge of the stage into nothing.

"Well," said Kai, after a moment. "She seems nice."

Billy didn't laugh. He was looking at his own card, still floating in the corner of his vision. WIN TOGETHER.

Then he looked up at the floating word PLAY, as big as a building in the purple sky, and took a deep breath, and thought, yes.

Gold letters bloomed over his head. READY.

And lower, at the side: FINDING NEXT MATCH...
