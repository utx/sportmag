Billy landed badly, which is to say he landed like a sack of potatoes dropped off a balcony.

The glider folded away above him with a snap, and his socks hit the grass, and his knees buckled, and he rolled twice down a slope and came to a stop on his back, staring up at a purple sky full of falling players. There were dozens of them, little dark shapes against the clouds, their gliders blooming open one after another like strange flowers. Some were heading for the temple. Some were heading his way.

"Get up," said Kai. "Billy. Get up, get up, get up."

"I'm up." Billy staggered to his feet. Nothing was broken. Nothing even hurt. His health bar sat at a cheerful, untouched 100. Apparently, on the Island, you could fall out of the sky and roll down a hill without getting so much as a grass stain. "Which way?"

"Left. Your left. Past the big tree. Can't you see it glowing?"

He could. In a hollow between two twisted trees, something was pulsing with a cold purple light. Billy jogged towards it, then ran, then, without quite meaning to, broke into the long, loping sprint his character always used, and found that his legs knew how to do it. He was faster than he had ever been at school. He was faster than anyone he knew. The trees whipped past in a blur.

And there it was. A Pumpkin Chest.

Billy had smashed hundreds of them through a screen. Up close, it was something else entirely. It was as big as a wheelie bin, dark orange going on black, with a jagged grin carved into its face, and inside it, where a candle should have been, a purple flame was flickering and licking at the edges of the eyeholes. It made a low, crackling, breathing sound. It smelled of cinnamon and burnt sugar.

Floating in the air beside it, in clean white letters, was a single word: SMASH.

"Okay," breathed Billy. "Okay. How do I..."

He reached behind his back, the way his character did, and his hand closed around a handle that had not been there a moment ago. He pulled. Out came his pickaxe, the plain old default one, heavy and solid and real. He hefted it in both hands, incredulous.

"Wait!" Kai hissed. "Wait wait wait. Do the 360."

"Kai."

"Do the three-sixty! The guy in the video got a Sprite every time!"

"That's a superstition. You said so yourself. You said it doesn't actually do anything."

"I know what I said. Just do it. Please. For me."

Billy rolled his eyes so hard it nearly hurt. Then he took two steps back, sprinted, crouched into a long, skidding slide across the grass, spun in a complete circle on the spot with the pickaxe held out like a helicopter blade, and swung.

The pumpkin exploded.

It went off like a firework in a biscuit tin: a burst of orange sparks and purple flame and a loud, wet, satisfying CRUNCH. Bits of pumpkin rained down. Billy ducked, and when he looked up, two things were floating in the air in front of him, turning slowly, waiting.

The first was a gun: a chunky submachine gun with a bony, rattly look to it, like a skeleton had been asked to design a weapon and had got a bit carried away. A Bonerattler SMG. Fortnitemares special.

The second was a Sprite.

It was mostly mouth. A short, round orange body, two stumpy legs, two goggling cartoon eyes on stalks, and a mouth so wide and so full of long, pointy fangs that it looked like a bear trap with legs. A card floated above it: TRICK OR TREAT VAMPIRE SPRITE.

The Vampire Sprite looked at Billy. Then it snapped its jaws, twice, clack-clack, in a way that suggested it was considering his fingers as a snack.

"Vampire!" Kai yelled, forgetting entirely about the sleeping household on his side of the headset. "Billy, that's an S-tier Sprite! It heals you when you hit people! Grab it! Grab it!"

Billy reached out, very gingerly, and the moment his fingers touched it, the Vampire Sprite shrank into a bright orange spark and zipped into his hoodie pocket. The gun he grabbed by the handle. It sat in his hands, heavy and awkward and not quite real-feeling, like holding a toy that someone had made out of iron.

He didn't like it much, to be honest. But he held on to it.

* * *

The second pumpkin was fifty metres further on, behind a fallen log. Billy didn't bother with the 360 this time, and then, at the last moment, he did, because Kai was making a pained noise in his ear and it seemed easier.

CRUNCH.

A pistol popped out (white, with a fat barrel) and, behind it, something small and round and orange tumbled through the air, bounced off Billy's chest, and landed on the grass at his feet.

It was a jack-o'-lantern. A tiny one, about the size of a football, with stubby little arms and stubby little legs and a curly stalk on top. Its carved face glowed from inside with a soft violet light, two round eyes and a big, gap-toothed grin, and as Billy stared at it, the grin got even bigger.

The card above it read: TRICK OR TREAT SPOOKY DASH SPRITE.

"No," whispered Kai.

"Yes," whispered Billy.

The Spooky Dash Sprite blinked its glowing eyes. It looked up at Billy. Then it said, in a voice like a squeaky toy being stepped on very gently, "Pip!"

And it climbed up his leg.

It climbed up his leg, scrambled across his back, and settled itself on his shoulders with its little legs dangling down, the way Sprites always rode on players' backs in the game. It was warm. It hummed faintly. It smelled, Billy noticed, of toffee apples.

"Pip," said Billy, and found he was grinning like an idiot. "Hello, Pip."

"Pip!" agreed Pip, and patted him on the head.

"Billy," said Kai. His voice had gone strange. "Billy, look at your inventory. Open it. Hold the... the tab thing. Whatever you do in there."

Billy didn't know how to open his inventory without a controller, so he simply thought about it, hard, and it opened, a glowing panel unfolding in the air in front of him like a menu on a smart TV. There were his weapons: Bonerattler, pistol. There were his empty healing slots. And there, under the heading SPRITES, was a row of little boxes. The first box held Pip, glowing orange, marked EQUIPPED. The second held the Vampire, chewing on nothing.

The third box held Billy.

It was him. A tiny, perfect, cartoony version of him, in his school hoodie and his pyjama shorts and his socks, looking out of the box with an expression of mild alarm. The card underneath read:

TRICK OR TREAT BILLY SPRITE
SPECIAL · Lvl 1

Player overridden. Extraction locked until Mastered.

Levels up by: opening containers · cracking Cheat Codes · Crown Wins

MASTERY: W_N T_G_TH_R

If eliminated: # # # # # # # # #

For a long moment, neither of them said anything at all.

"I'm a Sprite," said Billy, at last. His voice came out very small. "Kai. The game thinks I'm a Sprite."

"Extraction locked until Mastered," Kai read slowly. "That's... okay, that's how Sprites work. Any Sprite you find, you've got to extract it, or you lose it. Mastered is level five. So you need to level yourself up to five, and then you can extract yourself. And then... I guess you come home." He was talking faster now, the way he did when he was working out a maths problem he actually liked. "Containers. Cheat Codes. Crown Wins. Okay. Okay! That's not even that hard! You've just smashed two pumpkins, those are containers..."

"What about the bottom bit?" said Billy. "What about if I'm eliminated? What does it say?"

They both stared at the row of scrambled squares.

"It doesn't say," said Kai.

That was when the shooting started.

It came from the trees on the left: a sharp, rattling burst, and the bark beside Billy's head exploded into splinters. Something hit his shoulder like a flick from a wet towel, and a big white number, 23, floated up out of his own arm and faded away, and his health bar shivered and shrank.

It didn't hurt. Not exactly. It stung, and it buzzed, and it was the most frightening thing that had ever happened to him.

A player was running at him through the trees: a big player in a gold-and-black bodysuit, still firing. It wasn't a bot. Billy could tell straight away. Bots ran in straight lines and stopped to think. This one was zig-zagging, jumping, coming straight at him, the way a real person played.

Billy did what anyone would do. He ran.

The bullets chased him. The tree in front of him was huge, a solid wall of trunk, and there was no way round it in time, and he was going to hit it, and then Pip squeaked "PIP!" right in his ear, and Billy remembered what a Spooky Dash Sprite did.

Jump. Then jump again, in mid-air.

He jumped. He jumped again.

There was a whoosh like a giant turning a page, and the world went thin and purple and shivery, and Billy shot forward through the air in a streak of violet light. And through the tree. Straight through the solid trunk, as if it were nothing but smoke. Out the other side. Tumbling. Laughing. Alive.

"Pip!" said Pip proudly.

Behind him, the player in gold slammed into the tree, bounced off, and stood there shooting at the bark.

And far off, on the temple wall, a tall figure with antlers watched the whole thing, and did not move.
