Twenty-three became seventeen. Seventeen became eleven.

Billy watched the number in the corner of his vision drop like the timer on a bomb in a film, and every time it ticked down, somewhere out there in the purple dusk a gun went off, or a shotgun boomed, or a scream of engines rose and fell as somebody drove a car very fast into something very solid. The circle kept shrinking. The storm kept coming. And the players who were left kept being squeezed together into the same small patch of ground, closer and closer, like marbles rolling down into the bottom of a bowl.

"Eleven," said Kai. "Billy, you're top eleven. You've never got top eleven on a Sunday morning in your life."

"Thanks, Kai."

"I'm just saying. It's impressive. Considering you're a Sprite."

Billy was lying flat on his stomach on top of a long, low building made of black glass, about half a kilometre from the edge of Geno's Machine. It had been Kai's idea. "Don't fight," Kai had said. "Not yet. Let everyone else fight. The more they fight, the fewer there are, and the fewer there are, the closer you get to winning. That's not cowardly. That's maths."

So Billy was doing maths. He lay still and watched and waited, while Pip lay beside him on the roof with its little chin propped on its little arms, the purple light flickering in its eyes.

Below them, two players were fighting in the street. One was dressed as a medieval knight. The other was dressed as a carrot. The fight was going very badly for the carrot.

"Nine," said Kai.

The knight turned and ran, and almost immediately ran into a third player coming round the corner, and the two of them went tumbling into a building in a blizzard of gunfire.

"Seven."

Billy wasn't really listening. He was looking up at the tall storm turning over Geno's Machine. Up close, it was enormous, a slow, purple whirlpool hanging upside down in the sky, with lightning flickering deep inside it like fish in dark water. Every few seconds it gave a low groan, a sound so deep it wasn't quite a sound, more a feeling in your back teeth. The black towers of the Machine reached up into it like fingers. And the strangest thing of all was that, every so often, between the groans, Billy was almost sure he could hear something else.

Voices. Very faint. Very far away. Like a radio playing in another room.

He shook his head and made himself look somewhere else.

* * *

By the time there were three of them left, the circle was so small you could have walked across it in thirty seconds.

It sat right on the edge of Geno's Machine, a ring of rubble and twisted metal and cracked panels of glowing circuitry, with a single wrecked truck in the middle and a few half-smashed walls around the edges. Beyond the ring, in every direction, the storm roared and crackled. The light was strange and dim and purple. There was nowhere left to hide.

Billy crouched behind a slab of broken concrete, holding his Sweeper Shotgun so tightly his fingers ached. On the other side of the circle, behind the truck, was a big player in a bright-yellow llama costume. And somewhere else (Billy didn't know where, and that was the frightening part) was Nightantler.

"Llama's got a rocket launcher," said Kai, who could see the llama's weapon on Billy's screen better than Billy could see it in real life. "Don't let him have a clear shot."

"Where's the antler guy?"

"Don't know. He was on the right side a minute ago. He's gone quiet."

Gone quiet. Billy didn't like that at all.

The storm moved again, a final shudder. The circle shrank to the size of a tennis court. The llama, suddenly caught in the open, broke from behind his truck and sprinted for a wall, and in the same instant, from behind a pile of rubble Billy hadn't even noticed, something rose up: tall, dark, antlered, and very, very fast.

Nightantler and the llama met in the middle of the circle. It was like watching two storms collide. The llama's rocket went off in a ball of orange flame. Nightantler leapt straight over it, landed, fired, fired again, and the llama staggered, and his yellow costume flickered, and then he broke apart in a cloud of blue squares and was gone.

2 PLAYERS REMAINING.

And Nightantler turned around, and his red eyes found Billy behind the concrete slab, as if he had known exactly where Billy was all along.

"Billy," said Kai, very quietly. "Your shield's fine. Your health's fine. He took damage from the rocket. He's got less than you. You can do this."

Billy's mouth had gone completely dry. "What if I can't?"

"Then you can't, and we'll work it out. But you can."

Nightantler took a step forward.

Billy reached into his pocket, and his fingers closed on something small and soft and wrapped in crinkly paper. He had found a handful of Hop Drops in a chest near the edge of Reality's Reign: little Halloween sweets, sugar-crusted and fizzy. They only healed a tiny bit, Kai had told him. But they did something else, too.

He unwrapped one and put it in his mouth. It tasted of sherbet and bubblegum, and the moment he swallowed it, he felt light. Light as a balloon. Light as a dandelion seed.

Then he stood up from behind the slab, and he jumped.

He went up. Up and up, much higher than any normal jump, floating, drifting, as slowly and gracefully as an astronaut on the moon. Nightantler's shotgun boomed, and the shot went under Billy's socks, and Billy was still rising. Nightantler's head tipped back to follow him. And at the very top of the float, Billy jumped again, in mid-air, and dashed.

He came down out of the purple sky like a thunderbolt, in a streak of violet light, straight through the wrecked truck, and landed right behind Nightantler. So close he could have reached out and touched those antlers.

Nightantler began to turn.

Billy fired.

117.

Nightantler's shield shattered with a noise like a window breaking. He staggered sideways. For one frozen second, he and Billy looked at each other, close enough that Billy could see his own face reflected in those red eyes.

Billy fired again.

And Nightantler broke apart, the way all eliminated players broke apart, into a spinning cloud of tiny glowing squares that spiralled upwards and vanished into the storm.

But the squares were not blue.

Every other player Billy had seen eliminated that morning had dissolved into blue squares, the clean, bright blue of the game itself. Nightantler's squares were purple. The deep, electric, impossible purple of a bruise lit from inside. The purple of the light that had poured out of Billy's TV, an hour ago, a lifetime ago, in a bedroom on the other side of the world.

Billy stared at the place where Nightantler had been.

Then the sky went gold.

* * *

It came down out of the clouds like a falling flag: a huge, shining, orange-and-gold banner, as wide as a football pitch, unrolling as it fell, with a golden crown at one end and words in great fat letters that Billy had waited months to see.

#1 VICTORY ROYALE

Music burst out of nowhere: trumpets and drums and a choir, the whole triumphant roar of it. Fireworks went off above the Machine. Golden confetti fell like rain. Billy stood in the middle of it, with his shotgun hanging from one hand and Pip leaping up and down on his shoulder, squeaking "Pip! Pip! PIP!" at the top of its tiny voice, and in his ear, Kai was screaming.

"YOU WON! YOU WON! BILLY, YOU ACTUALLY WON!"

"I won," said Billy. He said it again, to see if it was true. "I won."

Messages tumbled across his vision faster than he could read them. ALL SPRITES EXTRACTED. Pip glowed white for a moment, and Billy panicked, but Pip didn't go anywhere. It just went on bouncing, and a little gold tick appeared next to it in his inventory. Saved. Safe forever. The Gold Storm Scout, too. REWARD UNLOCKED: OVERRIDE 'BRELLA, said another, and somewhere in his inventory, Billy felt something new: an umbrella, folded and waiting. The prize for a Victory Royale this season.

And then, last of all, in front of everything:

TRICK OR TREAT BILLY SPRITE · CROWN WIN · LEVEL UP! · Lvl 4

MASTERY: WIN TOGETHER

Extraction: locked.

The music played on. The confetti kept falling. The trumpets reached the top of their tune and went round again.

Billy waited for the white light. For the pull. For the floor of his bedroom and the beanbag and the smell of Nan's scrambled eggs. He waited, and he waited, with his heart beating so hard that it hurt.

Nothing happened.

It was the biggest anticlimax of his entire life.

"It's still locked," he said. His voice cracked in the middle. "Kai, I won, and it's still locked."

"Win together," said Kai slowly. "Not win. Win together."

The banner rippled. The fireworks faded. And then the world folded up around Billy like a map being put away, neatly, corner to corner, until there was nothing left but white.
