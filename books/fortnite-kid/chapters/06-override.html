"Don't," said Kai.

"I have to."

"You don't have to. That's the whole point of Override Consoles. Nobody has to. Billy, the second you touch it, your name goes up on the screen for the entire lobby. Everyone will know exactly where you are."

Billy was running. The storm was behind him, a purple wall grinding forwards through the red trees, and the pink beam of the console was in front of him, rising out of the heart of Reality's Reign like a lighthouse. And somewhere off to his left, running parallel to him, unseen but horribly certain, was the antlered player.

"He's going for it," Billy panted. "If he gets there first, he picks. And you said some Overrides are only good for the person who chooses."

"Some," admitted Kai. "The Squad ones."

"Then I'm not letting him choose."

Kai said a word that would have got him in serious trouble at school, and then, more calmly, "Okay. Okay. Go left at the big fan. There'll be guards."

* * *

Reality's Reign was even stranger from inside.

The ground was made of dark, smooth panels, like a circuit board, with thin lines of light running between them in every direction. The graphics-card towers loomed overhead, each one as tall as a block of flats, their cooling fans turning lazily with a deep whum, whum, whum that Billy felt in his chest. Everything hummed. The air smelled of hot plastic and electricity, the smell of a laptop that's been on all day. Neon cooling pipes looped overhead like giant glowing waterslides.

The console stood in a little square in the middle of it all. It looked like an arcade machine that had been to the gym: a huge, chunky cabinet, black and pink, with a glowing screen and three big buttons on its front. The pink beam rose from its top into the sky.

Around it, guarding it, stood the NITE Units.

There were three of them. They were robots, about twice Billy's height, smooth and grey and shiny, with heads like upside-down buckets and a single slit across the front of each where a single red light slid slowly from side to side. They stood absolutely still. Billy had seen them in the game hundreds of times. He had never noticed, until this moment, how very much he would not like to be noticed by one.

He crouched behind a cooling pipe at the edge of the square, and tried to make his breathing quieter.

"They've got a chest," whispered Kai. "And they're tough. But they're slow to turn. If you dash through the middle..."

"Through the middle of the robots?"

"You'd be past them before they could aim. Probably."

"Probably," said Billy.

"Look, have you got a better idea? Because the storm's forty seconds away and your antler friend is about to come round that corner, and when he does..."

Pip patted Billy firmly on the head. "Pip."

"Fine," said Billy. "Fine." And before he could think about it any longer, he jumped out from behind the pipe, jumped again in mid-air, and dashed.

* * *

Everything happened at once.

The world went thin and purple. Billy shot across the square in a streak of violet light. The nearest NITE Unit's red eye snapped towards him with a whine, too late, and he was through it (through it, through cold metal and tangled wire and a strange, ticklish buzzing, like walking through a room full of bees), and out the other side, and tumbling to a stop with his palms slapping against the front of the console.

The screen lit up under his hands.

CHOOSE ONE

Three cards slid onto it, side by side, glowing.

INFINITE STAMINA · Sprint, like, a lot.
ENDLESS SHOOTER · Bigger magazines. Unlimited ammo.
OVERSHIELD · Extra shield, for everyone.

"Overshield," said Kai instantly. "That's S-tier. Pick Overshield."

"But everyone gets it. He gets it."

"Yeah, but fights last longer, so you've got more time to dash away. Overshield. Trust me."

Behind Billy, the NITE Units were turning. Slowly, heavily, with a grinding of gears, like three fridges deciding to get up and go for a walk. Their red eyes slid towards him. There was a high, rising whine as something in their arms began to charge.

Billy slapped OVERSHIELD.

The console played a triumphant electric chord. The other two cards shattered into fragments of light. And then, from the top of the console, the pink beam turned white, and spread out, and became a vast glowing ring of letters that hung in the sky over Reality's Reign, slowly turning, readable from every corner of the Island:

BILLY HAS OVERRIDDEN THE MATCH: OVERSHIELD

Billy stared up at it.

Not his account name, which was BillyBlastz2016 and which he had chosen when he was seven and had regretted ever since. Just BILLY. His real name. Written across the sky in letters as tall as houses, for ninety-something strangers to read.

It gave him the strangest feeling, a feeling like being seen through a window when you thought the curtains were shut.

Then a fresh layer of shield poured over him, cool and tingling, like jumping into the sea on a hot day, and a second blue bar, brighter than the first, appeared on top of the old one in the corner of his vision, and he did not have time to feel strange about anything, because the first NITE Unit fired.

* * *

Later, Billy would not remember the next minute in order. He would remember it the way you remember a dream: in flashes, out of sequence, all the colours turned up too bright.

He remembered the NITE Units' blasts, which were not bullets but fat, slow, sizzling balls of red energy that left trails in the air like comets.

He remembered diving behind the console, and the console shaking like a jelly as the blasts hit it.

He remembered Kai shouting directions, left, no, your other left, the pipe, go up the pipe, and his own voice shouting back, high and breathless, words that didn't make sense.

He remembered the antlered player arriving.

He came over a rooftop at full sprint, black against the white letters in the sky, and leapt down into the square in a single bound, and landed in a crouch between the robots without even seeming to notice them. Up close, his antlers were huge, curved and pointed and darker than shadows. His skin was a deep, dark brown, rough like bark. And his eyes really were red, two steady points of light, like the standby lights on a TV in a dark room.

He looked at Billy. Billy looked at him.

Above the antlered player's head, in small white letters, floated his name: NIGHTANTLER.

Then Nightantler raised a shotgun, a heavy, black shotgun with a jagged barrel, and Billy jumped, jumped, and dashed straight up the side of the nearest cooling pipe, through the pipe wall, and found himself inside it.

It was full of glowing pink liquid. It was not wet. It was warm and fizzy and it moved, and it grabbed him and swept him away, and suddenly he was flying through the inside of the pipe like a marble down a marble run, round bends and down slopes and through loops, screaming, laughing, with Pip clinging to his ear and squeaking with joy, faster and faster, until the pipe spat him out of its end in a shower of pink sparks and dropped him into a bush at the edge of the fortress, a hundred metres from the square.

Behind him, Nightantler was shooting at a NITE Unit. The NITE Unit was shooting back. Neither of them had seen where Billy went.

"That," said Kai, "was the most beautiful thing I've ever seen."

Billy climbed out of the bush. He had leaves in his hair and a stitch in his side, and his two blue bars were full, and he was more alive than he had ever been. Then he glanced over his shoulder, and stopped.

The storm was right there.

It was ten metres away and coming closer, a wall of purple static as high as the sky. Up close it was not like cloud at all. It was like the hiss of a radio between stations, made into something you could see. It crackled. It crawled. It swallowed a tree, and the tree turned grey and flat and silent inside it.

Billy turned and ran. He ran harder than he had ever run before. But the storm was quicker, and its edge rolled over him, and for three long, horrible seconds he was inside it: in a grey, roaring, prickling world where every part of him fizzed, as though he were a can of drink that had been shaken. His health ticked down, one point at a time. 99. 98. 97.

Then he burst out the other side, back into colour, gasping.

On the top of his vision, a new message appeared: 23 PLAYERS REMAINING.

The storm wasn't going to stop. It never stopped. It was the one thing in the game that was relentless, that never got tired and never gave up and never changed its mind. Everything else could be fought or fooled or outrun for a while. Not the storm.

And in the very middle of the circle, where everyone was going, where the final fight would be, Billy could just see the black towers of Geno's Machine, and the other storm, the strange one, the tall one, turning slowly above it like a spinning top.
