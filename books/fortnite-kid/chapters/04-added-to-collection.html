"You've got to extract the Vampire," said Kai.

Billy was crouched behind a boulder at the top of a ridge, breathing hard, with Pip clinging to his hair like a small orange hat. Below them, the land changed so suddenly it looked as if someone had cut two different jigsaw puzzles in half and jammed them together. On this side: shadowy forest and spooky purple grass. On the other side: Green Hill Zone, all bright emerald hills and palm trees, giant loop-the-loops and cliffs with chequered stripes of brown earth, like a cake cut open to show its layers. The sun seemed to shine there even though it wasn't shining anywhere else.

"Why the Vampire?" Billy whispered. "Why not Pip?"

"Because Pip's the one who keeps you alive. You need the dash." Kai was talking fast. "But that Vampire's S-tier, Billy. If you get eliminated, you lose every Sprite you haven't extracted. So you bank it. There's an Extraction Site down there by the big loop. You call a crate, it flies in, you put the Sprite in, it flies off, and it's saved forever."

"And then," said Billy slowly, "maybe I can put myself in."

Kai didn't answer that. Billy could hear him chewing his lip down the line, which was a thing Kai did when he didn't want to say something.

"Let's just try," said Billy, and went over the ridge.

* * *

The first golden ring was floating in the air beside a palm tree, spinning slowly, glittering.

Billy knew about the rings. Every Fortnite player knew about the rings. They were one of the best things about Green Hill Zone, and also one of the strangest: collect one, and it gave you five health, or five shield if your health was already full, and if you kept collecting them they stacked up past your normal shield into extra, bonus Overshield. In a Zero Build match, with no walls to hide behind, that was worth more than gold.

What he hadn't known was the noise.

He ran through the first ring and it went CHING!, a bright, high, joyful sound like a coin hitting a bell, and a little shiver of warmth went through him from head to toe. The green bar at the bottom of his vision ticked up. CHING! Another. CHING! CHING! CHING! There was a whole trail of them, curving away across the grass, and he followed it at a full sprint with Pip whooping on his shoulders, and his health went up to a hundred, and then the blue shield bar began to fill as well, and by the time the trail ran out he was laughing out loud.

"Billy," said Kai. "You sound like a pinball machine."

"It's amazing," said Billy.

The Extraction Site sat on a flat stretch of ground at the foot of the biggest loop-the-loop, a round metal pad with a glowing blue spiral painted on it and a fat stone pedestal in the middle. Billy jogged up to it. A prompt appeared in the air: CALL EXTRACTION CRATE.

He pressed it. Or rather, he thought yes, that one, and it pressed itself.

Far above, something boomed. Billy looked up. A beam of white light had punched down out of the clouds, straight down onto the pad, and in the beam, very small and very high, something was falling. A clock appeared in the corner of his vision: 0:45.

"Forty-five seconds," said Kai. "Billy, that beam can be seen from everywhere. Hide."

Billy didn't need telling twice. He scrambled up a grassy bank and wedged himself into a gap between two chequered rocks, where he could see the pad without being seen. He crouched there, and he waited, and Pip pressed itself against the back of his neck.

The clock ticked down. Forty. Thirty-five. Thirty. Somewhere far off, someone was shooting. The sound rolled across the hills like distant thunder, and every crack of it made Billy's shoulders twitch.

He thought, suddenly and vividly, about Nan. About her making scrambled eggs in the kitchen at seven o'clock, humming to herself, and calling down the hall for him to come and eat. About Mum coming home at quarter past, grey-faced and tired after twelve hours at the hospital, and opening his bedroom door to say good morning, and finding an empty beanbag and a controller on the floor and the TV glowing purple.

He pushed the thought away. It came back. He pushed it away again.

Twenty. Fifteen.

"Someone's coming," said Kai sharply. "Billy, someone's on the hill behind you. I can see them on your screen. Top of the loop."

Billy twisted round. A player was walking along the very top of the giant loop-the-loop, upside down for a second at its peak and then the right way up again, as casually as someone strolling along a footpath. They were wearing a dark bodysuit and a helmet like a fly's head, with huge bulging lenses, and they were carrying a shotgun.

Ten. Five.

With a sound like a wardrobe being dropped from an aeroplane, the Extraction Crate slammed down onto the pad. It was a fat metal capsule with a domed lid and lights pulsing all along its seams, and as it landed it hissed, and a ring of steam puffed out around its base, and its lid swung open with a soft and welcoming ding.

Billy ran.

He slid the last few metres across the pad on his knees (and later, he would admit that this was mainly because it looked cool) and stopped right beside the crate. Inside, it was lined with soft blue light, like the inside of a swimming pool at night. A prompt appeared: EXTRACT SPRITES.

He took a breath. He climbed onto the edge of the crate, and lifted one sock, and put his foot inside.

The crate made a noise like a buzzer on a quiz show when somebody gets the answer badly wrong. BZZZT. A red message flashed up in front of his face:

TRICK OR TREAT BILLY SPRITE · EXTRACTION LOCKED
Mastery required.

Something invisible shoved him, gently but firmly, and he toppled backwards onto the pad on his bottom.

For a second, Billy just sat there. It was a strange thing. He had known it wouldn't work. Kai had known. The card had said it, plain as day: locked until Mastered. And yet, somewhere deep down, he had hoped. And now the hope went out of him all at once, like air from a punctured pool toy, and his eyes went hot, and his throat went tight.

"Billy." Kai's voice was very gentle. "The Vampire. Do the Vampire. Quick."

Billy reached into his hoodie pocket. The Vampire Sprite came out snapping, and he held it over the crate, and it gave him a look of deep betrayal, and he dropped it in. The lid swung shut. The lights along the seams flashed white, white, white, and with a WHOOSH the crate launched itself straight back up the beam of light and vanished into the clouds.

Gold letters bloomed in the air above the pad:

ADDED TO COLLECTION

And then, in smaller letters underneath, with a happy little jingle:

TRICK OR TREAT BILLY SPRITE · LEVEL UP! · Lvl 2

"Containers!" yelled Kai. "The crate's a container! You opened it! Billy, you levelled up!"

Billy looked at his own card. The little cartoon Billy in the box had a tiny 2 next to him now. And under the scrambled word at the bottom, one of the blanks had filled itself in:

MASTERY: W_N T_GETH_R

"Tigether?" said Kai. "Tugether? What's that?"

Billy didn't have time to think about it, because a shotgun blast hit the pedestal beside his head and turned a chunk of it into gravel.

* * *

The fly-helmet player had jumped down off the loop and was coming straight for him across the grass, pumping the shotgun. Billy scrambled up. His Bonerattler was in his hands. He didn't remember drawing it.

He fired. It was like trying to hold onto a washing machine on the spin cycle. The gun bucked and rattled and sprayed, and most of the bullets went into the sky, and some of them went into the grass, and three of them, by pure luck, went into the fly-helmet player, who staggered, and little white numbers floated up off his shield, 12, 12, 14, and then he fired back.

61.

It was like being hit with a pillow at a hundred kilometres an hour. Billy's whole blue shield bar vanished in one go, and a crack of light went across his vision with a noise like breaking glass, and he went over backwards. Overhead, a message flicked past: SHIELD CRACKED.

"Dash!" shouted Kai. "Dash, dash, dash!"

Billy jumped, jumped, and was gone in a violet streak, straight through a chequered boulder, out the other side. The fly-helmet player spun round, looking for him. Billy came down behind him in a crouch, on the edge of a cliff, with the long drop to a river below and his heart going like a drum machine.

And there, lying on the grass, gleaming, right in front of him, was a purple shotgun.

Billy knew it at once: a Sweeper Shotgun. An Epic one. The fly-helmet player must have dropped a spare. Billy didn't even have a shotgun to swap, so he grabbed it the ordinary way, and it slid into his hands like a key into a lock. It didn't feel like a toy made of iron. It felt right.

The fly-helmet player turned.

Billy pulled the trigger.

94.

He pulled it again.

The fly-helmet player didn't fall over, or cry out, or anything like that. He simply flickered, and broke apart into a swirl of little blue squares, like a picture on a screen dissolving, and the squares spun up into the air, and in their place was a small pile of loot: ammo, a bandage, a shield potion glowing blue.

A word drifted across Billy's vision in bold red letters: ELIMINATED.

And under it, in white: 98 PLAYERS REMAINING.

Billy stood very still on the clifftop, holding the shotgun, with Pip trembling on his shoulder. Somewhere, he told himself, a kid had just put down a controller and groaned and gone back to the lobby. That was all. That was all it was, for them.

It wouldn't be all, for him. He was almost sure of that.

"Billy?" said Kai quietly. "You okay?"

"Yeah," said Billy. He drank the shield potion. It tasted like blue raspberry and pins and needles. "Yeah. Where's the next pumpkin?"
