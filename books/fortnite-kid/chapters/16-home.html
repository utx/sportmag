The last silver player went down in a cloud of blue.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened at all. The crater was silent. The bees drifted away like sparks from a dying campfire. Billy and Mira stood twenty metres apart in the rubble at the foot of Geno's Machine, weapons still raised, breathing hard, staring at the place where the last enemy had been.

Then the sky went gold.

The banner came down, the way it always did, unrolling out of the clouds, huge and shining. But this time it was bigger than Billy had ever seen it, so big it seemed to cover the whole sky from one horizon to the other, and the gold of it was so bright and so warm that the purple storm above the Machine shrank away from it like a shadow when you switch on a light.

#1 VICTORY ROYALE

And underneath, in letters almost as big:

SQUAD: BILLY · NIGHTANTLER · KAI_THE_DESTROYER

The trumpets came. The drums came. The confetti came, a golden blizzard of it. In Billy's ear, from a kitchen on the other side of Sydney, Kai was making a noise that wasn't really a word, a long, high, joyful scream like a kettle coming to the boil, and in the background, very faintly, Billy could hear Kai's mum saying, "Kai! Kai! What on earth..."

And in front of Billy's eyes, his card appeared. Huge. Glowing.

TRICK OR TREAT BILLY SPRITE
✓ WIN TOGETHER
MASTERED
Extraction: unlocked.

Across the crater, Mira was staring at a card of her own. Billy couldn't read it. He didn't need to. He could see her face. She had both hands pressed over her mouth, and her red eyes were shining, and her antlers were trembling.

Then she was running across the rubble towards him, and Billy was running towards her, and they met in the middle and crashed into a hug so hard that one of her antlers bonked him on the head. Neither of them cared. Pip squeaked. Honey buzzed. Somewhere very far above them, the storm over Geno's Machine let out one last long, low groan, a groan that sounded, Billy thought, very much like disappointment.

"We did it," said Mira into his shoulder. "We actually did it."

"Together," said Billy.

"Together," she agreed, and laughed, and wiped her eyes, and laughed again.

* * *

The extraction beams came down a moment later: two pillars of soft white light, punching down out of the golden sky, one for each of them. Billy felt his landed on him like warm sunshine through a window. Mira's landed on her. And where the beams touched them, they began, very gently, to come apart.

Not purple, this time. Not unravelling. The squares that lifted off them were gold, and warm, and they felt like being tucked into bed.

"Mira," said Billy quickly. "Add me. Properly. When you're home."

"BillyBlastz2016," said Mira. "How could I forget?" She was fading, from the antlers down, her outline turning into golden light. "Hey, Billy?"

"Yeah?"

"Thanks for not shooting me."

"Thanks for not shooting me again."

She laughed, and the last of her went up into the light like sparks off a sparkler, and she was gone.

Billy looked down at his shoulders. Pip was looking up at him with its round violet eyes. It wasn't fading. Pip belonged here, on the Island. Pip would be in the Sprite Garden, on its little pedestal, whenever he went looking for it.

"I'll equip you every single game," Billy told it. "Every one. I promise."

"Pip," said Pip softly, and patted him one last time on the head, and hopped down onto the rubble, and stood there waving one stubby little arm.

"Billy," said Kai in his ear, very quietly. "Go home, mate."

The gold rose up over Billy's eyes, and the last thing he saw of the Island was a small orange jack-o'-lantern, waving, in a crater full of confetti, under a sky the colour of a Victory Royale.

* * *

Carpet.

That was the first thing. Rough, slightly scratchy carpet against his cheek, smelling of dust and old socks and home.

Billy opened his eyes.

He was lying on his bedroom floor, curled up beside the beanbag. Grey morning light was coming in round the edges of the curtains. The rain had stopped. A magpie was warbling somewhere outside, and a plane was grumbling overhead on its way down to the airport, and the TV was on, showing the ordinary lobby, the ordinary purple sky and ordinary pumpkins, with the music playing softly on its loop. His controller was lying exactly where it had fallen, on the beanbag, by his hand.

He sat up. He looked at his hands. They were hands. Not squares. He wiggled his toes inside his socks.

The clock on his desk said 7.14.

And from down the hall came the most wonderful sound in the whole world: Nan, in the kitchen, clattering a frying pan onto the stove and singing, badly and loudly, an old song about a yellow submarine.

"Billy!" she called. "Eggs in five minutes!"

And then, at 7.15 exactly, a key in the front door.

Billy was on his feet before he knew it. He was out of his room and down the hall, and there was Mum, in her blue hospital scrubs, grey-faced and yawning, putting her bag down on the hall table. She looked up, and saw him, and her eyebrows went up in surprise.

"Well, look at you," she said. "You're up early."

Billy hugged her. He hugged her so hard that she said "Oof!" and staggered back against the coat hooks.

"Gosh," she said, muffled, into his hair. "What's all this in aid of?"

"Nothing," said Billy. "Just. Good morning."

Mum held him out at arm's length and looked at him, the way mums look at you when they know perfectly well that something is going on.

"Couldn't sleep," said Billy. "Played a bit of Fortnite."

"Did you win?"

Billy thought about it.

"Yeah," he said. "We did."

* * *

After breakfast (three eggs, two pieces of toast, a glass of orange juice and a very long story from Nan about a man she had once met at a bus stop), Billy went back to his room and picked up his phone.

There were thirty-one messages from Kai. Most of them were just the word BILLY, in capital letters, with an increasing number of exclamation marks. The last one said: u home?? reply or i'm coming round. also mum says dishwasher until EASTER now. worth it.

Billy typed back: home. coming round at 10 to help with the dishwasher. ur a legend.

Then he picked up his controller and opened his Sprite Garden.

The glitched pedestal at the end of the row was gone. In its place was an ordinary empty one, waiting, like all the others, for a Sprite he hadn't found yet. And on the pedestal next to it, sitting very upright with its legs dangling over the edge, was a little orange jack-o'-lantern with glowing violet eyes. Its card said TRICK OR TREAT SPOOKY DASH SPRITE. And beside its name, where the level should be, there was a small golden star, and a single word.

MASTERED

As Billy watched, the little Sprite on the screen turned its head and looked straight out at him, and grinned.

Billy grinned back.

A notification slid into the corner of the screen. NIGHTANTLER has accepted your friend request. And then, a moment later, a message:

NIGHTANTLER: home!!! mum's making pancakes. tama has woken up EVERYONE. log flume at 11. thank u aussie. same time next sunday? (normal style this time. NO cartridges.)

Billy laughed out loud, all alone in his bedroom.

BILLYBLASTZ2016: deal. kai says he's bringing the banana.

He put the controller down. Outside, the sun was coming out over the wet rooftops of the inner west, and the magpie was still singing, and somewhere a dog was barking at a plane. In a while, he would walk round to Kai's house, and unstack a dishwasher, and try to explain everything, and fail, and laugh about it.

But just before he switched the TV off, he noticed something in the lobby's news panel. A picture of a beach, east of Sunken Shores, and on the sand, half buried, the giant pirate chest that had appeared on Thursday. Nobody had worked out how to open it yet.

Except that, in the picture, very faintly, from the crack under its lid, there was a light leaking out.

A deep, electric, impossible purple.

Billy looked at it for a long, long moment.

Then he switched off the TV, and went to find his shoes.

THE END
