"Explain it again," said Kai. "Slower. Like I'm a goldfish."

"Squads," said Billy.

He was on the Battle Bus again. Third time. He was getting used to it now, the way you get used to anything if it happens often enough: the roar of the wind, the blueberry smell, the rows of frozen players staring at nothing. Mira was sitting four seats in front of him, her antlers brushing the roof. She hadn't said a word since the lobby. But she had sat where he could see her, which felt like something.

"Win together," Billy went on. "In solos, there's no together. Mira's right about that. One winner, every time. But in squads..."

"In squads, the whole squad wins," Kai finished. "Everyone on the team gets the Victory Royale. Even if they've been eliminated." He was quiet for a second. Billy could practically hear him thinking. "That's actually... that's actually not stupid at all."

"Can you do it? Can you put us in a squad?"

"I can try. If I send her a friend request, and she accepts it in the lobby, I can invite you both into my party. I'm the party leader. I pick the mode." His voice sharpened. "But not now. You're already in a match. You've got to finish this one first. And Billy?"

"Yeah?"

"You're Level 3. You were Level 4 before. I don't know if it matters, but I don't want to find out at the end that it does. Get it back."

Billy looked out of the door. The bus was crossing the east side of the Island, and below them, blazing in the low morning light, lay a forest the colour of a bonfire.

"Golden Grove," he said. "Loads of chests."

"More than fifty," agreed Kai. "Go."

* * *

Golden Grove was the most beautiful place Billy had ever seen.

The trees were enormous, broad and round and soft-looking, like giant scoops of ice cream, and every one of them was a different shade of autumn: copper and amber and scarlet and gold, burnt orange, deep wine red. Their leaves drifted down constantly, slowly, spinning, and lay in drifts so deep that Billy's socks sank into them up to the ankle. The light that came through the canopy was warm and golden, the colour of honey held up to a window. It smelled of woodsmoke and apples. Every so often, a breeze ran through the treetops and they rustled and whispered like an audience settling down before a show.

Billy wanted to stop and look at it all. He didn't let himself.

He was tenacious, that third match. He went through Golden Grove like a vacuum cleaner. He opened chests in hollow tree trunks and chests in log cabins and a chest that was sitting, for no reason anyone could explain, in the middle of a pond on a lily pad. He smashed two Pumpkin Chests, and did the 360 for both of them, without being asked, because by now it felt rude not to. (Kai made a small, delighted noise each time.) His card ticked up and up, XP fizzing into the air above every box and barrel and crate he opened.

Near the middle of the forest, half hidden among the trees, was a building that looked as if it had been designed by a five-year-old with a sugar habit: a long, round-roofed diner painted candy blue, with stripy pipes curling over the top like the tubes of a water slide, and perched along its roof, three times as big as Billy, a row of giant yellow rubber ducks, smiling serenely down at the forest.

"Duck diner," said Kai, with deep satisfaction. "Best roof on the Island. You can see everything from up there."

Billy climbed one of the stripy pipes and pulled himself up onto the roof. The nearest duck gazed down at him with a huge painted eye. Pip, back on his shoulders now, reached out and patted its beak, and then looked at Billy as if to say, this is the best day of my life.

There was a chest on the roof, nestled between two of the ducks. Billy opened it. Inside, lying on a bed of golden light, was a long, slim, wooden-stocked rifle with a scope that glinted like a spoon.

"The Loot Hack's still on," said Kai. "It's yours again."

Billy picked up the Hunting Rifle. It fitted his hands as though it had been waiting for him. And his card, floating in the corner of his vision, gave a happy little jingle.

TRICK OR TREAT BILLY SPRITE · LEVEL UP! · Lvl 4

Billy closed his eyes and let out a breath he felt as if he'd been holding since the graveyard.

"Back to four," said Kai. "Told you. Comebacks are the best."

He was getting good at this. That was the strange thing. Not just the game: he had always been good at the game. He was getting good at being in it. At running without thinking about running, at seeing the glint of a chest through the trees, at hearing the difference between a shot that was close and one that was far away. His body had stopped arguing with the Island and started listening to it.

"Billy," said Kai, in a new voice, a careful one. "I found her."

"Who?"

"Mira. NIGHTANTLER. I searched for her. I've sent the friend request. It's just sitting there, pending. But Billy... her profile. It says last online: 3.02 a.m."

Billy didn't say anything. Three o'clock in the morning, Sydney time. Five o'clock in Auckland. Exactly when she'd said she was pulled in. As far as the outside world was concerned, Mira hadn't played Fortnite for more than three hours. As far as her mum and dad knew, presumably, she was asleep in bed, and in a few hours they would go into her room to wake her for her brother's birthday, and...

He made himself stop thinking it. He climbed back up onto the duck diner roof instead, lay down flat between two of the giant ducks, and raised the Hunting Rifle to his eye.

The storm was coming in from the south now, a purple wall gliding through the trees, and everyone left in the match was being squeezed north, towards the middle of the forest. From up here, through the scope, Billy could see a great deal. A player in a shark costume, crouching in a bush, convinced nobody could see him. A player in a white space suit, opening a chest in a cabin. And on the far side of the forest, beside a long, low research building with a glass roof and a satellite dish, half hidden among the red leaves...

Antlers.

Mira was crouched behind an upturned trolley at the back of the research base. Her health bar, floating over her head, was a thin sliver of green. She'd been in a fight, a bad one, and she'd lost most of it. She was trying to heal. Billy could see her hands fumbling with a bandage.

And creeping towards her, from two different directions, so that whichever way she ran she would run straight into one of them, were two players. One was enormous, in heavy black armour with a skull for a helmet. The other was small and quick, dressed as a pink cat in a hoodie, carrying a shotgun.

Mira hadn't seen them.

And the storm was nearly on top of her.
