The season so far

Before we start ranking things, you need to know what world we're in. Fortnite rebuilds itself every few months, and this season has been one of the busiest ever. Here's the whole story in five minutes.

Welcome to Override

This is Chapter 7, Season 4, and it's called Override. It began on 20 August 2026 and finishes at the start of November. The villain is Geno, who has built an enormous machine in the very centre of the Island. His plan is to hack reality itself.

Geno, this season's villain. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

The season's big idea is that the rules of the game can be overridden. Seven Override Consoles are dotted around the map. Activate one and you change the rules for the whole match. Some Overrides make everyone faster, some turn players invisible while they sprint, and some do something completely ridiculous. There's a whole chapter ranking them later on.

Fortnitemares: the Island gets spooky

On 1 October, Geno slotted a Cursed Cartridge into his machine, and the Island went dark. Fog now rolls between the trees, the sky has turned purple, and two new places have appeared: Nightmare Neighborhood (which replaced The Battlewoods) and Freaky Fields (which replaced Latte Landing). Pumpkin Chests have popped up all over the map, and a batch of spooky weapons and items has arrived.

Fortnitemares always invites guests from horror films and games. This year they include characters from Five Nights at Freddy's, A Nightmare on Elm Street and Scream. They're properly scary, so we mention them in this book but don't show them, and we've left their weapons out of our recommendations.

Five things that make this season different

1. Sprites

Sprites are tiny creatures you collect around the Island. Each one gives you a special power: a dash, extra healing, better loot. They've been one of the most popular ideas in years. See Sprite Fight for every one of them, ranked.

2. Override Consoles

Grab one and you change the rules for everyone. But be careful: activating one tells the whole lobby where you are.

3. Loot Hacks

Before a match, you can spend Sprite Dust (which you earn by playing) to "hack" certain weapons into the game. They'll only turn up in your chests, and nobody else's, which makes them exclusive. The current Loot Hack weapons are the Infantry Rifle, the Light Machine Gun and the Pumpkin Launcher (plus a weapon from a horror film, which we're skipping). Loot Hacks are switched off in Ranked matches, and Epic rotates the set every few weeks. The next swap is due on 15 October.

4. Exotics

Exotics are special versions of normal weapons with a superpower added: an assault rifle that ties balloons to your enemies, or a shotgun that gets stronger every time it hits. You can't buy them this season. You find them in secret vaults, by winning the Duck Race at Heatwave Harbor, or in special rifts.

5. Bosses and Mythics

Mythic weapons are the rarest of all. They usually come from beating a boss. Some bosses left in the Fortnitemares update, and some new (very spooky) ones arrived. The Mythic Proportions chapter has the full list.

Gone, but not forgotten

To make room for the new loot, Epic vaulted (took out of the game) the Drum Gun, the Ranger Assault Rifle and the Tetris Rift in the Fortnitemares update. The boss who used to guard Green Hill Zone has gone too. There are no sniper rifles in the normal loot pool right now. If you love long-range shots, the Infantry Rifle (a Loot Hack) is the closest thing.
