Chapter 3
Shotguns

Most Fortnite fights end up close. Really close. Close enough to see the colour of someone's eyebrows. That's where shotguns rule. A good shotgun shot can win a fight in a single devastating blast, which is why nearly every pro carries one.

Shotguns fire lots of small pellets at once (except for one strange shotgun we'll get to). The closer you are, the more pellets hit. Up close they're the most powerful weapons in the game; far away they're almost useless.

The king of shotguns, and the most famous weapon in Fortnite. It fires slowly, but each shot hits like a truck: 119 to the body at Legendary, and up to 205 on a headshot, which is enough to eliminate a player with full shield and health in one shot. It got a little stronger in September, and it equips faster too.

It's the best shotgun in Build mode, where fights are fast and close and one big hit decides everything. The downside is that if you miss, you have to wait a long moment before you can fire again. So don't miss.

Chug Jug tip: The pro trick is the "pump and swap": fire the Pump, then instantly switch to an SMG or AR to keep the damage coming while your shotgun gets ready again.

This season's brand-new shotgun is fully automatic: hold the trigger and it keeps firing. It hits slightly less hard than the Pump, but fires twice as fast and holds ten shells. Even better, it reloads all ten at once. It also keeps score on screen, with combo multipliers, like an old arcade game.

It's more forgiving than the Pump (miss once and you get another go almost straight away), which makes it a favourite of lots of Zero Build players. It was made a little weaker in September, which is why it's not quite S tier.

The strangest shotgun in the game. Instead of a spray of pellets, it fires one single wooden stake, which makes it far more precise at long range than any other shotgun. A headshot does a whopping 177 damage. The catch: you need to actually aim, because a stake either hits or misses. There are no "half hits" like with pellets.

On paper that sounds brilliant, so why is it down in C tier? Because almost nobody who plays for a living likes it. Evolve Jake, a Zero Build expert, says it's "not an item that I enjoy using at all", SypherPK admits he was "never a fan", and Beebom's October tier list puts it in C too. Missing a stake hurts far more than missing with a spray of pellets, and in a close fight you rarely get a second chance. Fun to try; hard to win with.

The Sweeper came back in September with a makeover. It fires quickly and holds eight shells, which is great while it lasts. But reloading all eight takes a painfully long time (more than five seconds), and in a fight, five seconds is forever. The good news is you can stop reloading at any moment to fire. Some guides rate it low because of that reload, but Zero Build players love it: Evolve Jake says he grabs "whichever one I find a higher rarity of" between the Sweeper and the 8-Bit. Treat it as a slightly clunkier 8-Bit.

Pump or 8-Bit? The great shotgun question

This is one of the biggest arguments of the season, so it gets its own spot in The Great Debates. The short version: Pump for Build, where one big shot after a quick edit wins fights, and 8-Bit for Zero Build or if you're still learning, because it keeps your momentum going even when you miss.

Two more shotguns, the Overpowered Pump and the Overclocked 8-Bit, are Exotics. And the Mythic High Stakes' Shotgun is a super-powered Wood Stake. You'll find all three in Mythic Proportions.
