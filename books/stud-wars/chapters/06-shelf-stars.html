Chapter 6
Shelf stars

These sets are for looking at. They have no stud shooters, no hatches and (mostly) no minifigures. What they do have is shape, detail and clever building tricks. Each one comes on a black stand with a printed nameplate, like an exhibit in a museum.

There are three kinds here. Helmets are life-size-ish models of famous helmets, about 18 to 21 centimetres tall. The Starship Collection is small, very detailed models of big ships, in microscale. And there's one giant yellow logo. The boxes say 18+, but that's because they're aimed at adult collectors, not because kids aren't allowed.

Word Watch

Microscalenoun
Building something huge at a tiny size, where a single round piece might be a whole person.
Silhouettenoun
The outline or shape of something, like a shadow. A good model has the right silhouette even from across the room.
Monochromeadjective
All in one colour, or shades of one colour. Most Imperial ships are monochrome grey.
Texturenoun
How a surface looks or feels: smooth, bumpy, ridged. Builders add texture with tiny pieces.
Aestheticnoun or adjective
To do with beauty and how something looks. (The "a" at the start is said like "ess": ess-THET-ik.)

Helmets

Din Djarin's beskar helmet, with shiny silver parts.

Win: Brickset's reviewer thought it "improves upon its predecessor" (the Boba Fett helmet) "in many respects", with an excellent shape.

Watch out: Lots of light grey "does appear relatively bland". It may leave shops soon.

Jango Fett's blue-and-silver helmet from Attack of the Clones, with its rangefinder sticking up.

Win: It takes "the best qualities from other helmets", including clever angled cheeks.

Watch out: Lots of stickers, many of them repeated, which the reviewer called "the model's most significant problem".

Kylo Ren's black-and-silver helmet from the Sequel Trilogy.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said "the model looks fantastic", with printed pieces for the tricky face pattern.

Watch out: "The only real problem is the price." It may leave shops soon.

The helmet of an AT-AT driver, the pilots of the giant walkers, with red Imperial symbols.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said it might be "my favourite helmet yet", with remarkable accuracy and unusual techniques.

Watch out: Lots of stickers. It may leave shops soon.

A white helmet worn by Imperial Remnant AT-RT drivers in The Mandalorian and Grogu.

Win: "Surprisingly good!", says Brickset's reviewer, with clever building on the face.

Watch out: The reviewer felt this helmet isn't famous enough for the series, because it looks so like the Shoretrooper helmet.

Starship Collection

The Millennium Falcon, small enough to hold in two hands, with tiny Luke, Obi-Wan, Han, Chewbacca and friends hidden in the docking tubes.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said "the sheer quality of this model has won me over", with brilliant detail.

Watch out: It's small, "perhaps too small for the price".

Kylo Ren's black shuttle, with its huge, tall wings, on a display stand.

Win: A dramatic shape that stands out on a shelf.

Watch out: 18.1 US cents per piece, expensive for its size, and Brickset fans give it only 3.7. It may leave shops soon.

A small AT-AT walker mid-stride, with a tiny Snowspeeder wrapping its tow cable round the legs, just like in The Empire Strikes Back.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "arguably the most accurate LEGO rendition of the AT-AT produced to date", which is amazing for its size.

Win: Tiny round-plate snowtroopers wait inside.

Watch out: "Definitely feels a bit too expensive".

A Venator-class Star Destroyer, the red-striped Republic warship from Revenge of the Sith and The Clone Wars.

Win: "Essentially faultless", says Brickset's reviewer: "it is hard to imagine a better Venator-class Star Destroyer at this scale!"

Win: It's built to the same scale as the Invisible Hand set, so they look great together.

Watch out: Expensive at full price. The reviewer would buy "a couple more" if it came down to around £50.

The logo

The Star Wars logo in big yellow letters, 30 centimetres across.

Win: Lift a panel on top of the T and there's a tiny microscale space battle hidden inside!

Win: Brickset's reviewer said the letter shapes are "near-perfect" and the price "quite reasonable".

Watch out: No minifigures (the reviewer joked that a George Lucas minifigure "would have been amazing").
