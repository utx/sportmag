Chapter 2
This is the way

The Mandalorian started as a TV show in 2019 and turned a quiet bounty hunter and a small green child into two of the most famous characters in the galaxy. In May 2026 the pair got their own film, The Mandalorian and Grogu, and LEGO launched a wave of sets to go with it, starting on 26 April, before most people had even seen the movie.

This chapter has sixteen sets, from a pocket-money paper bag to a walker the size of a cat. They're in order of price, cheapest first. Some are based on the film, some on the TV show, and a few are pure fun.

Word Watch

Spin-offnoun
A new show or film that grows out of an older one. The Mandalorian is a spin-off of the Star Wars films.
Exclusiveadjective
Only available in one place. An "exclusive minifigure" comes in just one set.
Compromisenoun
Giving up a bit of one thing to get more of another. Smaller sets cost less, but the compromise is fewer details.
Overpricedadjective
Costing more than it's really worth.
Potentialnoun
What something could become. "Unfulfilled potential" means it could have been much better.

A tiny Razor Crest that comes in a paper bag, for the price of a milkshake. In some places it's given away as a gift with bigger purchases.

Win: Two Brickset members who reviewed it loved the clever cockpit trick and said it "captures the ship's distinctive silhouette remarkably well" with just 74 pieces.

Watch out: No minifigures, and it's a quick five-minute build.

Mando and Grogu on a speeder bike, borrowed on Tatooine. Grogu rides in a satchel on the side, and there's a clip for Mando's blaster.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "essentially a perfect set": the two most popular characters around, for under US$10.

Win: The reviewer wished LEGO made more small sets like this, with one vehicle and a minifigure or two.

Watch out: The Mandalorian looks a bit awkward sitting on the bike.

A 4+ set for younger builders: a brick-built Grogu, a little home scene with a tree, and a catapult.

Win: Easy to build, so it's a good first Star Wars set for a little brother or sister.

Watch out: No minifigures at all, and only 107 pieces for US$19.99.

Watch out: Brickset fans give it 2.6 out of 5, the lowest fan score of any set in this book.

Another 4+ set: a chunky, simple N-1 Starfighter with Mando, Grogu and R5-D4.

Win: Three good minifigures, and a quick, easy build for little kids.

Watch out: It costs 32.6 US cents per piece, the worst value per piece in this whole book.

Watch out: A Brickset member said it "almost does not resemble the original spacecraft" and "falls apart easily".

Cobb Vanth, the marshal of a desert town, on his speeder made from an old podracer engine, plus the blue-skinned bounty hunter Cad Bane.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called the minifigures "outstanding and about as detailed as LEGO figures can be".

Win: A secret storage compartment hides inside the speeder.

Watch out: "Clearly too expensive", says the reviewer, and Cobb Vanth is "dwarfed by the seat".

A two-legged AT-RT walker from the film, and a white defence tower with a spinning turret and a hidden weapons rack. Mando, Grogu and an Imperial Remnant AT-RT driver are included.

Win: Brickset's reviewer liked "almost everything" about it, from the walker's poseable legs to the tower.

Watch out: The price is "basically a fatal blow". The reviewer would recommend it at about two-thirds of the price.

A showdown at a Hutt palace: Mando and Grogu against a big sentry droid, with the bounty hunter Embo and his wolf-like pet Anooba, called Keibu.

Win: Brickset was delighted to see Embo again, fifteen years after his last minifigure, and said Keibu "looks fantastic".

Win: The sentry droid has "lots of mechanical detail".

Watch out: No full review yet. Brickset fans give it 3.6 out of 5.

A blue-striped New Republic X-wing, flown by pilots Carson Teva and Colonel Ward. Pull a lever and the wings open into attack position.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "another fine entry" in LEGO's long line of X-wings, mixing the best features of earlier ones.

Win: Great minifigures with detailed orange flight suits, and stud shooters you can remove if you prefer.

Watch out: The astromech droid sticks up too much, and the laser cannons are shorter than usual, probably to stop them snapping during play.

A starship that belongs to the Anzellans, tiny alien droidsmiths so small that LEGO had to make special mini figures for them.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said it was "my favourite The Mandalorian and Grogu set so far", with a sleek shape and pale yellow details.

Win: Plenty of space inside for the tiny crew.

Watch out: It seems "fairly small for its price", and there are no normal-sized minifigures, only Grogu and two Anzellans.

The Imperial Lambda-class shuttle, with its tall folding wings, carrying Moff Gideon, Dr Pershing, a shuttle pilot and a stormtrooper. The Mandalorian comes too.

Win: Brickset said it "seems like the best Imperial Shuttle play set to date".

Win: Dr Pershing is "a particular standout", and there's a new version of Moff Gideon.

Watch out: Brickset also called the price "very expensive". It says 14+ on the box.

The Mandalorian's own ship, the Razor Crest, rebuilt for the film, with five minifigures including Zeb Orrelios from Star Wars Rebels.

Win: The outside is more accurate than the 2020 version, with fewer gaps, and the cockpit is "superb".

Watch out: Brickset's reviewer found the space in the main hold "completely wasted" and "very few play features for a model of this size".

Watch out: It's smaller than the 2020 Razor Crest, but costs more. "A good set, but one with unfulfilled potential."

A huge Imperial Remnant AT-AT walker, plus an INT-4 starfighter to build alongside it. Seven figures come with it, including a new Imperial Remnant Snowtrooper and Commander Barro.

Win: A Brickset member called it "2026 Set of the Year" and "the best play scale set" for Star Wars in years.

Win: Brickset fans rate it 4.5 out of 5, one of the highest scores in this book.

Watch out: Building four AT-AT legs gets repetitive (that's true of every AT-AT ever).

Best for: the big birthday or Christmas present. Our pick of the movie sets.

From the second ever episode of The Mandalorian: the Jawas' giant Sandcrawler, the horned Mudhorn beast, a mud-covered Mando, Grogu, Kuiil and three Jawas.

Win: Brickset said it "looks surprisingly good", and fans rate it 4.6 out of 5.

Win: 1,683 pieces, with a detailed inside for the Jawas (see the photo below).

Watch out: Brickset noticed the Mandalorian's shoulder armour is the wrong colour. It costs US$199.99 and says 14+.

A big brick-built Grogu and his egg-shaped floating pram, on a display stand with a plaque and a mini Grogu figure.

Win: Over 1,000 pieces for US$99.99, which is decent value per piece.

Watch out: It's a display model, so there's not much to play with. Brickset fans give it 3.9 out of 5.

A life-size-ish Grogu wearing his new beskar armour from the film, with poseable fingers and a small Grogu figure.

Win: Brickset's reviewer preferred the eyes and ears to the 2020 version.

Watch out: The head barely moves, which "really detracts from this figure's personality", and the armour isn't done as well as hoped.

Watch out: The reviewer's verdict: "simply not a worthwhile purchase" at US$129.99.

Five chunky BrickHeadz figures from the film: the Mandalorian, Grogu, Colonel Ward, an Imperial Remnant Snowtrooper and an Anzellan.

Win: 661 pieces for US$39.99 works out at 6 US cents per piece: the cheapest per piece of every set in this book.

Win: Five builds in one box, and they look great lined up on a shelf.

Watch out: They're for display. You can't sit them in vehicles or play battles with them.
