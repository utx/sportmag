Chapter 3
Send in the clones

The Clone Wars is the war fought before the original Star Wars films: armies of identical Clone Troopers against armies of Battle Droids, with Jedi leading the way. It's perfect for LEGO, because the best part of an army is that you can have lots of the same figure. Fans call collecting them army building.

The classic way to build an army is a Battle Pack: a small, cheap set with four or more soldiers and a little vehicle. There's a problem, though, and Brickset's reviewer kept noticing it. Battle Packs have become much more expensive. The 2024 Clone Trooper & Battle Droid pack cost US$29.99 for nine figures. A similar 2025 pack costs US$44.99 for eight. Brickset suggested that new US tariffs were partly to blame, but noted that prices went up in other countries too.

Word Watch

Army buildingnoun
Collecting lots of the same soldier minifigure, so you can build a whole army.
Battalionnoun
A large group of soldiers that fight together. The 212th and 327th are clone battalions.
Infantrynoun
Soldiers who fight on foot, rather than in vehicles or on horses.
Tariffnoun
A tax that a country charges on things brought in from other countries. Tariffs can make imported toys cost more.
Articulationnoun
Joints that let something bend and pose. A well-articulated droid can sit, crouch and point.
Faultlessadjective
Without any faults or mistakes. Perfect.

A red-and-white battle mech for a Clone Shock Trooper, with a big blaster.

Win: Good value per piece, and the colours match the trooper's armour, down to the red feet.

Watch out: Brickset's reviewer called it "decent" but wished for a more exciting design, or a rarer clone inside.

A tiny, chunky version of Jedi Master Plo Koon's blue-and-white starfighter, with the man himself on top.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called Plo Koon "an outstanding minifigure", the main reason to buy it.

Watch out: It "does feel rather expensive for a Microfighter", and his droid co-pilot is only a head.

Darth Maul in a black-and-red mech, with his double-bladed lightsaber and a little probe droid.

Win: Brickset's reviewer was "pleased to see Maul in a smaller set again" and found the price reasonable.

Watch out: It's quite basic, and the legs don't really look like Maul's robotic legs from the cartoon.

Two Mandalorian Super Commandos and two 332nd Clone Troopers face off with a speeder and a barricade.

Win: The Super Commandos "look excellent", with shiny reflective visors.

Watch out: Brickset's reviewer called it "a good set, but also a frustrating one", and found the price "an issue too".

From the Ahsoka TV series: two Death Troopers and two Night Troopers with a small dropship.

Win: Five troopers fit inside the dropship, which is great for play.

Watch out: The reviewer said the dropship "could hardly be any simpler", the Night Troopers are a re-used design, and "a discount is needed".

Four Clone Troopers (one in red Shock Trooper armour) with a speeder, against two Battle Droids, two Super Battle Droids, a Tri-Droid and a STAP. Nine figures in all.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "essentially a faultless Battle Pack!" with "fantastic" play value.

Win: Over 800 Brickset fans have rated it, giving it 4.1 out of 5. One of the most popular sets in this book.

Watch out: Very little. The reviewer prefers the old clone helmet shape.

Best for: anyone starting an army. It's the cheapest way to get both sides of the Clone Wars.

Yellow-striped Clone Troopers from the 327th Star Corps, with an AT-RT walker, against Super Battle Droids and a Dwarf Spider Droid.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said these might be "my favourite versions of the AT-RT and Dwarf Spider Droid to date".

Win: Eight figures, and both sides of the battle.

Watch out: The price "is the decisive issue": almost the same contents as the set above, for half as much again.

A V-19 Torrent, the little Clone Wars starfighter with wings that fold up, flown by a clone pilot with Obi-Wan Kenobi, and the Sith assassin Asajj Ventress.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said it was "definitely in the running for my favourite Star Wars set of the summer" and "really demands to be flown around the room!"

Win: Great minifigures, especially Ventress, back after ten years, and a fair price.

Watch out: It loses the clever function of the old 2008 version, where all three wings and the landing legs moved together.

Best for: swooshing. Our favourite small starfighter.

The ARC-170, a three-seat Republic starfighter with wings that open, flown by clone pilots Jag and Odd Ball with the droid R4-P44.

Win: A Brickset member called it "a fine model of a great ship", with a clean, sharp design.

Watch out: The same reviewer's advice: "wait for a sale to pick it up".

A huge blue Separatist MTT, a hover tank that carries a rack of Battle Droids, from the Battle of Felucia. There are 12 figures, including Aayla Secura and Commander Bly.

Win: The rack slides out and splits apart to deploy the droids. Brickset's reviewer called the mechanism "superb".

Win: Twelve figures, the most of any set under US$200 in this book.

Watch out: US$159.99 is "clearly far too expensive for a vehicle of this size", and there isn't much detail inside.

The Clone Turbo Tank, a giant ten-wheeled tank from Revenge of the Sith, with Ki-Adi-Mundi, Commander Bacara and clones.

Win: The minifigures are appealing, and the tank's shape has "arguably never been better".

Watch out: Brickset's reviewer was blunt: "75413 Republic Juggernaut should not exist". It's fragile, unfinished in places, and the price is "nothing short of madness".

Watch out: Brickset fans give it 3.0 out of 5. It's the only set in this book we gave one brick.
