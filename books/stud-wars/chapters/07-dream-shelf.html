Chapter 7
The dream shelf

Welcome to the Ultimate Collector Series, or UCS: the biggest, most detailed and most expensive LEGO Star Wars sets ever made. They're built for adult collectors, take days to build, and come with a printed information plaque, like a museum display. The cheapest costs about US$200. The most expensive costs US$999.99.

Let's be honest: nobody's getting one of these for a normal birthday. This chapter is for dreaming. It's still fun to know which of them are brilliant and which are just big, and to imagine where on earth you'd put a 1.36-metre Super Star Destroyer.

Word Watch

Colossaladjective
Enormous. Gigantic. It comes from the Colossus of Rhodes, a giant ancient statue.
Flagshipnoun
The most important ship in a fleet, where the admiral sits. Also used for a company's best or most famous product.
Dreadnoughtnoun
A huge, heavily armed battleship. The Executor is a "Star Dreadnought".
Accommodateverb
To have room for something. Can your bedroom accommodate a Death Star?
Lavishadjective
Very rich, generous or fancy. Jabba's barge has lavish details.
Iconicadjective
Very famous and instantly recognisable; a symbol of something.

Word Watch: story words

Look out for these in the "From the screen" boxes.

Eerieadjective
Strange and frightening in a quiet, creepy way.
Saganoun
A long story about the adventures of a family or group over many years.
Smithereensnoun
Tiny broken pieces. "Blown to smithereens" means completely destroyed.

A big AT-ST scout walker, 37 centimetres tall, with its driver.

Win: The designer paid careful attention to the original film models, and the cockpit details are good.

Watch out: Brickset's reviewer found the stance wrong ("you never see Imperial Scout Walkers standing like this") and the model "somehow unremarkable" for a UCS set.

The TIE Interceptor, the fastest TIE of all, with its pointy, split wings and a pilot minifigure.

Win: "Looks spectacular from all angles and the standard of detail is unmatched", says Brickset's reviewer.

Watch out: Some details are fragile, and the cockpit details are hard to see. It may leave shops soon.

The Mandalorian's silver N-1 Starfighter, with Grogu riding behind the cockpit.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said it "looks truly spectacular", maybe their "absolute favourite" UCS starfighter.

Watch out: "Rather expensive". The reviewer thought it should cost what the TIE Interceptor does.

Jango Fett's starship from Attack of the Clones, nearly 3,000 pieces, with Jango and young Boba Fett, seismic charges and all.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "an exceptional reward for our patience" after a 23-year wait for a new version.

Win: The reviewer found even the price "fairly reasonable, given the high standard of detail". Fans give it 4.5.

Watch out: Lots of stickers for such an expensive set.

Jabba the Hutt's sail barge from Return of the Jedi, with a lavish lounge inside and 11 minifigures, including Jabba, Max Rebo and C-3PO.

Win: "Capturing the shape of the onscreen vessel brilliantly", with "countless subtle details", says Brickset's reviewer.

Watch out: "This is another Star Wars set harmed by its price, unfortunately." It may leave shops soon.

The UCS Venator-class Star Destroyer: 5,374 pieces of red-striped Republic warship, with Captain Rex and Admiral Yularen.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said its accuracy and detail "far surpasses even" the UCS Imperial Star Destroyer: "a true flagship model".

Watch out: Stickers, and a price that "is more of an obstacle". It may leave shops soon.

Darth Vader's flagship, the Executor Super Star Destroyer: 6,130 pieces and 1.36 metres long, about as long as a nine-year-old is tall. It comes with a minifigure scene of the bounty hunters on the bridge, and a tiny Millennium Falcon stuck to the back of the command tower.

Win: "The engineering is amazing", says Brickset's reviewer, and the bounty hunter line-up is "brilliant".

Win: Brickset fans give it 4.8 out of 5, the highest fan score in this book.

Watch out: It's so big it is "genuinely tricky to accommodate". The reviewer felt that "a good model is not sufficient for one so expensive".

The legendary UCS Millennium Falcon: 7,541 pieces, 84 centimetres long, with two sets of crew (from the original films and the sequels). It came out in 2017 and is still on sale nine years later.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "massive in every respect", and after 1,498 ratings, fans still give it 4.4 out of 5.

Win: Brickset's 2026 Death Star review says this is "a set I would recommend to any Star Wars fan able to afford and accommodate it".

Watch out: The building takes forever. Brickset's reviewer had spent over 11 hours by bag 13, with hours still to go. Brickset expects it to retire at the end of 2026.

The Death Star: 9,023 pieces, the most of any set in this book, and 40 minifigures, 29 of them exclusive. It's a giant cut-away of the battle station, full of rooms, from the trench run to the Emperor's throne room.

Win: Forty minifigures! And Brickset's reviewer thought the price "feels relatively reasonable" compared with the UCS Falcon.

Watch out: The reviewer found the top rooms weaker, and said it "does not possess the same broad appeal" as the Millennium Falcon. Also: US$999.99.
