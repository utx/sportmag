Chapter 5
Big heads, bigger droids

Some of the most impressive LEGO Star Wars sets right now aren't vehicles at all. They're characters, built big out of bricks: a whole family of droids, a bounty hunter nearly 40 centimetres tall, a giant minifigure and some busts.

The droids are designed to the same scale, so R2-D2, C-3PO, Chopper, K-2SO and BB-8 look right standing together. Each one comes with a printed plaque full of facts and a normal-sized minifigure of the same character, so you can see the big and small versions side by side.

Word Watch

Scalenoun
How big a model is compared with the real thing. Sets "in scale" with each other look the right size next to each other.
Proportionsnoun
How the sizes of the parts compare. A figure with good proportions doesn't have a giant head or tiny arms (unless it's meant to).
Bustnoun
A sculpture of a person's head, shoulders and chest. Museums are full of marble ones.
Protocolnoun
The proper rules for how to behave. C-3PO is a "protocol droid": his job is manners, languages and etiquette.
Photoreceptornoun
Something that detects light. A droid's photoreceptors are its eyes.

A BrickHeadz Luke Skywalker in his orange X-wing pilot suit and helmet, with his lightsaber.

Win: Quick, cheap and cheerful. Brickset fans give it 4.2 out of 5.

Watch out: It's purely for display, and it may be leaving shops soon.

A bust of Yoda with his green lightsaber, one of the first two sets in LEGO's new Bust Collection.

Win: The robe, sleeves, hair and lightsaber look "fantastic", says Brickset's reviewer, and the eyebrows move.

Watch out: Straight lines around the eyes "really spoil the design". It's "one of those rare occasions where one major problem makes it difficult to recommend this set".

The other first bust: Darth Vader, with a head that turns.

Win: Brickset's reviewer found the helmet shape and mask details "exceptional" and called the execution "superb".

Win: A compact Darth Vader for a desk or shelf.

Watch out: It's quite small for US$49.99.

BB-8, the rolling ball droid from The Force Awakens, built to match R2-D2 and C-3PO.

Win: A Brickset member was "pleasantly surprised at how spherical the finished build was", thanks to big printed pieces rather than stickers.

Watch out: It's a much quicker build than the other droids, and Brickset thought US$89.99 "seems quite expensive".

K-2SO, the tall, grumpy security droid from Rogue One and Andor.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "potentially the best Star Wars character statue to date" and "close to faultless".

Win: His long, thin legs are built strong enough to stand, which the reviewer had doubted was possible.

Watch out: A few stickers, and the base is a bit plain.

R2-D2, the bravest droid in the galaxy, with a third leg that folds down and tools that pop out. The bonus minifigure is Darth Malak, a villain from an old video game.

Win: Brickset's reviewer found it more attractive on display than the bigger 2021 R2-D2, with "excellent" shaping.

Win: The size "feels reasonable for the price", and Darth Malak is "an exceptional bonus".

Watch out: The tools don't fold away inside his body.

Chopper, the cranky orange-domed droid from Star Wars Rebels and Ahsoka, who rocks on his wheels and waves his arms around.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said he "looks tremendous from all angles" and has more functions than the other droids.

Watch out: "Too expensive for a model of this size", especially next to the cheaper, bigger R2-D2. Wait for a discount.

A Darth Vader minifigure, but six times bigger: about 28 centimetres tall, with arms, legs, hands and head that move just like the real thing.

Win: A Brickset member called it "absolutely fantastic" and said the build was easy enough for an eight-year-old.

Win: Brickset fans rate it 4.4 out of 5.

Watch out: Another member didn't love the huge cloth cape.

Wicket the Ewok, 23 centimetres tall, with a minifigure version of himself.

Win: A Brickset member found the build "fantastic", "layering up" each part to make thick, furry-looking fur out of bricks.

Watch out: The same member said the price "feels way too much" and wouldn't have paid full price. It may leave shops soon.

A big Battle Droid riding a STAP, the flying scooter from The Phantom Menace, on a clear stand so it looks like it's floating.

Win: Brickset's reviewer said the STAP reached "a standard similar to Ultimate Collector Series models".

Watch out: The droid's arms are bulky, the knees don't bend much, and the price is "the major issue". It's 12+ and may leave soon.

C-3PO, 38 centimetres tall, in shiny gold, with arms you can pose.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "a wonderful surprise", "an exceptional model" and "a lovely counterpart" to R2-D2.

Watch out: His eyes are oversized, and the price is high, though partly because of all the new gold parts.

Boba Fett, the famous bounty hunter, 39 centimetres tall in his armour from Return of the Jedi, with his jetpack and blaster.

Win: Brickset's reviewer called it "a superb model", with realistic proportions and a natural pose.

Win: Brickset fans give it 4.7 out of 5, the second-highest score in this book.

Watch out: The rifle's shape bugged the reviewer, and the upper arms can't move inwards.
