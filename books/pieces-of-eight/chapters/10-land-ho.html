How to be a leak detective

Between now and launch, and again before Chapter 8, the internet will be flooded with "leaks". Some will be right. Some will be wrong. Some will be completely made up to get clicks. Here's how to tell real treasure from fool's gold.

The leak detective's five rules
1
WHO'S TALKING?
Epic's own posts beat famous dataminers. Famous dataminers beat random accounts.
2
FIND A SECOND SOURCE
Two independent sources saying the same thing are worth far more than one.
3
SEEN ISN'T KNOWN
A weapon in a trailer tells you it exists, not how strong it is.
4
SCREENSHOTS LIE
A picture of a "leak" is easy to fake. Where did it come from?
5
DATES MOVE
Until Epic announces it, every date is provisional.

The big idea behind rule 2 has a name. When you verify a fact by checking it against a second, separate source, you corroborate it. Journalists do it. Scientists do it. Historians do it, too: remember how the bell from the Whydah corroborated the story of the wreck? It's the single most useful habit for anyone who reads anything online.

Storm warning: new-season scams

Every new season brings out the scammers. Watch out for anyone offering a "free Battle Pass", "free V-Bucks", "early access" or "secret leaks" if you just log in on their website. They're all lies, designed to steal accounts. Never type your Epic password anywhere except the real game or Epic's real website, never share it with anyone, and if something feels too good to be true, show a grown-up.
