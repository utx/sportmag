How to be a leak detective

Between now and launch, and again before Chapter 8, the internet will be flooded with "leaks". Some will be right, some wrong, and some made up purely for clicks. Here's how to tell real treasure from fool's gold.

The leak detective's five rules
1
WHO'S TALKING?
Epic's own posts beat famous dataminers. Famous dataminers beat random accounts.
2
FIND A SECOND SOURCE
Two independent sources saying the same thing are worth far more than one.
3
SEEN ISN'T KNOWN
A weapon in a trailer tells you it exists, not how strong it is.
4
SCREENSHOTS LIE
A picture of a "leak" is easy to fake. Where did it come from?
5
DATES MOVE
Until Epic announces it, every date is provisional.

Rule 2 is the big one. When you check a fact against a second, separate source, you corroborate it. Journalists do it, scientists do it, and historians do it too: remember how the Whydah's bell proved the story of the wreck? It's the most useful habit for anyone who reads anything online.

Storm warning: new-season scams

New seasons bring out the scammers. Ignore anyone offering a "free Battle Pass", "free V-Bucks" or "secret leaks" if you log in on their website: they're trying to steal accounts. Never type your Epic password anywhere except the real game or Epic's real website, never share it, and if something seems too good to be true, show a grown-up.
