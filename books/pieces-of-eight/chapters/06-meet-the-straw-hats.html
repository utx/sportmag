I'm going to be King of the Pirates!

If you've never watched or read One Piece, don't worry. You're about to become an expert in one chapter. And if you are a fan, see how many details you already knew.

One Piece in one page

One Piece is a Japanese manga (comic) by an artist called Eiichiro Oda. It started in 1997 in a weekly comic magazine and it's still going, nearly thirty years later, with more than a hundred volumes. It has been turned into an anime (cartoon) series with well over a thousand episodes, films, games and a live-action TV show. It has sold more than 500 million copies around the world, and in 2015 it set a Guinness World Record for the most copies of a comic series ever published by a single author. In other words, it's colossal.

The story is set in a world that's mostly ocean, in a "Great Age of Pirates". Long ago, a legendary pirate called Gol D. Roger, the King of the Pirates, announced that he had left all his treasure in one place at the far end of the world. He called it the One Piece. Ever since, thousands of pirates have sailed off to find it.

One of them is a cheerful, endlessly hungry teenager called Monkey D. Luffy, who wants to find the One Piece and become the next King of the Pirates. Along the way, he gathers a crew of misfits, each chasing a dream of their own. Together they're the Straw Hat Pirates, named after the battered straw hat Luffy always wears.

And what actually is the One Piece? Here's the funny part: nobody knows. The story isn't finished, and Oda hasn't revealed it. As the creator of the video we watched put it: "it's not finished yet, so I don't think we know what that actually even is." That's what makes it the perfect theme for a treasure hunt.

The Devil Fruit rule

One more thing you need to know. In One Piece, there are rare, strange fruits called Devil Fruits. Eat one and you gain a superpower, but you pay a terrible price: you can never swim again. The sea itself drains your strength, and if you fall in, you sink like a stone. Which, for a pirate, is a bit of a problem.

Luffy ate the Gum-Gum Fruit as a little boy, and now his whole body is made of rubber. He can stretch his arms across a room, bounce off cannonballs and inflate himself like a balloon. That stretchy punch in the Treasure Hunters trailer? That's his famous "Gum-Gum Pistol".

Monkey D. Luffy, the captain

Luffy is brave, loyal, and not very good at thinking things through. He'll fight anyone who hurts his friends, and he'll eat an entire banquet in one sitting. His straw hat was given to him by a pirate he idolised as a child, and he's promised to give it back one day, once he's become a great pirate himself.

Roronoa Zoro, the swordsman

Zoro fights with a style called Santoryu: three swords, one in each hand and one clamped in his teeth. He wants to become the greatest swordsman in the world. He is also, famously, the worst navigator in the world. Zoro can get lost walking in a straight line. Fans have a running joke that, on a map, Zoro is always somewhere he shouldn't be.

Nami, the navigator

Nami is the crew's navigator: she can read the weather, the wind and the waves better than anyone, and her dream is to draw a map of the entire world. She's sharp, a brilliant thief and very fond of money. She also loves tangerines, which grew in the orchard of her childhood home. (Look closely in the trailer and you'll spot her carrying one.) She's the true cartographer of the crew.

Usopp, the sniper

Usopp has a very long nose and an even longer list of stories about his own bravery, most of which are not entirely true. He's a nervous, boastful teller of tall tales, the champion yarn-spinner of the seven seas. But when it matters, he's a deadly shot with his slingshot, and he's far braver than he thinks.

Sanji, the cook

Sanji is the crew's chef, and he learned to cook at the Baratie, the floating restaurant that appears on the new Fortnite island. He has a strict rule: he never fights with his hands, because a cook's hands are precious. Instead, he fights entirely with powerful kicks. His dream is to find the All Blue, a legendary sea where fish from every ocean in the world swim together.

Buggy the Clown, the rival

Buggy isn't a Straw Hat at all. He's a pirate captain with a painted clown face and a big red nose, and he's one of Luffy's first enemies. He ate the Chop-Chop Fruit, so his body can split apart into floating pieces, which means swords pass straight through him. He's vain, sneaky and hilarious, and he flies into a rage if anyone mentions his nose. Whatever you do, don't mention his nose.

Before you watch One Piece

The One Piece anime is brilliant, funny and full of heart, but it's made for teenagers, and some of its battles are quite intense. Ask a grown-up before you start watching, and maybe watch the first few episodes together. Fair warning: there are more than a thousand of them.

Real Pirates: the real "wanted" posters

Real governments offered bounties for pirates, too. In 1718, the governor of Virginia, in America, issued an official proclamation offering a reward of £100 for capturing Blackbeard, with smaller amounts for each member of his crew. That was a fortune in those days, many times what a sailor earned in a whole year. It worked: Blackbeard was caught that same year.
