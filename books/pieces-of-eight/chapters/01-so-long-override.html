Chapter 1
What. A. Season.

Picture it. Final circle. Three players left. One is invisible because somebody hit an Override Console and now everyone vanishes when they sprint. One has a Vampire Sprite bobbing over their shoulder, healing with every hit. And one has just smashed a Pumpkin Chest in the middle of the storm, purely out of habit.

Now picture that same moment happening a million times over. In a bedroom in Tokyo. On a sofa in São Paulo. On a school bus in Toronto. In a lounge room in Sydney, at the exact second someone yells "DINNER!" from the kitchen. For seventy-three days, Chapter 7, Season 4: Override was one enormous, jubilant, worldwide party, and everyone was invited.

It ends on 1 November. So before the pirates drop anchor, let's do what you do at the end of any great party: turn the music up and relive the best bits.

The greatest hits

The day the Sprites moved in

Tiny. Bouncy. Ridiculously cute. And secretly powerful. On day one, the Island filled up with Sprites, and players everywhere went collecting-crazy. The Vampire Sprite healed you as you hit. The X-Ray Sprite let you peek through walls. The Overshield Sprite wrapped you in extra armour. But a Sprite only became truly yours once you'd extracted it, so every match turned into a nail-biting dash to save your best find before someone sent you back to the lobby. Cue screaming. Cue celebrating. Cue "ONE MORE GAME!"

The Crown Sprite, for winners only. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

The rule-breakers' playground

Then there were the Override Consoles, the season's most audacious idea. Find one, press the button, and change the rules for the entire match: everyone faster, everyone invisible while sprinting, every shot a headshot. The catch? The whole lobby found out it was you. Which meant one brave (or foolish) player suddenly had ninety-nine new enemies. It was chaos. It was glorious.

A heart in the sky, and cake on the ground

In September, the moon turned into a giant heart, the first Exotic weapons arrived (an assault rifle that tied balloons to people, anyone?) and birthday cakes appeared all over the map, because Fortnite Battle Royale turned nine. Millions of players around the world spent the week eating cake for health. Delicious. Medically questionable.

Fog, pumpkins and pandemonium

On 1 October, the villain Geno jammed a Cursed Cartridge into his machine and the lights went out. Purple fog crept between the trees. Pumpkin Chests sprouted everywhere, each hiding a Sprite, and the Bonerattler SMG let you see enemies through walls. Fortnitemares was pure pandemonium: squads sprinting from pumpkin to pumpkin, yelling about which spooky Sprite they'd found, as twenty-four Trick or Treat versions turned the whole Island into one giant trick-or-treat street.

A Trick or Treat Sprite in costume. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

A champion makes history

In September, the world's best players met for the FNCS Global Championship, and after twelve matches the top three teams were separated by just four points. Swizzy and Pixie won by two, and Swizzy became the first player ever to win back-to-back world titles, with different teammates each time. That's tenacity.

The best squad moments

But the real magic of Override wasn't any one update. It was the camaraderie: the friend who revived you with one health point left, the squad that shared its last Chug Jug, the cousin who finally got their first Victory Royale and ran around the room. Those moments happened everywhere, every single day, and they're the reason any of this matters.

The story in sixty seconds

Geno: villain, hacker, traitor. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

Geno, whom everyone thought was one of the good guys, turned out to be a secret spy. He stole the power of the Zero Point (the energy at the heart of Fortnite's universe) and started rewriting reality like computer code. The heroes fought back with a "superhack" of their own, and in the final twist it downloaded something onto the Island: a giant treasure chest. Inside, a voice revealed, was a Sliver of the Zero Point. The key was hidden in another reality, so Jones and Hope set sail to find it. Which is exactly where Treasure Hunters begins.

One last dance

The season's final week, the Final Spritemare (27 to 31 October), was Override's swansong: double Sprite XP, double Sprite Dust, one last chance to fill your collection. Before the servers switch off on Sunday 1 November (Sydney time), finish your quests, check your season stats at fortnite.com/recap with a grown-up, and take one last look around the map. You'll feel nostalgic. That's allowed.
