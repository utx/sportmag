Chapter 1
Game over? Not quite.

Seventy-three days. That's how long Chapter 7, Season 4: Override lasted, from 20 August to 1 November 2026. In that time, a villain hacked reality, an army of tiny creatures moved onto the Island, the moon turned into a heart, the fog rolled in for Halloween, and a world champion made history. Then, in the final fortnight, a treasure chest the size of a house started singing on a beach.

Before the pirates drop anchor, let's give Override a proper farewell. Grab a Chug Jug. This is the season's greatest hits.

The story in sixty seconds

Geno: villain, hacker, traitor. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

The season began with a shock. Geno, who everyone thought was on the good guys' side, turned out to be a mole: a secret spy working for himself all along. During a mission to stabilise the Zero Point (the glowing energy at the heart of Fortnite's whole universe), he hacked the system and grabbed its power. He built an enormous machine in the middle of the Island and started rewriting the rules of reality like lines of computer code.

So the heroes fought back with code of their own. Wrixel and Slone built a supercomputer out of weapons (as you do) to craft a "superhack". Hope hid a secret in her artwork that cut Geno off from his own system. And Jones went hunting for six magical crystals, until an ancient tapestry revealed something astonishing: a tiny Sliver of the Zero Point was out there somewhere, calling to him across different realities.

The season's climax was a heist. The heroes ran their superhack, and instead of a weapon or a door, it downloaded something onto the Island: a giant treasure chest. A voice revealed that the Sliver was locked inside. The key? Hidden in another reality. And so Jones and Hope set off to find it, which is exactly where Treasure Hunters begins.

The best bits, one by one

The Sprites moved in

If Override is remembered for one thing, it'll be the Sprites: small, cheerful creatures you could find, collect and carry into battle. Each had its own power. The Vampire Sprite healed you as you dealt damage; the X-Ray Sprite let you peek through walls; the Overshield Sprite gave you an extra layer of protection. A Sprite wasn't truly yours until you extracted it, so every match became a nervous dash to save your best finds before someone knocked you out. By the end of the season there were dozens of them, including spooky Trick or Treat versions, golden ones and a Crown Sprite for the very best players.

The Crown Sprite, earned by winning. A true mark of a champion. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

Rules? What rules?

The season's big idea was right there in its name. Seven Override Consoles were scattered around the Island, and activating one changed the rules for the whole match: everyone faster, everyone invisible while sprinting, or every shot a headshot. The catch? The whole lobby found out who'd pressed the button. It was the most audacious idea Fortnite had tried in ages, and it made every match feel a little bit different. On top of that came Loot Hacks (spend Sprite Dust to put your favourite weapons in your own chests) and Cheat Codes (official ones, we promise: you tapped in a sequence of arrows to unlock a reward).

A heart in the sky

In September, a guest crossover filled the sky with a heart-shaped moon and brought in the first Exotics: ordinary weapons with a superpower bolted on, like an assault rifle that tied balloons to your enemies. The same update put birthday cakes all over the map, because Fortnite Battle Royale turned nine.

The fog rolls in

On 1 October, Geno slotted a Cursed Cartridge into his machine, and the Island went dark for Fortnitemares. Purple fog drifted between the trees. Nightmare Neighborhood and Freaky Fields appeared. Pumpkin Chests popped up everywhere, each hiding a Sprite, and the Bonerattler SMG arrived to become the weapon of the season. (Our sister book The Sprite Whisperer is all about hunting those pumpkins.)

A Trick or Treat Sprite, dressed up for Fortnitemares. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

A champion makes history

At the end of September, the best players in the world met in person for the FNCS Global Championship. Swizzy and Pixie won it by just two points, and Swizzy became the first player ever to win back-to-back FNCS world titles, with different teammates each time. That's what you call tenacity.

The goodbyes

Not everyone made it to the end of the voyage. Rocket Racing, Fortnite's speedy car game, was switched off on 1 October. In the last big update, Epic vaulted the Witch Broom and the golden Midas' Masterpiece. And the last week of the season became the Final Spritemare (27 to 31 October), with double Sprite XP and double Sprite Dust every day: one final chance to fill your collection, and Override's swansong.

Before the anchor drops: a last-chance checklist

The season ends on Saturday 31 October in America, which is Sunday 1 November here in Sydney. Before the servers go quiet, here's what to do:

  • Finish your quests. Any XP you earn now still counts towards the Override Battle Pass until the very end.
  • Look at your Season Recap. Epic has made a page at fortnite.com/recap showing your stats for the season: wins, eliminations, Sprites extracted, and what kind of player you are. You'll need to sign in, so ask a grown-up.
  • Take a last screenshot. The Override map is about to disappear forever. Our book Tier Pressure has the full guide to it, if you want to remember every corner.
  • Say goodbye to your Sprites. Many players expect Sprite Dust and gizmos to reset when Override ends Rumour. Nobody knows for sure whether Sprites will stay for the new season.

Real Pirates: farewell, Fortnite style

Sailors had a tradition for leaving port called "weighing anchor": hauling the heavy anchor up from the seabed so the ship could move. It's where we get the expression "anchors aweigh". So, as the old crews would say: anchors aweigh, Override. It's been a pleasure.

Feeling nostalgic already? Good. Now dry your eyes, because there's a ship on the horizon, and it's flying a flag with a skull wearing a straw hat.
