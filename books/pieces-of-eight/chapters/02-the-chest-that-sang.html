Chapter 2
Something inside wants to be found

Epic Games almost never just tells players what's coming next. It teases. It drops clues into the game, posts cryptic pictures online and lets millions of fans work it out together. The build-up to Treasure Hunters was one of the cleverest teases in years, and it all started with a box.

A chest appears

In mid-October, a gigantic treasure chest appeared on a beach on the southern side of the Island, east of Sunken Shores and south of Mega Maze. It was wrapped in golden chains, studded with gems and guarded by a grinning skull lock. And it wasn't finished. Like a game installing on a console, it was downloading, and a health bar above it slowly crept upwards.

Each time the bar jumped, players rushed to the beach to see what had changed. Sometimes the health bar glitched. Sometimes a mysterious voice spoke. And, strangest of all, the chest sang. Swim close and you could hear music drifting out of it, a different song each time, as if several bands were taking turns inside. It was the perfect way to herald a new season, and the anticipation was enormous.

Six flags, six songs

Then the clues got sharper. A picture in the game's own news feed showed six pirate flags, carved side by side into a wooden board Spotted. Dataminers worked out that each flag belonged to a different crew of treasure hunters, and the songs from the chest seemed to match them, one crew at a time.

The six crew flags from Epic's teaser. Can you spot the one with a straw hat? Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

Fans spotted that one flag looked almost exactly like the Jolly Roger of the Straw Hat Pirates from One Piece, only with the straw hat taken off. Others were linked to two virtual singers, Hatsune Miku and Kasane Teto, whose company posted its own teaser on 23 October, and to rappers A$AP Rocky and Lil Uzi Vert, who posted tantalising previews on their own social media. The night the chest opened

On Saturday 24 October at 2pm in New York (5am on Sunday in Sydney, so most Australian players slept straight through it), the bar hit 100% and the chest cracked open. A cinematic played in the game. Out of a moonlit fog bank sailed a small wooden ship with a sheep's head on the front, and on the screen appeared two logos side by side: Fortnite and One Piece Confirmed.

The Going Merry, the Straw Hat Pirates' first ship, from Epic's trailer. Notice anything odd? It's hanging from a balloon. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer).

Fans of One Piece recognised it instantly: it was the Going Merry, the first ship of Luffy and his crew. The same day, Epic posted the season's name, Treasure Hunters, with the words "The greatest adventure of all!" and the date "11.1": 1 November. The next morning, Fortnite's official account posted nothing but three straw hat emojis. Leaker HYPEX reported that the One Piece teaser had already become one of Fortnite's twenty most-liked posts ever, with more than 115,000 likes in three hours.

Wanted, all over the world

The chest wasn't the only clue. Real-life WANTED posters began appearing on walls in big cities, in the style of One Piece's famous bounty posters. A fan in London photographed a set showing Luffy, his brother Ace, and two Fortnite heroes, Jonesy and Hope Spotted. Posters reported in Japan showed Buggy the Clown, Oscar and Cuddle Team Leader Leak.

Posters spotted on a London wall: Ace, Jonesy, Hope and Luffy. Photo shared by a fan on Reddit, as shown in the video.

"Let the hunt begin"

Finally, on 27 October, Epic unveiled the full trailer. It was just 28 seconds long, and it was packed. Luffy stretched his arm right across the sand. Zoro drew his swords. Cannons fired from the decks of pirate ships. A blond pirate in round glasses and a long coat fired a brand-new weapon. And a narrator's voice growled four words: "Let the hunt begin."

The trailer in six frames. Pictures: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer).

Look closely at the title card: the name is written twice, once in English and once in Japanese katakana, the letters Japan uses for words borrowed from other languages: Torejā Hantāzu. That's a nod to One Piece, which comes from Japan.

Real Pirates: the original teaser

Real pirates were masters of the tease, too. When a pirate ship spotted a target, it often flew a friendly flag to get close, then swapped it for the black flag at the last moment. The whole point of the black flag was to terrify the other crew into surrendering before anyone fired a shot. A good tease, it turns out, has always been the best weapon.
