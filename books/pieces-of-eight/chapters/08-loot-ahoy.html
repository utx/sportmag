The Straw Hat Mythics

Mythic weapons are the rarest in Fortnite, usually carried by bosses or hidden somewhere special. This season, it looks as if several of them will be borrowed from the Straw Hats themselves Spotted.

Luffy's Gum-Gum punch

The trailer shows Luffy flinging a fist across a whole clearing. In the game, it seems you'll be able to use his power yourself: a stretchy, long-range punch. Some fans had hoped for Luffy's most powerful form from the latest part of the story, but this looks like the classic rubber punch.

Zoro's three swords

Three swords, one in his teeth. Don't try this at home. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer, as shown in the video).

Zoro's Mythic looks set to let you fight the way he does, with a sword in each hand and one gripped in your teeth. Fortnite has had swords before, and they've usually been brilliant for dashing into fights. Three of them at once? Our Hype Meter just ran out of jugs.

Usopp's slingshot

Usopp and his trusty slingshot. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer, as shown in the video).

Usopp is the crew's sniper, and his Mythic appears to be his giant green slingshot, flinging projectiles from long range. Fortnite had a slingshot during the Simpsons mini-season last year, so this could be a souped-up version.

The leaked ones

Leaks mention two more powers that haven't appeared in the trailer: Ace's Flame-Flame fire power, and Nami's Tangerine, a Mythic healing item Leak. A tangerine that heals you? That's the most Nami thing imaginable.

The great debate: are Mythics still exciting?

Not everyone's convinced. The creator of the video we watched for this book wasn't sure: "the Mythics… they just don't hit anymore," he said, meaning they don't feel as special as they used to. What do you think? Should Mythics be formidable, game-winning monsters, or should they be fun but fair? Argue it out with your squad.

The pirate arsenal

The blunderbuss-style shotgun

Jonesy's new weapon, with its flared barrel. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer).

Jonesy's new weapon has a short, wide barrel that flares out at the end like a trumpet Spotted. Leakers think it's the season's new shotgun. In real life, that shape belonged to the blunderbuss, a favourite weapon of sailors in the 1600s and 1700s. It sprayed a handful of small lead balls over a short distance, which was perfect for the cramped, crowded decks of a ship.

Real Pirates: the seven-barrelled gun

In 1779, a British engineer called James Wilson invented a gun with seven barrels that all fired at once. A gunmaker named Henry Nock built it, and Britain's Royal Navy used it from 1782. The idea was for sailors to climb up into the rigging and fire it down onto an enemy's deck during a battle. The trouble was the recoil: seven shots at once kicked so hard that it could bruise or even injure the shooter's shoulder. Its flash could also set fire to the ship's own sails. Not the Navy's finest idea.

Real Pirates: what pirates actually fought with

Real pirates had a very particular arsenal, chosen for fighting on a crowded, rolling deck:

  • The cutlass: a short, heavy, slightly curved sword. A long sword was useless on a cramped deck tangled with ropes, but a cutlass could be swung anywhere. It also made a handy tool for chopping through rope.
  • Flintlock pistols: each one fired only a single shot before it needed a slow reload, so pirates carried several at once. Look at the old picture of Blackbeard in Chapter 4: he's wearing a whole strap of them across his chest.
  • Cannons: big ships carried rows of them along each side. Firing all the cannons on one side together was called a broadside. But pirates usually avoided sinking ships with cannon fire. A sunken ship can't be robbed.

A short, curved sword with one sharp edge, used by sailors and pirates.
Broadsidenoun
Firing all the guns on one side of a ship at once. It also means a fierce verbal attack: "The coach fired a broadside at the referee."
Loose cannonnoun
Someone unpredictable who might cause damage. It comes from a real danger at sea: a heavy cannon that broke free of its ropes and rolled wildly around the deck in a storm.

So if your squad has one player who always charges in alone, pushes too early and accidentally blows everyone up, now you know exactly what to call them: a loose cannon.
