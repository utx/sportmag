The Straw Hat Mythics

Mythics are Fortnite's rarest weapons, and this season several seem to be borrowed straight from the Straw Hats Spotted.

Luffy's Gum-Gum punch should let you fling a stretchy fist right across a clearing. (Some fans hoped for Luffy's mightiest form from the latest part of the story. This looks like the classic rubber punch, and that's fine by us.)

Three swords, one in his teeth. Don't try this at home. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer).

Zoro's three swords look set to let you fight his way: a blade in each hand and one in your teeth. Fortnite's swords have always been brilliant for dashing into fights. Three at once? Our Hype Meter ran out of jugs.

Usopp's slingshot should turn you into a long-range sniper; think a souped-up version of Bart's slingshot from last year's Simpsons season. And leaks mention two more: Ace's Flame-Flame fire power, and Nami's Tangerine, a Mythic healing item Leak. A tangerine that heals you is the most Nami thing imaginable.

The great debate: are Mythics still exciting?

Some players grumble that Mythics don't feel as special as they used to. Should Mythics be formidable, game-winning monsters, or fun but fair? Argue it out with your squad.

The pirate arsenal

Jonesy's new weapon, with its flared barrel. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer).

Jonesy's new weapon has a short barrel that flares out at the end like a trumpet Spotted, and leakers think it's the season's new shotgun. In real life, that shape belonged to the blunderbuss, a sailor's favourite in the 1600s and 1700s. It sprayed a fistful of small lead balls at close range, which was perfect for the crowded deck of a ship.

Cannons are back too Spotted. Will they be any good? Fortnite's old pirate cannons were more fun than useful, so fingers crossed. There's also a chunky, painted rocket launcher that looks new Spotted, and a leaked icon of a flintlock gun with a whole bundle of barrels, possibly borrowed from Fortnite's old "Save the World" mode Leak.

The leaked multi-barrel gun. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (via leaks).

Real Pirates: the seven-barrelled gun

That bundle of barrels looks a lot like a real invention: the Nock gun. In 1779, a British engineer designed a gun with seven barrels that all fired at once, and Britain's Royal Navy started using it a few years later. Sailors were meant to climb into the rigging and fire it down onto an enemy deck. The trouble was the recoil: seven shots at once kicked so hard it could injure the shooter's shoulder, and its flash could set fire to the ship's own sails. Not the Navy's finest idea.

What real pirates fought with

Real pirates chose their arsenal for fighting on a crowded, rolling deck. The cutlass was a short, curved sword that could swing anywhere without tangling in the ropes. Flintlock pistols fired just one shot before a slow reload, so pirates carried several: look at Blackbeard in Chapter 4, with a whole strap of them across his chest. And big ships carried rows of cannons; firing every cannon on one side at once was a broadside. But pirates usually tried not to sink their targets. A sunken ship can't be robbed.

One last cannon fact. In a storm, a heavy cannon could snap its ropes and roll wildly across the deck, smashing everything in its path. Sailors called it a loose cannon, and that's what we still call someone unpredictable. So if your squad has that one player who charges in alone, pushes too early and accidentally blows everyone up, now you know their proper title.

Word Watch

Formidableadjective
So powerful or impressive that it makes people nervous. A formidable opponent is one you'd rather not face.
Blunderbussnoun
An old gun with a short, flared barrel that fired lots of small shot at close range. From Dutch donderbus, "thunder pipe".
Recoilnoun and verb
The backwards kick of a gun when it fires. To recoil also means to jump back in fright or disgust.
Arsenalnoun
A collection of weapons, or a building where they're stored. (Yes, the football club was named after a weapons factory.)
Broadsidenoun
Firing all the guns on one side of a ship at once. Also a fierce verbal attack: "The coach fired a broadside at the referee."
Loose cannonnoun
Someone unpredictable who might cause damage.
