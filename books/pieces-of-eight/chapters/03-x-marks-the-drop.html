It's a crowded, colourful island, broken into chunks by winding rivers and turquoise lagoons. The north is a sandy orange desert, with a long spit of sand stretching out to the east like a crooked finger. The middle is green jungle, with a striking ring of pink and purple blossom trees and, beside it, a huge, dark, rocky crater. The south breaks up into a scatter of little islets linked by bridges: an archipelago in miniature. And all around the edges are coves, docks and beaches, perfect for parking a pirate ship.

Some players have compared it with the current map and think it's quite a bit smaller Rumour. If that's true, the new season will be faster and more frantic, with less running and a lot more fighting.

Places you might recognise

If you've watched One Piece, you'll spot some famous names. Players who studied the new mini-map in leaked images found several locations from the anime Leak. None of the names are official yet, so treat them as working titles.

Loguetown

Loguetown, as it appears on the leaked mini-map. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (via leaks shown in the video).

In One Piece, Loguetown is the "town of the beginning and the end": the place where the legendary Pirate King's story ended, and where Luffy stops before sailing into the most dangerous sea in the world. On the new island it looks like a busy town of grey streets and tall blue buildings, right next to the water, just south of the centre. Towns mean lots of rooftops, lots of doors and lots of loot.

Arlong Park

Arlong Park: red roofs, blue pools, surrounded by sea. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (via leaks shown in the video).

In the anime, Arlong Park is the fortress of Arlong, a ferocious fish-man pirate with a nose like a saw, and the scene of one of the Straw Hats' most famous battles. On the map it sits on its own island off the west coast, a big red-roofed complex full of pools. Watery, walled in and tricky to escape from: expect a fierce fight to control it.

The Baratie

The Baratie, a floating restaurant, in Epic's trailer. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer).

The Baratie is a floating restaurant shaped like a giant fish. In One Piece, it's where the crew meets Sanji, the cook who fights only with his feet. The trailer showed it bobbing on the waves, decorated with bunting and little One Piece flags Spotted. We don't know where it sits on the map yet. A restaurant in the sea is either the safest place to land, or the most exposed one. We'll find out.

Early reports also mention Foosha Village (Luffy's home town) and somewhere called Marigold Leak, but nobody has pinned them to the map yet.

Real Pirates: how sailors found their way

There was no mini-map in 1700. Sailors navigated with a magnetic compass, the position of the Sun and stars, and a lot of careful arithmetic. To measure their speed, they threw a wooden board called a "log" over the back of the ship, tied to a long rope with a knot every few metres. They let the rope run out for a set time, measured with a small sand-glass, then counted how many knots had slipped through their fingers. That's why a ship's speed is still measured in knots today, and why a ship's diary is still called a logbook.

Word Watch

Navigateverb
To plan and steer a route from one place to another, especially across the sea. The person who does it is the navigator.
Knotnoun
A tie in a rope, and also a unit of speed at sea: one nautical mile per hour, a little under 2 kilometres per hour.
Cartographernoun
A person who draws maps. (From old words meaning "chart" and "drawer".)

All aboard the Going Merry

Here's the strangest sight in the whole trailer. Instead of the Battle Bus, players will apparently ride into each match on the Going Merry, the Straw Hats' little ship, which is hanging from a giant black balloon with a skull on it Spotted. If it works like the bus, you'll jump from its deck and glide down to the island.

The Going Merry, now with added balloon. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer).

It's not the first time the bus has been swapped out for something sillier. When Fortnite has a big crossover, the bus often gets a costume of its own. But a flying sailing ship might be our favourite yet. And there's a lovely bit of real nautical language hiding in it: when you jump off the side of the Merry, you'll be leaping overboard either from its port side or its starboard side.

Know your ship
Sailors never say "left" and "right". Here's why.
Left and right change depending on which way you're facing. Port and starboard never do, which matters a lot when you're shouting orders in a storm. Our own drawing.

Word Watch

Starboardnoun
The right-hand side of a ship when you face the front. It comes from "steer-board": old ships were steered with a big oar on the right-hand side.
Portnoun
The left-hand side of a ship when you face the front. Ships tied up to the dock on the left, away from the steering oar, so that side faced the port. (Memory trick: "port" and "left" both have four letters.)
Overboardadverb
Over the side of a ship and into the water. "Going overboard" also means doing far too much of something.

Where should you land?

Nobody has played on this island yet, so this is pure conjecture. But based on the trailer map, here's our early advice:

  • For your first few matches: try the little islands in the south. They're small, quiet and surrounded by water, so you can loot in peace and work out the new weapons.
  • If you want a fight: Loguetown. Big town, central spot, lots of players.
  • Be careful at the chest. If the treasure chest really is the heart of the season, half the lobby will be heading straight for it on day one. Fortune favours the bold, but it also favours whoever lands first.

We'll know far more once the season is live. Until then, keep your spyglass on the trailer.
