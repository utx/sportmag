It's a crowded, colourful island, chopped into chunks by winding rivers and turquoise lagoons. The north is sandy orange desert, with a long spit of sand poking out to the east like a crooked finger. The middle is jungle, with a ring of pink blossom trees and, right next to it, a huge, dark crater. The south shatters into a scatter of little islands joined by bridges, an archipelago in miniature. And all around the edges: coves, docks and beaches, perfect for parking a pirate ship.

Places you might recognise

Players studying leaked mini-map images found some famous One Piece names Leak.

Loguetown

Loguetown on the leaked mini-map. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (via leaks).

In One Piece, Loguetown is the "town of the beginning and the end", where the legendary Pirate King's story finished and Luffy's adventure into the world's most dangerous sea begins. In Fortnite, it's a busy waterside town just south of the centre. Rooftops, doors, loot: expect a crowd.

Arlong Park

Arlong Park: red roofs, blue pools. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (via leaks).

Arlong Park is the fortress of Arlong, a ferocious fish-man pirate with a nose like a saw, and the scene of one of the Straw Hats' most famous battles. Here it sits on its own island off the west coast: walled in, full of water and tricky to escape.

The Baratie

The Baratie, a floating restaurant, in Epic's trailer. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite (trailer).

The Baratie is a floating restaurant shaped like a giant fish, where the crew meets Sanji, the cook who fights only with his feet Spotted. A restaurant in the middle of the sea is either the safest place to land or the most exposed. We'll find out.

All aboard the Going Merry

Instead of the Battle Bus, players will apparently ride into each match on the Straw Hats' little ship, which is dangling from a giant black balloon with a skull on it Spotted. When you leap off, you'll be jumping from either its port side or its starboard side. Which is which?

Know your ship
Sailors never say "left" and "right". Here's why.
Left and right change depending on which way you're facing. Port and starboard never do, which matters when you're shouting orders in a storm. Memory trick: "port" and "left" both have four letters. Our own drawing.

Real Pirates: how fast is a knot?

Old sailors measured speed by throwing a wooden board over the back of the ship on a rope tied with a knot every few metres, then counting how many knots slipped through their fingers while a small sand-glass ran out. That's why ships still measure speed in knots, and why a ship's diary is still called a logbook: the board was called the "log".

Where should you land?

Nobody has played here yet, so this is pure conjecture, but here's our early advice. For your first few matches, try the quiet little islands in the south: loot in peace and learn the new weapons. If you want a fight, head for Loguetown. And be careful at the treasure chest: if it's the heart of the season, half the lobby will be heading straight for it on day one.

Word Watch

Cartographernoun
A person who draws maps. (From old words meaning "chart" and "writer".)
Archipelagonoun
A group or chain of islands close together. (Say it "ar-ki-PEL-a-go".) Indonesia is the biggest in the world.
Starboardnoun
The right-hand side of a ship when you face the front. It comes from "steer-board": old ships were steered by a big oar on the right. The left side is called "port", because it was the side that faced the dock.
Conjecturenoun
An opinion based on incomplete information; an educated guess.
