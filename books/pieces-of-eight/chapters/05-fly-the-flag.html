Choose your crew

Every pirate crew needs a flag. In Treasure Hunters there are six of them, carved side by side on Epic's teaser board. But first, a question: why did real pirates bother with flags at all?

Real Pirates: the flag that won fights

Here's the secret. Pirates didn't actually want to fight. A battle could sink the ship they were trying to steal, wreck the cargo and get pirates hurt. The perfect robbery was one where the other crew gave up without a single shot. And the best way to make that happen was intimidation.

So pirates flew flags designed to terrify. The message of a black flag was simple: surrender now and you'll be spared. Most merchant sailors, who weren't paid enough to die defending somebody else's sugar, took the hint. These flags became known as the Jolly Roger. Nobody is quite sure where the name came from. One theory says it's from joli rouge, French for "pretty red", after an earlier red flag; another says "Old Roger" was a nickname for the Devil.

Not every Jolly Roger was a plain skull and crossbones. Captains designed their own, and each picture was a symbol with a meaning. An hourglass meant "your time is running out". A skeleton stood for death. A sword or cutlass meant "we're ready to fight".

Three famous Jolly Rogers
Drawn for this book. Every symbol was a message.

Historians argue about who flew which flag; many of the designs were only linked to famous captains years later. But the idea was real, and so were the emblems.

A quick word about the two rappers: they're grown-up musicians, and their songs are written for grown-up audiences. They'll be in the game as characters, and that's about all we'll say about them in this book.

The other crews are a lot more interesting to us. The Straw Hats get the next chapter all to themselves. Hatsune Miku isn't a real person at all: she's a "virtual singer", a computer voice that anyone can use to make songs, with a cartoon character to go with it. She has been in Fortnite before, and this time she's in a pirate outfit. Kasane Teto is another virtual singer, with red hair in drill-shaped curls, appearing in Fortnite for the very first time. And the Jones and Hope crew are our own Fortnite heroes, who sailed off at the end of Override to find the key to the treasure chest. (One dataminer listed Peely in the Fortnite crew too Leak. How will the crews work?

That's the big unanswered question. Epic hasn't explained it yet. In the video we watched for this book, the creator guessed that the crews "could be the factions of the people trying to hunt for the treasure", like teams competing across the whole season. Maybe you'll choose a crew to support and do its quests. Maybe each crew will have its own boss, or its own part of the map. We'll find out on 1 November.

The same creator had an early prediction about who'll win: "the One Piece crew probably mugs everyone here." ("Mugs" is slang for "beats easily".) Do you agree? Pick your side now. A crew needs allegiance.

Try this: design your own Jolly Roger

Real pirate captains designed their own flags, so why shouldn't you? Grab a piece of paper (black paper and a white pencil is the real deal) and draw a flag for your Fortnite squad. Choose two or three symbols, and decide what each one means:

  • An hourglass: "your time is running out"
  • A Chug Jug: "we never run out of shields"
  • A crown: "we're going for the Victory Royale"
  • A llama: "we always share our loot"
  • Your own idea: what does your crew stand for?

Then give your crew a name. The best pirate names are a little bit menacing and a little bit funny.

Word Watch

To raise something up, especially with ropes. Sailors hoist flags and sails.

Once your flag is finished, hoist it somewhere everyone can see. Your bedroom door counts as a ship, as long as you're sailing it.
