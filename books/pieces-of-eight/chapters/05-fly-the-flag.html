Choose your crew

Every pirate crew needs a flag. Treasure Hunters has six of them. But first: why did real pirates bother with flags at all?

Real Pirates: the flag that won fights

Here's the secret. Pirates didn't actually want to fight. A battle could sink the ship they were trying to steal, wreck the loot and get pirates hurt. The perfect robbery was one where the other crew gave up without a shot, and the best tool for that was intimidation.

So pirates flew flags designed to terrify, with one simple message: surrender now and you'll be spared. Most sailors, who weren't paid nearly enough to die defending someone else's sugar, took the hint. These flags became known as the Jolly Roger, although nobody knows exactly why. Captains designed their own, and every symbol meant something: an hourglass meant "your time is running out", a skeleton meant death, a cutlass meant "we're ready to fight".

Hatsune Miku and Kasane Teto aren't real people at all: they're "virtual singers", computer voices that musicians all over the world use to make songs, each with a cartoon face. How will the crews work?

Epic hasn't said. Lachlan's guess is that they "could be the factions of the people trying to hunt for the treasure": teams competing across the whole season, maybe each with its own quests, or its own patch of the map. Who'll win? Lachlan's prediction: "the One Piece crew probably mugs everyone here." ("Mugs" is slang for "beats easily".) Each crew is a faction, and every faction needs fans. So where does your allegiance lie?

Try this: design your own Jolly Roger

Grab some paper (black paper and a white pencil is the real deal) and design a flag for your squad. Choose two or three symbols and decide what each means: an hourglass ("your time is up"), a Chug Jug ("we never run out of shields"), a crown ("Victory Royale or bust"), a llama ("we always share loot"), or something you invent. Then give your crew a name: a little bit menacing, a little bit silly. Hang it on your bedroom door. That's your ship now.
