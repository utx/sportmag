Chapter 1 · WonkeeLand
Thrills, spills and grind rails

If you want action the second your boots touch the ground, there is nowhere on the Island quite like WonkeeLand. It's a theme park built around Wonkee, a grinning mascot who appears on just about every wall, sign and sweet wrapper. There's a roller-coaster, a Ferris wheel, a water slide and a lazy river. There's an arcade, a stage, a shooting range called Zip Zonk Bang and food stalls with names like Corndawg's Sauce Express and Wonkee's Chomp House.

WonkeeLand is also a survivor. It first opened on the old Island in November 2025, and when Epic rebuilt the whole map in June 2026, WonkeeLand was one of the few places that came along for the ride, moving to the far north-east corner of the new Island.

A theme park with a dark side

Then, on 1 October, Fortnitemares arrived and WonkeeLand turned haunted. The water now glows a sickly, luminous green. Fog drifts between the rides under a purple sky, and the Ferris wheel stands against the sky like a giant skeleton. On a sunny day this would be the happiest place on the map. Right now, it looks ominous, as if something is waiting for you behind the candyfloss stand.

Don't worry: the scariest thing in WonkeeLand is usually the other players.

Park rules (probably)

We couldn't find WonkeeLand's official rules, so we've written some for them.

  1. Please keep arms, legs and shotguns inside the ride at all times.
  2. Visitors who build a ramp over the queue will be asked to wait at the back of the queue. Then eliminated.
  3. The lazy river is not a hiding place. Everyone can see you. You are wearing a bright orange outfit.
  4. Do not feed Wonkee.
  5. Management accepts no responsibility for Overrides, storms, or the fact that the water is now green.

Why land here?

Because it's loaded. WonkeeLand has 34 chests, 31 ammo boxes, three vending machines and a Reboot Van for bringing squadmates back. There are usually cars parked around the edge, too, for a quick getaway.

One guide calls WonkeeLand "the chaos drop of Chapter 7 Season 4", and that's the attraction. Players who land here aren't looking for a quiet start. They want to come out of the first two minutes exhilarated, with a full inventory and a handful of eliminations. If that sounds like you, you have an audacious streak, and WonkeeLand is your kind of place.

Secret 1: surf the roller-coaster

Here's the trick that turns WonkeeLand from a theme park into a playground. The roller-coaster's track isn't just scenery. Epic's own description of the ride says you can use it to "rail grind around the park". Jump onto the rail and you slide along it at speed, swooping over the paths and stalls below.

That makes the coaster two things at once. It's a lookout: from up on the track you can see who has landed where. And it's an escape route: if a fight goes wrong, hop on the rail and you're gone before your opponent has finished reloading.

There is a catch, though, and we'll be honest about it. Up on that bright track, silhouetted against the sky, you're conspicuous. Everyone in the park can see you. A moving target is harder to hit, but it's not impossible, and while you're grinding you're also vulnerable, because you can't hide behind anything. Use the rails to travel, not to show off.

Off the Island: why roller-coasters feel amazing

Here's a surprise: most roller-coasters don't have an engine pushing them around the track. A chain or a cable drags the cars slowly up the first big hill (that's the clack-clack-clack you hear), and after that, the whole ride runs on gravity.

At the top of that first hill, the cars are packed with stored-up energy, just from being high up. As they plunge down, that height turns into speed. At the bottom, the speed carries them up the next hill, where they slow down, and the swap happens again: height into speed, speed into height. It's why the first hill is usually the tallest. A little energy is lost on every hill to friction (the wheels rubbing on the track) and to air pushing against the cars, so each hill has to be a bit lower than the one before.

And the feeling at the bottom? Your body releases a chemical called adrenaline, which speeds up your heart and sharpens your senses. Your brain thinks you might be in danger, and then works out that you're not. That mix of "help!" and "hooray!" is exactly what being exhilarated feels like.

Word origins: Ferris wheel

A Ferris wheel is named after a real person: an American engineer called George Ferris. He built the first one for the World's Fair in Chicago in 1893, and it was meant to outdo the Eiffel Tower, which had been the star of the fair in Paris four years earlier. His wheel stood about 80 metres tall. Sydney has its own famous fun park too: Luna Park, which opened in 1935 and is still welcoming visitors through its giant grinning face. Wonkee would approve.

Secret 2: the Override Console

A short run south-west of WonkeeLand, on the road towards Reality's Reign, sits an Override Console: a big machine that lets one player rewrite the rules of the whole match. Some Overrides make everyone faster; some do something completely ridiculous.

Consoles don't work all the time, though. According to the Fortnite Wiki, at the start of each of the first storm phases the game may switch on one console somewhere inside the circle. It takes about 25 seconds to power up, and while it does, it shows up on every player's screen. Once it's live, a player can use it to choose an Override, and their name appears on the console's screen for everyone to see.

So if that console starts glowing, a fight there is almost inevitable. Half the lobby will be heading your way. Decide quickly: are you racing to claim it, or slipping away while everyone else argues over it?

An Override Console. Whoever uses it picks the rules, and everyone else has to live with them.

Secret 3: Cheat Codes

WonkeeLand is one of the places where Cheat Codes turn up. Don't panic: these aren't actual cheats. They're glowing panels, an official part of the game this season, each showing a short sequence of arrows. Enter the arrows in exactly the right order and you're rewarded with a Sprite. Look on the sides of buildings and rides as you loot.

Story: ten seconds at WonkeeLand

You open your glider late, much later than feels sensible, and the theme park rushes up at you like a pop-up book. Green water. Purple fog. The Ferris wheel looming like the ribs of some enormous animal. Below, four other gliders are drifting lazily towards the arcade.

Not you. You're aiming for the coaster.

Your boots hit the track with a clang. There, ten steps away, glowing on the rail: a shotgun. You scoop it up just as the first of the other players lands in the plaza below, looks up, and realises, too late, that you were here first.

You leap onto the rail. The world becomes a blur of wind and noise. Stalls whip past beneath you: Corndawg's Sauce Express, Wonkee's Chomp House, a candyfloss stand. Someone fires at you and misses. Someone else fires and misses by more. The rail swoops down, curls round, and drops you, gasping, at the far side of the park, next to an unopened chest and a parked car.

You open the chest. You get in the car. You drive away laughing.

Total time since landing: ten seconds. Heart rate: extremely high. Feeling: exhilarated.

That story is written in the second person, using "you", so that the reader becomes the main character. It's also written in the present tense ("You open your glider" rather than "You opened your glider"), which makes the action feel as if it's happening right now. Writers choose both on purpose when they want a scene to feel fast and close. Try it in your own writing: it's like strapping your reader into the front seat.

Pro Tip: In a hot drop, the player who lands first usually wins the first fight. Open your glider late and dive straight down at the roller-coaster. Landing on a high track also means you can see the next weapon spawn before anyone on the ground can.

How busy is it, really?

Guides don't agree. One calls WonkeeLand "the chaos drop"; another describes it as not especially popular. Both can be true: it depends on where the Battle Bus flies. If the bus passes right over the north-east, expect a crowd. If it flies along the south, you might have the whole park to yourself.
