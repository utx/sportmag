Answers

Round 1

1 diminished · 2 conceal · 3 conspicuous · 4 ominous · 5 tranquil · 6 scrutinised · 7 apprehensive · 8 resilient · 9 inevitable · 10 exhilarated

Not used: indifferent and luminous. The trap was gap 10: an indifferent winner wouldn't leap to their feet. And gap 3 needs a word about being noticed, not about glowing, so luminous doesn't fit.

Round 2

11 B flourish · 12 A intricate · 13 C conceal · 14 A gullible · 15 B ascertain · 16 B deteriorate · 17 A monotonous

Gap 14 is the clever one: someone who believes a trick works "in every maze" without checking is too easily convinced, which is what gullible means.

Round 3

18 B · 19 A · 20 B · 21 B · 22 A

The poem is full of things ending and going quiet (the bus has gone, the gliders drift away, the lighthouse is dark), which makes it melancholy. Line 3 describes emptiness and stillness, the heart of a desolate scene. Passage A is funny because the ducks ignore all that fuss; Passage B is silent and serious, which is sombre.

Round 4

23 A · 24 B · 25 A · 26 B · 27 C

Marco ignores good advice three times: obstinate. Ana is cheerful and determined after defeat: resilient, not obstinate, because she isn't refusing advice, she's bouncing back.

Round 5

28 B · 29 B · 30 B · 31 B · 32 A · 33 B

28: Jack's claim is "always". One example of the race not giving an Exotic is enough to break it. 29: one key isn't enough, so the vault stays shut until the second pedestal is filled. 30: "hardly anyone" is not the same as "nobody", so Zoe is assuming this match will be empty. 31: the guides agree about the movement and disagree about the appearance, so the appearance is ambiguous. 32: only option A gives evidence about beginners and survival. 33: three places across ten matches is 30 chances; one in five of 30 is 6.

Round 6

34 B · 35 B · 36 A · 37 B · 38 B · 39 C · 40 B · 41 D

41: ominous, sombre and melancholy all describe dark or sad moods. Exhilarated is thrilled and joyful.
