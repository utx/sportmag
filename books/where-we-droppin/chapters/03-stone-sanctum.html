Chapter 3 · Stone Sanctum
Key moments

South of the Island's centre, half-swallowed by jungle, stands an ancient temple of grey stone. Water lies still in a ring around its heart. Even with a match raging around it, Stone Sanctum has a sombre feel, like a museum after closing time.

But this temple is not as solid as it looks. Some of its walls and platforms appear and disappear depending on how close you are to them. A staircase that was there a moment ago can flicker out of existence; a wall can shimmer into place where there was a gap. Stone Sanctum arrived on 20 August, when this season began, and it's been one of the most talked-about places on the map ever since.

Word origins: sanctum

Sanctum comes from the Latin word sanctus, meaning "holy". A sanctum is a sacred, private place, and an "inner sanctum" is the most secret room of all, the one only a chosen few may enter. The same Latin root gives us sanctuary (a safe place), and even saint. So Stone Sanctum's name is a clue: it's telling you there's an inner room. You just have to find the way in.

Why land here?

The loot above ground is substantial on its own: 32 chests, 34 ammo boxes and four vending machines. But the real treasure is underneath your feet. Stone Sanctum conceals a secret chamber called the Temple Vault, and opening it is one of the best puzzles in the game.

The secret: opening the Temple Vault

You need two Temple Vault Keys. Here's the method, step by step.

1. Find the keys. The keys are hidden inside small breakable containers, and the containers are invisible until you get close. Two guides agree that you should head for the towers on the east side of the temple and check the small stone plinth in the bottom room of each one: as you approach, a container shimmers into view. Smash it, and the key is yours.

2. Carry them one at a time. You can only hold one key at once, so this takes two trips. Take the first key to the middle of the temple, where you'll find two round pedestals beside the water, one on each side. Put the key in, then go back for the second.

3. Watch the water vanish. When both pedestals have a key, the water around the centre drains away, revealing the entrance to the vault. Drop down inside.

4. Raid the vault. Inside are three Rare Chests, plus Slurp Barrels to top up your health and shield, and even some Cheat Codes. Since 17 September, those Rare Chests have had a chance of containing one of the four Exotic weapons from the Duck Race.

Where the guides disagree

Here's a candid confession: the guides don't all agree on where the keys are. Nintendo Life and Beebom point to the plinths in the east towers. Other guides say the keys are on small stone pillars up on the rooftops. And the Fortnite Wiki says there are four containers that can hold a key, not two. Our guess is that they're all partly right, and that the keys can turn up in more than one place. Start in the east towers; if they're empty, try the roofs.

The detective's method

Opening the vault rewards a meticulous player: someone who searches every corner carefully instead of charging about. Here are two tricks for finding invisible containers.

  • Listen. According to the Fortnite Wiki, the key containers make a sound. Switch on Visualise Sound Effects in the settings and an icon will point towards them, even through walls.
  • Look for the pulse. The containers give off a glowing pulse, a bit like a shockwave, and it keeps going even after the container is smashed. If you see one pulsing with nothing inside, someone has beaten you to it.

When you reach a plinth, stop and scrutinise the room before you swing your pickaxe. An enemy who knows the vault trick might be waiting for you to do the hard work.

Off the Island: "wonderful things"

In November 1922, in the Valley of the Kings in Egypt, an English archaeologist named Howard Carter made a small hole in a sealed doorway and held up a candle. Behind him, his sponsor, Lord Carnarvon, asked whether he could see anything. Carter is said to have replied: "Yes, wonderful things."

He was looking into the tomb of Tutankhamun, a young pharaoh who had died more than three thousand years earlier. Inside were golden shrines, chariots, jewellery and, eventually, the famous golden mask: more than five thousand objects in all. Carter and his team spent about ten years recording every single one. Now that is meticulous.

Did the tomb have booby traps, like temples in films? No. Real Egyptian tombs relied on hidden entrances, sealed doors and heavy stone blocks, not spinning blades or rolling boulders. They didn't work very well, either: robbers broke into most royal tombs in ancient times. Even Tutankhamun's tomb had been broken into soon after his burial, then tidied up and sealed again. What saved it was luck. Rubble from a later tomb, dug nearby, buried its entrance, and it was forgotten for over three thousand years.

So when you're standing in the Temple Vault, surrounded by Rare Chests, feel free to whisper it: wonderful things.

Off the Island: the invisible gorilla

In a famous experiment from 1999, two scientists showed people a short video of students passing basketballs, and asked them to count the passes made by the team in white. Partway through, a person in a full gorilla suit strolled into the middle of the game, thumped their chest, and walked off.

About half the viewers never noticed the gorilla at all.

They weren't silly. They were concentrating so hard on one job that their brains simply filtered out everything else. It happens to all of us, and it happens in Fortnite constantly: you're so focused on smashing a key container that you completely miss the player creeping up the stairs behind you. That's why the best players stop, every so often, and deliberately scrutinise the whole scene, not just the thing they're looking for.

Getting out alive

Here's where many vault raids go wrong. When the water drains, anyone nearby can see that the pool is empty. Anyone nearby knows there's a player in the vault, loaded with fresh loot and, for a few seconds, looking down at a chest instead of up at the entrance. If you feel a little apprehensive at that moment, good. You should.

The vault has emergency exits with walls that slide open, so you don't have to climb back out the way you came in. Use them. The arrogant player opens three chests, stands in the middle admiring their new Exotic, and gets surprised from above. The smart player loots fast and leaves by the side door.

Busy or quiet? Both!

In more than 350 tournament matches studied by one esports website, nobody landed at Stone Sanctum at all in over a third of games. Yet other guides list it among the busiest spots on the map. Those facts sound like they contradict each other, but they don't: pros and ordinary players think differently. In public matches, a famous vault attracts a crowd. Expect company.

The perfect vault heist, by role

Opening the vault with a squad is like a bank-robbery film, only legal and with more llamas. Every great heist needs a team. Assign these roles before you jump:

  • The Key Runner (North): sprints to the north-east tower, smashes the container, and runs the key to the pedestal without stopping to open a single chest. This takes enormous self-control.
  • The Key Runner (South): does the same in the south-east tower, while trying not to bump into the North Runner.
  • The Lookout: stands somewhere high and reports what they see. A good lookout says "Two players, west steps, coming fast." A bad lookout says "Uh-oh."
  • The Pedestal Guard: protects the middle of the temple, so that nobody steals the moment your keys go in.

And yes, someone will shout "THE WATER'S GONE!" when the pool drains. That's not a role. It just always happens.
