The Greatest Hits
The Emirates, London5 May
2009

Sixty-one minutes gone. Arsenal have a corner.

Sixty thousand people inside the Emirates Stadium are still hoping. Their team is 2–0 down on the night and 3–0 down over the two legs of a Champions League semi-final. They need a miracle. A goal from this corner might be the start of one.

The ball swings in. It's cleared. It bobbles out towards the edge of Manchester United's penalty area, deep in their own half, where Cristiano Ronaldo is waiting with his back to the Arsenal players. He doesn't control it. He doesn't even look. He flicks it with his heel, round the corner, to Park Ji-sung.

And then Ronaldo starts to run.

Park takes one touch and slides the ball out to the left, where Wayne Rooney is already galloping into the space Arsenal have left behind. Rooney carries it forward, head up, legs pumping. Arsenal's defenders are sprinting back, but they're looking the wrong way, at the ball, at Rooney. Nobody is watching the player in red tearing up the right-hand side of the pitch, who has now run almost the whole length of it.

Rooney sees him. Of course he does. The pass comes across, Ronaldo arrives at full speed, and the ball is in the net before the goalkeeper can move.

Three–nil. Game over. Manchester United are going to the Champions League final, and all over the stadium, Arsenal fans are standing up and walking out. Ronaldo had started the move on the edge of his own penalty area and finished it in Arsenal's. It's one of the greatest counter-attacking goals ever scored, and it shows you everything that made these two players so devastating together: the trick, the speed, the vision, and the understanding.

Oh, and earlier that night? Ronaldo had scored from a free kick measured at 39 yards. Rooney, it turned out afterwards, had played the whole match feeling ill.

Box to box: the perfect counter-attack

Two teenagers

The story of this partnership starts with two very different boys.

Cristiano Ronaldo grew up on Madeira, a small Portuguese island in the Atlantic Ocean, the youngest of four children in a family without much money. At 12 he left home to join the academy of Sporting Lisbon, crying himself to sleep because he missed his mum. In August 2003, aged 18, he played for Sporting in a friendly against Manchester United and ran rings around them. On the flight home, United's players told Alex Ferguson to sign him. Days later, he had.

Wayne Rooney grew up in Croxteth, a tough part of Liverpool, and supported Everton. At 16 he scored a famous winner against Arsenal and became the most talked-about prodigy in England. In August 2004, aged 18, he joined United for a huge fee.

At first they looked like opposites. Ronaldo was a flamboyant winger who loved stepovers, flicks and tricks, sometimes a few too many. Rooney was all power and relentless energy: he would chase a lost cause all the way back to his own corner flag. But that's exactly why they fitted together. Ronaldo needed someone to find him; Rooney loved finding people. Ronaldo needed space to run into; Rooney, who was strong enough to hold off two defenders, made it for him. And both of them were versatile: either could play in the middle, out wide, or just about anywhere Ferguson asked.

The Greatest Hits: a countdown

They played together at Old Trafford for five seasons. These are the moments United fans still talk about.

7. The debut (28 September 2004)

Rooney's first game for United was a Champions League match against Fenerbahçe of Turkey. He scored. Then he scored again. Then, in the second half, he curled in a free kick to complete a hat-trick. He was 18, and nobody that young had ever scored a Champions League hat-trick before. United won 6–2. Old Trafford knew it had found someone special.

6. The first trophy (26 February 2006)

The League Cup final against Wigan in Cardiff. United won 4–0, Rooney scored twice, and Ronaldo got one too. It was the first trophy the two of them won together. It wouldn't be the last.

5. The wink (1 July 2006)

Then came the moment that nearly broke them up. At the 2006 World Cup, England played Portugal in the quarter-final. Rooney was sent off for stamping on a Portuguese defender, and as Rooney walked off, the cameras caught Ronaldo, who had rushed over to complain to the referee, giving his own bench a cheeky wink. England went out on penalties. English newspapers turned Ronaldo into a villain, and everyone said the two of them could never play together again.

Everyone was wrong. When they came back to Manchester, the two of them shook hands in Ferguson's office and got on with it. Rooney has since said there was never really a problem between them. In their first league game back, United beat Fulham 5–1: Rooney scored twice and set up a goal for Ronaldo. They reconciled so completely that United won the league that season, and Ronaldo was voted the best player in the country by both the players and the football writers.

4. Roma 7–1 (10 April 2007)

A Champions League quarter-final, and United had lost the first leg 2–1 in Rome. At Old Trafford, they scored three goals in eight first-half minutes, Rooney grabbing one of them, and Ronaldo added two more either side of half-time. By the end it was 7–1, and the Roma players were reportedly begging Ronaldo to stop dribbling. It's still one of the biggest wins ever in a Champions League knockout match.

3. Forty-two goals (2007–08)

In 2007–08, Ronaldo turned from a tricky winger into a goal machine. He scored 42 goals in all competitions, 31 of them in the league, an astonishing total for a player who started most games out wide. Rooney's job changed too: he dropped deeper, ran further, and kept feeding his team-mate. It was a selfless thing to do, and it won United the league again.

2. Moscow (21 May 2008)

The Champions League final, against Chelsea, in pouring rain in Russia. Ronaldo rose to head United in front, a towering header from Wes Brown's cross. You'll hear the full story of that extraordinary night in Chapter 9. For now, all you need to know is that it ended with United as champions of Europe, and Ronaldo as the best player in the world. He won the Ballon d'Or that December.

1. The Emirates (5 May 2009)

You already know about this one. A 39-yard free kick, and a counter-attack so fast and so clinical that Arsenal never recovered. It was the perfect Ronaldo and Rooney goal. It was also, as it turned out, close to the last.

The end of the beginning

Three weeks after the Emirates, United lost the 2009 final in Rome to Lionel Messi's Barcelona. And that summer, Real Madrid paid a world-record £80 million for Ronaldo. He left with 118 goals for United, three league titles and a Ballon d'Or. (He came back to Old Trafford for one more season in 2021, but that's another story.)

Rooney stayed. He played on for eight more years, won more titles, and in 2017 became the greatest goalscorer in Manchester United's history. You'll find out how in Chapter 9.

The two of them had seemed almost telepathic, but there was a third person behind every one of those goals: the man who signed them both, picked them both, argued with them both, and turned them both into champions. In 2009 he was the most successful manager in British football.

Twenty years earlier, he had been about to lose his job.
