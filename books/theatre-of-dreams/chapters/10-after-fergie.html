Paris, 6 March 2019. Ninety minutes gone, and it's 2–1.

Manchester United are winning at Paris Saint-Germain, but it isn't enough. They lost the first leg 2–0 at Old Trafford, and nobody, in the entire history of the Champions League, has ever lost a home first leg by two goals and gone through. United are missing ten players through injury and suspension. Some of the substitutes on their bench are teenagers nobody has heard of.

After that first-leg defeat, a reporter had asked Solskjær about the size of the task. He just smiled. "Mountains are there to be climbed," he said.

The hardest job in football

When Ferguson retired in 2013, he picked his successor himself: David Moyes, a fellow Scot who had done a good job at Everton. The fans hung a huge banner from the Stretford End calling Moyes "The Chosen One". It didn't work out. Ferguson had kept an ageing squad winning through sheer force of personality, and without him it fell apart. United finished seventh, and Moyes was sacked before the season ended.

What followed was a decade of turbulence. There were seven permanent managers in thirteen years. Some of them brought trophies; none of them brought the league title. Here's the story in one picture.

There were caretakers in between, too, including Ryan Giggs, Ralf Rangnick and Ruud van Nistelrooy.

There were some proper highlights. Van Gaal's team won the FA Cup in 2016 with Lingard's thunderous volley. Mourinho won the League Cup and the Europa League in 2017, United's first European trophy since Moscow. In 2021 Cristiano Ronaldo came home, aged 36, and scored twice on his second debut against Newcastle; Old Trafford shook. Ten Hag won the League Cup in 2023, and then, in 2024, beat Manchester City in the FA Cup final. (That story deserves the full treatment, and it gets it in Chapter 11.)

There were some proper lows, too. In 2024–25 United finished 15th, their worst league position since they were relegated in 1974, and lost the Europa League final to Tottenham. Old Trafford, meanwhile, was getting old: the roof leaked, and on rainy days water poured down the stairs.

A Moscow hero comes home

In January 2026, with United seventh in the league, Amorim left, and the club turned to someone who knew exactly what Manchester United should feel like. Michael Carrick had been one of Ferguson's most reliable midfielders, a calm, clever passer who won five league titles with United. On that rainy night in Moscow in 2008, he had stepped up and scored the second penalty of the shoot-out.

Carrick was meant to be a short-term solution. Instead, the results were remarkable: United won ten of his first fifteen games, lost only two, and climbed from seventh to third. They qualified for the Champions League with three games to spare, and in May 2026 the club gave him the job permanently. United finished third, with 71 points.

Michael Carrick: Moscow penalty-scorer in 2008, head coach in 2026. Photo: Timmy96, CC0.

In September 2026, Manchester United played in the Champions League for the first time in two years, and they celebrated with a 4–0 win over Sabah of Azerbaijan at Old Trafford. They'll need more nights like that, and fewer last-minute equalisers like the one Spurs scored on 10 October. But for the first time in a long while, United fans feel optimistic.

The captain

If one player has carried United through the hard years after Ferguson, it's Bruno Fernandes. He arrived in January 2020 from Sporting Lisbon, the same club that produced Cristiano Ronaldo, and in July 2023 he was made captain. Bruno is a restless, tenacious midfielder who would always rather try the difficult pass that might win a game than the safe one that won't. By 2025 he had scored or set up well over a hundred goals for the club.

He's also a captain who never stops talking: pointing, demanding, waving his arms, cajoling team-mates into one more run. Some people find it frustrating to watch. Most United fans wouldn't swap him for anyone.

A new theatre

There's one more big change coming. In March 2025, United announced plans for a brand-new stadium right next to Old Trafford, designed by the famous architects Foster + Partners. It would hold 100,000 people, which would make it the biggest club stadium in Britain. Instead of a normal roof, it would sit under a giant umbrella-like canopy, held up by three huge masts that you could see from miles away, and the canopy would collect rainwater and solar power.

The club has started buying the land it needs, and hopes to move in around 2030. Building a stadium that size is a gigantic job, and dates like that often slip. But one day, a new Theatre of Dreams will rise beside the old one. What happens to Old Trafford itself hasn't been decided yet.
