Make do, me hearties

Picture the scene. You leap from the Going Merry, glide down over the jungle, land on a rooftop, and sprint into the first house you see. There's a chest in the attic. Inside: a grey pistol, a handful of ammo and a single, solitary tangerine. You peer out of the window and spot two other players running towards your house, and a third, further off, who has spotted all of you.

Last season, you might have shrugged, jogged to the nearest Vending Machine, and bought yourself a golden rifle and a pocketful of Overdrive Grenades. Not any more. On Crossbones Isle, the Gold Bars have been vaulted and the Vending Machines have vanished. What you find is what you get. And that, says the creator Evolve Jake, changes everything.

This book is built around one of Jake's videos, 15 Tips Every Fortnite Player Needs To Know In Treasure Hunters. Jake makes clear, careful, no-nonsense guides, and this one is packed with clever ideas, from a secret sword trick to a puzzle involving three stone frogs. We've taken every tip, explained exactly how it works, tested it against other guides, and added a few challenges of our own. By the end, you won't just know what to do on Crossbones Isle. You'll understand why.

One quick note

Jake's tips are for Zero Build, the mode where nobody can build walls or ramps. Most of them work in normal Battle Royale too, but whenever we say "cover", we mean things you find or carry, not walls you build.

A season with no shortcuts

Jake noticed something the moment the season began. Without gold to spend, Fortnite feels, in his words, "a lot more like a standard battle royale." Every player starts with nothing and has to scavenge for the rest. Nobody can simply buy their way to a perfect loadout.

That has two big effects. The first is that the things which used to be cheap and easy, like healing items and travel items, have suddenly become scarce. You can't count on finding them, so when you do, you have to use them wisely. The second is a change of attitude. As Jake puts it, "sometimes you're just going to have to make do with what you would consider to be a less optimal loadout." In plain English: you won't always get the weapons you want, so you'll have to be resourceful and win with the ones you've got.

That's a skill, and like any skill, it can be learned. The best players aren't the ones with the luckiest chests. They're the ones who can look at a grey pistol, a single tangerine and a bad situation, and still find a way through.

Beware the third party

There's one more thing you need to understand before the tips will make sense, and it's a phrase Jake uses again and again: getting third-partied.

Imagine two players having a fierce battle in Loguetown. They're both firing, both healing, both completely focused on each other. Neither of them notices a third player creeping up behind them. The moment the fight ends, and the winner is low on health and out of shield, the newcomer strikes. That sneaky newcomer is the "third party", and being third-partied is one of the most frustrating ways to lose.

Crossbones Isle is a small island. Fewer players drop in (80, instead of 100), but they're squeezed into much less space, so fights are close together and noise travels. Jake warns that "anytime you fight, you're pretty likely to get third-partied." That single fact explains half the tips in this book. If an ambush could come at any moment, then you need two things above all: a way to escape quickly, and a way to heal quickly. Hold on to that idea. It's the key that unlocks everything else.

How this book works

The fifteen tips are grouped into five chapters, and each one follows the same pattern:

What Jake says. The tip, explained, with short quotes in Jake's own words.
Why it works. The reasons behind it, because a tip you understand is a tip you'll remember.
Try it. A small challenge to practise in your next match.
Chug Jug check. Did we find the same thing in other guides? Is it confirmed, or something to test for yourself?

Along the way you'll find Word Watch boxes, packed with words worth stealing for your own writing (they're all collected at the back of the book), and, later on, some surprising true stories from science and history that connect to the game in ways you might not expect. Ducks lost at sea. Fireworks invented a thousand years ago. Sailors who sang while they worked. You'll see.
