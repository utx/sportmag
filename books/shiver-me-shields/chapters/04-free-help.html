Chapter 4
Free help

Losing the gold took a lot away from Fortnite: no more buying weapons, no more buying Overdrives, no more buying your teammates back. But Jake spotted that it also gave something back, and most players haven't noticed yet. Some things that used to cost gold now cost nothing at all. And there's a second kind of currency still floating around the island, one you can earn, save and spend to make your weapons stronger.

This chapter is about getting help without spending a single coin.

Tip 13: Talk to everyone

What Jake says. Crossbones Isle is home to a crowd of characters, the computer-controlled people that gamers call NPCs (short for "non-player characters"). Some will join your team and fight alongside you. Some offer services. Last season, most of them wanted gold first. Now, Jake explains, "With gold being removed, NPC interactions are free."

His advice is simple: "if you land at an area that has an NPC, I think it's worth checking out what that NPC does and seeing if it's one that you want to hire." He especially mentions characters who offer a Rift service, which can whisk you out of an area in an instant, perfect for escaping the storm or a crowd of enemies.

Why it works. A hired helper is an extra pair of eyes and an extra gun on your side, and in Solos, that's like having a teammate when nobody else does. A Rift is a free escape, and you already know from Chapter 1 how precious escapes are this season. The only cost is a few seconds of conversation. Most players run straight past NPCs out of habit; the observant player stops to chat.

Hiring fighters, by the way, is one of the oldest ideas in history. Soldiers who fight for pay, rather than for their own country, are called mercenaries, and kings and cities hired them for thousands of years. The most famous survivors of that tradition are the Swiss Guard, who have protected the Pope in Vatican City since 1506. Over five hundred years later, they're still there, still recruited from Switzerland, and still wearing a bright uniform of blue, red and yellow stripes that looks like it marched straight out of a storybook. Your Crossbones Isle helpers are less colourful, and much cheaper.

And what about the Rift? In Fortnite, a Rift is a shimmering tear in the sky that zaps you somewhere else. In real science, the closest idea is a wormhole: a shortcut through space, like folding a piece of paper so that two distant dots touch, and then poking a pencil through both. In 1935, the famous scientist Albert Einstein and his colleague Nathan Rosen showed that the mathematics of space and time seemed to allow such a bridge to exist. Nobody has ever found one, and many scientists doubt that one could ever be travelled through. For now, Rifts belong to Fortnite and science fiction. But the idea came from real maths.

A Rift item from an earlier season. On Crossbones Isle, some NPCs offer Rift services instead. (Image: Epic Games / Fortnite)

Try it: In your next match, talk to every NPC you meet before you do anything else. Write down in your Ship's Log what each one offered. Which one would you hire again?

Chug Jug check: ✅ Epic's own announcement for the season says that, with Gold Bars vaulted, follower services are now free. Exactly which characters offer Rifts may change from week to week.

Tip 14: Spend your Sprite Dust on upgrades

The gold may be gone, but there's still one currency on Crossbones Isle: Sprite Dust, the sparkly stuff you earn from finding and extracting Sprites. Most players think of it as Sprite money and nothing else. Jake has a better idea.

You can spend Sprite Dust on Portable Extractors, but that's not all it's good for. (Image: Epic Games / Fortnite)

What Jake says. High-rarity weapons are hard to find this season, "but you can still upgrade weapons with sprite dust at the extraction sites." Walk up to an Extraction Site, choose a weapon, and spend some Dust to raise its rarity: grey to green, green to blue, and so on. And there's a reason you can afford to: this season, "we don't have things like loot hacks to spend dust on," so there's less competition for your Dust.

Jake then explains four ways to build up a healthy pile of Dust.

1. Master your favourite Sprites. Once you've mastered a Sprite (levelled it all the way up), Jake says "the cost of resummoning that sprite is way cheaper." So if you master the Sprites you use most, you'll spend less Dust on them and have more left over for upgrades. It's like buying a season ticket instead of paying at the gate every week.

2. Fire the Golden Firework. Sometimes a chest gives you a Golden Firework, which lives in your gizmo section. Set it off and, Jake says, "it'll say your name in the sky and then there will be little containers of sprite dust that fall down." Grab them quickly before anyone else does!

Your name in lights! Jake's name sparkles across the sky (the start of it is hidden in the original video). (Frame: Evolve Jake)

The Golden Firework: your name in lights, and Sprite Dust raining down. (Image: Epic Games / Fortnite)

3. Extract on Mondays. Every Monday is Mastery Monday, with double Sprite XP and double Sprite Dust. Jake calls extracting Sprites on Mondays "the best way for you to quickly get your dust going."

4. Win, then go shopping. When you win a match, you get a little time to walk around afterwards. Jake reminds us that if you pick up the Sprites your opponents dropped, the game "will extract them for you and that will give you dust." So after a Victory Royale, don't just celebrate. Go collecting!

Why it works. Think of Sprite Dust as savings, and weapon upgrades as a way of turning those savings into power. A blue weapon you've upgraded yourself beats a green one you were hoping to swap. Players who save their Dust carefully, and spend it where it matters most, will have stronger weapons than players who leave it sitting in a pile, and that's a lesson about money that grown-ups spend their whole lives learning.

The Golden Firework, meanwhile, has a fascinating real-life history. Fireworks were invented in China more than a thousand years ago. The story goes that Chinese alchemists, early experimenters who mixed substances to see what would happen, were searching for a potion to help people live forever. Instead, they discovered a powder that fizzed, flashed and exploded: an early form of gunpowder. Packed into bamboo tubes and set alight, it made bangs and sparks to celebrate festivals and, people believed, to scare away evil spirits. Fireworks are still a huge part of Lunar New Year celebrations today.

And here's the science behind the colours. Firework makers mix in tiny amounts of different metals, and each metal glows its own colour when it gets hot enough. Strontium burns red. Barium burns green. Copper burns blue. Sodium, the metal in table salt, burns a bright golden yellow, the same colour as some old street lights. Chemists use exactly this trick in the lab, holding a sample in a flame to see what colour it turns, to work out which metals are inside. Every firework display over Sydney Harbour on New Year's Eve is, quietly, a chemistry lesson.

Try it: Next Monday, plan your session around Sprites: extract as many as you can, and keep a tally of how much Dust you earn. Then spend some of it on one weapon upgrade, and notice the difference it makes.

The Golden Firework really does write your name in glittering letters: in Jake's video, "Evolve Jake" sparkles across the sky. Weapon upgrades at Extraction Sites come from Jake's two videos, and another creator has made a whole guide to farming Dust with the Golden Firework. The cheaper re-summon for mastered Sprites is Jake's tip: check the price in your own Sprite collection.

Do the maths

Imagine extracting a Sprite earns you 500 Sprite Dust on a normal day.

1. How much would the same Sprite earn on a Mastery Monday?

2. On Monday you extract 4 Sprites. How much Dust is that altogether?

3. Imagine one weapon upgrade costs 750 Dust. How many upgrades could you afford with your Monday Dust, and how much would be left over?

Answers
1: 500 × 2 = 1,000 Dust
2: 4 × 1,000 = 4,000 Dust
3: 5 upgrades (5 × 750 = 3,750), with 250 Dust left over

Chapter 4 in one breath

The gold has gone, but help is free. Talk to every NPC: hire them, or use their Rifts to escape. Treat Sprite Dust as savings, and spend it on weapon upgrades. Build your pile by mastering your favourite Sprites, firing Golden Fireworks, extracting on Mondays and collecting Sprites after every win.
