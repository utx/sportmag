Chapter 2
Heal smart

Remember the second golden rule from the start of the book? Always keep a way to heal fast. On a small island where third parties lurk around every corner, the player who heals quickest is the player who's ready first, and the player who's ready first usually wins.

But there's a snag. Without Vending Machines, healing items have become surprisingly hard to come by. Jake says heals "can also be a bit of a struggle at times," and he's right: a match can easily go by where you open chest after chest and find nothing but ammunition. So this chapter is about becoming a healing forager, someone who knows exactly where to find a top-up when the chests let you down.

Tip 6: Smash the fruit baskets

What Jake says. Jake doesn't hold back: "The oranges are far and away the best healing item this season." He means Nami's Tangerines, the little orange fruit from the One Piece collaboration. (They look exactly like oranges, so you'll hear players call them both.)

On paper, a tangerine doesn't sound like much. Jake thinks each one gives you 25 shield and 5 health, "which isn't a lot of heal." But the secret is speed. A tangerine takes about one second to eat. A big Shield Potion takes several. And when you've just survived a fight and can hear footsteps getting closer, those extra seconds can be the difference between victory and the lobby.

Here's the really useful part. Tangerines aren't only found in chests. Jake points out that "you can actually find these oranges in the fruit or vegetable baskets around the map." You know those wooden produce boxes outside houses and market stalls, the ones most players run straight past? Give them a whack with your pickaxe. Jake says these baskets are something "most players have ignored for quite a while," and now they're one of the best places to stock up.

Nami's Tangerine: small, fast and surprisingly powerful. (Image: Epic Games / Fortnite. One Piece © Eiichiro Oda/Shueisha, Toei Animation)

Why it works. Healing speed matters more than healing size whenever you're under pressure. A slow, big heal leaves you vulnerable for longer. A fast, small heal gets you back into the action almost instantly, and because you can carry several tangerines in one slot, you can eat two or three in a row if you need to.

There's a lovely bit of history hiding in the tangerine's name, too. Tangerines are a kind of mandarin, a fruit that came originally from China and South-East Asia. But the word "tangerine" comes from Tangier, a busy port city in Morocco, on the northern tip of Africa, where ships stopped on their way in and out of the Mediterranean Sea. Over two hundred years ago, "Tangerine" simply meant "from Tangier", and when the little mandarins arrived in Europe through that port, the name stuck to them. So every time you eat one, you're saying the name of a city. Nami, a navigator who loves maps more than anything, would surely approve.

Try it: Land somewhere with market stalls or houses (Loguetown and Windmill Village are good bets) and smash every produce box you pass. How many tangerines can you collect before you find your first chest?

Chug Jug check: ⚠️ Tangerines are definitely this season's speedy healing item, and the One Piece quests even ask you to collect 16 of them. But creators disagree about the exact numbers: Jake thinks 25 shield and 5 health; TyluhFryz thought 25 shield and 10 health. Everyone agrees about the 25 shield. Check the item's description in your inventory and settle the argument yourself!

Tip 7: The berry swap

This is the cleverest tip in the chapter, and it uses a machine most players forget exists.

What Jake says. In Zero Build, everyone carries a pocket item (your Pocket Shockwave, from Chapter 1). You can change your pocket item at special vending machines. Last season, swapping cost gold. This season, with gold gone, Jake explains that "it's free to buy anything and there's no cooldown or anything."

So here's the trick. If you're desperate for healing, walk up to one of these pocket-item machines and swap your Shockwave for the berries. Jake believes you get three berries, and that each one gives 20 health or shield. Gobble all three, which is 60 healing, and then immediately swap straight back to your Pocket Shockwave, so you're ready for the next fight.

The berry swap
1
Find a pocket-item machine.
2
Swap your Shockwave for the berries. It's free!
3
Eat all three: 3 × 20 = 60 healing.
4
Swap straight back to your Shockwave.

Why it works. Most players think of the pocket-item machine as a place to make one choice and walk away. Jake noticed that because swapping is now free and instant, the machine is really a free healing station in disguise. It's a brilliant example of what's called lateral thinking: looking at something ordinary from a completely different angle until you spot a use nobody else has noticed.

Jake suggests it's especially handy at the very start of a match, if you've landed near one of these machines but haven't found any heals yet, or if you've cleared out a whole location and come up empty.

Try it: Next time you see a pocket-item machine, don't walk past. Check which items it offers, and try the berry swap once, just so you know exactly how long it takes. Then, in an emergency, you'll do it without thinking.

Chug Jug check: ❓ We couldn't find the berry pocket item in any written guide yet, so the numbers (three berries, 20 each) are Jake's best memory. He said himself, "I believe." The bit about free swaps fits what Epic announced: with Gold Bars vaulted, things that used to cost gold are now free. Test it, and tell your friends what you find.

Tip 8: Slurp cactus and Slurp barrels

The last healing tip is about two things that are hiding in plain sight.

The Slurp cactus. Jake says there are Slurp cactuses in the desert, the orange area across the north of the island, and smashing one gives you health or shield. If you're landing near Loot Lagoon or Fortune's Fort, or just passing through, keep your eyes open for them.

It's a fun idea, because real cactuses really are nature's water tanks. A cactus like the giant saguaro (say it "suh-WAR-oh"), which grows in the deserts of Arizona and Mexico, has deep folds running down its body, called pleats. When it rains, the cactus drinks as fast as it can, and the pleats stretch out, like an accordion being pulled open, so the whole cactus swells up with stored water. Then, through the long dry months, it slowly uses up its supply, and the pleats fold back in. A big saguaro can store an astonishing amount: some estimates say over a thousand gallons, which would fill more than a dozen bathtubs. But please don't try drinking from a real cactus! Most cactus juice is bitter and can make people sick. Leave the slurping to Fortnite.

The Slurp barrels. Slurp barrels, which heal you when you break them, are still around the island. But they've had a makeover for the pirate season. Jake warns that "they do look like wooden now to fit the theme." The good news: "they still say slurp on them," so once you know what to look for, they're easy to spot.

Barrels are a perfect choice for a pirate island, because for thousands of years, barrels were how ships carried almost everything: fresh water, salted meat, biscuits, gunpowder. The craftspeople who made them were called coopers, and their barrels were cleverly designed with a fat middle and narrower ends. That bulge wasn't just for looks. It meant a sailor could tip a heavy barrel onto its side and roll it along, or spin it round on its belly to change direction, which is much easier than dragging a box. Watch a barrel roll and you'll see it.

Why it works. Jake's real advice here is to learn the healing spots in the places you like to land. If you know there's a Slurp barrel behind a particular house, or a patch of cactuses on your favourite route, you can top up in the early game without using any of your precious tangerines. A player who knows their territory well can replenish their health almost anywhere.

Try it: Choose your favourite landing spot and draw a simple "healing map" of it in your Ship's Log: mark every produce box, Slurp barrel and cactus you find. Next time you land there, you'll know exactly where to go.

Chug Jug check: ❓ Slurp barrels have been in Fortnite for years, and this season's wooden look comes from Jake's own playing. The desert Slurp cactus is also Jake's tip. We didn't find a written guide mentioning either yet, so keep an eye out and see for yourself.

Do the maths

Your shield is empty and your health is full. You want 100 shield.

1. If each tangerine gives 25 shield, how many do you need?

2. If you do the berry swap first (60 healing, all going into your shield), how many tangerines do you need after that? Will any be wasted?

3. A tangerine takes about 1 second to eat. Imagine a big Shield Potion takes about 5 seconds. How much longer would you be stuck drinking if you used two big potions instead of four tangerines?

Answers
1: 100 ÷ 25 = 4 tangerines
2: 100 − 60 = 40 to go. 1 tangerine isn't enough, so 2 (50), and 10 shield is wasted.
3: 2 × 5 = 10 seconds vs 4 × 1 = 4 seconds: 6 seconds longer

Chapter 2 in one breath

Healing is scarce, so forage for it. Smash produce boxes for speedy tangerines. Use the free pocket-item machine as a secret healing station. Learn where the Slurp barrels and cactuses are in your favourite places. And remember: a fast heal beats a big heal when trouble is coming.
