Chapter 3
Secret loot

Every good treasure island has its secrets: a hidden door, a riddle, a cave that only opens for those who know the trick. Crossbones Isle is no exception. Jake explains why they matter so much this season: "there's not vendings where you can just buy gold guns off the rip." ("Off the rip" is gamer slang for "right at the start".) If you can't buy powerful weapons, you have to find them, and the surest way to find them is to know where they're hidden.

This chapter reveals four secrets, from a lost key to a stone frog. Some are confirmed by several guides; one is Jake's discovery alone. All of them reward the curious.

Tip 9: Grab every key

What Jake says. As you loot, you might stumble across a key. It could be in a chest, or lying on the floor. Don't ignore it! Once a key is in your inventory, Jake explains, "it will show you where the nearest key room that you can open is." Follow the marker, unlock the door, and inside you'll find, in Jake's words, "several rare chests", along with Sprite Chests and ammo boxes.

Inside a key room. Piles of gold, Rare Chests and ammo: worth the detour. (Frame: TG Plays)

Why it works. Rare Chests are far more likely to contain high-rarity weapons and movement items than ordinary chests, and a key room contains several of them in one place. Jake's verdict is strong: if you find a key, "that's going to be your ticket to probably have really good loot for the rest of the game." In a season where good loot is so scarce, a key is like finding a winning lottery ticket in the gutter.

Keys and locks are among humanity's oldest inventions. Thousands of years ago, the ancient Egyptians built wooden locks with little pegs inside, which dropped into holes in the bolt so it couldn't slide. Only a key with the right pattern of bumps could lift every peg to exactly the right height at once, and then the bolt would slide free. In the 1860s, an American inventor called Linus Yale Jr. perfected a small metal version of the same idea, the pin tumbler lock. Take a look at a front-door key: the jagged edge is a pattern of hills and valleys, each one cut to lift a tiny pin inside the lock to just the right height. It's a secret code, written in metal.

Try it: In your next few matches, keep a tally in your Ship's Log: how many keys did you find, and what was in the room each time? After five key rooms, you'll know whether Jake was right.

Chug Jug check: ✅ Several guides list the vault locations, and TG Plays found a "Rare Vault Key" and opened a vault packed with Rare Chests. Where keys turn up is random, so keep your eyes peeled.

Tip 10: Race the ducks

What Jake says. This one sounds like a joke, but it's real. Somewhere on the river west of Cursed Cavern, there's a duck race, a set of toy ducks waiting at the starting line. Start the race, and, as Jake describes it, "they'll make their way down the river, and then at the end, you will get some loot and a sprite."

Jake is honest that the loot isn't spectacular, but in a season where loot is precious, he says, "if this is on your loot path at all, maybe you want to take advantage of this." A free Sprite is a free Sprite.

Why it works. The duck race rewards players who explore. Most people sprint past rivers looking for chests; the patient player who stops to look around finds extra treasure. That's a lesson which works far beyond Fortnite.

Floating ducks have a surprising place in science, too. In January 1992, a cargo ship crossing the Pacific Ocean was hit by a storm, and several huge shipping containers tumbled overboard. One of them was packed with 28,800 plastic bath toys: yellow ducks, red beavers, blue turtles and green frogs. The container burst open, and the little toys bobbed away across the ocean.

Most people would have forgotten about them. But an American oceanographer (a scientist who studies the sea) named Curtis Ebbesmeyer realised they were a gift. He asked people to report every bath toy that washed up on a beach, and wrote down where and when each one was found. Because all the toys had started from the same place on the same day, their journeys showed exactly where the ocean's currents, the great rivers of moving water inside the sea, had carried them. Hundreds turned up in Alaska within a year. Others drifted for many years, and some were reported much further away still. Ebbesmeyer's records helped scientists improve their computer models of how the ocean moves, all thanks to a container of lost toys. Some people affectionately call them the "Friendly Floatees", after the brand name printed on their packets.

So next time you start the duck race on Crossbones Isle, spare a thought for their real-life cousins, quietly helping science from a beach in Alaska.

Try it: Find the duck race and follow the ducks all the way down the river. How long does the race take? Time it, and write it in your Ship's Log.

Tip 11: Turn the frogs

Here's a proper puzzle. Near the middle of the island, at a landmark called Giant's Battlefield, just south of Lantern Landing, there's a pond surrounded by cherry-blossom trees. In the middle of the pond is a small island. And around the edge of the pond sit three stone frog statues, all looking the wrong way.

What Jake says. "If you turn these statues to face the center area, you will get two rare chests that will kind of rise from underneath."

To turn a frog, walk up and interact with it, and it rotates. Keep going until it's staring straight at the island. One guide suggests hopping across the lily pads to reach the statues quickly. Once all three frogs are facing inwards, a podium rises from the island with two Rare Chests on it.

Why it works. Two Rare Chests for a few moments of puzzle-solving is a terrific deal. But Jake adds a wise warning: he wouldn't land at Giant's Battlefield "strictly for that", especially in Solos, because the spot is right in the middle of the map and busy. It's best in squads, where "each of you can quickly turn one of those statues", which makes the puzzle three times faster. That's collaboration in action: a job that takes one person ages takes a team a moment.

Frogs, as it happens, are some of the most ingenious survivors on the planet, and Australia has one of the cleverest. The water-holding frog lives in some of the driest parts of the outback, where it might not rain for months. When the rain does come, the frog drinks deeply, soaking water in through its skin and storing it in its body. Then it digs backwards into the mud and wraps itself in a cocoon made from layers of its own shed skin, which stops the water escaping. Tucked away underground, it waits, barely moving, until the next big rain arrives, sometimes for years. Aboriginal Australians have long known how to find these frogs underground, and how to gently squeeze a drink of water from them in the desert. A frog that's also a water bottle! Remember the saguaro cactus from Chapter 2? It's the same clever trick: soak it up when you can, and save it for later.

Try it: With a friend in a squad, land near Giant's Battlefield, give everyone a frog, and see how fast you can raise the chests. Can you beat 30 seconds?

Chug Jug check: ✅ Confirmed by several guides (Beebom, Games.gg, GameRant and Destructoid): three frogs, all facing the centre, two Rare Chests. One guide says the puzzle can be solved again in later matches.

Tip 12: The waterfall cave

The last secret is the most mysterious of all, because it needs a password, of a sort.

What Jake says. Near Fortune's Fort, there's a waterfall, and behind it, a hidden cave. "You have to go through this waterfall here and then break this rock that will be right in front of you." Inside there's a first room with a little loot. But the real treasure is further in. At the very end of the cave is a blank stone wall, and Jake reveals the secret: "if you emote next to this wall, the rock will lower." Behind it waits a chest, which the guides say is a Rare Chest.

Open sesame
1
Walk through the waterfall, east of Fortune's Fort.
2
Break the rock wall with your pickaxe.
3
Crouch or slide through the gap.
4
Dance! Emote at the wall at the very end.
5
The wall lowers. Open the Rare Chest.

Fans of old stories will recognise this trick. In the tale of Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves, from the famous collection of stories called One Thousand and One Nights, a poor woodcutter overhears a band of robbers standing in front of a solid rock and calling out the magic words "Open, Sesame!" The rock swings open to reveal a cave crammed with gold. On Crossbones Isle, the magic words are a dance move. Whoever designed this cave had clearly been reading the classics.

And there's real science behind caves like this one. Many waterfalls form where a layer of hard rock sits on top of softer rock. The river pours over the hard edge, and the falling water, along with the stones and grit it carries, slowly batters and scours the soft rock at the bottom. Over thousands of years, the soft rock wears away faster than the hard rock above it, carving a hollow, sometimes a whole cave, tucked behind the curtain of falling water. Geologists call this wearing-away erosion, and it's one of the most patient sculptors on Earth.

Try it: Which emote opens the wall fastest? Try a few and time them. (We'd recommend something with a bit of swagger. It is a pirate cave, after all.)

Chug Jug check: ✅ Confirmed by Beebom and Games.gg: walk through the waterfall east of Fortune's Fort, break the first wall, crouch or slide through, emote at the end wall, and claim one Rare Chest. Games.gg says it can be done again in later matches.

Do the maths

1. Imagine each tap turns a frog a quarter of a full turn (90 degrees). Frog 1 is facing directly away from the island. How many taps does it need?

2. Frog 2 is facing to the left of the island, at a right angle. What's the smallest number of taps it could need? What's the largest?

3. Of the 28,800 bath toys, imagine a quarter were ducks. How many ducks is that?

Answers
1: Facing away = half a turn = 180° = 2 taps
2: 1 tap if it turns the right way; 3 taps if it turns the long way round
3: 28,800 ÷ 4 = 7,200 ducks

Chapter 3 in one breath

Good loot hides from lazy players. Grab every key and follow it to a room full of Rare Chests. Start the duck race if it's on your way. Turn three frogs to raise two Rare Chests (easiest in a squad). And dance your way into the waterfall cave. Curious explorers get rich.
