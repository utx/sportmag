Chapter 1
Move smart

Here is the single biggest problem on Crossbones Isle, according to Jake: "one of the biggest things that I've seen a lot of people struggle with this season has been the lack of mobility."

Mobility just means your ability to move: to sprint, dash, leap, fly and generally get from one place to another faster than your enemies would like. Last season, mobility was everywhere. You could buy Overdrive Grenades from any Vending Machine for a handful of gold, and good players carried a stack of them, flinging themselves around the map like pinballs. This season, the Vending Machines are gone, and every way of moving fast has to be found, earned or saved.

The five tips in this chapter are all about one idea: treat movement as treasure. Spend it carefully, find cheaper alternatives, and never be caught without a way out.

Tip 1: Treat your Overdrives like gold

The Overdrive Grenade. Rarer than ever this season. (Image: Epic Games / Fortnite)

What Jake says. The Overdrive Grenade is one of the best movement items in the game, and last season people used them for everything: to cross the map, to chase enemies, to show off. Now that you can't buy them, Jake says you need to be "a little bit smarter about how you use them and not just spam them like pushing people all the time."

Why it works. Think back to the third party (the sneaky player who attacks right after a fight). If you use your only Overdrive to charge at an enemy, you might win the fight, but you'll be left standing in the open with no shield, no escape and a stranger lining you up in their sights. If you conserve your Overdrive instead, you have a get-out-of-trouble card: the moment someone ambushes you, you can zoom away, heal up and reset. Jake calls having an escape "essential."

It's a bit like saving your last biscuit. Eating it now feels great, but you'll be very glad you saved it when you're starving later.

Try it: In your next three matches, make a rule: you may only use an Overdrive to escape, never to attack. Count how many times having one saved you. Were you surprised?

Chug Jug check: ✅ The Overdrive Grenade is back this season, and Gold Bars and Vending Machines really have gone (Epic's own announcement). How often you'll find Overdrives depends on your luck, so treat every one as precious.

Tip 2: The sword hop

This is the most unusual trick in the whole book, and Jake admits it takes practice: "It's kind of weird to do, but once you get the timing for it right, it'll kind of like click in your head."

It uses Zoro's Three Sword Style, the One Piece weapon that lets you swing three swords at once. Most people use it for slashing. Jake discovered that it can also help you travel.

Zoro's Three Sword Style: for slashing, and, it turns out, for hopping. (Image: Epic Games / Fortnite. One Piece © Eiichiro Oda/Shueisha, Toei Animation)

What Jake says. Here's the sequence, step by step:

The sword hop
1
Take out Zoro's swords.
2
Sprint until your character's stance changes.
3
Press attack, then jump, almost at the same moment.
4
You lunge forward. Slide straight away.
5
Jump, then slide again: hop, slide, hop, slide!

The tricky part is step 3. Jake explains: "I hit my attack button slightly before jump while sprinting." Not at exactly the same time, and not one after the other, but a tiny fraction of a second apart, like the two beats of a heartbeat. Get it right and your character does a lunge attack that throws you forward. Then you slide, jump, slide, jump, and your speed keeps rolling along with you. Players call this kind of movement bunny hopping, because you bound along like a rabbit.

Why it works. Every time you lunge, slide or jump, you pick up a little bit of speed. Normally that speed fades away quickly. But by chaining the moves together without stopping, you carry the speed forward from one move to the next, so it never gets the chance to fade. Jake calls this "carrying that momentum forward." The result is a burst of movement that costs you nothing: no Overdrive, no item, just skill.

Try it: This one needs practice somewhere safe. Pick up Zoro's swords early in a match, find a quiet field or beach, and try ten hops. Don't worry if it feels awkward at first. Every skill does. Jake's advice is simply to "experiment with it. And once you get it, you get it."

Chug Jug check: ❓ This trick comes from Jake's own testing, and we haven't found it in a written guide yet. Another creator, TyluhFryz, said he'd seen some surprisingly speedy movement tricks with Zoro's swords, which suggests Jake's onto something. The timing may change in an update, so test it yourself.

Tip 3: Save your Pocket Shockwave

The Shockwave Grenade, which lives in your pocket in Zero Build. (Image: Epic Games / Fortnite)

What Jake says. In Zero Build, everyone starts the match with a Pocket Shockwave Grenade, a special item that doesn't take up a normal inventory slot. Throw it at your feet and the blast hurls you into the air. Brilliant! The catch is that it takes time to recharge: 40 seconds before you can use it again.

Jake admits he used to fire his off whenever he felt like it, just to travel. This season, he says, "I think you do want to try to conserve this pocket shock for like important moments." And he describes exactly what can go wrong. You use your Shockwave to leap into a fight. Your enemy uses an Overdrive to escape. Now your Shockwave is recharging, your enemy has gone, and you're stuck in the open with no way out, just as a third party arrives.

Why it works. A Shockwave that's ready is a promise: if anything goes wrong, I can get out. A Shockwave that's recharging is just a timer. So unless Jake is "very certain" he can win a fight, he won't use his only Shockwave to engage. He keeps it for emergencies.

Try it: Count the seconds out loud next time your Shockwave is recharging. Forty seconds feels like a very long time when someone is chasing you! That feeling is exactly why it's worth saving.

Chug Jug check: ✅ The Fortnite Wiki confirms that the Pocket Shockwave Grenade has a 40-second cooldown after its first use (and a 60-second wait at the very start of the match). In Jake's video, the pocket-item machine lists it as an Epic item.

Tip 4: Cars, cannons and Skylines

If your Overdrives and Shockwave are precious, the smart move is to use something else whenever you can. Jake suggests three free or cheap ways to travel, saving your best items for emergencies.

Cars. There are vehicles scattered around the island. Hop in, drive to your next location, hop out. Simple, fast and completely free. Cars are noisy, though, so other players will hear you coming.

Cannons. You can load yourself into one of the island's cannons and fire yourself across the map. Jake is honest about them: "This isn't the greatest or most practical thing, but it can save you a little bit of time." They're fun, they're free, and they're very dramatic. Just aim carefully, or you might end up in a tree.

Something just went flying over Lantern Landing. (Frame: TG Plays)

The Skyline Deployer. This item fires a line with a big red magnet on the end. The magnet sticks wherever you aim, and then you ride along the line. Jake didn't like it much in an earlier season, when Overdrives were everywhere, but now he says, "I do find myself carrying the skyline deployer in situations where I don't have mobility."

Try it: Play one whole match using only free travel: cars, cannons, Skylines and your own legs. No Overdrives allowed. It forces you to plan your route instead of zooming about, and planning ahead is a skill that wins games.

Chug Jug check: ✅ Cannons are confirmed in this season's patch notes and in several guides, and the Skyline Deployer's name appears on screen in Jake's video ("Rare Skyline Deployer").

Tip 5: No escape? Carry cover

A Shield Bubble: instant cover, wherever you need it. (Image: Epic Games / Fortnite)

Sometimes you'll search every chest and find no movement items at all. What then? Jake's answer is clever: if you can't run from danger, make sure you can hide from it.

What Jake says. When you don't have Overdrives, carry cover items instead: things like Shield Bubbles, which create a protective dome around you, or bunker items, which create instant walls. Jake explains that in Zero Build, movement is useful for two things: chasing enemies, and "react[ing] to bad timing or react[ing] to third parties." And if you can't have movement, "the next best thing you can have to kind of neutralize some of this bad timing is going to be a cover item."

Why it works. Remember the two golden rules: escape, and heal. If an ambush comes and you can't escape, a Shield Bubble buys you something just as valuable: time. Inside it, you can heal up, think, and plan your next move, while bullets bounce off the outside. Instead of being a sitting duck, you've built yourself a little fortress in a split second.

This is the "make do" attitude from the start of the book. The ideal is an Overdrive. The alternative is cover. A good player always has a plan B.

Try it: Before every fight, ask yourself one question: "If someone else turns up right now, what will I do?" If you don't have an answer, find one before you start shooting.

Chug Jug check: ✅ Shield Bubbles are one of Zero Build's pocket items, so you can swap one in at a pocket-item machine (Chapter 2 explains how). Jake also mentions bunker items, which you'll find as loot.

Chapter 1 in one breath

Movement is rare, so treat it like treasure. Save your Overdrives for escapes. Learn the sword hop for free speed. Keep your Pocket Shockwave for emergencies. Travel by car, cannon or Skyline whenever you can. And if you have no way to run, carry a way to hide.
