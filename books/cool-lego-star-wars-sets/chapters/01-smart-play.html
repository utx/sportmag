Chapter 1
The brick that talks back

On 5 January 2026, at a giant technology show in Las Vegas, LEGO announced what it called one of the biggest changes to LEGO play since the minifigure was invented in 1978. It was a single 2×4 brick. But this brick has a tiny computer chip inside, a speaker, lights, and sensors that can feel movement.

It's called the SMART Brick, and the very first sets to use it are Star Wars. On 1 March 2026 they went on sale in just six countries: the USA, the UK, France, Germany, Poland and Australia. LEGO said it was starting small because the system was so complicated to make. So for once, Aussie kids got something before most of the world.

How it works

SMART Brick

Speaker, lights, motion and light sensors, a tiny chip. Charges without a cable.

+
SMART Tags

Flat tiles that tell the brick what it's part of: an X-wing, a TIE, a cantina band.

+
SMART Minifigs

Minifigures the brick can recognise, so Vader sounds different from Luke.

= lights, sounds and reactions when you swoosh, shoot and play. No screen needed.

Only three of the sets actually come with a SMART Brick in the box: the TIE Fighter, the X-wing and the Throne Room. LEGO calls these "All-In-One" sets. The other five are "SMART Play Compatible", which means they come with tags and SMART minifigures but no brick. Without one, they're ordinary LEGO sets. That matters a lot when you're deciding which to ask for.

Word Watch

Interactiveadjective
Reacting to what you do. A book is not interactive; a video game is.
Sensornoun
A part that detects something, like movement, light or sound, and sends a signal.
Accelerometernoun
A sensor that measures movement and changes in speed. Phones use them to know which way up they are; the SMART Brick uses one to know when you're swooshing.
Synthesisernoun
An electronic device that creates sounds. The SMART Brick's speaker is driven by a tiny one.
Compatibleadjective
Able to work together. "SMART Play Compatible" sets work with a SMART Brick, but don't include one.
Gimmicknoun
A clever trick or feature that grabs attention but might not be useful for long.

So, is it any good?

Honestly? The grown-up reviewers mostly aren't convinced, but they also aren't the people it was made for. Brickset's reviewers found that the sounds don't always match the films (and Star Wars fans know what a TIE Fighter should sound like), that the lights sometimes flash without any clear reason, and that the minifigures have odd printed patches on their sides because they contain electronics. One Brickset member called the TIE Fighter "crude" and "annoying". Another member, a parent, said the shooting and engine sounds really do make swooshing more fun.

By June, some online shops in the UK had cut SMART Play prices by up to half, which Brickset took as a sign the sets weren't selling well. Brickset's team also thought the idea might suit LEGO City or Pokémon better than Star Wars, where everyone already knows exactly how everything should sound. Our verdict: it's a clever idea that's still finding its feet. If you get one, start with an All-In-One set, so you actually have the brick.

The All-In-One sets (brick included)

Darth Vader's personal TIE Advanced, with the SMART Brick slotted inside so it roars, shoots and refuels with sound effects. There are two minifigures: a SMART Darth Vader and a Rebel Fleet Trooper.

Win: It includes a SMART Brick, the thing every other SMART set needs.

Win: Vader is a SMART minifigure, so the brick reacts to him being on board.

Watch out: One Brickset member called the engine noise "grating" and said the sound effects were "crude" and repetitive.

Watch out: Fans on Brickset give it 2.9 out of 5, the lowest score of any SMART Play set so far.

Luke's X-wing from the end of A New Hope, with the SMART Brick behind the cockpit making engine and laser noises as you fly. You get five minifigures, including SMART versions of Luke, Leia and R2-D2.

Win: Five minifigures and a SMART Brick, the best mix of figures and tech in the range.

Win: A parent who reviewed it on Brickset said the shooting sounds and engine noise "do improve swooshing".

Watch out: The X-wing's shape has been squashed to fit the brick. That same parent bluntly called it "ugly".

The big finale of Return of the Jedi: Luke and Darth Vader duel in front of the Emperor, while an A-wing fights outside. It comes with a SMART Brick, six minifigures, two Royal Guards and a cannon turret with its own SMART Tag.

Win: Six minifigures, including SMART Luke, Vader and Palpatine, and an A-wing starfighter as well as the throne room.

Win: Brickset fans rate it 4.3 out of 5, by far the best-liked SMART set.

Watch out: At US$159.99 it's expensive, and Brickset's team noticed the lights flash during the duel with no clear connection to what's happening.

Watch out: Brickset thinks the A-wing alone "could be an enjoyable $30 set by itself".

The Compatible sets (brick sold separately)

The Millennium Falcon, built for play: the top lifts off to show a lounge, a holochess table and lots of room for SMART Han, Chewbacca, Luke and C-3PO. The SMART Brick goes in the back and lights up the engines.

Win: Brickset's reviewer found it "actually pretty enjoyable", with lots of features for an 885-piece Falcon.

Win: The SMART Brick is "integrated quite well", using its lights for the engine glow.

Watch out: No SMART Brick in the box, and it looks chunky next to other LEGO Falcons. The reviewer also disliked the dialogue sounds.

Best for: younger pilots who already have a SMART Brick from another set.

The famous wretched hive of scum and villainy, split into four parts. Press a lever and an ejector sofa sends a minifigure flying. There's a bar, a stage for the band and a Dewback to ride. With a SMART Brick, the band actually plays.

Win: Lots of play features and five minifigures, including SMART Obi-Wan and SMART Greedo.

Win: 666 pieces, so plenty to build for the price.

Watch out: Brickset fans give it only 3.3 out of 5, and there's no SMART Brick included.

An Imperial AT-ST stomps through the forest moon of Endor, chasing a speeder bike hijacked by Wicket. The walker has a turning head, a dual stud shooter and a flick shooter.

Win: Two vehicles and three minifigures, including SMART Wicket and a Scout Trooper.

Win: Shooters on the walker make it fun even without a SMART Brick.

Watch out: Brickset fans rate it 3.3 out of 5, and you need a brick from another set for the sounds.

Luke's Landspeeder and a little service station where you can refuel, repair and grab some blue milk. You get SMART Luke, a Jawa and a Gonk droid.

Win: The cheapest way into SMART Play, if you (or a friend) already have a brick.

Watch out: Without a SMART Brick it's a small, simple set. Brickset noticed one UK shop cut it to almost half price within three months.

Watch out: Fans on Brickset give it 3.3 out of 5.

Yoda's mud hut on the swamp planet Dagobah, with a bed and cooking area inside, plus a Force-training tower where you balance things on scales.

Win: SMART Yoda and SMART Luke are included, and the hut has plenty of space inside.

Watch out: Brickset's reviewer saw "little appeal here for anybody": only two tags, and very little to do without the SMART Brick.

Watch out: US$69.99 is a lot for a set that isn't "smart" until you buy another one.
Brick tip: If you only get one SMART set, get an All-In-One one first. Then, if you love it, the Falcon is the best Compatible set to add.
