Chapter 3 · Sprites and Overrides
Power trip

This season, Fortnite handed out superpowers. Sprites are small collectable creatures that ride on your back and give you a special ability while they're equipped: a dash, a vampire bite, X-ray vision. Overrides are bigger still. Grab an Override Console and you can rewrite the rules of the whole match, for everybody.

Every power has a price, though. And that's where the arguments start.

Dash or bite?

The facts: With Spooky Dash equipped, jump and then jump again in mid-air, and you dash forward, even phasing through some objects. You get up to three charges, which refill over time. With the Vampire Sprite, every time you damage an enemy, you siphon some of that damage back as health. Both get stronger as they level up.

The case for A: Movement is everything in Fortnite. Three dashes means three chances to dodge a shot, close the gap on an enemy, or vanish round a corner. It's the most popular Sprite on the Island for a reason.

The case for B: The Vampire turns every fight you're winning into one you finish on full health. No stopping to heal, no twelve-second Chug Jug. You just keep going, fight after fight.

The catch: You can't shoot while you're dashing, and it's easy to set off a dash by accident at the worst possible moment. And the Vampire only helps if you're actually hitting people. If you're losing a fight, it does nothing at all.

Underneath, this is a question about personality. Would you rather be hard to catch, or hard to stop?

Heads or pumpkins?

The facts: These are the two Overrides that exist purely for fun. Big Head Mode gives every player in the match an enormous head. Spooky Prop Hunt lets players crouch, stay perfectly still and turn into a Halloween prop. Suddenly, every pumpkin you see is a suspect.

The case for A: It's funny. It stays funny. It is, if anything, funnier in the final circle, when the last two players are bobbing towards each other like enormous balloons.

The case for B: It turns a shooting game into a game of hide-and-seek. Sneaky players get to be ingenious: which prop would you become? A pumpkin among pumpkins? A gravestone in a graveyard? A lonely candle on a windowsill?

The serious bit: Big Head Mode makes everyone easier to hit, which sounds bad, until you realise it's the same for everyone. Spooky Prop Hunt rewards patience and punishes players who rush. One makes the match louder; the other makes it quieter. Which kind of match do you enjoy more?

See or survive?

The facts: The X-Ray Sprite marks nearby enemies every so often, so you can see where they are. As it levels up, it reaches further and pings more often. (It was designed by a fan, in a competition Epic ran called #DesignASprite.) The Overshield Sprite gives you an extra layer of shield on top of your normal one: 10 points for each level, up to 50 when it's fully levelled up.

The case for A: Knowing where your enemy is before they know where you are is one of the biggest advantages in the whole game. With X-Ray, you're never surprised. You get to choose every fight.

The case for B: Information is nice, but shield is survival. Fifty extra shield means you can take a shotgun blast that would have finished anyone else, and still win.

The deeper question: Would you rather know more or be stronger? Generals, chess players and football coaches have argued about this for centuries. Most of the time, the answer is "know more", because a player who's always vigilant rarely needs the extra shield. But when you get caught anyway, nothing beats it.

The great health puzzle

This one hides a clever little maths problem. Normally, a fully healed player has 100 health and 100 shield. That's 200 points to get through. Under More Health, you have 200 health and no shield. That's… also 200 points.

So does it make any difference? Yes, a big one, and it's not in the numbers. It's in the items. Under More Health, every Shield Potion, Small Shield and Shield Keg on the Island becomes completely useless, while Med Kits, Bandages and food become precious. Players who've been hoarding shield items suddenly have pockets full of nothing.

The Overshield Override gives everyone more to get through, so fights last longer. That helps whoever is better prepared and better at aiming, because a longer fight gives skill more time to show.

So which would you pick? If you're carrying three Med Kits, pick A, and watch everyone else panic. If you're the better shot, pick B and let the long fights do the work. The best choice depends on what's in your pockets, which is the kind of "it depends" that counts as a proper answer.

Trophy or tool?

The facts: The Crown Sprite only levels up when you win a match, and its power is giving you extra Crown Wins: a sign that shows everyone you've won before. It looks magnificent and does almost nothing in a fight. Klombo is a big pink monster who gives you a random item every time it levels up, and it only levels up when you eat or drink healing items.

The case for A: Everyone in the lobby would know you're a winner. Other players would be envious. Some would even avoid you. A reputation can win fights before they start.

The case for B: Klombo actually helps. Heal a lot, get a lot. Every potion becomes a lucky dip.

The catch: Showing off the Crown might make you a target instead. Some players love nothing more than knocking out someone who looks like a champion. Would you rather look powerful or be useful?

The classic

This is probably the most famous would-you-rather in the world, and Fortnite has a version of it. The Spooky Sprinter Override makes you almost invisible while you sprint. The catch? It makes everyone else invisible too.

The case for A: Nobody could ever catch you, find you or sneak up on you. You'd be the ultimate spy.

The case for B: You could go anywhere. Over mountains, across oceans, to school in ninety seconds. And you'd never be stuck in traffic again.

The thing people forget: Invisible people are still there. They bump into things, get stepped on, and can't wave to their friends. Flying people are very conspicuous: everyone looks up when someone flies past. Every superpower comes with a downside. Writers know this, which is why the best superhero stories are always about the problem the power causes, not the power itself.

Off the Island: real animals, real superpowers

You don't need a Sprite for superpowers. Nature got there first.

  • The peacock mantis shrimp punches with clubs on its front legs so fast that the water around them briefly boils into bubbles, which collapse with a tiny flash of light and a burst of heat. It can smash a snail's shell, and some can crack aquarium glass.
  • The tardigrade, or water bear, is a creature about half a millimetre long that can survive being dried out, frozen and even sent into space. In 2007, scientists sent some into orbit, exposed them to the vacuum of space, and brought them home. Some survived.
  • Octopuses are masters of camouflage. They can change colour and even the texture of their skin in a fraction of a second, to match rocks, sand or seaweed. Some can copy the shapes of other sea animals.

So, would you rather punch like a mantis shrimp, survive like a tardigrade, or disappear like an octopus?

Lightning round

  • A Sprite that makes you jump twice as high, or one that makes you run twice as fast?
  • Choose every Override in every match, or never let anyone else choose one?
  • Read minds for one match, or see through walls for one match?
  • Be invincible for ten seconds once per match, or have one extra life?
  • Carry ten Sprites but none of them can level up, or carry one that's already fully levelled?

One last thought: if you chose invincible for ten seconds, ask yourself which ten seconds. Using it at the wrong moment would be futile. Timing is a superpower too.
