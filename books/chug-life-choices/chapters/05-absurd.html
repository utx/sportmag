Chapter 5 · Ridiculous choices
Absolutely absurd

Everything so far has been sensible. Strategy. Teamwork. Maths. Well done for getting this far.

Now forget all of it. This chapter is absurd. The choices make no sense, the situations are impossible, and nobody will ever, ever make you do any of them. Which is exactly why you have to take them completely seriously. Choose carefully. Your dignity depends on it.

Dance or shout?

The case for A: Dancing is fun. Emotes are a huge part of Fortnite. And your squad would always know when you'd found a chest, because you'd be busting a move next to it.

The case for B: It's quick. "CHUG!" takes half a second. And it's useful: your squad would always know when you're healing, so they could cover you.

The catch: A full dance emote, next to a chest, in the middle of WonkeeLand, while three players run towards you. Picture it. Now picture shouting "CHUG!" every time you eat a single apple, in the final circle, when everyone's trying to be quiet. Both options would leave you mortified. The question is which kind of embarrassment you can live with: silent and slow, or fast and loud.

Back to school

The case for A: Every lesson would take place on a roller-coaster. Fractions would be explained using slices of candyfloss. Times tables would be sung by a chorus of animatronic mascots. You'd never forget anything, because you'd be too surprised.

The case for B: Peely is one of Fortnite's most famous characters, and he has been around for years. PE with a banana sounds ridiculous, but think about it: bananas are full of energy. Every lesson would end with a healthy snack. (Please don't ask what the snack is.)

The catch: A teacher who's always smiling, every second, without blinking, is a bit unsettling. A teacher who might get peeled is a constant worry. Both are deeply eccentric. Discuss.

Llama or duck?

The case for A: You'd be the most popular creature on the Island. Every player in the lobby would be delighted to see you. Admittedly, they'd be delighted because they want to break you open, but popularity is popularity.

The case for B: A gentle float down a canal in the sunshine, through some boxes, past some palm trees, ending with a celebration involving cannons. It's basically a holiday. A duck has no enemies. Players only chase you because they want you to win.

The catch: As a duck, you'd go wherever the water takes you. No steering, no stopping, no choices at all. As a llama, you'd have a very short, very exciting day. Would you rather be wanted or free?

Sounds and smells

The case for A: Music is good. One song is still music. And if it's a song you like, it would be fine… for the first week. Maybe two.

The case for B: A smell would actually be useful. The closer the storm got, the stronger the burnt-toast smell would be, and you'd know to run. It's a built-in warning system, like a smoke alarm.

The catch: Hearing the same song a thousand times would become so monotonous that you might start to hear it in your sleep. And a pungent smell of burnt toast every match might ruin toast for you forever. Which would you sacrifice: your ears, or your breakfast?

Rhyme time

The case for A: "There's someone on the hill, I think they're coming for the kill!" "I'm low on health, I need some stealth!" Rhyming callouts are funny, and they're easy to remember. Plus you'd become a poet without even trying.

The case for B: Questions are surprisingly useful. "Is that a player on the hill?" "Shall we go left?" "Does anyone have a spare Shield Potion?" You might find you can say almost anything as a question.

The catch: Try rhyming "Override Console" in a hurry. Or try warning your squad about an enemy using only a question, while being shot. ("Would anyone… like to know… that I'm being… eliminated?")

Off the Island: the masters of nonsense

Some of the most famous poems ever written are complete nonsense, on purpose. In 1871, the English artist and writer Edward Lear published The Owl and the Pussy-cat, in which an owl and a cat sail away "in a beautiful pea-green boat", get married, and eat "mince, and slices of quince, which they ate with a runcible spoon". What is a runcible spoon? Nobody knew. Lear invented the word.

In the same year, Lewis Carroll (who also wrote Alice's Adventures in Wonderland) published a poem called Jabberwocky, which begins: "'Twas brillig, and the slithy toves / Did gyre and gimble in the wabe." Almost every important word in it is made up. And yet, somehow, you can tell exactly what's happening.

Carroll explained that some of his invented words were like a portmanteau, an old kind of suitcase that opens into two halves: two words packed into one. Slithy means "slimy" and "lithe". Some of his inventions became real words. Chortle, a mixture of "chuckle" and "snort", is in the dictionary today because of him. So is galumph.

You use portmanteau words all the time without noticing: brunch (breakfast and lunch), smog (smoke and fog), motel (motor and hotel). Try inventing a Fortnite one. A slurpocalypse, perhaps, for when every Slurp barrel on the map explodes at once.

Boing or squeak?

The case for A: You only jump sometimes. The rest of the time you'd be silent. Just don't jump near anyone.

The case for B: Squeaky footsteps are adorable. And at least you'd be quiet in the air.

The serious bit hiding in the silly bit: Sound is one of the most important things in Fortnite. Good players listen for footsteps to work out where enemies are. A squeak with every step would give you away constantly, everywhere you walked. A boing only gives you away when you jump. So, oddly, the preposterous BOING is the smarter choice. Sometimes the silliest-sounding option is secretly the sensible one, and you only find out by thinking it through.
