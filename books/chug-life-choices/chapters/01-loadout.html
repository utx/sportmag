Chapter 1 · Weapons and items
Loadout lunacy

You have five inventory slots. Five. Not six, not twenty, not "whatever fits in my backpack". Every time you pick something up, you have to decide what it's worth, and what you're willing to drop to make room for it. Players have been agonising over those five slots since 2017.

This chapter makes it harder. Every choice here is about loot, and every one of them is a trade-off.

The Big Gulp

The facts: A Chug Jug heals your health and your shield, all the way to full: up to 200 points in one go. But it takes about twelve seconds to drink, and you can only carry one. A Small Shield Potion gives you 25 shield in about two seconds, and you can carry six. The catch is that Small Shield Potions only work until your shield reaches 50. After that, they do nothing.

The case for A: One Chug Jug can turn a player on 1 health into a player on full health and full shield. Nothing else in the game does that. It's the ultimate comeback in a can.

The case for B: Twelve seconds is an eternity in Fortnite. You're standing still, glugging away, and anyone nearby has twelve seconds to notice. Small Shields take two seconds each, and six of them can top you up across three or four different fights instead of just one.

Do the maths: Six Small Shields add up to 150 shield, which sounds better than it is, because they stop working at 50. If you start a fight on zero, the most they can ever do at one time is get you halfway. That's a real limit, and the kind of detail a sharp player spots and a hasty one misses.

The Chug Jug verdict

B, for most players. Twelve seconds of standing still is a big price, and speed wins fights. But we know some of you are already shaking your heads, because there is no feeling in Fortnite quite like that final glug.

The Golden Goodbye

The facts: The Overpowered Pump is an Exotic shotgun that gets stronger every time it hits: each hit adds 5 damage to the next shot, up to 25 extra, so it climbs from 119 to 144 per shot. Midas' Masterpiece is a solid-gold pistol that once belonged to Midas, one of Fortnite's most famous characters. A headshot does 313 damage, enough to eliminate anyone. But you only get seven bullets, and then it disappears. Epic took it out of the game in the 15 October update, so this choice is hypothetical: imagine it came back, just for you.

The case for A: The Pump lasts. It never runs out of uses, it gets stronger in the middle of a fight, and it'll still be in your hands in the final circle.

The case for B: Seven shots. Seven chances to end a fight instantly. Land them well and that's seven eliminations, which is more than most players get in a whole match. And there is nothing in Fortnite more satisfying than watching a golden bullet do its job.

This is an old argument in a new form: something scarce and spectacular, or something ordinary and reliable? A prudent player takes the Pump. A daring one takes the gold and promises not to miss.

Quantity or quality?

This is a completely hypothetical rule change, but it's a great way to think about something mathematicians call expected value: what you can expect to get, on average, if you do something lots of times.

Suppose you open ten chests in a match.

  • Under rule A, you get ten weapons, all grey. You'll never be empty-handed, but you'll never have anything special either.
  • Under rule B, you'll get about two Legendary weapons on average. But "on average" hides a scary truth. In some matches you'll find three. In others, you might open chest after chest and find nothing, and be holding your pickaxe when the first enemy arrives.

How scary? The chance that a single chest is empty under rule B is four in five. The chance that your first three chests are all empty is 4/5 × 4/5 × 4/5, which works out at about one in two. Half your matches would start with three empty chests in a row!

So the real question isn't "grey or gold?". It's "Do I prefer a sure thing, or a gamble with a bigger prize?" People answer that differently, in games and in life. Some people would rather have $10 for certain than a fifty-fifty chance at $30. Others would roll the dice every time. Neither is wrong, but it's very useful to know which kind of person you are.

Balloons or rockets?

The facts: Two of the silliest weapons on the Island. The Balloon Popper is an Exotic assault rifle, and its 1st, 12th and 24th shots in every magazine burst into balloons that tie themselves to anyone nearby. One balloon makes them floaty. Three lift them clean off the ground. The Easy Ride Pumpkin Launcher fires a pumpkin rocket that starts slowly, slowly enough for you to jump on and ride it, before it speeds up.

The case for A: Watching an enemy drift helplessly into the sky, tied to three balloons, is one of the funniest things that can happen in a video game. You can even use it to float your opponent out of cover.

The case for B: You get to ride a rocket. Through the air. Shaped like a pumpkin. Do we really need to say more? It's also a tribute to "rocket riding", a trick from Fortnite's early years that players still remember fondly.

The serious bit: Neither is the strongest weapon in the game. The Balloon Popper does less damage than an ordinary Legendary assault rifle, and the Pumpkin Launcher only holds one rocket at a time. This dilemma is really asking a different question: would you rather win, or have the best story to tell afterwards? Plenty of players would say the second. They might be the wisest players of all.

Fort or flight?

The case for A: Throw it, and a little metal fort appears around you. It's the closest thing Zero Build players get to building, and in the final circles, when there's nowhere left to hide, a bunker is gold.

The case for B: A bunker can only be used once. The Crash Pad Jr comes back again and again, every thirty seconds, for the whole match. Throw it, bounce high into the air, and reach the high ground or leave a bad fight behind you.

The deeper question: Is it better to defend yourself, or to escape? Animals face exactly this choice. A tortoise hides inside its shell; a gazelle runs. Both strategies have kept their species alive for millions of years, which tells you something important: there isn't one right answer. There's a right answer for you, depending on how you like to play.

One gun, forever

The case for A: Master one weapon and you'll know exactly how it behaves: how far it reaches, how fast it fires, how it kicks. Unlimited ammo means you'll never be caught reloading at the worst possible moment.

The case for B: What if the first weapon you find is a grey pistol? Under rule A, you're stuck with it forever. Under rule B, you might have a sniper rifle one minute and a shotgun the next. You'd have to be versatile, able to use anything well, and that's a skill that wins matches.

The twist: Rule B is a gamble every minute. You might swap a Legendary for a grey. Would you be the kind of player who complains, or the kind who adapts? (The second kind wins more.)

Off the Island: the knife that does everything

In the 1890s, the Swiss army needed a pocket knife for its soldiers: something that could open tins of food and take apart a rifle. A Swiss craftsman named Karl Elsener started making them, and over the following years the design grew. A blade. A screwdriver. A tin opener. A corkscrew. Later versions added scissors, a saw, a magnifying glass and even a toothpick.

After the Second World War, American soldiers found it hard to pronounce the knife's German name, so they started calling it the "Swiss Army knife", and the name stuck. Today it's famous all over the world as the symbol of being versatile.

But here's the catch, and it's the same catch as dilemma #7. A Swiss Army knife can do a hundred jobs, but it isn't the best tool for any of them. A chef's knife cuts better; a real screwdriver turns screws better. There's an old saying for this: Jack of all trades, master of none. Would you rather be the Swiss Army knife, or the chef's knife?

Lightning round

No arguments supplied. You're on your own. Decide in five seconds each.

  • Never run out of ammo, or never run out of building materials?
  • A lavish inventory of ten average items, or a frugal one of two perfect ones?
  • Find a Chug Jug every single match, or a Legendary weapon every single match?
  • A Fishing Rod that catches a chest every time, or a vending machine that's always free?
  • Your favourite weapon, but it's bright pink and plays a kazoo sound when it fires, or a plain grey one that sounds normal?
