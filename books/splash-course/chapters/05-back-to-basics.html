Chapter 5
Some things never change

Fifteen tips in, you know more about this season than most players on the Island. But here's an uncomfortable truth: in a few weeks, a new season will arrive, and much of what you've just learned will be out of date. Rings may vanish, codes may expire, and the Duck Race could sink without trace. That's how Fortnite works.

Some things, however, never change. Underneath every season's new weapons and gimmicks lie the same basic principles of winning a fight, and they work whether you're holding a Mega Buster or a wooden stick. Master these five habits and you'll be ready for whatever the next season brings.

1. Hit first

The single biggest advantage in any fight is to deal the first damage. If you hit someone before they've spotted you, they begin the fight already wounded and surprised, while you begin it at full health and fully prepared. It's like starting a football match one goal up. Taking the initiative in this way is why being quiet matters so much (remember the silent Crash Pads in Tip 8): the best fights begin with an ambush, not a fair contest.

So before you fire, ask yourself: have they seen me? If not, take a moment to line up your best possible shot. A patient first shot is worth three hurried ones.

2. Push in

Once your opponent is hurt, the clock is ticking. Give them a few seconds and they'll heal, their Overshield will begin to regenerate, and your advantage will evaporate. So don't hesitate. Use your mobility to close the gap while they're still recovering. This is where Chapter 3 earns its place: a Crash Pad bounce, a Mega Buster dash or a burst of Overdrive carries you across open ground before they're ready for you.

3. Keep moving, use cover

In Zero Build, you can't build a wall, so you must use the walls the Island has already built for you: rocks, trees, buildings, cars, the brow of a hill. Before every fight, look for the nearest cover, and try to move from one piece of cover to the next rather than across open ground.

And never stand still. A player who keeps sliding, jumping and changing direction is far harder to hit than one who plants their feet. Unpredictable movement throws your opponent's aim off and buys you precious time.

4. Look around

This is the habit most players forget, and it costs them more matches than any other mistake. You've just won a fight. You're excited, you're injured, and there's loot scattered everywhere. And somewhere nearby, another player heard all that gunfire and is creeping towards you, hoping to catch you at your weakest. That player is a third party, and they are the reason so many winners of a fight lose the match a minute later.

So the moment you win a fight, stop and look. Spin your camera all the way round. Listen for footsteps and gunfire. Check the map. This habit is called situational awareness, and it's what separates good players from great ones.

Beyond the Island: the footballers who never stop looking

Situational awareness matters far beyond Fortnite. Pilots are trained in it, so are lifeguards and racing drivers. And in football, researchers who filmed Premier League midfielders discovered something fascinating: the very best players, such as Frank Lampard and Steven Gerrard, glanced over their shoulders far more often than ordinary players did, in the seconds before the ball arrived. By the time they received a pass, they already knew where every opponent and teammate was. Next time you watch a match, watch the midfielders' heads. The best ones are never still.

5. Then loot, fast

Only once you're sure the area is safe should you start looting, and even then, be quick. Tunnel vision is when you're so absorbed in choosing between two shotguns that you fail to notice the player sneaking up behind you. Take what you genuinely need (ammunition, heals, any upgrade to your weapons), heal up behind cover, and move on.

Chug Jug tip: Say the five habits out loud before your next match: hit first, push in, keep moving, look around, loot fast. Saying something aloud helps your memory store it, so it's there when you need it in the middle of a fight.

Be your own fact-checker

You'll read and watch hundreds of game guides, and every one of them goes out of date eventually. Here's how to check whether a tip is still true, the same way we checked every tip in this book:

  1. Look at the date. When was the guide made? Has there been an update since?
  2. Read the update notes. Epic publishes notes for every update, listing what's new, what's changed and what's been vaulted.
  3. Find a second source. If two independent guides agree, the tip is much more likely to be accurate.
  4. Test it yourself. Try the trick in a match. The game is always the final judge.

Professional journalists do exactly the same thing. It's called verifying your sources, and it works just as well for school projects and news stories as it does for Fortnite.
