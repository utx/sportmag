Chapter 5
Some things never change

At the end of his video, after fifteen tips about this season's new stuff, Jake says something important: "It's Fortnite at the end of the day, so a lot of these same basic principles apply."

Items come and go. Two of his tips have already gone, and in a couple of weeks a new season will change things again. But the basic habits of winning a fight work in every season, with every loot pool. Learn these and you'll be ready for whatever the next season brings.

1. Hit first

Jake says you should "ideally start fights with getting opening damage on enemies." If you hit someone before they've spotted you, they start the fight already hurt, and you start it at full health. It's like starting a football match one goal up. That's why being quiet matters so much (remember the silent Crash Pads in Tip 8?): the best fights start with an ambush.

2. Push in

Once they're hurt, don't wait for them to heal. "Use your mobility to push in." This is where Chapter 3 comes in: a Crash Pad bounce, a Mega Buster slide or a Shockwave gets you close before they're ready.

3. Keep moving, use cover

In Zero Build you can't build a wall, so use the walls that are already there: rocks, trees, buildings, the edge of a hill. And never stand still. Jake's advice is to "use movement to your advantage in fights to try to throw the enemy off." A player who keeps sliding, jumping and changing direction is much harder to hit.

4. Look around

This is the one most players forget. You've just won a fight. You're excited, you're hurt, and there's loot everywhere. And somewhere nearby, someone else heard all that shooting and is creeping towards you. That's a third party. "Once you get that elimination," says Jake, "immediately scan the area, look for third parties." Spin your camera all the way round before you do anything else.

5. Then loot, fast

Jake's warning: "Don't get too tunnel vision on just looting." Tunnel vision is when you're so busy choosing between two shotguns that you don't notice the player sneaking up behind you. Grab what you need, heal up, and move on.

Chug Jug tip: Say the five habits out loud before your next match: hit first, push in, keep moving, look around, loot fast. Saying them helps your brain remember them in the middle of a fight.

Be your own fact-checker

You'll watch hundreds of game videos. Here's how to check whether a tip is still true, the same way we checked this book:

  1. Look at the date. When was the video made? Has there been an update since?
  2. Read the update notes. Epic posts notes for every update, saying what's new, what's changed and what's been vaulted.
  3. Check a second source. If two different guides agree, it's more likely to be true.
  4. Test it yourself. Try the trick in a match. The game is the final judge.

That's what real reporters do, too. It's called checking your sources, and it works for school projects and news stories just as well as for Fortnite.
