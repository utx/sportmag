Chapter 3
No ramps? No problem.

Picture this. You've landed, opened every chest in the building, and assembled a respectable loadout: a decent shotgun, an assault rifle, a couple of Shield Potions. But there's nothing at all to help you move. Now the storm is closing in, you're on foot, and there's a long stretch of open ground between you and the safe zone. In Build mode, you'd simply build a ramp and sprint across the top of it. In Zero Build, you're exposed.

Running out of mobility is one of the most frustrating situations in Zero Build, and one of the most common. The four tips in this chapter solve it: how to bounce, slide, spin and blast your way around the Island, and how to escape when a fight turns against you.

TIP8
Swap to Crash Pads
Your pocket item
EARLY GAME

As you read in the briefing, every Zero Build player carries one pocket item in its own special slot. Use it, wait for its cooldown, and it's ready again, all match long. Most players stick with the Pocket Shockwave Grenade, which launches you a long way through the air. It's excellent, but it has a weakness: you only get one at a time, and the first recharge takes a whole minute.

Here's the tip. If you've finished looting your landing spot and found no other mobility, swap your Shockwave for the Pocket Crash Pad Jr., a small inflatable bounce pad that you throw onto the ground and jump on. You can change your pocket item at the special pocket-item vending machines around the Island.

Compare the numbers. Two pads instead of one, and each recharging in half the time, means you can bounce far more often: roughly four bounces in the time it takes a Shockwave to recharge once. When mobility is scarce, that frequency matters more than distance. And if you later find a better mobility item, or you simply prefer the Shockwave, you can always swap back.

Two sneaky bonuses

  • They're almost silent. A Shockwave makes a loud bang that every nearby player recognises. Crash Pads make hardly any noise at all, so they're perfect for a stealthy approach: bounce in on two players who are busy fighting each other, and they won't hear you coming.
  • Shoot your own pad. If you bounce away to escape, turn round and shoot the pad you just used. A Crash Pad deflates when it's shot, so anyone in pursuit can't use it to follow you.

For an item that most players ignore, it's remarkably powerful. The Pocket Crash Pad Jr. is one of the most underrated items in Zero Build.

Beyond the Island: the physics of a bounce

Why does a bounce pad throw you upwards? When you land on it, your falling body has kinetic energy. The pad stretches, and that energy is stored in the stretched material as elastic potential energy, rather like the energy in a pulled-back catapult. Then the material snaps back to its normal shape and gives the energy back, pushing you into the air. Real trampolines work exactly the same way: their springs and mat stretch and recoil. A game pad is more generous than a real one, though. It launches you far higher than you fell, which real physics would never allow.

Since launch day

Both pocket items are still in Zero Build with the same charges and recharge times. One small thing to watch: the vending machines can charge Gold Bars for a swap, so check the price on the button before you press it.

Charge the Mega Buster
Mega Man's arm cannon
IN A FIGHTfixed 15 Oct

The Mega Buster is the famous arm cannon from the Mega Man games. (It belongs to another company, so there's no picture of it here.) Tap the trigger and it fires small energy pellets, quickly, and even while you're sprinting. But hold the trigger and it charges up. After two seconds it's fully charged, and releasing it fires a powerful blast that explodes on impact, damaging anyone nearby.

That explosion is the key to using it well. In Zero Build, a player crouching behind a rock or hiding in a bush is hard to hit with ordinary bullets. A charged blast doesn't need to hit them directly: aim at the ground or the cover beside them, and the explosion does the work. It's one of the best ways to force an opponent out of a hiding place and into the open.

Look at the chart: a full charge does exactly three times the damage of a quick shot (48 × 3 = 144). Patience is rewarded.

The secret slide

The Mega Buster has a second power that many players never discover. While you're holding it, slide, and instead of an ordinary slide you'll zoom forward in a dash, floating with low gravity. That makes the Mega Buster a mobility item and a weapon at the same time.

There's one important rule. You get two dashes, and they only recharge while the Mega Buster is in your hands. If you use both dashes to travel somewhere, then switch to your shotgun straight away, your dashes won't have recharged when the fight begins. So if you're planning to use the dashes in combat, make sure both are ready before you engage, and don't swap away from the Mega Buster while they're empty.

Since launch day

When Fortnitemares arrived on 1 October, a bug stopped the charged blast from damaging players nearby, which made the Mega Buster much less useful. Reports of the 15 October update say the blast has been repaired and works properly again.

Shoot out of a Spin Dash
Sonic Power Sneakers
IN A FIGHTchanged

The Sonic Power Sneakers are Sonic the Hedgehog's famous red shoes. Use them and you curl into a spinning ball, just like Sonic. You can charge up a Spin Dash and roll rapidly along the ground, or launch yourself through the air at a nearby enemy with a Spin Attack.

Rolling up to an opponent at high speed is a brilliant way to start a fight, but there's a moment that catches many players out. When you want to stop spinning, you swap to one of your weapons, and your character plays a short animation as they uncurl. It looks as though you have to wait for the animation to finish before you can do anything. You don't. You can fire immediately, while the animation is still playing.

That might sound like a tiny detail, but in close combat, fractions of a second decide everything. Roll in, swap to your shotgun, and fire at once, and you'll often land the first shot before your opponent has worked out what just bounced into them.

Beyond the Island: how fast are you?

Scientists measure reaction time: how long it takes you to respond to something you see. For most people, reacting to a simple signal, such as a light turning on, takes about a quarter of a second. Gamers and athletes can train themselves to be a little quicker, but nobody can react instantly. That's why surprise is so powerful. If you fire the moment you uncurl, your opponent needs a quarter of a second just to notice, and by then you've already shot.

Since launch day

When the season began, there was also a gold Mythic version of the sneakers, which let you sprint at Sonic-like speed. It was carried by Dr. Eggman, Sonic's arch-enemy, who roamed the Island as a boss and didn't always drop them when defeated. In the Fortnitemares update, Dr. Eggman left the Island, and the Mythic sneakers left with him. His crashed hovercraft is still there, smoking in a crater at Green Hill Zone. The ordinary sneakers remain, and the shoot-straight-away trick still works.

TIP11
Escape in Overdrive
Overdrive Grenade
IN A FIGHT

Every good player loses fights. What separates the best is that they know when a fight is lost, and they leave before it's too late. In Zero Build, with no walls to hide behind, a clean escape is a skill worth practising, and the Overdrive Grenade is the perfect tool for it.

Throw an Overdrive Grenade and it bursts, knocking everyone nearby a short distance through the air. Anyone caught in the blast then goes into Overdrive for ten seconds: unlimited sprinting and a small speed boost. And the initial blast protects you from fall damage until you next touch the ground, so you can use it to leap off a cliff or a rooftop safely.

Throw it at your own feet

Here's the clever part, and the part many players get wrong. The Overdrive effect is given to everyone in the blast, enemies included. So if you throw one at an opponent, you're handing them ten seconds of extra speed, which is the last thing you want. Instead:

  • To escape: throw it at your own feet, ideally behind cover, then sprint away at full speed while your opponent is still reloading.
  • To chase: throw it at your own feet and use the boost to catch a wounded opponent before they can heal.
  • To rotate: if the storm is coming and you're far from the safe zone, Overdrive covers ground quickly.

You can find Overdrive Grenades in chests, and you can also buy them from vending machines, so it's worth keeping a couple in your inventory at all times. They stack up to six in a single slot.

Chug Jug tip: The blast stops fall damage, but the Overdrive effect on its own doesn't. If you sprint off a cliff a few seconds after the blast, you'll still feel the landing.

Since launch day

The Overdrive Grenade has been in the game since the season began, and it was added to vending machines on its second day. Nothing important has changed since.
