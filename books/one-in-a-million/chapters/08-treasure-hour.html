How a multiplier works

A multiplier makes the chance bigger, which makes the "1 in" number smaller. To find the new odds, divide the old "1 in" number by the multiplier.

  • Pirate Shop Access at ×2: 137,500 ÷ 2 = 1 in 68,750.
  • The Gold Outfit at ×10: 1,562,500 ÷ 10 = 1 in 156,250.
  • The Vehicle at ×10: 7,813 ÷ 10 = about 1 in 781.

Notice something sneaky. Even with a ten-times boost, the Gold Outfit (1 in 156,250) is still rarer than the token at its ordinary, un-boosted odds (1 in 137,500).

Word Watch

Multipliernoun
A number you multiply by. A "×2 multiplier" doubles something; "×10" makes it ten times bigger.
Time zonenoun
A region of the world that keeps the same clock time. There are time zones because the Sun can't be overhead everywhere at once.
Relativeadjective
Compared with something else. "Twice as likely" is a relative change: it tells you nothing until you know what the chance was to start with.
Meridiannoun
An imaginary line running from the North Pole to the South Pole. The Prime Meridian, through Greenwich in London, is where the world's time zones are counted from.

Twice a tiny number is still a tiny number

"Double the odds!" sounds enormous. This is where a lot of people, and a lot of advertisements, play a trick on your brain. A doubling is a relative change. To know what it's worth, you have to ask: double what?

Double a 40% chance of rain, and you'd better take an umbrella. Double a 0.0007% chance, and you get 0.0015%. Still basically nothing.

The Vehicle is the big winner. A ten-times boost lifts it from a 1-in-20 long shot to a 2-in-5 real possibility. If you'd like the golden car, Treasure Hour is clearly the time to hunt.

The token? It goes from 0.29% to 0.58%. Out of every 172 players who hunt flat-out for the whole two hours, one gets it, and 171 don't. Better than before. Still a very long shot.

Epic has said the boosts "may be higher in future Treasure Hours", and that it will say so if they are. So the numbers in this chapter might be out of date by the time you read it. The method won't be. Whatever multiplier is announced, you can now work out what it's really worth.

Work it out: Suppose a future Treasure Hour boosts the token by ×10. What are the new odds, and roughly what is the chance of at least one in 400 chests?

Answer: 137,500 ÷ 10 = 1 in 13,750. In 400 chests: (13,749 ÷ 13,750) to the power of 400 = 0.971, so the chance is about 2.9%. Roughly one hunter in 35.

When is nine o'clock in New York?

There's a second puzzle hiding in Treasure Hours. The windows were announced as 2 pm to 4 pm and 9 pm to 11 pm Eastern Time, on Saturday. Eastern Time is the clock they use in New York. If you live in Sydney, when is that?

The Earth spins once every 24 hours, so the Sun reaches different places at different times. To keep noon somewhere near the middle of the day everywhere, the world is divided into time zones, each counted as so many hours ahead of or behind the time at Greenwich in London.

  • In November, New York is 5 hours behind Greenwich.
  • In November, Sydney is on daylight saving time, 11 hours ahead of Greenwich.
  • So Sydney is 5 + 11 = 16 hours ahead of New York.

Add 16 hours to 9 pm on Saturday. Three hours takes you to midnight; the other thirteen take you to 1 pm on Sunday. So the evening Treasure Hour in America was Sunday lunchtime in Sydney, and the afternoon one was 6 am to 8 am on Sunday.

Did you spot that it's a different day? When it's Saturday night in New York, it's already Sunday in Sydney. Australia is one of the first places in the world to start each new day, because it sits just to the west of the International Date Line, the imaginary line in the Pacific Ocean where the calendar ticks over.

One warning: the 16-hour gap isn't fixed all year. America and Australia both change their clocks for daylight saving, in opposite directions and on different dates, so the gap is 16 hours in the Sydney summer, 14 hours in the Sydney winter, and 15 for a few weeks in between. Always check.

Work it out: A Fortnite live event is announced for 2 pm Eastern Time on a Saturday in early December. When is that in Sydney?

Answer: 2 pm + 16 hours = 6 am on Sunday. Set an alarm, and ask a grown-up first.
