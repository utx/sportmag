Feeling lucky?

Somewhere on Crossbones Isle, right now, a player is opening a chest. The lid creaks. Gold light spills out. And for one tiny moment, before the loot pops up, anything could be inside.

Usually it's a shotgun and some bandages. But this season, every chest also hides a secret roll of the dice. Very occasionally, a chest coughs up a treasure: a music track, a backpack, a golden car, or the rarest prize of all, a little token that opens the whole Item Shop for free.

For the first week of the Treasure Hunters season, nobody knew how rare those treasures were. Players opened thousands of chests and found nothing. Rumours flew around. Some people got cross. Some got very cross. Then, on 7 November 2026, Epic Games did something unusual: it published the exact odds of every single treasure.

The rarest one turned out to be 1 in 1,562,500. That's where this book gets its name. It's really a one-in-a-million-and-a-half book, but that wouldn't fit on the cover.

Word Watch

Oddsnoun
A way of saying how likely something is. "The odds are 1 in 6" means it should happen about once in every six tries.
Probabilitynoun
The branch of maths that measures how likely things are. Also the measurement itself: "the probability of heads is one half".
Randomadjective
Happening by chance, with no pattern you can use to predict what comes next.
Scepticaladjective
Not believing something straight away; wanting to see the proof first.

What this book is really about

This is a book about luck. Not lucky charms and four-leaf clovers, but the real thing: the maths of chance, which grown-ups call probability.

Probability is one of the most useful kinds of maths ever invented. Weather forecasters use it. Doctors use it. Football managers use it when they decide who takes the penalty. And it started, about 370 years ago, with two French mathematicians writing letters to each other about a dice game. (You'll meet them in Chapter 3.)

The best thing about probability is that it gives you a superpower: you can stay calm when everyone else is losing their head. When a friend says "I've opened 500 chests, so I'm due a treasure," you'll know why that's wrong. When someone online says the game must be rigged, you'll know how to check. And when a squad finds six treasures in one match, you'll be able to work out whether that's a miracle or just a Tuesday.

How to read this book

  • Every number has been checked. The odds come from Epic's own announcement. Every sum built on top of them was worked out twice, once by hand and once by computer. You should still be sceptical: Chapter 9 shows you how to test the ideas yourself.
  • Word Watch boxes explain the trickier words. All of them are collected at the back, in the Treasure Chest of Words.
  • Work It Out boxes are small puzzles. The answer is always underneath, so cover it with your hand first.
  • You'll want a calculator for some chapters. The one on a phone or tablet is fine. Ask before you borrow it.
  • The quiz at the back has twenty questions and four pirate ranks.

The small print (it's important)

This book covers the Treasure Hunters mini-season of Chapter 7 (update 43.00), which began on 1 November 2026 and is due to end with a live event on 5 December. The odds are the ones Epic published on 7 November. Epic has said it may change the boosted rates in future Treasure Hours, so if the numbers in the game news don't match the book, the game has moved on.

This is an unofficial fan book. It isn't made, approved or endorsed by Epic Games.

A note for grown-ups

Nothing in this book involves spending money. Treasures can only be found by opening chests in ordinary matches; they can't be bought. The book's message is the opposite of "keep grinding": it uses the season's very long odds to teach probability, averages and healthy scepticism, and Chapter 8 is about deciding when a prize isn't worth the time. Fortnite carries an age rating on every platform, so check the one on yours.
