About this book

One in a Million is an unofficial fan book made by The Chug Jug, a fan magazine about Fortnite. It isn't made, approved or endorsed by Epic Games. It's free, and it will never ask you to spend money. If you ever want to buy anything in Fortnite, ask a grown-up first.

Version

This book describes Fortnite Battle Royale in the Treasure Hunters mini-season of Chapter 7 (update 43.00), as it was on 12 November 2026. The treasure odds are the ones Epic published on 7 November 2026. Epic has said that boosted rates may be higher in future Treasure Hours, so check the in-game news for the latest.

Where the facts came from

The treasure odds, and the statement that they are constant outside Treasure Hours: Epic Games, via the official Fortnite Status account, 7 November 2026, as reported by Destructoid, Game Rant, GameRiv, This Week in Videogames and Timesaver.gg. Where reports disagreed on a figure, we used the one that most sources (and Epic's own list) agreed on.

Treasure Hour windows and multipliers (7 November 2026): Epic's announcement as reported by Timesaver.gg and Destructoid. Time-zone conversions are our own.

What Pirate Shop Access does: Epic's in-game description, as reported by Timesaver.gg and Game Rant. That a find is shared with the whole squad is widely reported by players and news sites but hasn't been confirmed by Epic.

The lucky match in Chapters 2 and 7: a squad match played by SypherPK, Typical Gamer and friends in November 2026. The treasure count is the squad's own on-screen tally. Words in quotation marks are what the players said during the match.

The Gizmo collection in Chapters 5 and 6 belongs to the Fortnite creator FireMonkey.

The sums: every percentage, average and "at least one" figure was worked out by The Chug Jug from Epic's published odds, and checked by computer. Hunting speeds (200 chests an hour) and the number of chests a squad opens in a match (about 100) are assumptions, not measurements, and we say so wherever they're used.

The true stories: the letters of Pascal and Fermat (1654) and the Chevalier de Méré's dice problems; the Monte Carlo roulette run of 18 August 1913; R. D. Clarke's 1946 study of flying-bomb hits on south London; B. F. Skinner's 1948 paper "Superstition" in the Pigeon; the birthday problem. These are all well documented in maths and science histories.

Play it safe

  • Nobody is giving away free V-Bucks or free Pirate Shop tokens. If a website, video or message offers them, it's a scam. The token can only be found in a chest.
  • Never share your password or your account with anyone, not even a friend.
  • This book never recommends cheats, hacks or glitches.
  • Be kind online, to other players and to the people who make the game.
  • Take breaks. The chests will still be there after dinner, and they won't remember you were gone.
