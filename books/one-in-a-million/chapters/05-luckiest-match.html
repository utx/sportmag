Chapter 5
The luckiest match

This is a true story about a single match of Fortnite.

The players were a squad of treasure hunters that included two of the best-known names in the game: SypherPK and Typical Gamer. Like thousands of others that week, they'd been opening chests for days in search of the Pirate Shop token. On one squadmate's screen, a counter kept a running total of the hunt so far. It read: Chests Opened: 80,122.

Eighty thousand chests, and still no token. Then came the match where everything seemed to go right at once.

Chest by chest

It started within moments of landing. One of the first chests of the game burst open, and a golden banner dropped onto the screen: YOU FOUND JAM TRACK. That's a 1-in-391 treasure, before most squads have even found a shotgun.

Treasure number one, seconds into the match: the weekly Jam Track, a 1-in-391 find. (Screenshot: Epic Games / Fortnite)

Soon after, a second banner: a Gizmo. Two treasures already. The squad kept moving through Busy Bay, chest after chest.

Then, behind a green door, a third: YOU FOUND BACK BLING. The weekly Back Bling is a 1-in-568 treasure, and two squadmates saw the banner at the same moment. (Players have reported all season that when one member of a squad finds a treasure, the whole squad receives it. Epic hasn't confirmed exactly how that works.)

Treasure number three: the weekly Back Bling, 1 in 568. The squad's tally was now higher than most hunters manage in an evening. (Screenshot: Epic Games / Fortnite)

By now the squad could hardly believe it. One of them announced that "the tables are hot". Another had to stop and take a breath.

The match was far from peaceful. Other squads kept attacking. One of our hunters was eliminated in the Cursed Cavern and had to wait to be rebooted, which cost them half a match's worth of chests. And still the banners came: a fourth treasure, YOU FOUND SPRITE GIZMO, on a wooden walkway. A fifth, another Gizmo, in the streets of Loguetown as the storm closed in. And a sixth, late in the game, with only a few players left alive.

Treasure number four. Gizmos are the commonest treasure at 1 in 127, but three or four in one match is another matter. (Screenshot: Epic Games / Fortnite)

Then, to finish it off, they won. Victory Royale, with a treasure chest still sitting unopened on the deck beside them.

Victory Royale, and one last chest. The squad's on-screen tally finished on six treasures; by their own final count it was seven. (Screenshot: Epic Games / Fortnite)

Six treasures on the tally: one Jam Track, one Back Bling and four Gizmos. The squad wondered aloud whether it might be a world record for treasures in a single match. Nobody keeps official records of that, so nobody can say for sure. But it raises a brilliant question, and it's a maths question.

How unlikely was it?

Step 1: what's the chance of any treasure?

So far we've looked at each treasure on its own. But a chest can drop any of them. When different things can each happen, and you want the chance of one or another, you add. (Multiply for "and", add for "or".)

Add up all seven chances from Chapter 2: 0.79% + 0.37% + 0.26% + 0.18% + the three tiny ones = about 1.6%. That's roughly 1 chest in every 62.

That number alone explains a lot. One chest in 62 isn't so rare. A solo player opening 30 chests in a match has roughly a 2-in-5 chance of seeing a golden banner.

Step 2: how many chests did the squad open?

Here we have to make an assumption, because nobody counted exactly. One squadmate finished with 28 chests despite being eliminated for half the match. A fast squad of four, landing separately and looting hard, might open somewhere between 80 and 120 chests in a game. Let's say 100.

At 1 treasure per 62 chests, 100 chests should produce 100 ÷ 62 = about 1.6 treasures. So a good, hard-working squad should expect one or two treasures per match. That's the average.

Step 3: how rare is six?

Six is nearly four times the average. Working out the exact chance takes more maths than fits in this book, so we did it by computer, and the answer is: about 1 match in every 165.

If the squad opened 120 chests, it's more like 1 in 70. If they only opened 80, it's nearer 1 in 500. So the honest answer is "somewhere in the low hundreds". Call it a one-in-a-couple-of-hundred match.

So was it lucky? Certainly. If you played ten squad matches a day, you'd see a match like that about once or twice a month.

Was it a miracle? Not even slightly. And here's why.

Lots of matches means lots of lucky matches

That same week, the counter in the Fortnite lobby showed about 2.5 million people playing Battle Royale at one time. Imagine, just for the sake of the sum, that they were all in squads of four. That's 625,000 squads, all opening chests at the same time.

If a six-treasure match happens to one squad in every couple of hundred, then during that single round of matches it happened to thousands of squads. And then a new round started, and it happened to thousands more.

Mathematicians have a wonderfully blunt name for this: the law of truly large numbers. It says that with enough tries, even very improbable things are bound to happen to somebody. One-in-a-million events happen all the time, as long as there are millions of chances.

The thing that fools us is that we only hear about the lucky ones. Nobody tells the story of the match where they opened forty chests and found four bandages and a fishing rod. The lucky matches get noticed, talked about and remembered; the ordinary ones vanish. So luck looks more common, and more meaningful, than it really is.

The birthday surprise

Here's the most famous coincidence puzzle in maths. How many people do you need in a room before it's more likely than not that two of them share a birthday?

Most people guess 183, half the days in a year. The real answer is 23. With just 23 people, the chance of a shared birthday is 50.7%.

Why so few? Because you're not asking "does anyone share my birthday?" You're asking "does any pair share any birthday?", and among 23 people there are 253 different pairs. Lots of chances means coincidences are common. Count your class: if there are 23 or more of you, there's a good chance two of you share a birthday.

And the 80,122 chests?

One last sum. If that counter was right, and 80,122 chests had been opened at normal odds, what was the chance of having found at least one Pirate Shop Access by then?

The pirate's trick again: (137,499 ÷ 137,500) to the power of 80,122 comes to 0.558. Take it from 1, and the answer is 44%.

So even after eighty thousand chests, it was still more likely than not that the token hadn't turned up. They weren't cursed. They weren't doing it wrong. They were exactly where the maths said most hunters would be.

Work it out: A solo player opens 62 chests across a few matches. Roughly what's their chance of at least one treasure of any kind? (You don't even need a calculator. Remember Chapter 3.)

Answer: About 63%. Sixty-two tries at a 1-in-62 chance: the magic 63% again.
