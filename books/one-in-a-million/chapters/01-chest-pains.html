Chapter 1
Chest pains

Chests have been part of Fortnite since the very beginning. You hear the hum, you follow the glow, you hold the button, and out pops your loot. This season the chests look different: fat and golden, wrapped in chains, with a jewelled skull on the front. And they work differently too.

Every time you open a chest in a normal Battle Royale match, the game does two things. First, it gives you the usual weapons and healing. Second, quietly, it rolls an invisible dice to decide whether you've also won a treasure. Almost every time, the answer is no. But not every time.

A Treasure Hunters chest, mid-open. Somewhere inside the game, an invisible dice is rolling. (Screenshot: Epic Games / Fortnite)

The seven treasures

There are seven kinds of treasure. Five of them change every week, which is why Epic calls them "weekly" treasures. From most common to most rare, here they are.

1. A Gizmo. Gizmos are little gadgets for your Sprites. In the lobby they sit in their own row: the Golden Firework (which rains Sprite Dust across the map), the Portable Extractor (which lets you extract your Sprite from anywhere on the Island), the Extraction Accelerator, and two kinds of locator for finding Sprites. Gizmos are the only treasure you can win again and again.

2. The weekly Loading Screen. A piece of art that shows while your match loads. One early one was called Plunder in Paradise.

3. The weekly Jam Track. A song for Fortnite Festival and for your lobby.

4. The weekly Pickaxe or Back Bling. Something to swing, or something to wear on your back.

5. The weekly Vehicle and Decal. A car body for your locker, with a paint job to match. In week one it was a gold Mercedes-AMG G 63.

6. Pirate Shop Access. The big one. A token that makes everything sold for V-Bucks in the Item Shop free for 14 days. You can switch it on once in 2026 and once more in 2027. Nobody can buy it. It can only be found in a chest.

7. The weekly Gold Outfit. A solid-gold version of that week's guest star. In week one it was a golden A$AP Rocky; in week two, a golden Lil Uzi Vert. When the week ends, that week's gold outfit is replaced by the next one.

The week-one Vehicle treasure: a golden G-Wagon. The odds of finding it in any one chest were 1 in 7,813. (Screenshot: Epic Games / Fortnite. Mercedes-AMG is a trademark of Mercedes-Benz Group)

Apart from Gizmos, you can't win the same treasure twice. Once you have this week's Jam Track, the game stops rolling for it.

Word Watch

Drop ratenoun
How often a game gives out a particular item. A drop rate of 1 in 127 means the item appears, on average, once in every 127 tries.
Speculationnoun
Guessing about something without having the facts. "Before the odds were published, there was a lot of speculation."
Transparentadjective
Open and honest, with nothing hidden. (It also means see-through, like glass. The two meanings are connected!)
Cosmeticnoun
In games, an item that changes how something looks but gives you no advantage: an outfit, a pickaxe, a loading screen.

The week nobody knew

When the season began on 1 November, Epic didn't say how rare any of this was. So players did what people always do when they don't have facts: they filled the gap with speculation.

Some said only a few tokens were switched on each day. Some said certain chests were luckier than others. Some said you had to break the chest afterwards, or that you mustn't. A few said Epic's staff were secretly handing treasures to famous players so they would brag about it online. As the days went by with almost nobody finding the Pirate Shop token, the mood turned sour, and some people sent truly nasty messages to the people who make the game. (More on that in Chapter 6. It matters.)

Then, on 7 November, the official Fortnite Status account posted the whole list. Epic said these drop rates had been the same since the season launched, and that they don't change through the day, except during special Treasure Hours (Chapter 7). In other words: no secret switch, no lucky chests. Just very, very long odds.

Look closely at that list, because the rest of the book is built on it. Notice how the numbers don't climb steadily. The first four are all in the hundreds. Then there's a jump to nearly eight thousand. Then a leap to 137,500. Then another leap to over a million and a half. Each of the last three treasures is more than ten times rarer than the one before.

Work it out: How many times rarer is the Gold Outfit than Pirate Shop Access? (Divide the big number by the smaller one.)

Answer: 1,562,500 ÷ 137,500 = about 11.4. The Gold Outfit is more than eleven times rarer than the token everybody was already calling impossible.

Why publishing the odds was the right thing to do

Being transparent about odds isn't just polite. In many countries, games that sell random prizes for money are required by law or by the app stores to publish their odds, so that players know what they're getting into. Fortnite's treasures can't be bought, so this is a different case. But the same idea applies: people make better choices when they can see the real numbers.

And look what happened once the numbers were out. The wild rumours had nowhere left to hide. You can't claim that only three tokens are released per day when the people who made the game have shown you the dice. Facts are the best cure for speculation.

The Treasure tab's adventure map. Each week brings a new Captain, new items, and a new set of weekly treasures. (Screenshot: Epic Games / Fortnite)

So now we have the numbers. The question is: what do they actually mean? What does a chance of 1 in 137,500 feel like? To answer that, you need a coin.
