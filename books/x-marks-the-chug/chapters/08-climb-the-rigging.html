Collect all six Map Fragments, one a week, and you unlock the X Marks the Zero Point Sliver loading screen: "Map up front, ancient pirate riddle on the back." The week 1 fragment was worth 10,000 XP on its own. The full guide to the week 1 fragment is in Chapter 6.

Sprite Mastery

The Sprite Mastery track lives in the Treasure tab in the lobby. A new page opens every week, with rewards along the way. Master 40 Sprites (that's 40 Sprites levelled all the way to Level 5) and you earn the Navigator's Nosedive, an umbrella glider. Use Mastery Mondays to level up twice as fast: every Monday from 9 am New York time, which is 1 am Tuesday in Sydney, you get double Sprite XP and double Sprite Dust all day.

Word Watch

Riggingnoun
All the ropes and chains on a sailing ship that hold up the masts and control the sails. Sailors climbed the rigging to reach the sails. Word origin: an old word "rig", "to fit out a ship".
Boatswainnoun
The officer in charge of a ship's sails, ropes and anchors. It's spelt "boatswain" but said "bosun"! Word origin: Old English bāt, "boat", and swain, "young man" or "servant".
Starboardnoun
The right-hand side of a ship, when you face the front. Word origin: Old English stēorbord, "steering side", because old ships were steered with a big oar on the right.
Portnoun
The left-hand side of a ship. With the steering oar on the right, ships tied up in port on their left side. Memory trick: "port" and "left" both have four letters.
Quartermasternoun
On a pirate ship, the person elected to share out the treasure fairly. Word origin: "quarter" (a place to live) and "master".

Real treasure: climb the ranks of a pirate ship

Real ships had a strict ladder of jobs. A brand-new sailor started as a deckhand, scrubbing decks and hauling ropes. A good sailor might become the bosun, in charge of the sails and rigging, then first mate, the captain's second-in-command, and maybe, one day, captain.

Pirate ships had a surprise twist. Many pirate crews voted for their captain, and could vote them out again! They also elected a quartermaster to share out the loot fairly. Most crews wrote down their rules in a list called the articles, and every pirate signed it. So, strange as it sounds, some pirate ships were run a bit like a club with elections, at a time when most countries didn't let ordinary people vote at all.
