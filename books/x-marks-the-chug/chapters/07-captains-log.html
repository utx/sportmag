Captain's Days kick off at 9 am in New York. In Sydney, that's 1 am the next day, so a Saturday Captain's Day really starts on Sunday morning for Australian players. (Remember the time-zone box in Chapter 0?)

Captain 1: A$AP Rocky

The very first captain is the American rapper A$AP Rocky, and he didn't arrive in a rowing boat. He parked an enormous grey warship, The $.$. Rocky, on the east coast (Chapter 1), and he's standing guard as the island's first boss.

The boss in action: A$AP Rocky leaps into the air with his Shock Rocket. (Frame: EveryDay FN)

Be ready for a fight. TG Plays found that the boss jumps around a lot and fires rockets at you, which makes him hard to hit. Beat him, and he can drop two prizes: his Shock Rocket (Chapter 3) and the Lil' Flacko Sprite (Chapter 5). Notice the word "can". TyluhFryz beat him many times without ever getting the Shock Rocket!

Pro Tip: The boss is popular, so you won't be the only one hunting him. Let other players fight him first, then swoop in. Just like a real pirate. (Well, a sneaky one.)

Word Watch

Privateernoun
A sailor with official permission from a government to attack enemy ships. Word origin: from "private", because privateers' ships belonged to private owners, not the navy.
Mutinynoun
When a ship's crew refuses to obey the captain and takes over. Word origin: old French mutin, "rebellious".
Admiralnoun
The commander of a whole fleet of ships. Word origin: Arabic amīr, "commander", as in "the commander of the sea".
Embellishverb
To decorate something, or to add made-up details to a story to make it more exciting. Word origin: old French embellir, "to make beautiful" (bel means beautiful).
Legacynoun
Something handed down from someone who lived before you. Word origin: Latin legare, "to leave in a will".

Real treasure: pirate or privateer?

Here's a question that got real sailors into a lot of trouble: what's the difference between a pirate and a privateer?

A pirate attacks any ship, for themselves, and breaks the law. A privateer carries a special document from a king, queen or government called a letter of marque. It says: "You may attack our enemies' ships during this war." Privateers were a bit like a hired navy, and they usually kept part of what they captured.

The trouble is, the same person could be a hero to one country and a pirate to another! Sir Francis Drake was a privateer for Queen Elizabeth I of England. The English knighted him. The Spanish, whose treasure ships he raided, called him a pirate. And remember Captain Kidd from Chapter 6? He set out as a privateer with official papers, and ended up being tried as a pirate.

So when Fortnite calls him Privateer Oscar, it's a clue: he's on somebody's official side. Which side? We'll find out on 21 November.
