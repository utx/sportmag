Chapter 1
Welcome to Crossbones Isle

Forget everything you knew about the old Island. It's gone. In its place is Crossbones Isle: smaller, wilder and wetter, with deserts, jungles, a lantern-lit town, a cursed cave, a giant treasure chest and a rapper's warship parked on the beach.

Because it's a smaller island, only 80 players drop in each match instead of the usual 100. And you don't drop from the Battle Bus any more. This season you jump from the Going Merry, the ship that Luffy and his crew sail in One Piece, hanging underneath a giant balloon painted with the Straw Hat skull. And here's the fun part: in every match, one lucky player is made the Captain. Their name flashes up on screen ("THE CAPTAIN IS..."), and, as the creator Evolve Jake explains, they get to steer the ship and choose its route. So watch out for some very strange flight paths!

All aboard the Going Merry! Press jump when you're over the spot you want. (Frame: TG Plays)

The grand tour

Here are the twelve named locations, from north to south. The letter and number after each name is its grid reference on the map (there's more about grid references at the end of the chapter).

1. Rubber Resort (B1). A holiday island in the far north-west, with a yacht shaped like a giant duck. Rubber duck, rubber resort... and Luffy's body is made of rubber. Coincidence? We think not. It's right on the edge of the map, so it's a calm place to start.

2. The Chest (B2). A little group of islands with an enormous treasure chest sitting on the beach. Is it Captain X's treasure? Nobody knows yet (see Chapter 9).

Luffy rockets across the water to The Chest. Yes, that chest is the size of a building. (Frame: EveryDay FN)

3. Loot Lagoon (D2). Orange desert in the north, where the river meets the sea. The name is a promise.

4. Fortune's Fort (E2). A fortress with thick stone walls on the edge of the desert. Keep an eye on it: one early map called this spot Lil' Uzi's Stronghold, so it might get a makeover for Lil Uzi Vert's Captain's Day on 7 November (Chapter 7).

5. Lantern Landing (C3). A maze of narrow streets lit by hundreds of orange paper lanterns, with pagodas, pink blossom trees and bamboo. It's beautiful, it's busy, and it's a great place to find Wanted Posters (Chapter 6).

Lantern Landing at dusk. (Frame: Perfect Score)

6. Arlong Park (A3). A walled fortress on the west coast. In One Piece, it's the base of Arlong, a villain who is half man, half fish (a shark, to be exact). TG Plays dropped here in his very first match, and so did at least four other players. It's popular!

7. Cursed Cavern (D3). ★ Treasure hot spot. A dark crater in the middle of the island, surrounded by spooky purple trees. Perfect Score's video shows a treasure vault here, behind a green door and piled with gold. Vaults like this need a vault key: TG Plays found one, opened a vault and couldn't believe how much loot was inside. Cursed Cavern is also brilliant for Sprites (Chapter 5). Peek inside a vault in Chapter 4.

8. The $.$. Rocky (E4). ★ Treasure hot spot. A$AP Rocky's enormous grey warship, moored on the east coast, with a helicopter on the deck and "DON'T BE DUMB" painted along the side. The boss lives here (Chapter 7), and so did the first Map Fragment (Chapter 6). Expect company.

Coming in to land on The $.$. Rocky. (Frame: kevinsmak)

9. Loguetown (C4). A busy harbour town in the south. In One Piece, Loguetown is the last stop before the Grand Line, the most dangerous sea in the world. Here it's a great place for a fight, and for Wanted Posters.

10. Windmill Village (B5). A quiet seaside village full of windmills. In One Piece, it's where Luffy grew up. Quiet... until a Devil Fruit appears in the middle of it, as it did in one of TG Plays' matches.

11. Plunder Port (D5). Docks on the south coast, with pirate ships moored offshore. Pirates. Ships. Plunder. You get the idea.

12. Busy Bay (E5). Two islands in the south-east corner, home to the Baratie: a floating restaurant shaped like a fish. In One Piece, it's where the cook Sanji worked before he joined Luffy's crew.

Secret spots

Epic's own map also names a few smaller landmarks. Can you find them all?

The Salty Skull (D4), right next to Cursed Cavern. Giant's Battlefield (C4), between Lantern Landing and Loguetown. The Boogie Boat (E3), a party boat off the east coast. And a mystery landmark called just Henry (D5), near Plunder Port. Who is Henry? We don't know! If you find out, write it in your Captain's Log.

Word Watch

Peninsulanoun
A piece of land almost surrounded by water. Word origin: Latin paene, "almost", and insula, "island". So a peninsula is literally an "almost-island"! Look for the long one poking east from the desert.
Lagoonnoun
A stretch of calm salt water, cut off from the sea by sand or rocks. Word origin: Italian laguna, from the Latin lacuna, "a pool" or "a gap".
Harbournoun
A sheltered place where ships can stay safely. Word origin: Old English herebeorg, "a shelter for an army".
Archipelagonoun
A group of islands close together. Crossbones Isle is really an archipelago! Word origin: Greek, once the name of the sea between Greece and Turkey, which is full of islands.

Real treasure: grid references

How do you tell someone exactly where something is on a map? With a grid. Our map has letters along the top (A to F) and numbers down the side (1 to 6). To give a grid reference, say the letter first, then the number. A handy way to remember: "along the corridor, then up the stairs."

So Cursed Cavern is in D3: go along to column D, then down to row 3.

Real maps work the same way. Street directories, hiking maps and even the game Battleship all use grids. Ships' navigators use a more exact grid that covers the whole planet: lines of latitude (how far north or south) and longitude (how far east or west). Sydney, for example, is about 34 degrees south and 151 degrees east.
