Chapter 6
X marks the spot

Every pirate story has one: a crumpled old map, a big red X, and somebody digging on a beach. In Treasure Hunters, you get to be that somebody. Crossbones Isle is full of hidden treasure, and this chapter shows you how to find two kinds. First, the Sprite Chests you dig up with the help of a Wanted Poster, which you can do in any match. Second, the Map Fragments, one each week, that are slowly building a map nobody has seen in full.

Most of the step-by-step tips here come from a short video guide by Perfect Score, who completed the whole Wanted Poster quest in a single match. We watched it frame by frame, checked what we saw against the game's own words, and added the parts we think a treasure hunter should know.

Wanted: Sprites (only alive)

Sprite Wanted Posters look like the bounty posters in One Piece, with one big difference. The top says Wanted, but instead of a pirate's face there's a giant question mark, and instead of "Dead or Alive", the bottom says "Only Alive: Sprite", because nobody wants to hurt a Sprite. You'll find them pinned to walls and doorways, and when you walk up to one, a Wanted Poster button prompt appears.

A Sprite Wanted Poster on a wall in Loguetown. Walk up to it and press the button. (Frame: Perfect Score)

Here's what Perfect Score's guide says about them: the posters appear in random places in every match, and you can often find more than one in the same named location. So if you spot one, keep looking around before you leave. In the video, two posters turned up in Loguetown and a third in Lantern Landing.

Poster number three, on a wooden building in Lantern Landing. (Frame: Perfect Score)

Word Watch

A reward offered for catching someone or finding something. Word origin: from the old French word bonté, "goodness", which comes from the Latin bonus, "good".
Landmarknoun
Something big and easy to spot that helps you work out where you are, like a lighthouse or a giant chest. Word origin: Old English landmearc, a marker showing where someone's land ended.
Excavateverb
To dig something out of the ground carefully. Word origin: Latin ex, "out", and cavare, "to hollow out". You can hear "cave" in it.

Map in hand

Use the poster and it turns into a Sprite Treasure Map, which goes into your inventory like an item. The game rates it Legendary, the same rarity as the best weapons. Take it out and your character holds the map up in front of them like a real pirate. Your hands are full while you read it, so find a safe spot first.

"Sprite Treasure Map, Legendary." It's yours. (Frame: Perfect Score)

Reading the map on a balcony in Lantern Landing. Treasure first, sightseeing later. (Frame: Perfect Score)

Follow the red dashes

Now look at the ground. A trail of red dashes appears, like the dotted lines on a pirate map, and it leads you towards the treasure. Follow it out of town, through the grass or the bamboo, until you reach something you really can't miss: a giant red X painted across the ground, much wider than you are, with a little mound of dirt in the middle. A glowing column of light rises out of it, so you can spot it from a distance.

There it is: X marks the spot, just outside Loguetown. (Frame: Perfect Score)

The red trail running through the bamboo outside Lantern Landing. (Frame: Perfect Score)

Pro Tip: The red X is big and bright, which means other players can see it too. Before you start digging, have a quick look around. A treasure hunter who gets knocked out halfway through a dig doesn't get any treasure.

X marks the spot

Get out your pickaxe and hit the mound in the middle of the X. A few swings and the dirt flies everywhere, then a Sprite Chest rises up out of the ground. Press Search to open it, and a Sprite pops out, along with some loot. In the video, each Sprite Chest also gave 900 XP.

Dig in the middle of the X. You can already see the top of the chest poking out. (Frame: Perfect Score)

Up comes the Sprite Chest, just outside Lantern Landing. Press Search. (Frame: Perfect Score)

Do it three times and you finish the quest "Use Sprite Wanted Posters to dig up and open Sprite Chests", worth 20,000 XP. Perfect Score's three chests gave three Sprites, including Wayfinder from the second chest and Chester from the third.

Quest complete: three posters, three maps, three chests, and Chester floating next to the last one. (Frame: Perfect Score)

The week 1 Map Fragment

The second kind of treasure is bigger and slower. There are six Map Fragments to collect, torn pieces of an old map, and a new one unlocks each week. In week 1, the quest in the Treasures tab says "Collect Map Fragment at The $.$. Rocky", and it's worth 10,000 XP.

The $.$. Rocky is a huge grey ship moored on the east side of the island, near Cursed Cavern, with giant letters painted on its side. That's also where the week 1 boss, A$AP Rocky, hangs out (see Chapter 7), so land carefully. A creator called kevinsmak spotted the trick that catches lots of players out: the fragment isn't on the top deck. Lots of people land, search the whole deck and find nothing.

3
Break through that floor too, down to the metal level.

Look for a small torn piece of map lying on the floor next to a wooden crate, and collect it.

Look closely at the front. It's a skull-shaped island, just like Crossbones Isle, with a big red X near the top-left corner. Is that where Captain X's treasure is hiding? Nobody knows yet. Word Watch

Fragmentnoun
A small piece that has broken off something bigger. Word origin: Latin frangere, "to break". It's a cousin of "fracture" and "fragile".
Slivernoun
A thin, small piece of something, like a sliver of cake or of glass. Word origin: an old English word meaning "to split".
Ciphernoun
A secret code where letters are swapped for other letters or symbols. Word origin: the Arabic word sifr, meaning "empty" or "zero".

How we checked

Videos are brilliant for seeing how something works, but you have to check the details. Here's what we found.

The auto-captions got a name wrong. YouTube's automatic subtitles called the loading screen "X Marks the Zero Point Fragment". The game's own item list says Sliver. Computers mishear words too!

Names don't always match. One video says "Cursed Cave" and the map says Cursed Cavern. One video title says "Lil' Flaco", but Epic writes Lil' Flacko. We always use the spelling from the game.

A guess, then a check. In his video, one creator guessed there must be six fragments. We didn't print that until we saw proof: the game's own Treasures tab, showing 1/6. When a fact isn't confirmed yet, a good treasure hunter waits for the evidence.

Real treasure: myth or fact?

Myth: pirates always buried their treasure and drew maps with an X on them.

Fact: hardly ever! Most real pirates shared out their loot and spent it quickly. The most famous exception is Captain William Kidd, a Scottish sea captain who was sent out in 1696 to catch pirates and was accused of becoming one himself. In 1699, before he was arrested in Boston, he left a chest of gold and silver buried on Gardiners Island, near New York. It didn't stay buried for long: within a few weeks the governor's men had dug it up and written a list of everything inside. Kidd was taken to London for trial and never came back, and people have been digging for the "rest" of his treasure ever since. Nobody has found any.

So where did "X marks the spot" come from? Mostly from a book. In 1881 the writer Robert Louis Stevenson drew a map of an imaginary island to entertain his stepson, and then wrote a story to go with it: Treasure Island. Its map, its buried gold and its marked spot have been copied ever since, all the way to Crossbones Isle.

Real pirates were criminals who stole from ships and the people on them, which is why Kidd ended up on trial. Fortnite pirates are a lot friendlier.

Crack the pirate cipher

The loading screen promises an "ancient pirate riddle on the back". Here's a riddle of our own. Every letter in this message has been moved one place forward in the alphabet, so A became B, B became C, and so on. To crack it, move each letter one place back.

MPPL VOEFS UIF EFDL

Stuck? It's a tip from this very chapter. The answer is upside down in the box below. Then try writing your own secret message for a friend, moving every letter two places, or three. This kind of code is called a Caesar cipher, because Julius Caesar is said to have used one to send secret messages more than 2,000 years ago.
