Chapter 2
Cannonball run

On Crossbones Isle, nobody walks if they can fly. You can be fired out of a cannon. You can stretch your rubber arms and slingshot yourself across a bay. You can bounce into the sky on a rapper's rocket launcher. This chapter is all about getting around, fast, and (mostly) landing in one piece.

Why does it matter? Two reasons. First, the storm. Second, a creator called Evolve Jake noticed something important about this season: with no Vending Machines to buy travel items from, "it feels much harder to get mobility." So knowing every way to move is a big advantage.

Step one: the jump

Every match starts high in the sky aboard the Going Merry (Chapter 1). Jump when you're over a good spot, then skydive to fall fast, and open your glider near the ground. The trick is to dive straight down if your spot is underneath you, and to glide early if it's far away.

Gliding down over the middle of the island, with Lantern Landing's purple and pink trees below. (Frame: TG Plays)

Cannons: the free flight

Cannons are dotted around the island, and they're not just for shooting at ships. As one guide puts it, you "climb in, aim and fire yourself toward the next POI." (A POI is a "point of interest": a named location.) No items needed, no cost. Just you, a cannon and a lot of courage.

But be careful where you aim. In one match, the creator TG Plays climbed into a cannon, picked his spot, fired... and landed in a tree. We've all been there.

You can also find an item called the Explosive Cannon Ball. Throw it like a grenade, or load it into a cannon for a much bigger bang.

Pro Tip: The Chester Sprite (Chapter 5) cancels fall damage. Cannon flights, cliff jumps and slips off the side of The $.$. Rocky won't hurt you at all. Chester and cannons: best friends.

Something just flew over Lantern Landing. A cannonball? A rocket? On Crossbones Isle, it could easily be another player. (Frame: TG Plays)

The Gum-Gum Rocket

Eat the Gum-Gum Fruit (Chapter 3) and you turn into Luffy, the rubber pirate. His powers include a move called the Gum-Gum Rocket: press aim and you launch yourself through the air like a stretchy rubber slingshot. There's even a quest for it: "Travel distance using Gum-Gum Fruit's Gum-Gum Rocket."

Gum-Gum Rocket! Luffy launches across the bay towards The Chest. (Frame: EveryDay FN)

Evolve Jake's honest verdict: it's fun, but "it isn't the greatest mobility item of all time." Use it to cross water or reach a rooftop, not to cross the whole map.

The Shock Rocket

Beat the A$AP Rocky boss (Chapter 7) and you can grab his Shock Rocket, a rocket launcher with a secret. While you're holding it, you get Shock Rock charges: use one and it blasts you high into the air, and then you can rain rockets down from above. The charges take time to come back, so don't waste them, and they disappear if you swap to a different item.

Zipline Deployer and Overdrive Grenade

Two old favourites are back. The Zipline Deployer fires a zipline to wherever you aim, and then you can ride it across a gap, up a cliff or away from trouble. The Overdrive Grenade is back too. Evolve Jake says he's been using ziplines much more than usual this season, because with no Vending Machines, they're one of the few ways to move fast.

Into the water

Crossbones Isle is mostly sea, so you'll swim a lot. Three things help:

Nami's Tangerine, a new healing fruit, also boosts your speed through water (more in Chapter 3).
Barry O'Fish means less stamina used when you sprint through water.
Kelper slowly heals you while you're in the water.

Word Watch

Projectilenoun
Something that is thrown, fired or launched through the air, like a ball, an arrow or a cannonball. Word origin: Latin pro, "forward", and jacere, "to throw".
Trajectorynoun
The curved path a projectile follows through the air. Word origin: Latin trans, "across", and jacere, "to throw" again. Projectile and trajectory are word cousins!
Parabolanoun
A special kind of symmetrical curve, the shape a thrown ball makes. Word origin: Greek parabolē, "placing side by side".
Mobilitynoun
The ability to move around easily. Word origin: Latin mobilis, "easy to move". A mobile phone is one you can carry around.

Real treasure: the science of a cannonball

Throw a ball and watch its trajectory. It goes up, slows down, curves over and falls back down. About 400 years ago, the Italian scientist Galileo Galilei worked out that if there were no air to get in the way, this curve would be a perfect parabola, the same shape on the way down as on the way up.

So what's the best angle to fire a cannon if you want to go as far as possible? Without air, the answer is exactly 45 degrees, halfway between flat and straight up. Fire flatter and you hit the ground too soon. Fire steeper and you waste your speed going up. And here's a cool fact: 30 degrees and 60 degrees land in exactly the same spot. So do 20 and 70. (Can you see the pattern? The two angles always add up to 90.)

In real life, though, air pushes back on everything that flies. That's called air resistance, or drag. It slows the projectile down, so the real path is lopsided, and for things like balls and arrows the best angle is usually a bit lower than 45 degrees.

And yes, real people have been fired out of cannons! In 1877, a young performer called Zazel became one of the world's first "human cannonballs", at the Royal Aquarium in London. Her cannon didn't use gunpowder at all: it used giant springs to fling her through the air and into a net. Please don't try this at home. Or anywhere.

Try it (safely): On a sunny day, point a garden hose at different angles: almost flat, about halfway up, and almost straight up. Which angle sprays the water furthest? Is it exactly halfway?

How we checked

YouTube's automatic subtitles for Evolve Jake's video kept saying "skyline deployers". We were puzzled, until we checked the list of items that came back this season. He's talking about the Zipline Deployer! Subtitles are a great help, but always check the names against the game.

Do the maths

The Gum-Gum Rocket quest asks you to travel 250 metres with the rocket. In EveryDay FN's video, the quest tracker showed 70 metres done so far.

1. How many metres were still to go?

2. Imagine each rocket carries you about 45 metres. How many more rockets would you need to finish? (Careful: you can't do part of a rocket!)

Answers
1: 250 − 70 = 180 metres to go
2: 180 ÷ 45 = exactly 4 more rockets
