True story #6
No violence, please. We're Tetris.

When a famous game teams up with Fortnite, there are always negotiations behind the scenes. The other company wants its characters to look right, sound right and behave the way fans expect. Sometimes there are rules about what a character is allowed to do. And sometimes, as Epic discovered this season, there's one rule that changes everything.

The Override season was built around famous video games: Sonic the Hedgehog, Pac-Man, Mega Man and, perhaps most surprisingly of all, Tetris, the falling-blocks puzzle game that has been played by hundreds of millions of people since it was invented in Moscow in 1984. Epic's designers had an idea for how Tetris could appear in Battle Royale: a magical item that would rain Tetris blocks from the sky. And, because this was Fortnite, the blocks would hurt anybody they landed on.

The Tetris Company said no.

"We wanted only to do good things"

In an interview with the gaming news site GamesBeat, published on 28 August, the chief executive of The Tetris Company, Maya Rogers, explained what happened. Her words are worth reading in full:

"Tetris is a game for everyone. We're nonviolent, we're an inclusive game, and we wanted to make sure that whatever happened within Fortnite wasn't damaging… Initially, the Tetriminos were coming down and would do damage, but we were very clear that we wanted only to do good things. As it stands, right now the Tetris pieces come down from the sky, and when they clear lines, they heal you. They also act as a shield. We thought it was a perfect way to honor Tetris."

Maya Rogers, CEO of The Tetris Company, speaking to GamesBeat, August 2026

Think about how unusual that is. Fortnite is a game where a hundred players try to eliminate each other. And yet one of its biggest partners said, in effect: you can use our blocks, but only to help people. Rather than walk away, Epic's designers turned the whole idea upside down. The result was the Tetris Rift.

How the Tetris Rift worked

Throw a Tetris Rift and a portal opened in the sky above you. Out of it fell tetrominoes, the famous four-square Tetris shapes, which stacked up in front of you to form a wall. While the blocks were falling, they healed nearby players a little at a time. Even smashing one of the blocks released a little extra healing. And when the falling pieces completed their lines, exactly as they do in the original game, everyone standing close by got a big burst of healing all at once: 100 health, according to the Fortnite Wiki. The PlayStation Store's description of the season called it "a large area-of-effect heal when 4 lines of blocks are cleared". In Tetris, clearing four lines at once is the best move in the game. It's so famous that it's simply called a "Tetris".

The Tetris Rift
WHILE BLOCKS FALL: a small heal for players nearby, over and over.
BREAK A BLOCK: a little bonus healing.
FOUR LINES DONE: +100 health for everyone close by.
AND: the wall is cover to hide behind.
Numbers from the Fortnite Wiki. The Tetris Rift was taken out of the game on 1 October 2026.

That last detail matters. In Fortnite, most healing items only heal the person using them. The Tetris Rift was an area-of-effect heal, which means it healed everyone in an area. Used well, a whole squad could top up together while hiding behind a freshly built wall of blocks. It was one of the most team-friendly items of the season, and it existed only because a company that cared about being inclusive had insisted on it.

Tetris turned up in other places too. The Sunken Shores area filled up with enormous tetrominoes; secret spots there could turn players into Tetris blocks; and a mid-match event called the Tetris Tower let players climb a skyscraper of blocks, with springs and Cheat Codes along the way, to a prize at the top.

The Tetris Rift was vaulted, meaning taken out of the game, in the Fortnitemares update on 1 October. But the story behind it is a good one to remember. The best compromise isn't always halfway between two ideas. Sometimes it's a completely new idea that nobody had thought of until they had to.

Epic listens

Tetris wasn't the only time this season that somebody spoke up and Epic changed the game. Players did it too.

The spin-dash problem. Sonic's Power Sneakers let you curl into a ball and race around. But players complained that the ball was far too easy to shoot. In late August Epic wrote: "We've seen feedback about how easy it is to get hit while spindashing so we've reduced its hitbox." (A hitbox is the invisible shape around a character that bullets have to hit.)

Too much loot. Loot Hacks let players spend Sprite Dust to add powerful weapons to their own chests. Players quickly worked out how to stack the strongest ones. In September Epic said it was "seeing it lead to too many high-powered weapons too often", and capped hacked loot at Legendary instead of Mythic.

Mobile controls. When Epic changed how the camera moved on touch screens, mobile players objected. Epic's September update notes said: "Thanks to your feedback, we reverted it."

The lesson? Telling a game's makers what you think, politely and clearly, really can change things.

How to pull it off

You can't throw a Tetris Rift any more. But area-of-effect healing is still one of the smartest ways to play in squads, and there are other items that do it:

  1. Jellyfish (caught by fishing) heals you and nearby teammates.
  2. Spicy Tacos can be shared with your squad for what Epic calls a "Taco Time party": healing, a speed boost and unlimited stamina.
  3. Shield Kegs (in Zero Build) let your squad share shields from one barrel.
  4. Stick together before you heal. An area heal is wasted if your teammates are spread across the map. Call it out: "Group up, I'm healing!"
  5. Heal behind cover. The Tetris Rift was brilliant because it gave you a wall and healing at the same time. Always find the wall first.
