True story #7
The storm doesn't care who's winning

If you'd asked fans in August to name the best duo in Fortnite, a lot of them would have said the same two names: vic0, from Austria, and Malibuca, from Russia, who play together for the team BIG.

They had good reason. In May, the pair had won a major in-person tournament, the kind where players compete side by side in a giant arena rather than from their bedrooms. Then, in the very week the Override season began, they flew to Paris for the Esports World Cup, one of the biggest gaming events on the planet, and won that too. This time the game was Reload, a faster version of Fortnite on a smaller island, where, for much of the match, eliminated players can come back while a teammate is still alive. Forty of the world's best duos competed for a prize pool of about a million US dollars, and the pressure in the arena was immense.

Match point in Paris

The Paris final used a nerve-racking format called match point. Teams collect points until they pass a target score; after that, points no longer matter, because the first team "on match point" to win a game becomes champion. It's a format designed to produce drama, since a team on match point becomes the target that every other team in the lobby is desperate to stop. vic0 and Malibuca reached match point in the 11th game. They didn't win it. Nor did they win the 12th, as opponent after opponent hunted them across the island. Finally, in Game 13, they won, and with it the championship and 260,000 US dollars. vic0 was named the tournament's most valuable player, with 32 eliminations.

Afterwards, Malibuca was asked about vic0. "He's just the greatest player of all time," he said. vic0 was more interested in the feeling: "I just love it. I could do this all day, every day. It's the greatest feeling every time, y'know?" They had become the first duo ever to win two major in-person events in a row.

So when the Global Championship came round at the end of September, they arrived as the team everybody was trying to beat, and for most of the weekend, astonishingly, nobody could.

Twelve games, ten brilliant ones

As you read in the chapter "Two Points", vic0 and Malibuca led the world championship after the first day without winning a single game. They won Game 7 on the second morning. By the end of Game 10, they were 78 points clear of everybody. Then, in the last two games, they scored nothing at all: 34th place, then 43rd, no eliminations in either. They finished third, four points behind the champions.

What went wrong? Nobody outside the team can know exactly what went through their minds in those final, frantic minutes. But the gaming news site Hotspawn, which reported on every game, described one decisive moment on the second day. Their plan, it said, had been to gather healing items and sit on top of a tower. Then: "They miscalculated the storm when it moved to a five tick and fell, despite being covered in med kits."

In other words, the best duo in the world was beaten not by another player, but by the storm.

Why the storm gets stronger

The storm in Fortnite doesn't merely shrink; it also becomes steadily more dangerous. When you're caught outside the safe zone, the storm hurts you in regular little bursts, called "ticks", and the longer the match continues, the more damage each tick inflicts. In the first few circles you can stroll through the storm, losing a handful of health points that are easily healed. In the final circles, it's an entirely different beast. Pros use phrases like "a five tick" to describe how hard each tick of the storm is hitting, and late in a match, every tick hits much harder than it did at the start.

That's the trap. Healing items take time to use, and late in the game the storm can drain your health faster than a Med Kit can restore it, so it simply doesn't matter how many heals you're carrying if you can't use them quickly enough. vic0 and Malibuca's tower plan only works if the storm isn't on top of you, and for once, the best duo in the world got the timing wrong.

Rotate early, not late

It's worth emphasising that this was not a careless mistake by inexperienced players. It was a tiny miscalculation by brilliant ones, made under enormous pressure after nearly two days of intense competition, and that's precisely why it's such a valuable lesson. If the storm can catch vic0 and Malibuca, it can catch anyone. The storm is relentless. It doesn't care how many points you have, how many tournaments you've won, or how many Med Kits are in your pockets.

Storm Surge: the tournament storm

Tournaments add an extra twist called Storm Surge. If too many players are still alive at certain points in a match, the storm starts damaging some of them, even inside the safe zone. At the 2026 Global Championship, which teams got surged depended on how much damage they'd dealt and taken during the match. The idea is to stop teams from simply hiding and waiting: if you want to be safe from the surge, you have to fight. It's one more reason pros never stop thinking about the storm.

Storm helpers this season

The Storm Scout Sprite gives you a burst of Overdrive (unlimited sprinting) after you take enough storm damage, which can help you escape. When it reaches Level 5, it does something extraordinary: it shows you where future storm circles will be. And watch out for the Dynamic Storm Override: if someone picks it, the storm closes faster as fewer players remain.

How to pull it off

  1. Check the map every time the circle moves. Know where the next safe zone is before the storm starts closing.
  2. Rotate early. Moving while the storm is still far away is calm and safe. Moving when it's right behind you is a scramble.
  3. Never plan to "heal through" a late storm. In the final circles, the storm can do more damage than your heals can fix in time.
  4. Don't get complacent when you're winning. The moment you're leading is exactly the moment to be most careful.
  5. Count the ticks. Each time you take storm damage, notice how much it is. If it's bigger than last time, the storm is getting serious.
