True story #8
The boss you can't beat

Every season of Fortnite tells a story, and every story needs a villain. This season's is Geno: tall, armoured in black and gold, with a glowing symbol on his chest and an enormous machine at the very centre of the Island. His plan is to hack reality itself.

For weeks, Fortnite players worked their way through Geno's story quests, a chain of missions that slowly revealed what he was up to. And then, near the end, the quests asked them to do something that every player has wanted to do since the season began: fly to the centre of the Island and take Geno down.

There was just one problem. You can't.

"Be eliminated by Geno"

The quest chain is cleverly built. It begins with Geno trying to recruit you. In Fortnite's story, you are "the Looper", the hero who keeps coming back to the Island, and Geno wants you as his second-in-command. His early quests seem harmless enough: eliminate some opponents, talk to other characters about him, find pieces of his memories, activate an Override Console. Then the requests get stranger. He asks you to extract Sprites, so that he can, in the quest's own words, "distill their magic essence".

Finally, Geno gives his last order: defeat his own daughter, a character called The Order. And at that point the story turns. The next quest reads: "Take down Geno and defy his commands to save The Order from her father."

So players flew to the middle of the Island, found Geno floating inside a glowing red bubble, and opened fire. And nothing happened. There's no health bar to wear down. Geno is invulnerable. He gives a short speech, and then he eliminates you. It's not a fair fight, because it isn't meant to be one. It's a scripted moment, designed so that every single player loses in exactly the same way.

And Epic didn't hide it. The very next quest in the list simply says: "Be eliminated by Geno".

Why would a game make you lose?

It might seem strange for a game to force you to lose. But storytellers have been doing it for thousands of years. In almost every great adventure, there's a moment when the hero faces the villain too early and gets beaten. In The Empire Strikes Back, Luke Skywalker loses his duel with Darth Vader. In countless superhero films, the hero is beaten badly halfway through. The hero has to lose, learn something, and come back stronger.

That moment does an important job. It shows how powerful the antagonist is, so that when the protagonist finally wins, the victory means something. A villain who can be beaten on the first try isn't very frightening. By making every player lose to Geno, Epic made sure that everybody understood how dangerous he is, and set up a showdown for later. According to the Fortnite Wiki, when the Looper refused to obey him, Geno declared that if they wouldn't join him, they would fear him.

Finishing the quests also unlocked a Lobby Hack code: YourThoughtsAreMine. Fitting, for a villain who wants to control everything.

Will the 1-Up Token save me?

Of course, Fortnite players being Fortnite players, some of them wondered whether there was a way to cheat fate. This season introduced the 1-Up Token, a rare Legendary Power Up that works like an extra life: if you're eliminated while carrying one, you're sent back into the match through a Rift, with your whole inventory and 100 health. So what would happen if you faced Geno with a 1-Up Token in your pocket?

The Canadian creator Typical Gamer decided to find out. In September he posted a short video on his family-friendly TG Plays channel with the title "Will the 1-Up Token Save Me?". It was watched more than 700,000 times. Judging by the reactions in the comments under it, the answer was no: viewers joked about "RIP one up token" and the "aura of Geno" that wouldn't let him respawn. A few other players said their tokens had worked, so this may not be a fixed rule. But it's a good reminder that a scripted story moment isn't a normal fight, and the usual tricks might not apply.

Where to find a 1-Up Token

They're rare. Bosses such as Hush can drop them. They can turn up in boosted Llama Supply Drops. And during the Saturday "Power Hours" events, Epic announced that "1-Up Tokens and Portable Extractors may drop from Chests". A 1-Up Token doesn't take up an inventory slot, but you can only carry one at a time, so don't go hunting for a second.

How to pull it off

  1. Before you face Geno, extract your Sprites. His quest ends with you being eliminated, and if you're carrying unextracted Sprites, you'll lose them. Guides were warning players about this within days.
  2. Treat a 1-Up as insurance, not a plan. It brings you back with your loot, but with only 100 health, and you'll need to heal up fast.
  3. After a 1-Up respawn, get to cover first. Whoever eliminated you knows you might be coming back.
  4. Know your squad's comeback options. Pick up a fallen teammate's Reboot Card and get them to a van. It's free, and it works every match.
  5. Enjoy the story. Losing to Geno isn't a failure. It's chapter one of the hero's comeback.
