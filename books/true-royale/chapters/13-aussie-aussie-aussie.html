True story #10
Aussie, Aussie, Aussie

Playing Fortnite from Australia comes with a few quirks. The big events happen while you're asleep. The servers are a long way from most of the world's players. And yet Australians have always punched above their weight in Fortnite, from some of the most famous YouTubers on the planet to young professionals fighting their way into world championships. This season was no different.

The Great Aussie Sprite Race

Lachlan, who was born in Brisbane, and Loserfruit, who was born in Melbourne, are two of the best-known Fortnite creators in the world, with millions of subscribers between them. They've been friends, teammates and rivals for years, and their friendly rivalry is a big part of what makes their videos fun to watch.

Five days into the new season, Lachlan posted a video on his second channel, More Lachlan, with the title "We Raced To Find Season 4's RAREST Sprites!" The description put it simply: "I raced Loserfruit for the rarest sprites in Fortnite Season 4!" Each new Sprite they found earned points, and rarer Sprites were worth more.

Going by the comments under the video, Lachlan came out on top, and one of the things that helped was winning a match. That matters, because one of the rarest Sprites of all can't be found in any chest or Cheat Code. The Crown Sprite, a Mythic, can only be earned by getting a Victory Royale. It's the only Sprite that is literally a trophy.

The Crown Sprite: you have to win to get one. Picture: Epic Games / Fortnite.

The Crown Sprite only levels up when you win, too, which made it nearly impossible to master at first. In late August, Epic changed the rules so that a single Crowned Victory Royale, a win while you already hold a crown, masters it in one go. It doesn't give you a fighting power at all, just extra Crown Wins, and bragging rights. Some things are worth having simply because they're hard to get.

How to hunt Sprites like a pro

Most Sprites come out of Cheat Codes: glowing panels hidden around the Island, sprayed on walls and tucked into buildings. Walk up to one and it shows you a sequence of arrows. Press the matching directions in the right order and it releases a Sprite. The codes come in colours: blue (Rare), purple (Epic) and orange (Legendary). Harder codes have longer sequences, and better rewards; Epic says orange codes always give a special "variant" Sprite. During Fortnitemares, every code got one arrow shorter.

A few tips that experienced Sprite hunters swear by:

  • Can't find a code? A Cheat Code Locator creates a new code somewhere between 80 and 300 metres away inside the safe zone and marks it for you. Use one right next to an existing code and it may upgrade that code's rarity instead.
  • Arrows look backwards? One guide pointed out that you might be reading the code from the wrong angle. Step round to face it straight on.
  • Cheat Master Sprites make every input count as correct, so you can just mash buttons.
  • Open everything. Chests, Pumpkin Chests and buried treasure from a Lucky Locator can all hold Sprites.

Meanwhile, at the world championship…

Three Oceania duos qualified for the Global Championship in Belgium. The best of them were Phazma, from Australia, and Crusades, from New Zealand, who had grabbed the region's last available place by winning the Oceania Last Chance Finals on 14 August, just before the season began. At the championship itself, against the fifty best duos on Earth, they finished 20th, winning 20,000 US dollars. For Oceania fans watching at midnight, it was something to cheer about.

And then, on 10 October, came the Solo Fast Track: the first big solo qualifier of the season. A hundred of Oceania's best players fought four matches in about two hours, and the top eight went straight through to the regional finals. With only four games, a single bad one costs you a quarter of your whole tournament, so there's no time to warm up.

The winner was Anonbot, an Australian player from the team PWR, the organisation that Lachlan founded. Across the four games, Anonbot finished with 220 points, 15 eliminations and one Victory Royale, a formidable 31 points clear of second place. Phazma, fresh from the world championship, finished fifth, and is through to the finals too.

Want to play in tournaments one day?

Official Fortnite tournaments like the FNCS have rules about who can enter. For this season's solo series, players had to be at least 13 (or older, in countries that require it), have permission from a parent if they were under 18, and have two-factor authentication switched on, an extra security step that protects your account. If you're interested, talk to a grown-up about it first.

What time is that in Sydney?

Epic usually announces times in US Eastern Time, which is 15 hours behind Sydney now that daylight saving has started. So:

Saturday Power Hours, Epic's weekly boosted events, at 2 to 4 pm and 9 to 11 pm Eastern, are Sunday 5 to 7 am and 12 to 2 pm in Sydney.
Big updates usually arrive in the evening, Sydney time. Gaming news sites expected the servers for update 42.40 to go down at about 7 pm on Thursday 15 October, Sydney time, for a couple of hours.
The season is due to end at the very start of November, Sydney time. Epic hasn't announced the exact hour.

One more Aussie quirk: Fortnite came back to Apple's App Store in May 2026 in every country except one. As of October, Epic's own help pages still said it wasn't available on the App Store in Australia, because of an ongoing legal dispute about app payments.

How to pull it off

  1. Race a friend. Copy Lachlan and Loserfruit: set a timer, and score points for every new Sprite you find (more for rarer ones). Most points wins.
  2. Learn the code colours. Blue, purple, orange: the further up that list, the better the Sprite, and the longer the code.
  3. Stay calm on the arrows. There's no timer, and mistakes just reset the code. Slow and accurate beats fast and wrong.
  4. Go for the Crown. You can't find it, only win it. Every Victory Royale is a chance.
  5. Extract your best finds before the end game, unless you're confident you're about to win.
