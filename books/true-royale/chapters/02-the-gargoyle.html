True story #2
The gargoyle that wasn't

Picture the scene. It's the middle of a match, the storm is closing, and you're sprinting across the rooftops looking for an opponent you know is somewhere nearby. You glance at a building. There's nothing on it but an old stone gargoyle, one of those grumpy-looking statues that people used to put on churches and castles. You run straight past.

The gargoyle is SypherPK.

SypherPK is one of the best-known Fortnite creators in the world, with more than ten million subscribers on YouTube. In October 2026, he posted a video with a typically excitable title, "The Prop Hunt OVERRIDE is BROKEN!", and a short clip that was watched more than 120,000 times in a few days. His description summed up the trick: "The gargoyle is the perfect hiding spot… would you have found it?"

To understand how a famous player ended up disguised as a piece of stone architecture, you need to understand the big idea of the whole season: the Override Console.

The machine that rewrites the rules

There are seven Override Consoles on the Island. At the start of a match they're all switched off. Then, as each of the first few storm circles begins, there's a chance that one of the consoles inside the safe zone will power up. According to the Fortnite Wiki, which tracks Epic's changes in detail, the chances since a late-August update have been 30 per cent at the start of the first storm, 50 per cent at the second and 20 per cent at the third, and if no console has switched on by the third circle, one is guaranteed to.

A powering-up console takes about 25 seconds to come fully online, and while it does, it appears on everybody's map. That's when the race begins, because only the first player or squad to reach it gets to use it. The winner is offered three random cartridges, each one a different Match Override, and picks one. The other two disappear for good.

And here's the catch: an Override changes the rules for the rest of the match. Some only help the squad who picked them. But many are lobby-wide, meaning they change the rules for everyone. Big Head Mode gives every single player an enormous head. Infinite Stamina, in Epic's own words, means "you can sprint, like, a lot". And Prop Hunt let anybody in the match crouch still for a few seconds and transform into an object from their surroundings: a bin, a crate, a lamp post or, if you happened to be on the right roof, a gargoyle.

There's one more twist, and it's the reason Override Consoles make every match so tense. When you activate one, a beam fires into the sky, the cartridge slots in with a twenty-second animation, and then your name scrolls across the console's screen, so the whole lobby knows exactly who did it, and where they were. Pick one of the rarer "Squad" Overrides, which only help your own team, and it's even worse: your whole squad is marked as "Hackers" on everyone's map for the rest of the game. Using a console is like setting off a firework that says "I'M OVER HERE".

Hiding in plain sight

So imagine you're SypherPK, and you've just claimed a console and chosen Prop Hunt. Everybody now knows your name. Everybody is coming. And everybody, including you, can now turn into an object. What do you do?

The answer is the oldest rule of camouflage: don't just hide, belong. A bin in the middle of a field looks wrong, and a wrong-looking object gets shot. A gargoyle perched on the edge of a stone building, on the other hand, is completely inconspicuous. It's exactly what you'd expect to see there, so your brain doesn't bother looking twice. That, as far as we can tell from his description, is what made SypherPK's hiding spot so effective: other players ran straight past a gargoyle on a roof without a second glance.

It's a trick that real animals have been using for millions of years. A stick insect doesn't look like a leaf; it looks like a stick, because it lives among sticks. An Arctic hare is white in winter and brown in summer. Predators learn what "normal" looks like, and the best hiders simply match normal perfectly.

Epic changes its mind

Here's the twist in this story. SypherPK's video came out on 10 October, but the original Prop Hunt Override had already been switched off by Epic in a hotfix on 2 October, along with a handful of others. (A hotfix is a small update that Epic sends out without needing a big download.) The clip had been recorded earlier, from one of his live streams. So the trick in the video is one you can no longer do in exactly the same way.

But Prop Hunt didn't vanish completely. For Fortnitemares, Epic added Spooky Prop Hunt, which works the same way except that you turn into Halloween props instead. Every pumpkin on the Island is now a suspect.

Epic switches Overrides on and off surprisingly often. In late August, it took several of the most powerful ones out of the game, then put them back again the very same day. Its official status account admitted cheerfully: "Welp that didn't work. Vaulted Overrides are back…"

That first number is worth thinking about. Twenty million Overrides in a single weekend means twenty million times that somebody, somewhere, sprinted to a console, made a choice, and broadcast their name to everybody else in the match. That's twenty million little gambles.

How to pull it off

  1. Watch the map as each storm circle starts. A console waking up shows on the map and takes about 25 seconds to come online, so you have a short head start if you're close.
  2. Only go if you're ready. Your name will appear on the console, and other players love to rush consoles. Be healed up, carry decent loot, and plan your exit before you arrive.
  3. Think about who the Override helps. A lobby-wide Override helps everybody, but you know about it first. Picked Big Loot (better weapons in chests)? Go and open chests straight away, before everybody else realises.
  4. Squad Overrides are a gamble. They help only your team, but you'll be marked as Hackers for the whole match.
  5. If you get (Spooky) Prop Hunt: crouch still beside objects that match. A pumpkin in a pumpkin patch, not a pumpkin in a bathroom. Then stay still. Moving gives you away.
  6. Hunting a hider? Look for the object that doesn't quite fit, or the one there are suddenly two of. Then test it with a shot.
