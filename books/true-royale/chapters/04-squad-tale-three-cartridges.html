Squad Tale #1 · Made-up story, real tricks
Three cartridges

Meet the squad

Priya plans everything, and is usually right, which is annoying. Mo wants to land wherever the most people are landing, every single time. Tilly hardly ever talks, but hears everything. Jacko collects Sprites the way some people collect footy cards, and talks constantly. None of them are real. Everything they do is.

The second storm circle had just started to shrink when the corner of Priya's screen lit up.

"Console," she said. "Heatwave Harbor. It's waking up."

On the map, a small icon was pulsing beside the water, just inside the new safe zone. A console takes about twenty-five seconds to come fully online, and for every one of those seconds, the icon would be glowing conspicuously on the map of every single player in the match. Priya did the maths without meaning to. Sixty-three players still alive. Twenty-five seconds. Every one of them could see it.

"Go, go, go!" Mo was already sprinting. "We're closest. We get it, we win."

"We get it," Priya corrected him, "and then everyone knows we've got it."

But she was sprinting too.

The choice

They reached the console with eight seconds to spare and crouched, panting, behind a stack of rusting shipping containers while the machine finished its start-up sequence. Then the screen flickered, and three game cartridges rose majestically out of the console, each one glowing a different colour.

Jacko read them out loud, faster than anyone could follow. "Big Loot, that's better chests for everyone, Squad Extra Life, that's Mythic, that's the one where we all come back once, and Squad Level Up, which is also Mythic, and gives every one of us a Kingdom Key."

"Extra Life," said Mo instantly. "Obviously. A whole extra life each."

"And a giant sign over our heads for the rest of the match," said Priya. "Squad Overrides mark you as Hackers on everyone's map. Every squad in the lobby would know where we were until the game ended."

"So we'd have an extra life," said Mo, "and we'd need it."

This was the dilemma at the heart of every Override Console, and all four of them understood it perfectly: the most valuable rewards always came with the most serious risks. Big Loot was safe, but it would help every opponent on the Island as much as it helped them. Squad Extra Life was tremendously powerful, but it would transform them into the most hunted squad in the game.

Tilly spoke for the first time in three minutes. "Footsteps," she said quietly. "North. Two, maybe three players. Coming fast."

That settled it. Priya looked at the third cartridge. "Squad Level Up," she said. "It's the one Squad Override that doesn't reveal our location. We still get our names on the console, but no Hacker markers. And we each get a Mythic weapon."

Mo groaned, but he was too busy watching the north side to argue. Priya chose Squad Level Up.

Twenty seconds

The top of the console rose with a mechanical groan. A beam of light blazed into the sky like a searchlight, and for twenty agonisingly long seconds the cartridge spun down through a shimmering rift before locking into place. Then, letter by letter, the squad's names began to scroll around the base of the console, announcing to the entire lobby exactly who had been there.

"That's it," said Priya. "Everyone knows it was us. Now we leave."

Four Kingdom Keys appeared in their hands: enormous key-shaped swords borrowed from the KINGDOM HEARTS games. Jacko was so excited that he forgot to run. Tilly grabbed his arm.

The squad to the north arrived exactly where Priya expected: on the roof of a tall building overlooking the harbour, the perfect vantage point for shooting down at anyone foolish enough to still be standing next to a console. Their shots cracked off the crates. Mo took a hit and dived for cover.

"They've got high ground," he said. "We can't get up there."

"We don't need to," said Priya. "Read what the Key does."

Jacko had already read it. Of course he had. "Thundaga! It's a lightning attack, and it always strikes the highest ground. It can even go through walls and up cliffs."

There was a short pause while everybody understood what that meant.

"The highest ground," said Tilly slowly, "is that roof."

Four lightning strikes hammered the rooftop almost simultaneously. On the Mythic version, Thundaga strikes in a circle instead of a line, and each strike does 65 damage. The squad on the roof had chosen the best spot on the map for a gunfight, and the worst spot on the map for a thunderstorm. Two of them were knocked down before they could work out what had happened. The third tried to retaliate, jumped down, and discovered that a squad holding Kingdom Keys gets low gravity: Mo floated over a wall towards him like an astronaut and finished the fight with a three-hit combo.

Each elimination with a Kingdom Key released a cluster of glowing health orbs. They scooped them up without breaking stride and kept moving, out of the harbour and towards the next circle, while behind them their names continued to scroll, uselessly, around an empty console.

"Fine," said Mo reluctantly, after a while. "Squad Level Up was the right choice."

Priya didn't say anything. She didn't need to.

Real or made up?

Made up: Priya, Mo, Tilly, Jacko, and this particular fight.

Real: consoles taking about 25 seconds to wake up and showing on the map; the choice of three cartridges; the twenty-second activation; names scrolling around the console; Squad Overrides marking you as Hackers; Squad Level Up being the exception; the Kingdom Key's low gravity, three-hit combo and health orbs; and Thundaga striking the highest ground for 65 damage, even through walls. Squad Level Up was still switched on in early October, but Epic changes the list of active Overrides often, so it might not be there in your next match.

How to pull it off

  1. Don't stand at the console admiring it. Once your name appears, assume a squad is on its way.
  2. Read all three cartridges before choosing. Ask: who does this help, and does it reveal us?
  3. Squad Level Up is the stealth pick among Squad Overrides: Mythic weapons for the whole team, with no Hacker markers.
  4. Thundaga loves high ground. If enemies are on a roof or a cliff above you, the Kingdom Key's lightning will find them.
  5. Use the low gravity. Holding the Key lets you float over obstacles, which is perfect for closing the gap.
