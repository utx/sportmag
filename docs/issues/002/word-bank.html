Word Watch · Issue 2

Every word from this issue's Word Watch boxes, in one place. Try using three of them in a sentence this week — bonus points if one of the sentences is about Manchester United.

Accoladenoun
An award or a big piece of praise. p.38
Adversitynoun
A difficult or unlucky situation. "Character is how you behave in adversity." p.4
Averagenoun
A typical amount, found by sharing the total out equally. Ten goals in five games is an average of two per game. p.17
Capitulateverb
To give in or surrender, often suddenly. p.20
Collapseverb / noun
To fall down suddenly. A team that collapses throws away a lead. p.32
Contentiousadjective
Likely to cause an argument because people strongly disagree about it. p.4
Deadlinenoun
The latest time by which something has to be finished. p.42
Dismantleverb
To take something apart piece by piece. A team that dismantles its opponents beats them completely. p.20
Double-headernoun
Two matches played one after the other at the same ground, on the same day, for the same ticket. p.38
Dynastynoun
Originally a family of rulers. In sport, a team that keeps winning for years. p.28
Emulateverb
To copy someone's achievement, usually because you admire it. p.24
Exhilaratingadjective
Thrilling and exciting, in a way that makes your heart race. p.20
Exodusnoun
A mass departure, when lots of people leave a place at the same time. p.32
Finalenoun
The last part of a show, a season or a piece of music, usually the most exciting bit. p.42
Formidableadjective
Impressive and slightly frightening because it is so strong or difficult. p.24
Hostileadjective
Unfriendly and aggressive. A hostile crowd boos, whistles and tries to put the other team off. p.10
Improbableadjective
Unlikely to happen. An improbable comeback is one hardly anybody expected. p.24
Lethaladjective
Deadly. In football, a lethal striker almost never misses. p.17
Nemesisnoun
An opponent who always seems to beat you. Named after a Greek goddess of revenge. p.10
Outliernoun
Something that sits far away from all the others in a set of data. On a chart, it's the dot all on its own. p.13
Pandemoniumnoun
Wild, noisy chaos. The word was invented by the poet John Milton for the capital city of demons. p.32
Pedigreenoun
A long record of quality or success. "Sydney FC have real pedigree." p.28
Premiershipnoun
In the A-League, the prize for finishing top of the table after the regular season. Not to be confused with the Championship, which is won in the Grand Final. p.28
Prioritiseverb
To decide which things are most important and do them first. p.42
Profligateadjective
Wasteful, especially with something valuable. A profligate team keeps missing good chances. p.4
Prolificadjective
Producing a lot of something. A prolific striker scores lots of goals. p.13
Proportionnoun
A part of a whole, often written as a fraction or a percentage. "A small proportion of players do both." p.13
Resiliencenoun
The ability to bounce back quickly after something goes wrong. p.10
Rivalrynoun
A long-running competition between two sides who badly want to beat each other. p.10
Ruthlessadjective
Showing no mercy. In football, a ruthless team takes its chances without hesitating. p.4
Silverwarenoun
A fun word for trophies, because so many of them are made of silver (or look like it). p.28
Siphonverb / noun
To draw something off from somewhere else. In Fortnite, siphon gives you health or shield back when you deal damage. p.42
Suspensionnoun
A ban that stops a player from taking part for a number of matches. p.24
Telepathicadjective
Able to read someone's mind. Players with a "telepathic" understanding seem to know where each other will be without looking. p.17
Tournamentnoun
A competition in which lots of teams play a series of games to find one winner. p.38
Trionoun
A group of three people or things that work together. p.17
Upsetnoun
In sport, a surprise result where the team or player expected to lose actually wins. p.38
Versatileadjective
Able to do lots of different things well. p.13
