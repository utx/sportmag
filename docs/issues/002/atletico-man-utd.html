Forty-seven seconds

A goal before most fans had found their seats. Four big chances missed. A red card that even the pundits called soft. United's night in Madrid, and what it means.

Match report · Riyadh Air Metropolitano · Tuesday 13 October
AtléticoBarrios 1′
Lookman 79′
2–0
Man UtdYoro sent off 74′
Champions League · League phase, Matchday 2 · Riyadh Air Metropolitano, Madrid · Tuesday 13 October 2026 · Crowd: 69,493 · Referee: Glenn Nyberg

Some United supporters were still finding their seats when it happened. Inside the very first minute, Atlético Madrid played a simple ball over the top, Álex Baena got in behind United's defence and cut it back, and Pablo Barrios swept it in. Ayden Heaven, sliding back, couldn't keep it out. Sky Sports had the goal at 47 seconds. Before most of us at home had taken a sip of breakfast, United were losing.

That is the worst possible start at the Metropolitano. Diego Simeone's teams are brilliant at protecting a lead, and the crowd, roaring and whistling at every United touch, made it feel like a cauldron again. But here's the thing. United didn't collapse. They kept the ball, they kept pushing, and they made chances. Lots of them.

So closeFour big chances, no goals

Bryan Mbeumo was the first to go close, nudging the ball cleverly through the legs of Atlético's defender Cristian Romero (yes, the one who used to play for Spurs) before firing just wide. Bruno Fernandes, playing on the right for this game, tried an ambitious lob that drifted narrowly past the post. Later, after a clever corner routine, Fernandes smacked a shot against the crossbar. According to Sky Sports, United created four "big chances" on the night, their most in a Champions League game since September 2012. They didn't score any of them. On another night, against another goalkeeper, that's at least one goal. On this night, United were profligate, and Atlético were not.

Atlético had their moments too, to be fair. Alejandro Grimaldo's free-kick clipped the top corner where post meets crossbar, and early in the second half Julián Álvarez volleyed against the post. Just before half-time Johnny Cardoso thought he had made it 2–0, but the video assistant referee spotted that the ball had hit Marcos Llorente's hand in the build-up, and the goal was ruled out. At half-time it was still 1–0, and still anybody's game.

The turning pointA red card nobody saw coming

With 74 minutes gone, Atlético substitute Jonathan David ran towards goal and went down under a challenge from Leny Yoro. The Swedish referee, Glenn Nyberg, didn't think it was a foul at first. Then the VAR, Pol van Boekel, asked him to look at the pitchside screen. After watching the replay, Nyberg sent Yoro off for denying a clear goalscoring opportunity.

Plenty of people thought that was harsh. "It is soft! No way, that should not be a red," said the former striker Clinton Morrison, watching for Sky Sports. Five minutes later, with United down to ten men and chasing the game, David's shot was saved by Senne Lammens, and the ball fell kindly for Ademola Lookman, another Atlético substitute, to tap in from close range. Two–nil. Atlético were ruthless at exactly the moment United were wobbling.

The manager"I just don't understand it"

Michael Carrick is usually one of the calmest people in football. Not on Tuesday night. "I don't see it being a red card, I have to say. I really don't agree with the decision," he said. Yoro, he pointed out, "wasn't really moving in a quick direction to goal," and the challenge was "very soft and slow" to be given a red card, "especially to be taken to VAR. I just don't understand it."

He was honest about United's part in it, too: "I'm bitterly disappointed in certain things, and the boys are as well as a group." That seems fair. A red card that contentious deserves an argument. But a team that makes four big chances and misses all of them can't only blame the referee.

The Touchline verdict

Two moments decided this game: a defensive mix-up in the first minute and a red card that most neutrals thought was soft. In between, United carried the game to one of Europe's toughest teams in one of its loudest stadiums. That's not nothing. The finishing has to improve, quickly, but the chances are there.

Our player of the match: Bruno Fernandes, who hit the bar, nearly scored with a lob and was United's most dangerous player all night. Honourable mention to Carlos Baleba, who looked composed on his first start.

The big pictureWhere this leaves United

Nobody at Old Trafford is pretending this is a good run. United have now gone five games without a win in all competitions, and the pressure on Carrick is growing. There was more noise after the match, too: in a television interview, defender Lisandro Martínez criticised the club for not spending enough in the summer transfer window. "I expect more from our technical director," he said. Carrick said he hadn't seen the interview, and that emotions can run high after a disappointing result.

But it's worth keeping some perspective. In the Champions League, United have three points from two games, and there are six still to play: in the new 36-team league phase, the top eight go straight into the last 16 and teams finishing 9th to 24th get a play-off. One defeat in Madrid is a setback, not a disaster. Yoro will be suspended for the next European match, away at Como on Wednesday 21 October (3.45am Thursday in Sydney). Before that comes a trip to Leeds on Sunday. Turn the page.

Rule check: DOGSO

Referees have a funny-sounding name for Yoro's offence: DOGSO, short for "denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity". To decide, they ask four questions. How far was the attacker from goal? Was he heading towards it? Did he have control of the ball? Were any other defenders close enough to help? If the answer to all four points to a near-certain goal, it's a red card. Carrick's argument was about the second question: David wasn't moving quickly towards goal. You be the judge.

Three things we learnedWhat the match told us

  1. Concentration from the first whistle. Conceding inside a minute away from home in Europe is a gift no team can afford. United spent the rest of the night in adversity, and they did well to stay in it.
  2. The chances are coming. Four big chances against Simeone's Atlético is a lot. Turn even one of those into a goal and this is a very different report.
  3. VAR giveth, VAR taketh away. It ruled out Cardoso's goal, which helped United, and then sent Yoro off, which didn't. Expect plenty of arguments about the red card this week.
