The one that got away

Bryan Mbeumo's left foot. A red card for Spurs. Fifteen minutes with an extra man. And still, somehow, Manchester United could not hold on.

Match report · Old Trafford · Saturday 10 October
Man UtdMbeumo 73′
1–1
SpursSolanke 90+2′
Bentancur sent off
Premier League · Matchweek 6 · Old Trafford · Saturday 10 October 2026

For seventy-two minutes, this was a slightly grumpy afternoon at Old Trafford. Neither side could find a way through. Then, in the 73rd minute, Matheus Cunha slipped the ball to Bryan Mbeumo, and the Cameroonian did something wonderful. He shifted the ball onto his left foot and hit a low, fizzing drive into the bottom corner. Old Trafford, which had been muttering, suddenly roared.

Five minutes later things got even better for United. Rodrigo Bentancur had only been on the pitch for about twelve minutes when he shoved Luke Shaw and then appeared to lean his head into the United defender. The referee showed a straight red card. Spurs, who began the day in the relegation zone, were a goal down with ten men and a quarter of an hour to survive.

That should have been the end of the story. A team with a one-goal lead and an extra player is expected to win, and most of the time they do. But football has a cruel sense of humour. In the second minute of stoppage time, Dominic Solanke rose to meet a ball into the box and headed it past the United goalkeeper. One–one. A reprieve for Spurs, and an exasperating finish for everyone in red.

The goalMbeumo's moment

Mbeumo has already become one of the first names on United's team sheet, and goals like this explain why. The finish took real composure. He didn't snatch at it or try to blast the ball through the goalkeeper. He made a yard of space, chose the corner and kept the shot low, which is the hardest kind for a keeper to reach. It's worth remembering that he also scored both goals when United beat Atlético Madrid 2–1 in a pre-season friendly in August. Atlético, as it happens, are next.

The red cardBentancur loses his head

There was no great mystery about Bentancur's dismissal. After a tangle with Shaw he pushed him and then pressed his head towards him, a textbook example of an impetuous moment that a professional footballer cannot afford. Michael Carrick said afterwards that it looked like a red card from where he was standing, and even Spurs' head coach, Roberto De Zerbi, said his player's reaction had been a mistake and was not the right way to behave. Bentancur is expected to be banned for the next three matches.

Strangely, De Zerbi also argued that going down to ten men gave his team more energy and courage. It is the kind of thing managers say. Annoyingly for us, on this occasion it turned out to be true.

The Touchline verdict

This was a game United should have won. They led, they had an extra player for the last fifteen minutes, and they were at home. Closing out a match, keeping the ball and calmly running down the clock, is a skill, and right now it's one United are still learning. The good news: Mbeumo and Cunha are combining beautifully, and on another day this finishes 1–0 and everybody goes home happy.

Our player of the match: Bryan Mbeumo. Obviously.

The big pictureWhere this leaves United

The draw keeps United 12th in the Premier League with six points from six games. That is not where a club of United's size wants to be, and some frustrated supporters booed at the final whistle. Carrick has plenty to fix. But it's worth keeping some perspective: it's October, the gaps between teams are tiny, and United have a goal-scoring forward line that would make most clubs envious. They also start their Champions League campaign with a 4–0 win already in the bag.

Football talk: stoppage time

The referee adds extra minutes at the end of each half to make up for time lost to injuries, substitutions, goal celebrations and VAR checks. It's called stoppage time (or "added time"), and it's written as 90+2′, which means the second minute after the ninety. The fourth official holds up a board to show the minimum amount of time being added. It is, as United discovered, plenty of time to concede a goal.

Three things we learnedWhat the match told us

  1. Mbeumo and Cunha are a proper partnership. The goal came from the two of them combining, and they look like they're thinking the same thoughts.
  2. Game management is the missing piece. Leading late with an extra man, United needed to keep the ball and calm everything down. Instead, Spurs got one more chance, and took it.
  3. Spurs are in a scrap. They're 18th, but they showed real fight. It would be unwise to write them off, even if it's tempting.
