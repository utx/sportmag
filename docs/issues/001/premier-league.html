Around the grounds

Arsenal fight back, Chelsea run riot, Brighton keep flying, and on Sunday Liverpool meet City. A day when, for once, we don't mind who loses.

Round-up · Premier League, Matchweek 6

The Premier League came back from its first-ever three-week international break with a busy Saturday. There were comebacks, a thrashing, a late winner and a ten-man triumph on Wearside. Here's everything you need to know, in the time it takes to eat a bowl of cereal.

Comeback
Arsenal2
Leeds1
Calafiori 62′, Guimarães 70′ · Justin 55′

Leeds led through James Justin and the Emirates went very quiet. Then Riccardo Calafiori bundled one in from close range and substitute Bruno Guimarães won it. Arsenal had lost 3–0 at Brighton before the break, so this was a relief for them.

Rampage
Chelsea5
Bournemouth1
Henderson 2, João Pedro 2, Rogers · Evanilson 51′

Jordan Henderson scored after 68 seconds and Chelsea never looked back. Morgan Rogers' shot took a deflection on the way in, João Pedro scored twice, and Henderson added another. A rampant display that lifts them to fifth.

Ten-man win
Sunderland0
Brighton2
De Cuyper 53′, Hadjam 90+3′

Brighton had to play the last half-hour with ten men and still won comfortably. That's three league wins in a row without conceding a goal. They're third, and nobody should be surprised any more.

Late, late show
Ipswich2
Fulham1
Goals in the 88th minute and stoppage time

Ipswich left it until the very end, scoring twice in the dying minutes to win. Fulham are now bottom of the table, still waiting for their first win.

A point apiece. It keeps Brentford unbeaten in the league this season (two wins, four draws), which is more than plenty of bigger clubs can say.

Word Watch

Wild and unstoppable. A rampant team keeps attacking and scoring.
Schadenfreudenoun
A German word for the feeling of enjoying somebody else's bad luck. Not very kind. Very common among football fans.
Talismannoun
Originally a lucky charm. In sport, the star player a team relies on to make things happen.

Sunday's big oneLiverpool v Man City

Every now and then, the fixture list gives Manchester United fans a gift: a match between two of their biggest rivals where at least one of them is guaranteed to drop points. Liverpool host Manchester City at Anfield on Sunday (4.30pm in England, which is 2.30am on Monday in Sydney, so this one's a highlights-at-breakfast job).

City arrive top of the table with five wins from five, under Enzo Maresca, Pep Guardiola's successor. They also arrive two weeks after being found guilty of 114 charges (see page 14), which is an awkward thing to be carrying around. They'll be without Phil Foden, who is suspended, but Erling Haaland is fit, and Haaland against Virgil van Dijk is the battle everyone will be watching. The last time these teams met, in April, City won 4–0.

Liverpool, in their first season under head coach Andoni Iraola, have an injury list as long as your arm. Cody Gakpo and Hugo Ekitike are out, and Alexander Isak is a major doubt after picking up an injury with Sweden. Without their talisman up front, Liverpool may have to be creative.

The Touchline ideal result

A 0–0 draw. A dull one, with plenty of misplaced passes and maybe a long VAR check about nothing. Both teams drop two points, United fans everywhere enjoy a quiet moment of schadenfreude, and everybody goes to bed early.
