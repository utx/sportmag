Word Watch · Issue 1

Every word from this issue's Word Watch boxes, in one place. Try using three of them in a sentence this week — bonus points if one of the sentences is about Manchester United.

Ambushnoun / verb
A surprise attack from a hidden position. p.40
Anticipationnoun
The excited feeling of waiting for something you know is coming. p.27
Attritionaladjective
Wearing the other side down slowly, bit by bit, rather than beating them quickly. p.10
Audaciousadjective
Bold and daring, in a way that surprises people. p.10
Cacophonynoun
A loud, jumbled mixture of sounds, like 40,000 people singing different songs at once. p.27
Camouflagenoun
Colours or cover that help you blend into the background so you're hard to spot. p.40
Cauldronnoun
A huge cooking pot. In sport, a stadium so loud and fierce it feels like it's boiling. p.10
Composurenoun
Staying calm and in control, even under pressure. p.4
Contriteadjective
Feeling genuinely sorry for something you did wrong. p.24
Derbynoun
A match between two teams from the same city or area. It's pronounced "dar-bee" in England, "der-bee" in Australia, and either way it means trouble for the losers. p.27
Disreputenoun
A state of having a bad reputation. To "bring the game into disrepute" means to make people think less of football. p.14
Exasperatingadjective
Extremely annoying, especially when something keeps going wrong. "United's habit of conceding late goals is exasperating." p.4
Formidableadjective
Impressive and slightly frightening because it is so strong or difficult. p.10
Hiatusnoun
A pause or break in something that usually carries on. p.36
Immaculateadjective
Perfect, without a single mistake or mark. p.24
Impetuousadjective
Acting suddenly, without stopping to think. Bentancur's headbutt was impetuous. p.4
Impregnableadjective
Impossible to break into or capture, like a castle with very thick walls. p.36
Integritynoun
Being honest and fair, and sticking to the rules even when nobody is watching. p.14
Nervelessadjective
Completely calm under pressure. (Strangely, it means the same as "nerves of steel".) p.36
Partisanadjective
Strongly supporting one side. A partisan crowd cheers its own team and boos the other. p.27
Petulantadjective
Sulky and bad-tempered, like a toddler who's been told no. p.24
Rampantadjective
Wild and unstoppable. A rampant team keeps attacking and scoring. p.20
Reconnaissancenoun
Scouting an area to gather information before you act. Often shortened to "recon". p.40
Reprievenoun
A last-minute escape from something bad, like a punishment or a defeat. p.4
Sanctionnoun
A punishment for breaking a rule. (Confusingly, it can also mean "permission". English is like that.) p.14
Schadenfreudenoun
A German word for the feeling of enjoying somebody else's bad luck. Not very kind. Very common among football fans. p.20
Strategicadjective
Planned carefully to help you win in the long run, not just right now. p.40
Systematicallyadverb
Done in a planned, organised way, again and again, rather than by accident. p.14
Talismannoun
Originally a lucky charm. In sport, the star player a team relies on to make things happen. p.20
Unprecedentedadjective
Never having happened before. p.14
